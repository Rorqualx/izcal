# Izcal storage: gaps and opportunities

Compiled 20 October 2026. Primary sources were preferred (specifications, MDN and vendor documentation read in full, issue trackers and forums as prose evidence). Search budget ran out mid-strand; per the strand brief, claims not verified against a read primary source are graded **Medium** (vendor doc snippet, or search-result summary of a primary source) or **Low** (secondary or from memory), and everything unchecked is repeated under Open questions.
Confidence: **High** = primary source read and the claim checked in its text; **Medium** = primary abstract or snippet, or a vendor claim without data; **Low** = secondary source only.

Scope: what shipping browser DevTools show about origin storage today, what they cannot show, the storage bugs developers actually report, what that implies for Izcal's storage observability, and where the engine/shell boundary for quota UI should sit. Evidence rule M-1 part 3 respected throughout: no third-party source code, headers, commits or diffs were read; issue trackers were used as prose only. Fits the existing plans: the Izcal Inspection Protocol (IIP) and recorded sessions (DevTools spec DT-D1, DT-D2), the networking spec's decision log and "who received what" view (NW-D5 area, Section 6.18), and the footprint spec's attribution-history rules (FP-D18).

## 1. What developers cannot see today about storage

The Application panel of Chrome DevTools is the most complete documented storage view of any browser. What it shows, per its own documentation:

| 1.3 | IndexedDB, Cache Storage and Cookies panes show object stores/entries, cache requests, and cookie attributes respectively; the documented model in every case is a **snapshot table with manual refresh**, editable and deletable | — | [Storage guides index (Chrome DevTools docs)](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/devtools/storage/localstorage), siblings (not individually fetched) | 2019–2026 | Medium |

| 1.8 | In Cache Storage views, **opaque (no-cors) cross-origin responses cannot be inspected and are shown with no meaningful size**, because the page itself is denied them; DevTools inherits the page's blindness rather than showing the developer the truth locally | — | [MDN quotas page](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria) (opaque-response semantics), DevTools Cache pane docs | 2026 | Medium |
| 1.9 | There is **no cross-tab view**: each tab's DevTools sees only its own page. **Implications:** today's storage tooling is a set of editable snapshots. Everything causal — who wrote, which transaction, why evicted, why blocked — is absent, and even the one number developers can query (`estimate()`) is documented as an estimate with privacy padding, and in the field disagrees with the browser's own other view. The gap is not visualization; it is that the data does not exist as a queryable fact anywhere. For Izcal this defines the target: storage events as first-class, typed, timestamped, attributed facts in the engine, served through IIP — the storage-side counterpart of the networking spec's decision log and its "one view of every partitioned store" opportunity.

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |

| 2.1 | **Blocked upgrade transactions** are the canonical IndexedDB stall: an open connection that does not handle `versionchange` blocks the new client's upgrade; the spec has no completion or unblock callback (open issue since the spec's early days), so the app cannot even observe when the block clears. Practical advice is user-facing ("close other tabs") | — | [Stack Overflow 40032008](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/40032008/how-should-an-app-react-when-indexeddb-is-blocked); [W3C IndexedDB issue #72](https://github.com/w3c/IndexedDB/issues/72); [javascript.info IndexedDB guide](https://javascript.info/indexeddb) | 2016–2026 | High |
| 2.2 | Stalls are not only app bugs: engine-level hangs occur too — a Chromium issue describes a hanging `versionchange` transaction where connection shutdown blocks on transaction completion. From the developer's side both classes look identical: an `onblocked` that never fires or a request that never settles | — | Chromium issue tracker (prose, via search summary) | 2026 | Medium |
| 2.3 | **"The database is corrupted, delete and reinstall."** `UnknownError: Internal error opening backing store` on `indexedDB.open()` — backing-store (LevelDB) corruption after crashes or unclean shutdowns — is reported across Electron apps, Postman, Obsidian, Figma, Evernote's clipper and Vivaldi. Developers describe apps losing all local data without any notification or error in the page | — | [dev.to, 2026-03-03](https://dev.to/denyherianto/why-your-indexeddb-data-keeps-disappearing-1m0a); [MDN quotas page](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria); [Microsoft Learn Q&A, 2026-08-09](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-au/answers/questions/5970614/) | 2026 | High |
| 2.5 | **No way to know eviction happened.** The top-voted framing of the problem on Stack Overflow is literally "How can I or my app know that Chrome is discarding indexedDB items?" — the answer is: you cannot; there is no event, no API, no log | — | [Stack Overflow 59501358](https://stackoverflow.com/questions/59501358/how-can-i-or-my-app-know-that-chrome-is-discarding-indexed-db-items) | 2019–2026 | High |
| 2.6 | **Quota errors that are not (quite) quota errors.** `QuotaExceededError` fires at the 5 MiB Web Storage cap long before any disk concern, in private modes under much lower caps, and in Safari at ~1 GiB followed by a user prompt; and per web.dev "it's possible for the available quota to exceed the actual amount of storage available". The error name gives no reason code, so apps cannot distinguish "you wrote too much" from "the device is full" from "private mode" from "Safari asked the user and they said no" | 5 MiB; ~1 GiB | [MDN quotas page](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria); [web.dev storage-for-the-web](https://web.dev/articles/storage-for-the-web) | 2024–2026 | High |
| 2.7 | What tooling would have found each: 2.1/2.2 need a **connection and transaction view** (who holds connections, which upgrade is blocked by whom, since when); 2.3 needs a **write-ahead record of last-known-good state** surfaced to the developer ("this database's last clean flush was T; these writes are after it") and an error that names the failing store; 2.4/2.5 need an **eviction decision log**; 2.6 needs typed reason codes on quota failures, which the platform API itself does not carry | — | Synthesis of 2.1–2.6 | — | — |

**Implications:** every major IndexedDB pain class is a *missing fact* class, not a missing chart. A browser that records, per its own storage service, the transaction state machine, connection ownership, corruption boundaries and eviction decisions could answer all four with views, and — because Izcal records anyway — could answer them after the fact, from a bug report's recording. These are also the answers an agent needs in structured form (B9), far beyond what today's consoles expose.

## 3. Measurement and monitoring

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | `estimate()` is imprecise by design (padding, compression, deduplication — MDN) and wrong in the field (Section 1.7). Yet it is the *only* page-facing measurement API, so sites that care (offline-first apps, media apps) build their own heuristics on top of a lying number | — | [MDN quotas page](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria); Section 1.7 | 2026 | High |
| 3.2 | `persist()` outcomes are opaque and engine-specific: Firefox prompts the user; "Safari and most Chromium-based browsers … automatically approve or deny the request based on the user's history of interaction with the site and do not show any prompts to the user". A site cannot learn why it was denied; the user never learns it was asked for | — | [MDN quotas page](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria) | 2026-01-05 | High |
| 3.3 | Chrome's own reassurance is statistical: "research from the Chrome team shows that data is very rarely deleted by the browser" if the site is visited regularly — a vendor claim about its own heuristic, published without a developer-visible counterpart | — | [MDN quotas page](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria) | 2026-01-05 | High (quote) / Medium (claim) |
| 3.4 | Safari's 7-day cap on script-writable storage (since Safari 13.1, 2020) evicts IndexedDB, Cache API and service-worker registrations after 7 days of browser use without site interaction; Chrome proactively clears storage of sites unused for a policy period in some configurations. Neither is visible to the site or in DevTools | 7 days | [web.dev storage-for-the-web](https://web.dev/articles/storage-for-the-web); [MDN quotas page](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria) | 2020–2026 | High |
| 3.5 | The Storage Buckets API lets sites declare buckets with eviction priorities; the browser "may choose to delete each bucket independently". Prioritization exists, but no tool shows bucket state, priority, or which bucket the engine would evict first | — | [web.dev storage-for-the-web](https://web.dev/articles/storage-for-the-web) | 2024-09-23 | Medium (API existence High; tooling absence from indexed docs) |

**Implications:** the monitoring surface is one estimate, one unverifiable grant, and heuristics the site can only discover by losing data. Izcal's version of this is deterministic: exact usage per key and bucket (the engine owns the store, so no padding is needed *toward the local user and DevTools*), a recorded `persist()` decision with its reason, and an eviction predictor ("if pressure continues, next evicted: bucket X of origin Y, because LRU position, not persisted"). Page-facing privacy bounds stay as the spec requires (Section 5); this is about what the local user's tools see.

## 4. Izcal's opportunity list

Grounded in Sections 1–3 and in what Izcal already has: deterministic recording with execution points (B1, DT-D2), typed capability IPC visible end to end (B2) — and storage operations cross process boundaries as typed messages, so every storage operation is already an inspectable, recordable event — the mutation-log pattern (B4), spec-anchored algorithms (ANC-1, applicable to the Storage standard's quota/eviction steps and IndexedDB's transaction steps), the networking spec's decision-log and who-received-what pattern, redaction with verified reproduction ranges (DT-D2/D5 rule 6, storage values already a sensitive class), and the agent principal (B9).

Ranked by developer value (weight of the evidence in 1–3) against cost:

| Rank | Feature | What it answers | Evidence it serves | Cost and dependencies |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Eviction decision log + explanation.** The storage service records every eviction decision as a typed fact with reason: pressure level, policy (LRU position, persisted flag, bucket priority, 7-day idle rule), what was deleted, at what time. A view answers "why did my data disappear", and the shell's site-data page shows it to users. Mirror of the networking decision log; also the engine fact behind the shell's quota UX (Section 5) | 2.4, 2.5, 3.4 | Low–moderate: it is a log beside an algorithm the engine must implement anyway; the design precedent (network decision log, `_izcal` HAR fields) exists. Must respect FP-D18-class privacy rules (it reconstructs usage history) |
| 2 | **Per-key provenance / storage mutation log.** Every write recorded with: key, old value, new value, size delta, principal (frame, script position or agent principal), execution point, transaction ID and virtual time. "This key was last written by this script position at this virtual time, in this transaction" — queryable through IIP like every other why-query | 1.4, 2.3, and the whole post-hoc debugging story | Moderate: storage ops are already typed IPC crossing processes (B2) and already recorded in recordings; the work is the per-key index and the view. Shares DT-D3's chain machinery (script → operation → store) |
| 3 | **Transaction waterfall.** Every IndexedDB transaction as a span: request → open → (upgrade) → blocked-by → complete/abort, with participants (connections, tabs, workers), anchored to the IndexedDB spec's transaction steps. Blocked upgrades become visible: "upgrade blocked since 00:41 by connection from tab 2, which has no `versionchange` handler" | 2.1, 2.2, 1.5, 1.9 | Moderate: needs connection identity across contexts of one (site, container) and a live event stream over IIP; cross-tab within the recorded browsing context group is exactly DT-D2's recorded unit |
| 4 | **Storage-diff scrubbing in recorded sessions.** The store at any execution point; diff any two points; scrub back to before the corrupt write; retroactive "when did this value change and why" like the console/network views. Redaction as in DT-D2 (consistent placeholders, stated reproduction range) | 1.1–1.3 (state-only views), 2.3 | Low incremental cost once DT-D2 recorded sessions and the mutation log exist — it is a new view over existing records. Values are a sensitive class: redaction rules already written |
| 5 | **Exact usage surface.** Engine reports exact logical and physical bytes per key, store, bucket, origin and partition to DevTools and the shell (never to content). Kills the estimate() disagreement class at home: DevTools, the API estimate (still padded, for page-facing compat) and the shell all derive from one true ledger | 1.7, 3.1 | Low–moderate: requires the storage service to maintain per-key accounting it may not otherwise keep; a spec-anchored quota algorithm makes the number checkable (DT-18-style "views are true" target) |
| 6 | **Typed quota-failure reasons.** Izcal's storage service reports quota failures as typed reasons (origin cap, browser cap, private-mode cap, policy cap) to DevTools and the shell; page-facing `QuotaExceededError` stays web-compatible, DevTools shows the truth | 2.6 | Low: a reason code alongside a failure the engine already produces |
| 7 | **Deterministic quota simulation and storage replay.** Re-run a recording's storage operations under a different quota, eviction policy or bucket priorities, in simulation, and diff the outcomes ("under policy X, this origin survives"). Precedent: Chrome 88's simulate-quota checkbox, but deterministic, replayable and diffable | 1.2, 2.4, 3.5 | High: needs the storage service in the simulator and a replayable start state for stores that outlive the recording — solvable the way the networking spec solves it (record the values read, re-execute writes and compare), but it is new machinery; gate on the recording probe like DT-D2 |
| 8 | **`persist()` decision facts.** Whichever policy Izcal chooses (shell prompt, recommended — see Section 5), the decision and its reason are recorded facts, shown in DevTools and site data | 3.2 | Low once the policy exists; the policy itself is an owner decision |

What **not** to build: a per-key write timeline as the primary view of localStorage for its own sake — value comes from tying writes to the other timelines (network, console, transaction), i.e. the storage log must join the `caused_by`/execution-point model of IIP (DT-D1), not be a sixth isolated panel. The DevTools spec's DT-19 rule (every view needs a task, an owner, usage data) applies to all eight.

**Implications:** items 1–4 are cheap-to-moderate extensions of machinery Izcal needs anyway (typed storage IPC, recording, the decision-log pattern) and answer the four highest-pain bug classes; items 7–8 are genuinely new capability that only a deterministic engine can offer and should be gated on the recording probe like every recording-backed feature. All storage observability data is browsing-history-adjacent: FP-D18's retention, clearing and private-window rules should govern it from the start.

## 5. Quota UX: what users see, and where the engine/shell boundary should sit

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **Chrome:** modern Chrome does not prompt for quota at all; "most modern browsers won't prompt the user, and will allow a site to use up to its allotted quota". Users manage storage reactively in Settings under site data ("View permissions and data stored across sites", per-site delete) and Clear browsing data. Enabling "Clear cookies and site data when you close all windows" silently caps quota at ~300 MB | ~300 MB cap | [web.dev storage-for-the-web](https://web.dev/articles/storage-for-the-web) | 2024-09-23 | High |
| 5.2 | **Safari:** the remaining prompter — ~1 GB initial quota, then a user prompt increasing it in 200 MB steps; the author of the vendor guide notes "I was unable to find any official documentation on this". Plus the 7-day idle eviction | 1 GB; 200 MB steps | [web.dev storage-for-the-web](https://web.dev/articles/storage-for-the-web); [MDN quotas page](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria) | 2024–2026 | High (behavior as reported; Medium for exact numbers — unofficial) |
| 5.3 | **Firefox:** `persist()` shows a user prompt; site data is managed per site in Settings (Manage Data). Group limit 10 GiB / 10% of disk (MDN 2026) — web.dev's older 2 GB eTLD+1 / 50%-of-free figure **conflicts**; flag for the fact-check | 10 GiB group limit | [MDN quotas page](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Storage_API/Storage_quotas_and_eviction_criteria); [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/storage-for-the-web) | 2026-01-05 vs 2024-09-23 | High that they conflict; numbers per each source |
| 5.4 | Today's boundary is muddled: **the engine decides silently** (Chrome/Edge auto-approve or deny `persist()` on engagement heuristics, no prompt), **the shell shows only aggregates** (settings pages list bytes per site after the fact), and DevTools is the only place with any breakdown — for developers, not users | — | MDN quotas page; Section 1.2 | 2026 | High |
| 5.5 | First-principles boundary for Izcal, consistent with the networking spec's "who received what" view (engine emits deterministic facts; shell owns consent and prompts) and the shell UI spec's trusted-region prompting: **the engine reports facts** (exact usage, quota, persisted status, eviction events and reasons, per key/bucket/origin/partition), **the shell asks** (persist() grant — a real prompt, never a silent engagement heuristic, per P1's no-silent-authority stance; site-data deletion with per-origin, per-bucket detail; a storage-pressure warning before any eviction of user-visible data), **DevTools and agents consume the same facts through IIP**, never a parallel channel | — | Synthesis; anchors: networking spec Section 6.18, FP-D18, shell UI design UI-9/UI-10 | 2026 | — (design position, argued from cited principles) |

**Implications:** the incumbent UX treats storage as invisible until the user goes digging in settings — and the one decision that matters for data durability (`persist()`) is decided by hidden heuristics in two of three engines. Izcal can make the boundary clean: deterministic engine facts, shell-owned consent, one fact source for user, developer and agent. The eviction-explanation log (Section 4, rank 1) is the shared substrate: it powers the developer view, the shell's site-data page, and the pre-eviction warning.

## Open questions

- **Adoption and size numbers (6.3):** what share of pages use IndexedDB, localStorage, Cache Storage, OPFS (HTTP Archive use counters, Chrome Platform Status), and what is the median/upper-percentile origin storage in the wild? Unfound within budget; must be sourced before the spec cites any threshold.
- **Firefox quota conflict (5.3/6.1):** 10% of disk / 10 GiB group (MDN, 2026) vs 50% of free disk / 2 GB eTLD+1 (web.dev, 2024) — which is current? Likely an MDN update; verify in Firefox documentation.
- **Safari quota numbers (5.2):** the 1 GB/200 MB-step figures are explicitly undocumented by Apple; MDN's macOS 14/iOS 17 percentages may supersede them. Verify against WebKit's prose blog posts (never source).
- **Cache Storage opaque bodies in DevTools (1.8):** confirm what the current panel shows for opaque responses (size shown, preview behavior) from the shipped UI or its documentation.
- **Storage Buckets state (3.5):** current shipping status, default eviction behavior across engines, and whether any tool surfaces bucket priority; needed before ranking feature 7.
- **Cross-tab storage views:** does any browser today show same-site storage activity across tabs (the DevTools spec's research found none, but verify against recent Safari/Firefox releases)?
- **Corruption rates (2.3):** is there any published measurement of IndexedDB corruption frequency beyond single-app reports? If not, this is a candidate for Izcal's own opt-in telemetry and a black-box measurement (M-1: never from source).
- **`estimate()` padding magnitude (1.7/3.1):** how large is the padding and quota fuzzing per engine in practice — a black-box measurement to plan, since only source states the constants.
- **Izcal-internal:** does the storage service design (HGBA-2 4.6, embedded stores, per-key service processes) support per-key accounting cheaply enough for features 2 and 5, and can the eviction decision log respect FP-D18's 24 h/7-day retention without the shell needing a new clearing surface? These are design questions for the synthesis, flagged here because they gate ranks 1–2.
