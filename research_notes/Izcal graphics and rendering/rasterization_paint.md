# Izcal graphics and rendering: rasterization, paint order, display lists, effects and determinism of raster

Compiled 19 October 2026. Primary sources were preferred (papers, specifications, maintainers' design documents and blog posts written as prose, vendor documentation).
Confidence: **High** = primary source read and the claim checked in its text; **Medium** = primary abstract or snippet, a vendor claim without data, or a fact stated from the researcher's knowledge of a published source not re-read this session; **Low** = secondary source only.

M-1 note: no engine source code was read for this note. No code files, headers, commits, diffs, code-review pages or source browsers of Chromium, Skia, Gecko, WebRender, WebKit, Servo, Vello, Pathfinder, Blend2D, wgpu, Dawn, Mesa or any other engine were opened. Two search results pointed at `skia.googlesource.com` mirrors of Vello READMEs; they were not opened. Stampfl's thesis (a published ETH thesis) contains short code listings and footnote links into Skia and Vello repositories; only its prose and measurements are used, and the links were not followed. The feTurbulence C code quoted by row 5.4 is **specification text** (Filter Effects 1), not engine source. Where a value is stated only in an implementation, it appears below as an open question for black-box measurement.

## 1. Fill rasterization algorithms

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **Coverage accumulation (signed area).** Each line segment deposits signed area and cover deltas into a dense per-pixel buffer, and a prefix sum along each row yields exact box-filtered coverage under the non-zero or even-odd rule after a final mapping. This is the libart/font-rs family. See RC 1.1 for font-rs and fontdue. Exactness depends on the arithmetic: with fixed-point inputs and integer accumulators the result is bit-exact and ISA-independent; font-rs as published accumulates in `f32`. | — | RC 1.1 | 2016 | Medium |
| 1.2 | **Sparse strips (Levien's design document, January 2024).** Output is **4-pixel-tall strips of variable width**: a block of anti-aliased alpha values followed by an optional "sparse fill" span, covering the path with no overlap, "a form of run length image compression". Pipeline: flatten and stroke-expand, generate **4×4 tiles**, sort tiles by (path id, y, x), scan to allocate strips and compute coarse winding, then render. The design borrows from Li et al. 2016 (row 1.6). Estimated against Li: about half as many tiles (4×4 versus 2×2 boundary fragments), tiles packed to **12 bytes**, **6.4× less atomic traffic**, **6× fewer primitives** (2× from 4-pixel strips, 2× from merging columns, 1.5× from combining alpha and fill). "Too early to make performance claims." Memory for retained strips grows **linearly** with glyph size, against quadratically for atlases. | tile 4×4; 12 B/tile; 6× fewer prims (estimate) | [Levien, "Sparse strip path rendering" (prose Google Doc)](https://docs.google.com/document/d/16dlcHvvLMumRa5MAyk2Du_MsjF32w0G-n7L9NOJUbRI/) | 2024-01 (date per Stampfl 2025 §1) | High (text read); estimates are the author's |
| 1.3 | **Sparse strips on the CPU (Stampfl's thesis).** A complete CPU renderer was built on the design. It is "competitive … for small-sized shapes and outperforms nearly all other renderers, including Skia or Cairo, as the shape size increases"; **Blend2D stays faster in "the vast majority of cases"**. Weakness on small geometry is attributed to the fixed 4×4 tile and to no special path for pixel-aligned rectangles. It has a **u8/u16 integer pipeline** (blend as `t·c1 + (255−t)·c2` with u16 intermediates and a shift-and-add approximation of ÷255) and an **f32 pipeline**. The thesis says integer is "usually faster" but loses precision as overlaps accumulate. Future work names a **CPU/GPU hybrid** (CPU up to coarse raster, GPU fine raster) and asks whether **8×8 tiles** suit the GPU better. | 1.4 | **Hybrid CPU/GPU sparse strips ("vello_hybrid").** Per the thesis (1.3) and the design doc (1.2): the CPU does flattening, tiling, sorting and strip generation; the GPU draws strips as rectangles with "a fairly simple fragment shader". Stated goals: run on GPUs **without compute shaders** and avoid Vello's "significant slowdown at small zoom levels". The crates are `Apache-2.0 OR MIT` ([crates.io vello_hybrid 0.2.0](https://crates.io/crates/vello_hybrid), [vello_cpu 0.3.0](https://crates.io/crates/vello_cpu)) but are **excluded by HGBA-2 2.3.0 (Vello named) whatever their licence**; evidence only. | 1.5 | **Compute-shader sort-middle rasterizer (piet-gpu, ancestor of Vello).** Paths, not segments, are sorted; segments go into per-tile lists with atomics; **backdrop** (winding entering a tile) is propagated with `atomicAdd` and a prefix sum; fills use **exact signed area**; strokes (then) used **distance fields**; flattening on the GPU computes the subdivision count first. | 1.6 | **Scanline rasterization on the GPU (Li, Hou, Zhou).** Parallel over **boundary fragments** (pixels the outline crosses). | 1.7 | **Massively-parallel vector graphics (Ganacim, Lima, de Figueiredo, Nehab).** A parallel-built **shortcut tree** gives random access to the scene; a sample scheduler shares samples between overlapping filter footprints (a 4×4 cubic B-spline with 512 samples per pixel, "4×4×32"); front-to-back rendering stops once a sample is opaque (**33%** faster on Paris 30k). | 1.8 | **Random-access vector graphics (Nehab and Hoppe).** A coarse lattice whose cells hold variable-length, cell-specialized encodings of the overlapping primitives, interpreted in a pixel shader. **Implications:** the strip/tile family (1.1–1.3, 1.6) is the strongest published basis for Izcal's **integer CPU reference rasterizer**: coverage from fixed-point signed area, strips as the interchange format, and the same strips consumable by a GPU fragment shader (1.4) without compute. 1.7 shows that per-path sequential GPU rendering (stencil-then-cover) collapses on dense content, so the GPU path must parallelize across paths.

## 2. Strokes, flattening and dashes

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Stroke-to-fill is "deceivingly difficult".** Nehab analysed **22 third-party strokers**: **14** treat stroking as curve offsetting and are wrong near high curvature; the other **8** handle it with no accuracy guarantee and often too many segments; **all but 2 ignore inner joins** (visible gaps); **all but 2 "fail spectacularly"** near cusps and "almost cusps". "Following the standards to the letter produces discontinuous results at cusps." The fix uses **evolutes** as well as offsets in high-curvature regions, plus robust cusp detection. He notes that virtually every engine converts strokes to fills before rendering. Floating-point error analysis is explicit (u = 2⁻²⁴ or 2⁻⁵³). | 22 / 14 / 8 / 2 | [Nehab, "Converting stroked primitives to filled primitives", TOG 39(4)](https://w3.impa.br/~diego/publications/Neh20.pdf); [project page with test suites](https://w3.impa.br/~diego/projects/Neh20/) | 2020-07 | High |
| 2.2 | **GPU-friendly stroke expansion (Levien and Uguray).** A fully parallel compute-shader stroker that outputs **lines or circular arcs**, using **Euler spirals** to approximate parallel curves and evolutes, and a parallel path encoding (tag stream plus monoid prefix sums). Typical tolerance **0.25 device px**. On Nehab's timings set: the whole set takes **< 1.5 ms on every GPU except the Mali-G78**, which is **~14× slower than M1 Max** (waves.svg: 13,308 segments → 475,855 lines or 181,229 arcs, **3.48 ms** on Mali). Arc output halves execution time. **Dashing was done on the CPU.** It was written in WGSL and run on Metal and Vulkan. | <1.5 ms; 14×; 2× | [Levien and Uguray, arXiv 2405.00127 (HPG 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00127) | 2024-04-30 (v2 2024-06-29) | High (text read); Medium (venue) |
| 2.3 | **Polar stroking (Kilgard).** Argues that PDF, SVG and PostScript define stroking only by a brush analogy, with no rigorous inside/outside rule for samples. It proposes a theory of stroking with a bounded tessellation error and no recursion, plus **uniform steps in tangent angle**, which also gives arc length for dashing. Implemented on GPUs; no numbers in the abstract. | — | [Kilgard, arXiv 2007.00308, TOG 39(4) 145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.00308) | 2020-07-01 | Medium (abstract) |
| 2.4 | **Analytic quadratic flattening.** Map the quadratic to y = x²; the number of segments is a closed-form integral of 1/(1+4x²)^¼ (approximated), rounded up, so **the count is known before emitting points** and each point is computed independently (parallel-friendly). Error is spread evenly across segments, unlike greedy subdivision. No percentage or speed figures are given, only "significantly fewer segments". | — | [Levien, "Flattening quadratic Béziers"](https://raphlinus.github.io/graphics/curves/2019/12/23/flatten-quadbez.html) | 2019-12-23 | High |
| 2.5 | **Euler spirals for parallel curves.** Background posts behind 2.2: [Cleaner parallel curves with Euler spirals](https://raphlinus.github.io/curves/2021/02/19/parallel-curves.html) and [Parallel curves of cubic Béziers](https://raphlinus.github.io/curves/2022/09/09/parallel-beziers.html). Titles and dates were confirmed from the blog index; the bodies were not read. | — | Levien's blog index | 2021-02-19; 2022-09-09 | Medium |
| 2.6 | **SVG 2 stroke shape is an "ideal" with leeway.** The spec gives an algorithm for the **ideal stroke shape** and dash positions, but "implementations are given some leeway to deviate from this description for performance reasons", and stroke shape "may in some cases, such as at extremely tight curves, differ across platforms". Dash positions are defined precisely (including `pathLength` scaling); an all-zero dash array draws solid; zero-length subpaths with butt caps are not stroked. | — | [SVG 2 §13.5–13.6, stroke shape](https://www.w3.org/TR/SVG2/painting.html#StrokeShape) | 2018 CR text | High |
| 2.7 | **Cusp continuity conflicts with literal spec reading.** 2.1(g) says a literal reading of the standards yields discontinuous output at cusps. Izcal needs a stated rule for which one wins: spec letter or continuity. | — | 2.1 | 2020 | High |

**Implications:** Izcal's stroker should be designed from Nehab 2020 (evolutes, inner joins, cusps) and Levien–Uguray 2024 (parallel encoding, arcs, 0.25 px tolerance), with **dashing defined by SVG 2's dash-position algorithm**. Strokes must be expanded **once, in exact or fixed-point arithmetic, on the CPU** for the reference path. A GPU expander is an optimization whose output must match within the GPU tolerance.

## 3. Anti-aliasing, conflation and the colour space of blending

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **Conflation artifacts.** Using coverage as alpha conflates two quantities. Where two shapes share an edge (a contour plot of abutting triangles, a shape drawn over an identical shape), coverage-as-alpha lets the background bleed through the seam. Ganacim et al. show this on a Mathematica contour plot. Supersampling with real per-sample compositing avoids it; analytic area coverage does not. Stampfl gives the same definition. | — | [Ganacim et al. 2014 §6 "Conflation"](https://w3.impa.br/~diego/publications/GanEtAl14.pdf); [Stampfl 2025 §2](https://ethz.ch/content/dam/ethz/special-interest/infk/inst-pls/plf-dam/documents/StudentProjects/MasterTheses/2025-Laurenz-Thesis.pdf) | 2014; 2025 | High |
| 3.2 | **Hybrid anti-aliasing (exact where provable, sampled otherwise).** Accumulate both the **mean winding number** over the pixel (analytic) and a **vector of per-sample winding numbers**. "When the minimum and maximum winding numbers within a pixel differ by at most one, the mean winding number produces an exact alpha value." Otherwise conflation would occur, and the sample vector is resolved as in MSAA. The predicate is a cheap bit test (AND with ~1, OR-reduce). | — | [Levien, sparse strip design doc, "Hybrid antialiasing"](https://docs.google.com/document/d/16dlcHvvLMumRa5MAyk2Du_MsjF32w0G-n7L9NOJUbRI/) | 2024-01 | High |
| 3.3 | **CSS compositing is specified in gamma-encoded sRGB by default.** Compositing 1 defines simple alpha compositing on premultiplied values and never names a colour space. SVG 2's `color-interpolation` (initial **sRGB**) covers "compositing and blending of graphics elements into the current background". CSS Color 4 says implementations "must handle alpha according to … Simple alpha compositing" and that interpolation between legacy sRGB colours happens "in gamma-encoded sRGB space" for web compatibility. So **edge coverage blended as alpha is applied in encoded sRGB** on the web. | — | [Compositing 1 §5.1](https://drafts.csswg.org/compositing-1/#simplealphacompositing) (ED 2026-10-08); [SVG 2 §13.9](https://www.w3.org/TR/SVG2/painting.html#ColorInterpolation); [CSS Color 4 §10.13, §12](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-color-4/#interpolation-space) (ED 2026-10-09) | 2026 | High |
| 3.4 | **Linear-light AA is "better looking" but not web-compatible.** Levien's piet-metal did "all antialiasing and blending in linear sRGB" to avoid "rope-like" artifacts. For text, RC 3.1 (gamma 2.2 makes 50% coverage look too dark) and RC 3.4 (LCD text needs an opaque destination) apply. Rendering shapes this way would diverge from reftests built on encoded-space compositing (3.3). | — | [Levien, "2D Graphics on Modern GPU"](https://raphlinus.github.io/rust/graphics/gpu/2019/05/08/modern-2d.html); RC 3.1, RC 3.4 | 2019-05-08 | High |
| 3.5 | **Filters run in two different spaces by default.** SVG filter primitives default to `color-interpolation-filters: linearRGB`, but CSS shorthand filter functions "must operate in the sRGB color space" ("color-interpolation-filters has no affect for Filter Functions"). So `filter: blur(4px)` and `filter: url(#f)` with `feGaussianBlur` differ by default. | — | [Filter Effects 1 §5 and §9.1](https://drafts.csswg.org/filter-effects-1/#FilterProperty) (ED 2026-09-22) | 2026 | High |
| 3.6 | **Gradients default to Oklab, except legacy colours.** CSS Color 4: if the host syntax does not define the space, "it defaults to Oklab"; polar hue defaults to `shorter`; legacy sRGB colours interpolate in gamma-encoded sRGB. Images 4 gradients interpolate in the specified space with **premultiplied alpha**. A WPT test named `css-color-4-colors-default-to-oklab-gradient` exists. | — | [CSS Color 4 §12–13](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-color-4/#interpolation); [CSS Images 4 §3.5](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-images-4/#coloring-gradient-line) (ED 2026-10-08) | 2026 | High |
| 3.7 | **MSAA quality is bounded by sample count and box filter.** NVPR single-pass 8×MS shows "significant amounts of noise"; "the amount of aliasing is a property of the box filter and remains the same regardless of the number of samples". A 4×4 cubic B-spline with sample sharing gives visibly less aliasing. | 8 / 32 / 512 spp | [Ganacim et al. 2014 §6](https://w3.impa.br/~diego/publications/GanEtAl14.pdf) | 2014 | High |
| 3.8 | **Shape AA versus text AA.** Text uses grayscale AA only (TX-D6), from atlas masks (TX-D13). Shape AA from the path rasterizer must use the same coverage-to-alpha mapping and compositing space, or text and an outlined copy of the same glyph render differently. No published cross-engine statement was found. | — | Frame (TX-D6, TX-D13) | — | Medium |

**Implications:** Izcal's canonical output should composite coverage-as-alpha in **encoded sRGB** (3.3) and use **analytic area coverage with a conflation fallback** (3.2), not MSAA. Filter spaces (3.5) and gradient spaces (3.6) are per-feature rules, so `hg-colour` conversions belong in the display list's effect nodes, not in the rasterizer.

## 4. Paint order, stacking contexts, 3D and display lists

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | **CSS 2.1 Appendix E painting order.** In each stacking context: root background over the canvas; block backgrounds and borders (tables in layer order); **negative z-index** descendants (most negative first, then tree order); in-flow non-positioned blocks; **non-positioned floats** (each painted *as if* a new stacking context, with positioned descendants and real stacking contexts going to the parent); inline content per line box (backgrounds, borders, decorations, text, atomic inlines); **positioned descendants with z-index auto or 0** in tree order (auto treated as if a stacking context); **z-index ≥ 1**; outlines last if not drawn earlier (the recommended place). ## 7. Determinism of GPU arithmetic

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7.1 | **WGSL does not specify a rounding mode.** Even `x + y`, which is "correctly rounded", may return "the smallest value in T greater than x, or the largest value in T less than x … WGSL does not specify a rounding mode". Implementations **may reassociate**, **may fuse** when at least as accurate, may **flush subnormals** of listed operations, may ignore the sign of zero, and may assume **no NaN or infinity** at runtime (giving "indeterminate" results otherwise). `fma` may expand to two roundings. | x/y 2.5 ULP | [WGSL §15.7.2–15.7.5](https://www.w3.org/TR/WGSL/#floating-point-evaluation) (ED 2026-09-21) | 2026 | High (extends RC 1.7) |
| 7.2 | **WGSL integers are exact and defined.** i32 uses two's complement with defined overflow; concrete integer overflow wraps modulo 2^bitwidth. Integer-only shader arithmetic is therefore **bit-reproducible by specification**. | — | [WGSL §6.2, §8.7](https://www.w3.org/TR/WGSL/#integer-types) | 2026 | High |
| 7.3 | **Vulkan SPIR-V defaults are permissive.** Unless an operation is decorated `NoContraction` or the module sets an FP fast-math mode, **AllowContract, AllowReassoc, AllowRecip and AllowTransform are assumed**. Correctly rounded operations use an "implementation-defined rounding mode" unless the entry point declares `RoundingModeRTE`/`RTZ` (optional device features `shaderRoundingModeRTEFloat32` etc.). Denormals may be flushed by default. | — | [Vulkan spec, Precision and Operation of SPIR-V Instructions](https://registry.khronos.org/vulkan/specs/latest/html/vkspec.html#spirvenv-precision-operation) | 2026-10 (latest) | High |
| 7.4 | **Vulkan repeatability excludes atomics and stores.** Rule 1 (same commands, same state, same result) "does not apply when using shaders containing side effects (image and buffer variable stores and atomic operations)". So **compute rasterizers that accumulate with atomics are not guaranteed repeatable even on one device**, unless accumulation is commutative and exact (integer add). `subPixelPrecisionBits` has a required minimum of **4** (8 in the roadmap profile); fixed-function rasterization precision is implementation-defined above that. | min 4 bits | [Vulkan spec, Appendix "Invariance"](https://registry.khronos.org/vulkan/specs/latest/html/vkspec.html#invariance); [limits](https://registry.khronos.org/vulkan/specs/latest/html/vkspec.html#limits-subPixelPrecisionBits) | 2026-10 | High |
| 7.5 | **Metal defaults to fast math.** `-fmetal-math-mode` defaults to **fast** (no NaNs, no INFs, no signed zeros, reciprocal, reassociation, fast contraction), and `-fmetal-math-fp32-functions` defaults to **fast**. Even `safe` sets "FP contract to **on**", which still allows fusion within an expression. With fast math, NaN/INF handling is undefined; subnormals may be flushed. Basic `+ − × ÷` are listed as correctly rounded. | — | [Metal Shading Language Specification 4.1, §1.6.3, §8](https://developer.apple.com/metal/Metal-Shading-Language-Specification.pdf) | 2026-06-04 | High |
| 7.6 | **Vendor evidence of cross-device divergence.** NVIDIA's floating-point guide lists FMA contraction, reordering in parallel reductions (different thread counts), independent math libraries ("neither is guaranteed to be correctly rounded"), and fast-mode flags (`-ftz`, `-prec-div`, `-prec-sqrt`) as reasons results differ between CPU and GPU. "Differences are not automatically evidence that the result computed by the GPU is wrong." | — | [NVIDIA, Floating Point and IEEE 754 Compliance](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/floating-point/index.html) | 2026-10-12 | High |
| 7.7 | **Observed rendering divergence is a fingerprint.** See RC 3.6 (50 distinct renderings in 300 samples of the same canvas text). | — | RC 3.6 | 2012 | High (via RC) |

**Implications:** across vendors, bit-identical GPU raster is achievable only with **integer or fixed-point compute** (7.2) and **commutative integer atomics** (7.4). That rules out fixed-function rasterization and MSAA (7.4), hardware texture filtering (sub-texel precision is implementation-defined), fixed-function blending, and float shaders on any API (7.1, 7.3, 7.5) for canonical pixels. This supports the frame's DET-1 scoping (GPU output out of scope) and TX-D13's split. Izcal should state per surface whether pixels are **canonical (CPU integer, or GPU integer-only)** or **tolerance-bound (GPU float)**.

## Open questions (to measure black-box or decide)

1. **Blend2D's and Skia's AA arithmetic** (fixed-point bit widths, cell sizes) are stated only in their sources. To compare, measure coverage outputs black-box on edge cases (near-horizontal edges, coincident edges, 1/256-px offsets).
2. **What major engines actually do for box-shadow and blur():** three-box approximation, true Gaussian, or downsample-and-upsample at large σ? Measure pixel profiles black-box against 5.2's 5% bound.
3. **Dashed and dotted border corners and spacing** in Blink, Gecko and WebKit: measure black-box to choose a rule that passes the existing fuzzy reftests (6.3: css-borders is 64% fuzzy).
4. **Does any engine composite shape coverage in linear light?** 3.3 says encoded sRGB; verify black-box with 50% coverage edges on known backgrounds.
5. **Gradient dithering:** which engines dither, and with what pattern? Measure on 8-bit and 10-bit outputs, then decide Izcal's ordered pattern (5.10).
6. **GPU integer-only fine raster cost:** measure a WGSL/MSL integer-only strip shader against float on the reference devices to price "canonical GPU pixels" (Section 7).
7. **Fixed-function blending precision** for unorm8 targets is implementation-defined (not verified this session). Measure across vendors before using hardware blending for any tolerance-bound path.
8. **Plane splitting numerics for 3D contexts** (4.4, 4.5): need an exact or integer formulation; no prose source was read.
9. **Chrome GPU raster rollout data and OOP-R motivation** (9.7): fetch Chrome's published prose when budget allows.
10. **Spinel** (1.14): find any published prose (talks, papers); its source is off-limits.
11. **Li et al. 2016 full text** and **Kilgard and Bolz 2012 full text**: read for numbers (only abstracts and the extension overview were read).
12. **Stroke cusp rule** (2.7): decide between continuity (Nehab) and spec letter, and file a spec issue if Izcal diverges.
