# Izcal distribution and updates: design critique (phase 8)

Date: 24 October 2026. Critic: independent; did not write the spec or its research notes. Method: `.claude/skills/first-principles-spec/SKILL.md` phase 8. The spec was checked against the existing specs and the M1 code for contradictions, missed constraints, unworkable mechanisms and M-1 violations. Fact-checking of external claims is the fact-checker's job; external facts are used here only where a finding depends on them, and are marked "to measure" where they are not established.

**What is sound.** The central idea is right and well argued. Making binary replacement a non-event, through versioned build directories, own-directory launch and a strict `BuildId` check with a restart-required state instead of a retry loop, answers the audit's Section 8 point 3 with a design rather than a fallback. It fits FP-D1's refusal of a fork server. The trust split is the right shape: OS signatures never authorise updates, TUF signatures never satisfy Gatekeeper, verification has no off switch, and a delta always falls back to a full target verified file by file. With findings 3 and 9 applied, "staged rollout without telemetry" is an original and defensible contribution. The store refusals are properly evidenced. The CRA mapping is the most complete in the corpus apart from findings 2 and 14. The ledger coverage is exact: Appendix A's 27 rows match the 27 "→ FI-2" rows in the ledger. The uninstall ledger, the byte-identical installers with no attribution, and the published distribution-builder policy are good first-principles breaks with the past. The spec respects FI-D10. Every Linux finding above makes the owner's decision work as he stated it ("so a running browser keeps launching children from its own build") and none reopens it.
