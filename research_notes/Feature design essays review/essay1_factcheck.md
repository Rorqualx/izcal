**Verdict: Partly correct.**
- The essay's list is incomplete. Brave and Firefox lack native workspaces, and the essay omits Chrome, which now ships vertical tabs and split view. That omission actually **strengthens** the commoditization point.
- "Solved" does not follow from "shipped". The best usage evidence available, Arc's own (5.52% of DAUs use more than one Space), shows these features have **low uptake**. That supports "low marginal value", not "solved".
- Microsoft is **cutting back** Workspaces and Collections in 2026, which is not what a solved feature looks like.

## 5. Reader mode "shipped everywhere, rarely used"

**Verdict: Unverifiable.**
- **No browser vendor has published reader-mode usage rates.** Mozilla collects telemetry (metric-hub "reader_mode" outcome, `toolkit/components/reader/metrics.yaml`) but has published no figure. A Mozilla Discourse request for the number went unanswered. Nothing public from Apple, Google or Microsoft.
- The best related evidence is Li et al., CHI 2019 (Microsoft Research / UW). Reader View raised reading speed by about 5%, and **only 41% of 1,100 popular pages successfully enabled Reader View**. That points to availability, not just disuse, as a factor.
- Google launched a full-page "immersive reading mode" in Chrome on 7 Apr 2026, which signals continued vendor investment. That says nothing about usage either way.

Sources: Mozilla metric-hub https://mozilla.github.io/metric-hub/outcomes/firefox_desktop/reader_mode/ ; Discourse https://discourse.mozilla.org/t/reader-mode-data/31656 ; Li et al. CHI 2019 (P) https://wildlab.cs.washington.edu/Publications_files/Li_CHI2019.pdf

## Reasoning errors

1. **Misattributed cause (central claim).** The essay says Arc's stated reasons included Chromium-fork maintenance and that "the public record does not separate the two". The record does exist: a primary letter that names adoption, novelty tax, lack of cohesion, incrementalism, AI and app-layer bloat (TCA/SwiftUI). It does not name engine cost. TBC then built Dia on the same Chromium and ADK base, which is evidence **against** the engine-cost hypothesis. The essay's first falsifier ("if Arc failed from Chromium maintenance cost…") is already answered, and largely in the negative. The HGBA-3.0 "engine reuse" inference built on it has no support.
2. **"Shipped" treated as "solved".** That every major browser now ships vertical tabs and split view shows commoditization, not that the design problem is solved. Arc's 5.52% multi-Space usage figure suggests low value or high learning cost. The essay also leaves out Chrome, the strongest evidence for its own point, and Microsoft's 2026 rollback of Workspaces sharing and Collections.
3. **Adoption treated as preference.** Low uptake of the Containers add-on is read as a verdict on identity-scoping. Other explanations: the add-on is not a default, Total Cookie Protection made it redundant, and identity is now widely shipped as **profiles** across all major browsers.
4. **False premise on cost precedent.** Data Saver, a proxy discontinued because data got cheaper, is used as the precedent for budgets, while real shipped budget mechanisms with measured effects are ignored: heavy-ad intervention, timer throttling, sleeping tabs, GX limiters. "Battery saver binary" is only true of Chrome Energy Saver. Edge Efficiency mode and Chrome Memory Saver are graded.
5. **False dichotomy on notifications.** "Mobile OSes broker; browsers give raw channel" ignores quiet UI, ML warnings, auto-revocation and, on Android, per-site OS channels. The defensible claim is narrower: no browser batches or summarizes web notifications.
6. **Outdated fact used as current.** Firefox cookie banner reduction is cited as working. It was confined to German private windows and is now withdrawn.
7. **Unsourced usage claims.** "Reader mode rarely used" has no published vendor data behind it. The essay admits it reasons "from general knowledge", but the maturity table states these points as settled.
8. **Overstated "coordination problem" for consent.** GPC has regulator enforcement in several states and a 2027 browser mandate in California. Measured compliance is about 45%. This is an enforcement and compliance gap, not purely a coordination problem.
9. **Internal tension.** "None requires AI" sits beside the claim that task-as-unit "needs AI but AI is off". Meanwhile Chrome's notification warnings, Brave's Cookiecrumbler and Chrome's scam detection already use ML/LLMs inside the brokering and consent areas the essay discusses.
