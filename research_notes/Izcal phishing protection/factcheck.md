## Findings

### Major

#### F1 — §6.2 (snapshot line 192): v5 canonicalization is described as lowercasing the path

**Quote.** "Izcal does **not** adopt v5's aggressive lookup canonicalization — repeated unescaping, lowercased path, dot-stripped and IPv4-normalized host — because a key computed by the parser and a request made by the network stack must name the same resource".

**Claim.** Google's v5 lookup canonicalization lowercases the path.

**Verified.** `https://developers.google.com/safe-browsing/reference/URLs.and.Hashing` (21 October 2026). The "Canonicalization of URLs" section is split into "To canonicalize the hostname:" and "To canonicalize the path:". The lowercasing bullet is numbered step 5 **under the hostname heading**: "Lowercase the whole string." The path section contains no lowercasing step. The same page's worked examples preserve mixed-case path segments.

**Verdict.** Wrong.

**Correction.** Delete "lowercased path" from the list. The other three contrasts (repeated unescaping, dot stripping, IPv4 normalization) are accurate. If the intended point is that v5 lowercases only the hostname, say that; Izcal's step 1 already does the same, so the contrast should be about the path only.

#### F2 — §6.2 (snapshot line 206): v5 matching is described as a literal string prefix

**Quote.** "v5's expression is a literal string prefix, so a producer that omits the trailing slash gets the `foobar` match ([PHP 6.7][PHP]); Izcal's matcher is stricter".

**Claim.** In v5, an expression `example.com/foo` produced without a trailing slash also matches `example.com/foobar`.

**Verified.** `https://developers.google.com/safe-browsing/reference/URLs.and.Hashing` (21 October 2026) and `/reference/Local.List.Mode` (same date). The client forms "up to 30 different possible host suffix and path prefix combinations". For the path it tries "at most six different strings": the exact path with query, the exact path without query, and "The four paths formed by starting at the root (/) and successively appending path components, including a trailing slash." Matching is on SHA-256 hashes of those candidate expressions (Local List Mode: "a list of suffix/prefix expressions generated by the URL u", hashed, first 4 bytes used as the prefix), not on an arbitrary string prefix of the URL. The candidate set generated for `example.com/foobar` does not contain `example.com/foo`; the boundary is the path component and the trailing slash.

**Verdict.** Wrong (mechanics).

**Correction.** Describe v5 as client-generated candidate expressions at path-component boundaries, and justify Izcal's segment-boundary matcher on its own terms (fewer false positives, a stricter producer contract). If the `foo`/`foobar` claim is about producer-side expression construction rather than client matching, mark it as an inference: Google's producer-side documentation was not read.

#### F3 — §2 (line 37) and §6.6.1 source table (line 377): PhishTank "no dispute path"

**Quote.** "registration closed in 2020, so no new reporter or site owner can join or dispute an entry"; and in the source table: "new registration closed, no dispute path".

**Claim.** PhishTank has no route to dispute a listing, and registration closed in 2020.

**Verified.** `https://www.phishtank.net/faq.php` (21 October 2026) documents a false-positive route: "How do I report a 'false positive,' where PhishTank wrongly labels a site as a phishing site?" — "Go to the Phish detail page for the site in question", click on the link "Something wrong with this submission?" and follow instructions; "These reports will be taken seriously." `https://phishtank.org/register.php` (21 October 2026) says "New user registration temporarily disabled" — no date. The 2020 date appears in Wikipedia ("In 2020 ... new user registration was removed"), a secondary source. The current terms (`https://phishtank.org/terms.php`, 21 October 2026) limit use to "personal and non-commercial use" and forbid distribution without OpenDNS's written permission — which is the real reason the feed is test-only.

**Verdict.** Partly wrong. The dispute path exists; "closed" overstates the page's "temporarily disabled"; the 2020 date is secondary.

**Correction.** "Registration has been disabled since 2020 (Wikipedia; the register page says 'temporarily disabled'); the FAQ publishes a 'Something wrong with this submission?' false-positive route whose account requirement is not stated." Keep the test-only status, but base it on the current terms (personal, non-commercial; no distribution without written permission), not on the dispute claim.

#### F4 — §6.6.1 source table (line 371): Phishunt is stale and unscreened

**Quote.** "| 1 | **Phishunt** | CC0 ... | 770–779 URLs, 2–11 new/h, global | **Ship now**: a public-domain dedication can be redistributed under MIT |".

**Claim.** Phishunt has about 770–779 URLs and is the one source that ships immediately.

**Verified.** `https://phishunt.io/feed.txt` and `https://phishunt.io/feed.csv` (21 October 2026, about 20:45 UTC). `feed.txt` is 51,928 bytes and 1,410 URLs; `feed.csv` has 1,410 data rows. Verdicts: low 881, medium 384, high 116, critical 13, noise 16. That is 129 high-or-critical (9.1%) and 897 low-or-noise (63.6%). The growth from the M7 count of 770–779 to 1,410 over about 60 hours is about 10.5/hour, inside the table's "2–11 new/h" band, so the rate is still accurate; the standing count is not. The ToS (`https://phishunt.io/tos/`, 21 October 2026) says the data is "suspicion, not verdict", "It is not a legal finding, judicial determination, or verified assertion", "False positives and false negatives occur routinely. Anyone relying on the data must perform their own verification before taking action", and forbids users from "publicly assert[ing] that a listed domain is confirmed phishing ... based solely on its presence in phishunt.io". The spec's warning wording at §6.5.2 already handles the last point ("listed as", never "confirmed"), but the source row's "Ship now" and the build's auto-accept set no confidence threshold, so the 16 noise and 881 low entries would ship.

**Verdict.** Stale count and incomplete source status. The ToS wording is correctly handled in §6.5.2, but not in the source table or the build gate.

**Correction.** Replace with "1,410 URLs, 2–11 new/h, global (2026-10-21 20:45 UTC)"; record the verdict distribution; require a threshold (for example, accept only medium-or-better, or require a second source for low/noise) or publish the reason for accepting all; re-run the false-positive proxy at the current size; add the "suspicion, not verdict" constraint to the source row and to PH-19's review criteria.

#### F5 — §6.6.2 (lines 408, 414) versus PH-19 (line 626), §9.2 (line 655) and §9.5 (line 717): the sampling audit is 2.5% and 1%

**Quote.** "**Sampling audit**: **2.5%†** of auto-accepted entries per week (at least 3,000† at the measured 124,000-entry scale)" and, four lines later, "the 2.5%† sampling audit". Against that: "the sampling audit covers 1%† per week" (PH-19); "the 1%† sampling audit" (§9.2 list-operation row); "feed entries are auto-accepted with a 1%† sampling audit" (§9.5 CB-D16 row).

**Claim.** Two different sampling rates in the same spec.

**Verified.** Internal arithmetic. 2.5% of 124,000 is 3,100 per week (about 443 per weekday), which supports the "at least 3,000" floor. 1% of 124,000 is 1,240 per week (about 177 per weekday), which cannot meet a 3,000 floor. At the stated 1.5–3 non-engineers, 3,100 reviews a week is roughly 103–207 a day per FTE; 1,240 a week is roughly 41–83 a day per FTE. The staffing row names the 1% figure, so the workload basis is the lower one.

**Verdict.** Internally inconsistent. **Fixed in the live 0.2**: all six locations now say 2.5% (lines 408, 414, 626, 655, 683, 717 of the live file). The finding is recorded against draft 0.1.

**Correction.** Keep 2.5% everywhere and re-check the list-operation staffing against 3,100 reviews a week; or keep 1% everywhere and drop the 3,000 floor. Do not leave the two rates in one spec.

#### F6 — §6.2 expression table (line 201) versus SEC-D7; §9.5 (line 710): host-suffix matching contradicts "never by whole domain", and the amendment is not recorded

**Quote.** The expression table says a host suffix covers "The host `example.com` and any subdomain, on any scheme, any port, any path" for "Domain feeds (CERT Polska)". SEC-D7 says "Matching is by full URL, never by whole domain, and the list build applies an allowlist of popular and shared-hosting domains." The §9.5 SEC-D7 row says "the source list stays as decided; the M8 gate is unchanged".

**Claim.** Host-suffix matching on the primary CERT Polska feed is compatible with SEC-D7.

**Verified.** `/Users/joederas/Documents/Cline/code/izcal-wt/security-scope/reports/Izcal security scope.md`, draft 0.3.1, SEC-D7 decision row (21 October 2026): "Matching is by full URL, never by whole domain". CERT Polska's warning-list page (`https://cert.pl/en/warning-list/`, 21 October 2026) says "subdomains of domains present on the List should be also blocked" — a domain feed that expects whole-domain blocking. The snapshot's §9.5 records no amendment to the matching sentence, so the spec implements a different matching rule from the decision it cites.

**Verdict.** Internally inconsistent (cross-spec). **Fixed in the live 0.2**: the SEC-D7 row now says "**Amend the matching sentence**: 'matching is by full URL, never by whole domain' means a **URL-feed entry is never widened to a host suffix**; a **domain feed may be expressed as a host suffix**, subject to the allowlist and the build gate (6.2)." The finding is recorded against draft 0.1.

**Correction.** Record the distinction as an explicit amendment (as the live 0.2 does), and cite CERT Polska's subdomain recommendation as the reason a domain feed is expressed as a host suffix.

#### F7 — §6.2 (line 210) and §6.6.2 (line 407): the allowlist depends on a "crawl ranking" that content blocking does not produce

**Quote.** "the top 10,000† registrable domains by **Izcal's own crawl ranking** (content blocking's crawler; no third-party popularity list is shipped unless its terms permit it)".

**Claim.** Content blocking's crawler produces a top-10,000 registrable-domain popularity ranking.

**Verified.** `Izcal content blocking.md`, draft 0.3.2 (21 October 2026). Its crawler section (§6.8) defines headed browsing, a Tranco top-10,000 seed plus a Certificate Transparency-derived per-region sample, and "visits enough". It defines no popularity ranking and no top-10,000 output. Its own Tranco use is a third-party list with an unresolved licence (Q-CB-2: "Tranco has no list-wide licence and one upstream provider is CC BY-NC 4.0"). No content-blocking deliverable produces the ranking this spec's allowlist and PH-8 depend on.

**Verdict.** Unverified dependency: not wrong as a design intention, but the sibling spec does not define the mechanism.

**Correction.** Define the ranking in this spec (inputs, cadence, published output, licence) or add it as an explicit content-blocking deliverable with an owner. State that the Tranco seed cannot ship as the allowlist under Q-CB-2.

## Summary

**Counts.** 26 findings: **0 blockers, 7 major, 19 minor.**

**The five findings that most affect the design:**

1. **F6, SEC-D7 matching conflict (major).** The primary source is a domain feed matched as a host suffix, while the controlling decision says "Matching is by full URL, never by whole domain", and draft 0.1 recorded no amendment. This is a decision-level contradiction, not a wording issue. The live 0.2 has already applied the fix.
2. **F5, the sampling audit is 2.5% in two normative passages and 1% in three governance/staffing rows (major).** The 1% rate cannot meet the 3,000-per-week floor, and the staffing row is priced at 1%. The workload basis of the governance decision is therefore ambiguous. The live 0.2 has already applied 2.5% everywhere.
3. **F4, Phishunt's count and composition are stale (major).** The one "Ship now" MIT-compatible source is 1,410 URLs today, not 770–779; 16 entries are labelled noise and 881 low, and the build sets no confidence threshold. The ToS "suspicion, not verdict" wording is handled in the warning text but not in the source status or the acceptance rule.
4. **F7, the allowlist's "own crawl ranking" does not exist in the content-blocking spec (major).** The top-10,000 popularity ranking that gates the allowlist and PH-8 is not a content-blocking deliverable, and its Tranco seed has an unresolved licence (Q-CB-2).
5. **F1/F2, the v5 protocol description is wrong in two load-bearing places (major).** v5 does not lowercase the path, and its matching is client-generated candidate expressions at path-component boundaries, not a literal string prefix. The design's stricter matcher is defensible on its own terms; the stated reason is not.

**Readiness.** Draft 0.1 is strong on the literature (PhishTime, Sunrise to Sunset, CrawlPhish, PhishDecloaker, FreePhish, Zimperium, APWG, Interisle, ICANN all check out), on the broad shape of Safe Browsing v5, on the licence facts for CERT Polska, Phishunt, URLAbuse, abuse.ch and Phishing.Database, and on the legal framing. It is not ready to be recorded as phase-8 clean: the three governance/decision majors (F4, F6, F7) and the two protocol-description majors (F1, F2) need correction or an explicit disposition. No finding invalidates the local-list design. Two majors are already fixed in the live working tree; the remaining 24, including all 19 minors, persist as of the 14:41 live text and are listed above with concrete corrections.
