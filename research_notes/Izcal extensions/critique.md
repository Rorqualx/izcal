| **C6** | Medium | **The `split` container mode contradicts the process model, the identity rule and the storage keying, and the cross-container story inside one host process leans on an assumption JS-D17 does not make.** (1) 6.5: `split` "runs the extension per container with per-container identity". ## Counts and verdict

**22 findings: 5 High (C1–C5), 9 Medium (C6–C14), 8 Low (C15–C22).** Of the High findings, C1, C2, C5 and part of C4 require design decisions, not only edits; C3 and the rest are amendment-text work that nonetheless gates the crate graph and the 9.5 table. No M-1 violation was found: no engine-source evidence, no non-MIT dependency or data implied, no template-copied design, and the † discipline is held throughout.

The spec's strongest parts are real and worth keeping: the capability-tuple model worn as a documented API face, the three-ring contract with refusals that carry reasons and reversal triggers, the observational-only `webRequest` adapter, the destructive-revocation and staged-capability-growth rules, the public decision log with ownership transfer as a review event, and a staffing section that names what it does not count. The architecture consumes the accepted seams (JS-D10, DM-D16, CB-D15, NW 6.19, GR-D28, ED-D21) in the ways those specs expect, and the 9.5 table is mostly accurate about the text it amends — the Stage 1, NW 6.22, CB 6.15 and editing rows all describe their targets correctly.

The revision must spend its effort, in order, on: the CB boundary (C1 and C2 — the DNR ring row and the tier-3 intent scope are the two places where the compatibility face and the capability spine disagree), the grant default versus declared content scripts (C5 — as written it silently breaks the platform's flagship classes), the incomplete ring table (C4 — the spec's central falsifiable artifact is claimed exhaustive and is not), the missing JS-spec amendment (C3 — `hg-ext-host` violates G6 as written), and the container `split` story (C6). The Medium findings are mostly commitments the design makes without an owner, a process or a budget line: parsing placement, the recording unit under the unresolved C-19, review operations, and the footprint lines the per-extension process model implies. None of these threatens the central idea; all of them are the difference between a spec the owner can accept and one that hands its unresolved parts to EX-M1.
