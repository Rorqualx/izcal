## 1. Where main-thread time goes: parsing and style on real pages

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |

**Implications:** The evidence moved from "selector matching dominates loads" (2009) to "parsing is on the load critical path and style recalc dominates some interactions" (2013–2023). Izcal's targets need **both** a load metric (parse throughput, first-byte-to-first-node) and an **interaction metric** (incremental restyle time per mutation on large-rule-count apps such as the 10k–28k-rule set in 1.5). Like the JS engine (execution_performance 1.6), Izcal must build its own per-phase time accounting, because nobody publishes a current split.

## 3. Selector matching cost

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **The classic toolkit and its measured gains.** Meyerovich and Bodík used rule **hashtables keyed by the rightmost tag/class/id**, **right-to-left matching**, redundant-selector elimination, hash tiling for cache locality, result pre-allocation and delayed set insertion. Simple ("L2") sequential optimizations gave **4.0×**; all sequential ("L1") optimizations **25.1×**; with TBB parallelism **55.2× / 64.8×** (with/without GMail). Average selector matching fell from **204 ms to 3.5 ms**. After that, "pre- and post-processing matched selectors" became the new bottleneck. | 2.3 GHz 4-core × 8-socket AMD Opteron 8356; their reimplementation within 30% of Safari, handled 99.9% of rules | [Meyerovich, Bodík, WWW 2010](https://archives.iw3c2.org/www2010/_lmeyerov/projects/pbrowser/pubfiles/playout.pdf) | 2010-04 | High |
| 3.2 | **Ancestor Bloom filter, Blink:** a **single-hash bitset** ("a Bloom filter with only one hash function, for cheaper lookups") of ancestors' tag, id, class and attribute hashes; rules carry precomputed ancestor hashes. "For practical web pages as of 2022, we've seen SelectorFilter **discard 60–70% of rules** in early processing, which makes the **1 kB** of RAM/cache it uses worthwhile." | 3.3 | **Ancestor Bloom filter, Stylo:** counting filter, **4096 8-bit counters (4 KiB), k = 2**; "KeySize of 12 gives false positive rates on the order of **0.25–4%**"; inserted per element: local name, namespace, id, classes, attribute names (excluding `class`, `id`, `style`). | 3.4 | **Ancestor Bloom filter, WebKit:** `CountingBloomFilter` with a 12-bit key; "with 100 unique strings in the filter, 2^12 slot table has false positive rate of **~0.2%**". | 4096 slots | [WebKit SelectorFilter.h](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WebKit/WebKit/main/Source/WebCore/css/SelectorFilter.h) | 2026-10 (HEAD) | High |
| 3.5 | **Blink also filters on the subject element:** each rule carries a small **subject Bloom filter** (`TinyBloomFilter`) and a flag for rules "entirely covered by bucketing", so a rule whose whole selector was proven by its bucket key skips matching. | 3.6 | **CSS selector JIT (WebKit, 2014):** compiling selectors to machine code gave "a gain of **2×**" on common selectors (microbenchmark ≈1,100 ms → <500 ms on a Retina MacBook Pro), more where the interpreter hit slow paths; unsupported pseudo-classes fall back to the interpreter. | 3.7 | **Selector statistics in DevTools** report per selector: elapsed ms, **match attempts**, **match count**, and **"% of slow-path non-matches"** (attempts that failed only after the less-optimised code). | 3.8 | **What selector stats revealed (Edge case study):** a stray descendant space in `.meta ::selection` (which means `.meta *::selection`), attribute-substring selectors `[class*=" gallery-icon--"]`, deep descendant chains, a duplicated RTL rule, and a universal `*` rule. | 3.9 | **Sticky invalidation flags as a pathology:** in the Microsoft case study, `+`/`~` selectors on `body` children set flags that were never cleared, so inserting a tooltip restyled every child of `body` (Chromium bug 1313632, open at the time). | — | [Brosset, CSS Day 2023](https://noti.st/patrickbrosset/NIyBLK/slides) | 2023-06 | Medium |

**Implications:** Three engines converged on the same filter idea with **different points on the size/speed curve** (Blink 1 KiB one-hash bitset; WebKit and Stylo 4 KiB counting filters with ≈0.2–4% false positives). Izcal should choose its own from first principles and **measure the rejection rate and false-positive rate on its corpus** (Blink's 60–70% is the only published rejection figure). Selector statistics are proven useful; Izcal's matcher should count attempts, matches, fast rejections and slow-path failures **per selector by construction** so DevTools gets them free (frame: "which selector is slow?").

## 5. Custom properties: inheritance cost and data structures

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **Before Chrome 84, changing a custom property restyled the whole subtree** with a full cascade on every node. Chrome 84 (r765278) let nodes that do not reference custom properties copy the parent's computed style. Test `ChangeStyleCustomPropertyDeclaration`: Chrome 83 **163.7 ms** → Chrome 84 **117.4 ms** (−28%) → after an MPC change **108.1 ms**; **Firefox 78: 28.4 ms** (parallel) and **38.3 ms with one Stylo thread**. | perf test, hardware not stated | [Igalia (Javier Fernández): Improving CSS Custom Properties performance](https://blogs.igalia.com/jfernandez/category/blink/) | 2020-08-13 | High |
| 5.2 | **Root-level variables were copied per node** in Blink until 2016; the fix made the root's set a shared fallback, so "adding 1000 child variables" became about as cheap as adding none. | — | [Chromium review 1858553002](https://codereview.chromium.org/1858553002) | 2016 | Medium (search snippet) |
| 5.3 | **`inherits: false` is the decisive factor**, not registration. | 5.4 | **WebKit and Stylo independently converged on a copy-on-write chain** for custom properties: each element's map holds its own changes plus a pointer to its parent's, with an **ancestor-chain limit of 4** in both; WebKit copies instead of chaining when the parent has **< 8** properties and always references the root's set "since it likely gets shared a lot". | limit 4; threshold 8 | [WebKit StyleCustomPropertyData.cpp](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WebKit/WebKit/main/Source/WebCore/style/computed/data/StyleCustomPropertyData.cpp); [Stylo custom_properties_map.rs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/servo/stylo/main/style/custom_properties_map.rs) | 2026-10 (HEAD) | High |
| 5.5 | **Real-world custom property shape:** defined on **28.6%** of mobile pages in 2021 (≈5% in 2019, 19.3% in 2020) and **43%** in 2022; **≈60% are set on `:root`/`html`** and about two-thirds act as global constants; dependency depth 0 for 62–68%, depth 1 for ≈30%, ≥3 under 1%. Values: colour 40%, dimension 27%. No per-page count is published. | — | [Web Almanac 2021: CSS](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2021/css); [Web Almanac 2022: CSS](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2022/css) | 2021-12; 2022-09 | High |
| 5.6 | Design-system pages with thousands of custom properties exist (the frame's concern), but no primary measurement of their per-element cost was found this session. | — | — | 2026-10 | — |

**Implications:** The cost driver is **inheritance fan-out**, and the common case is a large, rarely changing set on `:root` read shallowly. Izcal's design should make an unchanged inherited custom-property set **free per element** (shared by reference) and make a change cost proportional to the elements that **read** the changed name, not the subtree. The ≈1,000× gap in 5.3 says registered non-inheriting properties should be honoured as a real invalidation boundary. The convergent "chain with a depth limit" of 5.4 is evidence that lookup depth must be bounded; Izcal derives its own structure.

A gain that costs determinism or 2× style memory is not a win under FP-11.

## 7. Invalidation

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7.1 | **Blink invalidation sets** are built from rules into a `RuleFeatureSet` and change only when rules change. Mutations produce **immediate** invalidations or **pending** sets applied in one depth-first pass before recalc; four node flags (needs invalidation, child needs invalidation, needs recalc, child needs recalc) with local vs subtree recalc. They "err on the side of correctness"; the engine **never searches up the tree** during invalidation. **Implications:** Every engine's invalidation is **hand-written, conservative and unmeasured in public**. No engine publishes an over-invalidation rate. The two hard parts are upward invalidation and **sticky, never-cleared flags** (3.9); the proven mitigations are argument-feature filters, subject flags, per-argument result caches and subtree Bloom filters. Izcal derives its own from the Selectors spec.

**Implications:** Scanning text can run at **gigabytes per second**, so tokenising is unlikely to be the bottleneck of a well-built parser. A single spec-anchored parser with fast inner loops avoids the bail-out differential that Blink's design carries. Yield budgets should be **recorded decision points** (JS-D15), because Blink tunes them from field data and they shape first paint.

## 13. Gaps, flagged claims and unchecked items

- **Search budget.** The shared web-search budget was exhausted mid-strand. Not searched: Blink's lazy-parsing gain figures (2016), Chromium's background HTML parser removal and its numbers, a WebKit `:has()` invalidation performance post by Antti Koivisto, Firefox speculative-parse failure rates, current `:has()`/nesting/`@layer`/container-query adoption, per-page custom-property counts on design-system sites, and Blink `ComputedStyle` field-group size history after 2017.
- **Vendor and press claims:** Stylo's "2/4/18×" (6.1) has no published workloads; Chrome's 10% Speedometer 2.1 figure for the `innerHTML` fast path is combined with other changes and came via press (8.3); the 19%/20% preload-scanner figures (9.1) are second-hand.

- **Arithmetic by this researcher:** MB/s figures in 8.7 and 10.3; the ≈1.3× and ≈1.35× parallel ratios in 6.2–6.3; the 2.3× style-count ratio in 4.3.
- **Unknown everywhere:** a current cross-site split of main-thread time by phase (1.7), style-sharing hit rates (4.6), over-invalidation rates (7), full-restyle fallback frequency (12.4), container-query restyle cost (7.9), and document-parser MB/s in any shipping engine (8.8).
