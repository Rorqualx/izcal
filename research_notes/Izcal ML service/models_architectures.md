# Izcal ML service: models_architectures

Compiled 21 October 2026. Primary sources were preferred (papers, model cards, maintainers' posts, vendor documentation, licence files). No third-party source code, model source file, header, commit or diff was read. Model weights and licence files are data, not code; licences were checked from the model card or the repository LICENSE file.

Confidence: **High** = primary source read and the claim checked in its text; **Medium** = primary abstract or snippet, or a vendor claim without data; **Low** = secondary source only, or not verified in this pass.

Scope: the published architecture and quality evidence Izcal needs to design its own Stage 1 image-description, OCR and generated-caption models. Izcal writes its own inference stack from papers; models are trained or chosen under the MIT-only rule. Each licence in Section 6 states whether it was verified on 21 October 2026 from the linked page.

**Implications:** The shipping-proven Stage 1 point is a ViT encoder plus a small autoregressive decoder at roughly 180–250M parameters, quantized under 200MB, not a multi-billion-parameter VLM. Izcal should design its own encoder-decoder at that scale, then use a larger VLM only as an offline teacher or evaluation reference; the token arithmetic (196 tokens at 224²/patch-16, 256 at 224²/patch-14, 64 per 512² patch for SmolVLM) sets the vision-token budget the inference stack must handle. Published CPU/NPU latency is almost absent: Firefox's couple of seconds is the only model-level CPU figure, and no NPU figure was found for any captioner, so latency must be measured on Izcal's targets.

### 1.2 Quality metrics and what they miss

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.17 | **CIDEr** scores n-gram consensus against multiple human references, with a triplet-based annotation method to measure consensus. It does not measure correctness against the image beyond reference overlap. | — | [CIDEr paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5726) | 2014-11-20 | Medium |
| 1.18 | **SPICE** parses captions into scene graphs and compares semantic propositions. The paper argues n-gram overlap is "neither necessary nor sufficient" for judging captions, and reports system-level correlation of 0.88 with human judgments on MS COCO versus 0.43 for CIDEr and 0.53 for METEOR. | 0.88 vs 0.43 (CIDEr), 0.53 (METEOR) | [SPICE paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.08822) | 2016-07-29 | Medium |
| 1.19 | **METEOR** is a weighted harmonic mean of unigram precision and recall, with recall weighted higher, plus stemming, synonymy and a word-order penalty; the paper claims higher correlation with human judgment than BLEU. | — | [METEOR paper](https://aclanthology.org/W05-0909.pdf) | 2005 | High |
| 1.20 | **CLIPScore** is reference-free and scores image-text compatibility; the authors report the highest correlation with human judgments on their tested corpora, but say it is relatively weaker than reference-based metrics for news captions that need richer contextual knowledge. | — | [CLIPScore paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08718) | 2021-04-18 | Medium |
| 1.21 | None of the four metrics measures what an accessibility description needs: whether the text serves the reader's task, states uncertainty, marks AI provenance, or omits a harmful false detail. They reward reference consensus or image-text similarity, not task adequacy. | — | Analysis of 1.17–1.20 | 2026-10-21 | Medium |
| 1.22 | **Length is not fixed by WCAG.** WCAG 2.2 SC 1.1.1 requires a text alternative that serves the equivalent purpose, allows a brief alternative plus a long one when a short description cannot serve the purpose, and sets no character limit. | Level A; no length limit | [Understanding SC 1.1.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-content.html) | Updated 2026-09-06 | High |
| 1.23 | In the CHI 2024 study, description length was moderately correlated with better rank; more verbs, adjectives and adverbs correlated with better rankings, more nouns with worse; the vision-to-language model produced one-sentence terse descriptions while expert and creator text was more detailed. | 32 participants; moderate correlation | [From Provenance to Aberrations](https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3613904.3642325) | 2024-05-11 | High |

**Implications:** Izcal cannot ship a captioner on CIDEr or CLIPScore alone. It needs its own accessibility-quality evaluation that scores correctness, omission, uncertainty and provenance, with CER-style reference metrics only as a regression signal. The CHI evidence pushes against a single fixed length: the model should produce a short default and support a longer, context-aware description.

**Implications:** For Stage 1, the mature split is a text detector (DBNet-family) plus a recognizer (CRNN/CTC or a small attention decoder), with Tesseract/PaddleOCR as quality baselines and TrOCR/Donut as end-to-end alternatives. A 0.2–0.3B end-to-end model is plausible, but OCR-free models must be evaluated per document type; the published numbers do not establish photograph OCR quality.
