# Izcal ML service: inference kernels and runtime design

Compiled 2026-10-21. Primary sources were preferred (papers, specifications, maintainers' documentation, vendor tuning guides, black-box benchmark reports). No third-party project's source code, headers, commits, diffs or code reviews were read or cited. The ONNX specification is background only: ONNX is Apache-2.0 and would not be shipped.
Confidence: **High** = primary source read and the claim checked in its text; **Medium** = primary abstract or snippet, or a vendor claim without independent data; **Low** = secondary source only, or a figure derived here rather than measured.

**Scope.** This strand covers the published evidence needed to design Izcal's own inference stack for x86-64, AArch64 and riscv64: numeric formats and determinism, kernels and their measured performance, ISA features and dispatch, quantisation accuracy, runtime scheduling and memory, NPU/OS runtimes as opaque devices, and model-container safety. The target workload is small models, tens to a few hundred million parameters, CPU-first, with an NPU admitted only where HGBA-2 **R-3** measures a 1.5x end-to-end latency or energy win. Figures marked **derived** are arithmetic from a cited specification or paper, not a measurement; they are labelled so the spec never presents them as measured.

| 1.11 | GPTQ 4-bit is usable on small transformers but degrades monotonically with smaller scale: WikiText2 perplexity OPT-125M 27.65 to 31.12 (+3.47), OPT-350M 22.00 to 24.24 (+2.24), OPT-1.3B 14.63 to 15.47 (+0.84), BLOOM-560M 22.42 to 24.03 (+1.61). | 1.12 | Across 35,000 experiments and models from 19M to 176B parameters, 4-bit is the "almost universally optimal" total-bit/accuracy point; below 4 bits, small quantisation blocks and integer data types help most. | 19M-176B; 4-bit optimal | [The case for 4-bit precision](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09720) | 2022-12-19 | High |
| 1.13 | AWQ finds that protecting **1%** of salient weight channels, selected from activation statistics and scaled by an equivalent transform, sharply reduces 4-bit error without backpropagation or mixed precision. Reported speedup is more than 3x over an FP16 HF implementation on desktop and mobile GPUs. | 1% salient; >3x | [AWQ](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.00978) | 2023-06-01 | Medium |
| 1.14 | Floating-point addition is not associative, so a reduction's result depends on its bracketing. Dynamic parallel scheduling therefore makes plain vector sums and BLAS non-reproducible even on one machine. IEEE 754 fixes each operation, not the reduction tree. | Order-dependent | [Ahrens, Nguyen, Demmel](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2015/EECS-2015-229.html), [Demmel & Nguyen](https://doi.org/10.1109/ARITH.2013.9) | 2015-12-08 / 2013-04 | High |
| 1.15 | Order-independent reproducible summation exists. ReproBLAS uses a 6-word indexed accumulator, costs 7n additions for n summands, is communication-optimal (one pass or one reduction), and has a double-precision error bound up to 1e-8 times smaller than conventional summation. It is bitwise reproducible across platforms given the same rounding mode. | 6 words; 7n adds; 1e-8 better bound | [Ahrens et al.](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2015/EECS-2015-229.html) | 2015-12-08 | High |
| 1.16 | Bitwise reproducibility has a measured cost. A 4096-element double-precision dot product was **4x slower** than Intel MKL on a Core i7-2600; a separate report puts the single-core cost near 8x versus an optimised library and about 1.2x on a 1024-processor Cray XC30. | 4x (dot); ~8x single core; 1.2x at 1024 cores | [Ahrens et al.](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2015/EECS-2015-229.html), [ReproBLAS SCAN 2014](http://bebop.cs.berkeley.edu/reproblas/docs/talks/SCAN14.pdf) | 2015-12-08 / 2014 | High / Medium |
| 1.17 | "Deterministic on one machine" and "bit-identical across ISAs" are different contracts. The first can be met with a fixed reduction tree and fixed thread count on one binary. The second also requires identical rounding behaviour, no FMA contraction differences, identical transcendentals, identical denormal handling and no ISA-specific reassociation. ReproBLAS shows the second is achievable for sums; it does not make an arbitrary NN graph portable. | 2 distinct contracts | [Ahrens et al.](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/2015/EECS-2015-229.html), [IEEE MSC](https://grouper.ieee.org/groups/msc/ANSI_IEEE-Std-754-2019/background/) | 2015-12-08 | High / Medium |
| 1.18 | Integer accumulation is exactly associative modulo 2^32, so an int8 kernel with a fixed dequantisation order can be bit-identical across ISAs and reduction orders without any of the float machinery. This is the cheapest determinism lever available to Izcal: make the hot path integer, and reserve float reproducibility work for the float paths that remain. | Exact mod 2^32 | Derived from [Jacob et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1712.05877) | 2017-12-15 | Medium |

**Implications:** Izcal needs two declared numeric modes, not one. The **fast** mode may use FMA contraction, ISA-specific reduction trees and vendor transcendentals; the **reproducible** mode must fix the reduction tree, forbid contraction across the whole graph, use Izcal's own transcendentals (as the JS spec already does for `Math`), and accumulate in integer wherever the operator allows. Integer accumulation makes int8 GEMM, convolution and embedding bit-identical across x86-64, AArch64 and riscv64 at no accuracy cost, so determinism should be a property of the operator's numeric type, not a global switch. The measured price of float reproducibility is real (4x on a dot product), so the spec should publish the cost per operator and default to reproducible only where the model's output actually depends on it.

**Implications:** Izcal's kernel set should be small and shaped by the batch-1 decode case: a packed-weight GEMM/GEMV micro-kernel per ISA, a direct depthwise convolution, a tiled attention that never materialises the score matrix, fused norm+activation epilogues, and a gather. Winograd and FFT convolution are not worth implementing for the small CNNs Izcal is likely to run (3x3 filters dominate and Winograd's accuracy cost is real); im2col should be treated as a fallback lowering, not the primary convolution. Fusion is the highest-leverage optimisation after the GEMM itself, and it must be described in the graph IR so the reproducible mode can fuse without changing reduction order. Every published peak above is a ceiling for large matrices; the spec's targets should be bandwidth-relative for decode, not percent-of-peak.

**Implications:** Izcal's kernel layer needs four back ends across three ISAs: x86-64 at an AVX2 tier and an AVX-512/VNNI/AMX tier, AArch64 with NEON plus dotprod/i8mm/bf16 and SVE/SVE2, and riscv64 with RVV 1.0 and Zvbb/Zvk* for crypto. The dispatch unit should be the graph node, chosen once from a measured capability record, never a per-element branch. AVX-512 should be selected per generation: beneficial on Ice Lake and Sapphire Rapids, risky on Skylake-X, and unnecessary on AMD where it is two 256-bit halves. On AArch64, SVE must be compiled as VLA or specialised per width; assuming 512 bits will fail on Graviton and phone cores. On riscv64, the measured 7%-of-peak port-contention case means the micro-kernel must be tuned against the actual core, not translated from NEON.

This list is derived from 7.1-7.11, not from a single specification. | 10+ invariants | Derived from [ONNX IR](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/onnx/onnx/main/docs/IR.md), [Crucible](https://professor-moody.github.io/crucible/), [mmap(2)](https://man7.org/linux/man-pages/man2/mmap.2.html) | 2026 | Medium |
| 7.13 | Checksums must be stronger than the optional SHA1 of ONNX external data. A new container should use SHA-256 or BLAKE3 over the whole file and per tensor, and should authenticate the manifest separately if the file is downloaded; a checksum detects corruption, not a malicious publisher. | SHA-256/BLAKE3 | Derived from 7.2, 7.3 | 2026 | Medium |
| 7.14 | Structure-aware fuzzing is the right test strategy because the bugs live in cross-field relationships. A byte-level fuzzer will rarely construct a file whose rank, dilation count and weight shape disagree in exactly the way that reaches the bad index. Izcal should fuzz with a semantic mutator and a bounded, checked parser, and run it under sanitizers. | Semantic mutation | [Crucible](https://professor-moody.github.io/crucible/) | 2026 | High |

**Implications:** Izcal should define its own container rather than ship ONNX or any third-party format: a manifest with explicit dtype, shape, strides, offset, length, quantization metadata and a strong checksum, parsed by a bounded reader that validates every cross-field invariant before mapping. The parser is a security boundary in the same class as an image codec, so it belongs in the sandboxed side of the process model and must be fuzzed with a semantic mutator. ONNX's IR is a useful checklist of what a model graph needs (opsets, initializers, symbolic shapes, external data) but its optional SHA1 and absent mandatory checksum are exactly the gaps Izcal's format must close.

## Unverified and open items

These are recorded so the spec does not present them as settled:

1. **Qualcomm Hexagon**: no primary document was read for TOPS, precision support or dispatch. Treat QNN/AI Engine Direct as an opaque vendor runtime until measured.
2. **Core ML placement**: Apple's public pages read here do not document CPU/GPU/ANE placement. The claim that Core ML makes the final decision is architectural inference from the API shape, not a quoted document.
3. **Apple P/E cores**: no primary measurement of E-core versus P-core inference throughput was read. Measure on the reference Mac.
4. **NUMA and big.LITTLE scheduling**: no primary evidence read for inference on either. The design should defer to the OS and measure.
5. **im2col expansion and speedup**: no primary measurement read; the K_h x K_w factor is arithmetic only.
6. **Memory-arena benefit, page-cache hit rates and weight streaming**: no primary measurements read. These are design assumptions to be measured in the M-phase.
7. **Exact RISC-V ratification dates**: the v1.0 tag is dated 2021-09-20 but is a "frozen for public review" release; the crypto release listing omits years. Record exact dates only if a primary ratification notice is read.
8. **Arm feature introduction versions**: dotprod/FP16 (Armv8.2-A), i8mm/BF16 (Armv8.6-A), SVE2 (Armv9-A) and SME (Armv9.2-A) are stated from the architecture lineage; the Arm ARM text was not read in this session.
9. **x86 per-core sustained GEMM figures**: the peaks in 2.19 are derived from ISA width and issue rate, not measured. An independent black-box measurement on Izcal's reference laptops is still needed.
10. **NPU TOPS**: the 50/60/80-85 figures are secondary aggregators; Apple publishes none. They should appear in the spec only as vendor claims, never as scheduling inputs.
11. **Energy on desktop x86**: the NPU energy evidence read here is mobile and edge; no Lunar Lake package-energy measurement was read.
12. **Small-model CPU prefill**: the laptop measurement in 5.5 is decode-only. Prefill tokens/s on the same class of machine is unmeasured here.
