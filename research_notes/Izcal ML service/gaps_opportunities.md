# Izcal ML service: gaps and opportunities

Compiled 21 October 2026. Scope: the demand, product gaps, economics, privacy, standards, language and evaluation evidence that an on-device, MIT-only, spec-anchored browser ML service can own, and the adjacent work it should not attempt in Stage 1. Primary sources are preferred; the shared web-search budget was exhausted during this pass, so a few rows rest on known URLs fetched directly or on a prior strand's full-text read and are graded accordingly. No third-party project's source code was read, opened or cited; documentation, papers, standards prose, vendor blogs and market reporting only.

Confidence: **High** = primary text read at the source in this pass; **Medium** = official page or abstract read, or primary read by a prior strand and not re-read here; **Low** = secondary source, snippet, recollection or unverified absence. "Derived" means arithmetic from a cited figure, not a measurement.

## 7. Evaluation gaps

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7.1 | **There is no public benchmark for description adequacy.** The standard captioning metrics score reference overlap or image-text similarity; none asks whether a screen-reader user could act on the description, whether it marks uncertainty, or whether it invents a harmful detail. | 0 adequacy benchmarks | [CIDEr](https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.5726); [SPICE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.08822); [CLIPScore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08718) | 2014–2021 | Medium (absence) |
| 7.2 | **The metrics have known limits.** SPICE parses captions into scene graphs and argues n-gram overlap is "neither necessary nor sufficient"; it reports 0.88 system-level correlation with human judgments on MS COCO versus 0.43 for CIDEr and 0.53 for METEOR. CLIPScore is reference-free but weaker for news captions that need richer context. None measures false details. | 0.88 vs 0.43 / 0.53 | [SPICE](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.08822); [CLIPScore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.08718) | 2016-07-29; 2021-04-18 | Medium |
| 7.3 | **Hallucination metrics exist but measure a different thing.** CHAIR uses veridical visual labels to assess the rate of object hallucination, and the authors note that models scoring best on standard sentence metrics "do not always have lower hallucination". It is closer to a false-description metric than CIDEr, but it does not measure omission, uncertainty or task adequacy. | Object hallucination only | [CHAIR paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02156) | 2018-09-06 | Medium (abstract) |
| 7.4 | **WER is not comparable across papers without the same normalization and corpus.** WER is (substitutions + deletions + insertions) / reference words; tokenization, punctuation, casing, number formatting and domain all change the denominator. Whisper's 29.6% tiny versus 9.0% large-v2 is a controlled comparison on Common Voice 5.1 English; cross-vendor WER tables are not. | 29.6% vs 9.0%; normalization-dependent | [Whisper paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.04356) | 2022-12-06 | High (numbers); Medium (comparability analysis) |
| 7.5 | **OCR CER has the same problem, plus a distribution problem.** CER is edit distance over reference characters, but scene-text benchmarks (IIIT-5K, ICDAR, CUTE80) and document benchmarks (DocVQA, SROIE) are different distributions; Tesseract's own documentation says image quality drives accuracy, and no head-to-head photograph-versus-document CER study was verified. | 0 comparable cross-domain CER studies | [Tesseract](https://github.com/tesseract-ocr/tesseract#readme); [Baek et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.01906) | 2019 / 2026 | Medium |
| 7.6 | **No browser publishes a false-description rate.** The measurable target Izcal can own is a human-rated adequacy score plus a false-description rate on a fixed, consented test set, with per-language and per-image-class strata; the spec's provisional <5%† is an Izcal target, not an industry benchmark. | 0 published rates; <5%† proposed | Absence in sources read; Izcal ML-2 | 2026-10-21 | Medium |
| 7.7 | **Caption latency has no published comprehension threshold.** WCAG 1.2.4 requires live captions but sets no delay in milliseconds; no study found ties a specific end-to-end delay to comprehension for generated captions. The target must be measured with users, not copied from a vendor. | 0 published thresholds | [WCAG 1.2.4](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/captions-live.html) | 2023 | Medium (absence) |
| 7.8 | The opportunity is an open, reproducible evaluation: fixed test sets with licences, published model versions and canonical outputs, human adequacy and false-description ratings, provenance/uncertainty checks, per-language and per-script strata, and a replayable harness. No incumbent publishes this, and it would make Izcal's quality claims auditable rather than comparative. | 6 evaluation dimensions | Synthesis of 7.1–7.7 | 2026-10-21 | Medium |

## Opportunities ranked for Izcal

1. **A model artifact and ledger with delete-that-sticks.** Evidence: no browser publishes a model inventory or a per-model licence/provenance ledger (2.1–2.2); Chrome's 4 GB download restored after deletion (2.6); Apple's 8–14 GB has no documented separate deletion (2.6); SPDX's AI profile has licence fields but no provenance/model-card requirements (5.4). This is the trust gap a privacy browser can own outright.
2. **Two independent grants with testable off-states.** Evidence: Firefox's single AI toggle is not a test contract (2.3); Chrome deleted its no-upload sentence (2.3); no standard defines a model host or off-state (5.6). The accessibility grant must work with AI off, which no shipping browser demonstrates.
3. **An open accessibility-quality evaluation with a false-description metric.** Evidence: 53.1% of pages and 16.2% of images lack alt text (1.1); CHI 2024 users need provenance and aberrations (1.7); CIDEr/SPICE/CLIPScore do not measure adequacy (7.1–7.2); CHAIR measures only object hallucination (7.3). No incumbent publishes a rate (7.6).
4. **On-device OCR with a written privacy and reading-order contract.** Evidence: Google estimates 12% of web PDFs are image-only (1.5); 52% of sampled PDFs are untagged (1.5); Chrome's OCR page gives no no-upload or retention statement (2.4); WCAG 1.3.2 and PDF/UA require reading order (1.9, 6.6). This is the clearest Stage 1 task with an existing promise.
5. **Small, English-first description and caption models with published footprint and per-language gates.** Evidence: Firefox's 182M/<200 MB/~2 s proof (2.1); Whisper tiny at 29.6% WER versus 9.0% for large (6.3); Moonshine English-only (6.2); Edge retreated from a 3.8B model to a smaller CPU-reachable one. The opportunity is honest scope, not a language count.
6. **A deterministic numeric mode and a recording that replays without a model.** Evidence: IEEE 754 does not fix reduction order (5.7); no browser has an inference recording contract (2.5); no standard defines reproducibility (5.7). This turns "auditable local AI" from a slogan into a test.
7. **Publish the missing contracts as open specifications.** Evidence: WebNN has one implementation family and needs two (5.1); WebGPU is not Baseline (5.2); no signed model container, weight-provenance format or ARIA provenance flag exists (5.3–5.5). The browser that writes the contract first sets the vocabulary, even if adoption is slow.

## Claims not verified

- Chrome's `weights.bin` size (~4 GB) and download time (~14 minutes) are third-party measurements; Google does not publish the model size, and the Chrome docs point only to `chrome://on-device-internals`.
- The 77-language and seven-script OCR figures are Google's claims; no per-language accuracy or method was published.
- WHO/AFB vision-loss figures (2.2 billion; 49.5 million US adults; 9 million with vision difficulty) were read only as search snippets and an official-page summary, not as the full fact sheet or survey tables.
- The ASSETS 2021 "Minimum Viable Descriptions" details are from a search snippet; the DOI could not be fetched.
- EDPB Guidelines 2/2023's substantive text was not read (the page is a document listing); applying Article 5(3) to model downloads is analysis, and no regulator has decided the question.
- SPDX 3.0.1's formal-standard status and its treatment of model cards/provenance were not established beyond the AI-profile page; SPDX 2.2 is the ISO-adopted version, not 3.0.1.
- ONNX's `metadata_props` and the absence of standard licence/provenance fields were not re-read in this pass; the ONNX IR page was truncated.
- The $0.01/GB egress and $6/TB-month storage prices are from recollection of Backblaze B2's pricing page, not read in this pass; CloudFront's per-GB rate was not published on the page fetched.
- Alexa+'s $1.7 billion 2026 cost is from reported internal documents, not an Amazon disclosure.
- Apple's reported 1.2-trillion-parameter Gemini Siri at ~$1 billion/year and Firefox Smart Window's four hosted backends are secondary reports, not vendor confirmations.
- "No public benchmark for description adequacy" is an absence after a limited search, not a systematic literature review.
- The cumulative script count (~172) is arithmetic from Unicode 16.0's 168 scripts plus Unicode 17.0's four new scripts; the Unicode 16.0 page was not read in this pass.
- The ~100 MB browser-installer figure is a recollection, not a measurement; only the 4 GB model size is sourced (third-party).
- No published per-inference energy figure for browser image description, OCR or captioning was found; the 9.7 J/100-token figure is derived from a mobile NPU/LLM study and is not a browser measurement.
