# Izcal ML service: independent design critique

**Role:** independent design critic (phase 8). I did not write the report or any of its research notes, and I have no stake in the design's acceptance. **Date:** 21 October 2026.

**Files read:** the spec under review (`reports/Izcal ML service.md`, draft 0.1, in full); all seven research notes under `research_notes/Izcal ML service/` (`history_postmortems.md`, `models_architectures.md`, `inference_kernels.md`, `data_licensing.md`, `security_robustness.md`, `gaps_opportunities.md`, `specs_standards.md`); the first-principles-spec skill and its references (spec template); and the relevant sections of `HGBA 2 browser architecture spec.md` (4.5, D4, R-3, I-1 as amended by OD-8), `Future software browser engine.md` (B10, AG-0, DET-1, A11Y-1), `HGBA Stage 1 architecture design.md` (1, 2.1, 2.2, 3.3, 6, 8.3), `Izcal images and media.md` (6.12, 9.3, 9.5, MD-D25), `Izcal PDF.md` (6.10, PD-D11), `Izcal accessibility.md` (9.3, Q-10), `Izcal editing.md` (ED-D9), `Izcal animation.md` (AN-D12), `Izcal DOM, bindings and event loop.md` (DM-D18), `Izcal DevTools spec.md` (DT-D1, DT-D2, DT-D6, DT-15), `Izcal resource footprint.md` (FP-1, FP-22, FP-25, FP-D13, 8.2), `Izcal networking.md` (NW-D17, NW-36), `Izcal JavaScript engine.md` and `Izcal layout.md` (quality-bar comparison), `Izcal WebCrypto.md` (Appendix A format), and `research_notes/Phase 2 reconciliation/reconciliation_2.md` (OD-8) plus `research_notes/Phase 3 amendments/ledger.md` (G-15, A-FUT-3).

**Method.** I checked every quoted claim I rely on against the source document; verified the arithmetic of the staffing, distribution and data-cost numbers; checked each Section 9.5 amendment against the target document it amends; and cross-read the design's central claims (shared service, page invisibility, determinism contract) against the existing specs' invariant-level rules (I-1/OD-8, A11Y-1, DT-D2, PRIV-2, NW-D17). No third-party source code was read; findings rest on the Izcal corpus and first-principles reasoning.

**Independence statement.** This critique was written by an agent that did not author the report or its notes, has not been shown any author responses, and recommends neither acceptance nor rejection; it records what must change before the owner can accept ML-D1–ML-D12 as a block.

## Overall judgement

**Not ready for the owner with revisions.** The spec is a strong first draft: the two-grant semantics, the declarative container and its validation list, the licence ledger, and the recording-by-values contract are the best-worked parts of any spec in this corpus so far, and the flagged-claims discipline is exemplary. But two findings are structural and must be fixed before the owner sees it: **(C-1)** the central process decision — one shared service across all sites and profiles — rests on an owner-decided exception list (OD-8) that it is not part of and whose defining criterion ("parses no page payload") the ML service violates, and no amendment row proposes adding it; **(C-2)** the accepted "captions on" residual gives every page a grant-state oracle, contradicting the spec's own threat-model sentence and the accessibility spec's hard-won principle that no accessibility signal may be content-observable. Six Major findings follow (an unbudgeted load-bearing decision, an unreachable fast-mode recording path, an unmeasurable ML-4 comparator, an unamended download chain, amendments that presuppose or weaken the target documents, and a leakage check without a defined probe). With those fixed, this is an owner-ready spec.

## What is strong

- **The two-grant semantics (6.3)** are the best-executed part of the spec: the 2×2 matrix, the enable/download/disable lifecycle, and the independence tests (ML-10) turn B10's prose into something AG-0 can measure. The G4 exception is correctly argued against G4's purpose rather than its letter.
- **The container and loader (6.6, ML-19)** derive the validation list from the actual CVE classes (cross-field invariants) rather than generic hardening, and the validate-before-mmap rule is the right one; the semantic-mutator fuzzing requirement follows from the evidence.
- **The recording-by-values contract (6.8, ML-7)** is honest about the loss of re-execution fidelity and is the only workable resolution of determinism versus the shared service; the `input_unavailable` handling for ASR is a good catch.
- **The licence ledger (6.7, D0–D5)** has real teeth (build fails on digest mismatch, fail-closed trainer, deny-list, sample audit) and the spec says plainly that the proof is procedural, not mathematical.
- **The flagged-claims section (11.2)** is exemplary: every load-bearing number whose source is weak (Chrome's 4 GB, the 43 PB arithmetic, the NPU figures) is downgraded in the same document that uses it.
