# Izcal networking: fact-check (phase 8)

Checked 19 October 2026 against `reports/Izcal networking.md` (draft 0.1) by a checker who did not write the spec or its notes. Scope: the claims that carry a decision (NW-D1 to NW-D18), a target (NW-1 to NW-41) or the history; dates, numbers, quotes, RFC numbers and statuses, versions and API capabilities; whether each cited note row exists and supports the spec's wording; internal cross-references; and every external link.

Primary sources were read directly: RFC metadata (`rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfcNNNN.json`) and plain text for RFC 9110, 9406, 9659, 9842, 9846, 9848, 9849, 9850, 9875 and 9931; the live Fetch standard and `fetch.bs` at `main` (commit `59afe34`); `draft-ietf-happy-happyeyeballs-v3-04`; `draft-ietf-httpbis-rfc6265bis-22` and its Datatracker page; the WebDriver BiDi `index.bs`; docs.rs for rustls 0.23.46; the rustls 0.23.0 release notes and rustls issue discussions; Chromium's `docs/security/process-sandboxes-by-platform.md` and the Chrome Root Store FAQ at HEAD (prose documents); Chromium Dash's milestone schedule; the Bugzilla REST API; Ladybird's monthly newsletters; and the WWW 2024 (arXiv HTML), NOMS 2026 and SIGCOMM 2017 papers as full text. No web searches were needed. No personal data was sent in any request.

Verdicts: **Correct**; **Partly** (the substance holds but a number, scope, date or wording is off, or the spec overstates its note); **Wrong**; **Unverifiable** (the cited source does not contain the claim and no other source was found); **Not checked** (left to the note's own grading, usually High, for lack of priority). "Must-fix" marks a finding that a decision or target depends on, or a plain factual error in a load-bearing sentence. Every other fix is recommended.

## Must-fix

- **FC-M1 (Section 6.17, Q-NW-1, NW-D12, NW-M0, Section 10 risk row).** The spec treats rustls's GREASE and extension-order support as unknown. Both are answerable now. rustls has **randomized ClientHello extension order by default since 0.23.0** ([release notes](https://github.com/rustls/rustls/releases/tag/v%2F0.23.0), 2024-02-29: "Extension ordering in ClientHello messages are now randomised as an anti-fingerprinting measure"). rustls's maintainers **declined RFC 8701 GREASE**: [issue #357](https://github.com/rustls/rustls/issues/357) was closed on 2022-01-23 with "I think we're not going to do GREASE here. The complexity/value trade-off is not a good one for rustls." Only ECH GREASE (`EchMode::Grease`) exists. The "contribute upstream" path for GREASE therefore runs against a recorded maintainer decision, and M-1 forbids a fork. *(0.3.1: the pull-request link and a source-file comment that 0.3 also cited are removed under M-1 part 3; the release notes state the fact.)*
  - Section 6.17, replace the last two sentences before the outreach sentence with: "rustls already randomizes ClientHello extension order (since 0.23.0, February 2024). It does not send RFC 8701 GREASE values: its maintainers declined the feature in January 2022 (rustls issue #357), and only ECH GREASE is supported. Izcal will propose GREASE upstream with the bot-management evidence; if the maintainers decline again, the owner decides between shipping without GREASE and a provider-level or configuration hook that rustls accepts (Q-NW-1)."
  - Q-NW-1, replace with: "**Q-NW-1.** rustls randomizes extension order but declined RFC 8701 GREASE in 2022 (issue #357). Will its maintainers accept GREASE, or a hook that lets the application add GREASE values, given the fingerprinting evidence in [PP 4.1–4.3]? If not, is a ClientHello without GREASE acceptable for NW-23? (NW-M0: the spike measures Izcal's JA4 with rustls's default shape and opens the upstream discussion.)"
  - Section 10, row "rustls lacks a capability": likelihood **High** for GREASE (Medium stays for ECH detail).
  - NW-D12 cost: "Upstream work in rustls, which declined GREASE once (2022); outreach".
- **FC-M2 (Section 6.5 ECH paragraph; NW-24).** With ECH the outer ClientHello always carries a cleartext SNI: RFC 9849 §6.1 says the client "SHOULD place the value of ECHConfig.contents.public_name" in the outer `server_name`. "0 connections send cleartext SNI" is therefore unmeetable as written, and "never falls back to cleartext SNI" misdescribes RFC 9848 §5.1, which is about falling back to a connection without ECH. The §5.1 text is quoted correctly in [PR 5.4] and says: "ECH-capable, SVCB-optional clients MUST switch to SVCB-reliant connection establishment if SVCB resolution succeeded … and all alternative endpoints have an 'ech' SvcParam."
  - Section 6.5: "Per RFC 9848 §5.1, when SVCB resolution succeeded and every alternative endpoint advertises `ech`, Izcal **never falls back to a connection without ECH**, so the origin's name never appears in a cleartext SNI (the outer SNI carries only the ECH config's public name)."
  - NW-24 metric: "When SVCB resolution succeeded and every alternative endpoint advertises `ech`, 0 connections send a ClientHello whose cleartext SNI is anything other than the ECH config's `public_name` (RFC 9848 §5.1; RFC 9849 §6.1)."
- **FC-M3 (Section 6.5 first sentence; Section 7 dependency table, `aws-lc-rs` row).** "It is the only provider with X25519MLKEM768" overstates [PR 5.6] and [SR 5.3], which say only that the **ring** provider has no post-quantum key exchange. rustls has two built-in providers, and its documentation lists seven third-party providers (boring, graviola, mbedtls, openssl, rustcrypto, symcrypt, wolfcrypt) whose post-quantum support was not checked. The decision rests on this sentence (NW-D6). Replace with: "it is the only **built-in** rustls provider with X25519MLKEM768 (ring has no post-quantum algorithms), and rustls prefers it by default through the `prefer-post-quantum` feature". In the Section 7 table: "Only built-in provider with X25519MLKEM768".
- **FC-M4 (Section 6.7, HTTPS-First).** "matching what Chrome made default for public sites in October 2026" is a plan stated as a shipped fact, and the date is off. Google's post of 28 October 2025 ([Chrome Security](https://security.googleblog.com/2025/10/https-by-default.html)) says "with the release of Chrome 154 in October 2026, we will change the default settings". Chromium Dash's schedule lists Chrome 154's stable date as **22 September 2026** (early stable 9 September), and Chrome 155 is already on the stable channel. Whether the default flipped was not verified. Replace with: "matching the default Google announced for public sites in Chrome 154 (announced October 2025 for October 2026; Chrome 154 reached stable on 22 September 2026; whether the default shipped there was not verified)". Add the item to 11.2. The design (HTTPS-First by default) does not depend on Chrome having shipped it.
- **FC-M5 (Section 6.6, "Pools and coalescing").** The coalescing rule (certificate covers the origin, and the origin's resolved addresses include the connection's peer address) is in **RFC 9110 §4.3.3** ("https Origins": "In practice, a client will make a DNS query to check that the origin's host contains the same server IP address as the established connection"), not §4.3.4 (certificate verification). Under M-1 every algorithm carries an anchor, so a wrong section propagates into code. Change the link to `https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110#section-4.3.3`. Note in the same sentence that §4.3.3 lets an ORIGIN frame remove the address check; Izcal's "restriction, not licence" rule is a deliberate deviation in the strict direction, and should say so.

## Recommended

- **FC-R1 (summary paragraph, 2.1 Network Service row, Section 5 row).** "Chromium's network service is still unsandboxed outside macOS" omits Fuchsia. Chromium's own [process-sandboxes-by-platform.md](https://chromium.googlesource.com/chromium/src/+/HEAD/docs/security/process-sandboxes-by-platform.md) (a prose document) lists Network as "unsandboxed" on Android, Windows and Linux and as sandboxed on Fuchsia and Mac. Use: "Chromium's network service is still unsandboxed by default on Android, Windows and Linux in October 2026 (Chromium's own table)". The 2.2 phrase "still off on three platforms" is then correct as written. *(0.3.1: the 0.3 check also read Chromium feature-flag source and a field-trial configuration file; under M-1 part 3 those readings are withdrawn, and what Stable runs per OS becomes a black-box observation, spec Q-NW-12 (a).)*
- **FC-R2 (2.1 partitioning row).** "Firefox 85 partitions about 20 kinds of state": the [Firefox 85 post](https://blog.mozilla.org/security/2021/01/26/supercookie-protections/) lists 11 caches and 5 kinds of connection or session state (16). The "~20" in [PP 1.13] is today's MDN list. Use "16 kinds of state".
- **FC-R3 (Section 3; Section 6.4 "Receive path first").** The WWW 2024 Table 4 row is "Generate QUIC responses (e.g., ACK) 2.972s" of 8.5 s, and **parsing decrypted frames took more (3.468 s; HTTP/2 3.182 s)**. Write "generating QUIC responses such as ACKs took 2.97 s and frame parsing 3.47 s of the network thread's 8.5 s". In 6.4, "so ACK generation stops dominating CPU" should become "so ACK generation stops being a large share of receive CPU", since it was second to frame parsing.
- **FC-R4 (Section 3, NOMS 2026).** The paper says "the shares of cryptography and file I/O increase significantly, indicating that those pose the new bottlenecks after tackling packet I/O", observed for LSQUIC-DPDK. Write "after packet I/O, crypto and file I/O become the bottlenecks".
- **FC-R5 (Section 6.4, HyStart++).** RFC 9406 §5 says that "at the time of this writing" (May 2023) HyStart++ had been on by default for all Windows TCP for over two years, "with pacing disabled and an actual L = 8". Write "was on by default for all Windows TCP for over two years by 2023 (unpaced)", because Izcal's default is the paced variant.
- **FC-R6 (Section 6.3, priorities).** The 2.6 s → 1.9 s Google Flights LCP came from `fetchpriority=high` on the LCP image alone ([PT 5.3]). Write "`fetchpriority=high` on Google Flights' LCP image moved LCP from 2.6 to 1.9 s".
- **FC-R7 (Section 6.5, revocation bullet).** "because OCSP is already gone ([HP 6.6])" overstates the note. Let's Encrypt, the largest CA, turned its responders off on 2025-08-06 ([Let's Encrypt](https://letsencrypt.org/2025/08/06/ocsp-service-has-reached-end-of-life/)); other CAs still run OCSP. Write "because the largest CA has turned OCSP off and browsers do not query it live". Section 2.2's wording ("OCSP is gone at the largest CA") is already correct.
- **FC-R8 (2.2 last-but-one row; summary paragraph).** "60% of curl's CVEs were logic bugs": [SR 2.1] says 61 of 150 (40.6%) were C mistakes, so about 59% were not; that they are "logic bugs" is the note's classification (Medium). Write "about 60% of curl's CVEs were not C memory mistakes". "Every Rust QUIC panic was unchecked peer input" and "(every Rust QUIC panic on record)" overstate [SR 1.8]: CVE-2024-45311 was a `retry()`-then-`refuse()` state misuse. Write "every Rust QUIC panic on record came from peer input reaching an unchecked assumption (an `unwrap`, an overflow or a state misuse)".
- **FC-R9 (Section 4.2).** RFC 9110, 9000, 9846, 9113, 9114 and 9001 are 36,845 of 86,391 lines, which is 42.6%. Write "about 43%" for "half". The totals (43 RFCs, about 1,490 pages, 2,336 MUST tokens) recompute exactly from [PR 0], and RFC 9846's 7,397 lines and 345 MUSTs were re-measured.
- **FC-R10 (2.1 HTTP/2 row).** Chrome's [post](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/removing-push) says push "will be disabled by default in Chrome 106". Write "disabled by default in Chrome 106".
- **FC-R11 (2.1 2024 row).** The `[commits]` link pointed at the change that **adopted** libcurl (September 2024), not at 2026 hardening. *(0.3.1: under M-1 part 3 commit links are not evidence. The adoption is now cited to [Ladybird's September 2024 newsletter](https://ladybird.org/newsletter/2024-09-30/) ("We've replaced our in-house HTTPS implementation with the curl library"), and the 2026 change to [Ladybird's September 2026 newsletter](https://ladybird.org/newsletter/2026-09-30/) ("the network process now handles cookies for HTTP responses and WebSocket handshakes"). The 0.3 count of "ten hardening commits in one month", read from the commit log, is withdrawn.)*
- **FC-R12 (Section 6.10).** The "RFC 9875" link goes to the httpbis documents list. RFC 9875 "HTTP Cache Groups" exists (Proposed Standard, October 2025). Link `https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9875`.
- **FC-R13 (Section 6.6, Section 7 anchors, 11.2).** Discovery of Designated Resolvers is **RFC 9462** (Proposed Standard, November 2023). Cite it in 6.6, add it to the `hg-dns` anchor row, and drop the 11.2 bullet. Also drop "RFC 9659's 8 MB zstd window limit" from the 11.2 bullet: RFC 9659 §3 says "decoders MUST support a Window_Size of up to and including 8 MB, and encoders MUST NOT generate frames requiring a Window_Size larger than 8 MB". Note in passing that 9659 is Informational but updates RFC 8878.
- **FC-R14 (internal references).** (a) Section 6.13: "the adversarial servers of Section 6.17" should be "of Section 6.16" (`hg-net-oracle`; 6.17 is the wire shape). (b) NW-D2's reversal trigger says "NW-37 shows the per-connection log costs more than 2%† of network-service CPU", but NW-37 measures replay mismatches, not CPU. Either add the CPU metric to NW-37 ("and recording adds ≤ 2%† network-service CPU") or name the measurement ("the lab profile shows …").
- **FC-R15 (Section 9.3, engineer-years).** "About 17 in year one" does not follow from the stated ramp (15–18 at NW-M0, about 30 by month 12): a linear ramp averages about 23. Either say the ramp is back-loaded or recompute: 23 + 36 + 2 × 38 ≈ 135 and 23 + 36 + 3 × 50 ≈ 209, so "about 135–210 engineer-years†". The track total of 38–50 sums correctly (minimums 38, maximums 50).
- **FC-R16 (2.1 Firefox socket process row; Section 5).** Bug 1322426 was opened on 2016-12-07 and is still NEW (REST API). "Still open after ten years" should be "still open after nearly ten years". Bug 1509472 ("Socket Process Isolation work for only WebRTC support") is FIXED, as stated.
- **FC-R17 (Section 6.6, HEv3).** "(bounded 100 ms–2 s)" is Izcal's choice. The draft's recommended minimum is 100 ms, with a hard floor of 10 ms ("MUST NOT be started within 10 milliseconds") and a recommended maximum of 2 s. Say "bounded 100 ms–2 s (the draft's recommended range; its hard floor is 10 ms)".
- **FC-R18 (header).** "written from the six research notes" while seven are listed. `frame.md` is the synthesis author's own, so write "six research notes and the frame note".
- **FC-R19 (notes, not the spec).** [PP 3.2] cites rfc6265bis **-21**, but the spec pins -22. The limits are unchanged in -22 (checked, row 66), so cite -22 in the note. [PR 1.2] says WPT `fetch/http-cache` has 25 files and [PR 7.6] says 17. Reconcile them before NW-3's "100%" is set.
