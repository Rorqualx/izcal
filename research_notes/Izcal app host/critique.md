## Summary

**Counts by severity: 5 Blocker, 21 Major, 11 Minor (37 findings).** No positive-only rows; the decisions checked and found consistent are listed below.

**The five findings that most change the design**

1. **C1, identity and key rotation.** The id is derived from the author key, yet rotation is said both to keep the id and to create a new one. Until this is settled, tickets, storage keys, grants and the directory have no stable principal.
2. **C2, install-time verification.** The broker is given work it is forbidden to do, and no other process runs at install. Either the broker parses attacker bytes or declared >= computed is never checked; a named tier-P verifier is needed.
3. **C3, the app UI's web surface.** The sealed UI is an ordinary web document on a tuple origin. Without a rule that `app://` is denied every undeclared web API and permission, the manifest is not the app's capability surface and AH-1 is not true.
4. **C4, the device family grant at install.** It contradicts DV-D4's per-device chooser, the devices 90-day† model, §6.4's own table and AH-D11. It is the clearest corpus contradiction in the spec.
5. **C5, the `app.intent` binding.** Own-origin-only in §6.8, callable by another app and an agent in §6.14, and bound to all `app://` documents in A10. The sender path and principal check are missing.

**Corpus decisions checked and found consistent**

- **Process map.** The post-devices map is 37 process kinds and 39 threat-model files; the component-host row has the app and replay configurations; A9 adds neither kind nor file. The arithmetic is consistent with security scope 0.3.7, including the devices amendment.
- **Process model and isolation.** One app host per (app, container), no sharing with web content or another app, a 16 GB† cage with a trusted region, no OS handle, socket, file descriptor or device handle. Consistent with HGBA-2 I-1, I-2 and I-3 and with the device service's one-kind-in-roles precedent. The "one kind versus configuration" argument is questioned in C11 on the review-surface claim, not on the isolation claim.
- **G-rule registry.** G220 to G229 are unregistered in the coding standard 0.3.6 (its range ends at G219), and A7 and A15 register the same numbers. G220, G221, G224, G225, G226 and G227 are consistent with the crate layers and the one-funnel rule; the broker-verifier exception is C2.
- **Prompt classes and the person-present rule.** `app-file-read`, `app-file-write`, `app-network`, `app-intent`, `app-autostart`, `app-clipboard`, `app-open-url` and `app-notify` are new and do not collide with the existing classes. A11's extension of A-SH-12 to every `app-*` high class matches CON-R1, CON-R7 and SEC-18/19, and `app-file-write` as high risk is consistent.
- **Recording classes and retention.** The `app.wasi` class, the host-call log, the two-record join by app-session id, REC-R2 encryption and REC-R3's 30-day† retention, DT-D2's per-process segment rule and DT-D5's network views are consistent. The replay mode is C6, not a retention conflict.
- **Storage ownership and preopens.** G150 and SG-D9 leave the storage service as the only process that touches files; the app host holds no handle. `/data` and `/grant/<n>` preopens, directory tickets, read/write split, write-on-close and the five path rules at the OS boundary match storage 0.3.2 §6.14 and A21. The second home for the UI origin's web storage is C15; it does not contradict G150.
- **Device ticket format and LNA.** App tickets reuse DV-D2's versioned, fixed-layout Ed25519 format with the `app:<id>` principal and the 24 h† and 60 s† expiries; DV-D8's virtual device covers app sessions; NW-D4 LNA tickets for app ids with the same MAC and expiry are consistent with networking 0.3.7. The family grant is C4; the arbiter wording is C36; the incomplete NW-D7 tuple is C22.
- **No install telemetry.** AH-9's zero network requests and zero telemetry at install, A23's reuse of EX-D12, and the risk row's named substitutes are consistent with extensions 0.4.1 and with EX-9's review-throughput reversal trigger rather than a data-only update channel.
- **Shell endpoints and surfaces.** `shell.apps` bound to `izcal://shell`, the app panel's place in the shell, SH-D2's prompt surface, SH-D11's 90-day ledger and shell-owned app window chrome are consistent with shell 0.3.4. The amendment's section locators are C29.
- **Footprint.** FP-1's lazy rule and A27's zero-at-idle exclusion for an idle app host are consistent with the footprint spec. The numeric base and per-app line are C26.
- **DevTools.** DT-D2's recorded sessions at navigation, the two-record join and DT-D5's `app` IIP domain are consistent with DevTools 0.3.6.
- **X-3 and the two-engine gate.** A1's "for web exposure only" and A2's row change are consistent with HGBA-2 X-3 being optional and Stage 3, and with the rule that the app host does not depend on X-3. The A1 and A2 merge order is C29.
- **Stage 1 lines 420 and 567.** The out-of-scope bullet is at line 420 and the backlog row at line 567, as A5 says. Line 420 is in §6, not §4, and is scoped to Stages 1 and 2; that is C10 and C29.
- **Coding standard and tiers.** CS-ST-10's declared >= computed discipline, the tier-P rule for every parser, and G224's per-configuration loaded-library closure are applied consistently in §6.17 and A16.
- **M-1.** No part 1, 3 or 4 violation was found. The spec and the app-host research notes cite no third-party source code file, header, commit, diff or review; no shipped dependency is outside MIT or Izcal's own code; the WASI and Component Model documents are standards data; the `wasi-testsuite` and component-model WAST suite are declared test-only oracles with their licences. The one M-1 part 2 weakness is C24: the Component Model anchors are retrieval dates, not pinned revisions.

**Verdict on the two questions asked.** No M-1 violation was found. Corpus contradictions were found: C4 (DV-D4, DV-D5 and AH-D11 against §6.10), C5 (IPC-R12 and §6.14 against §6.8 and A10), C2 (IPC-R15 and G220 against §6.7's broker), C15 (AH-13 against the unaccounted UI origin storage) and C36 (A20 against §6.10). C10 is a misreading of a time-scoped Stage 1-2 line, C6 is an unstated REC-R6 replay mode, and C22 is an incomplete NW-D7 adoption rather than a contradiction.
