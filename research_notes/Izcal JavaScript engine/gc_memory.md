# Izcal JavaScript engine: garbage collection and memory management

Compiled 19 October 2026. Primary sources were preferred (papers, specifications, maintainers' posts, release notes, issue trackers).
Confidence: **High** = primary source read and the claim checked in its text; **Medium** = primary abstract or snippet, or a vendor claim without data; **Low** = secondary source only.

**Scope.** This strand covers phases 2–3 of the first-principles-spec method for the engine's garbage collector (GC): the collector algorithms that lead today, with measurements; how the three browser engines manage the JS heap and the DOM; reference cycles between the DOM and JS, and wrapper design; rooting (precise or conservative), safepoints and barriers; safe GC APIs in Rust; what Wasm GC, `WeakRef` and shared-memory JS require of a host GC; determinism; and leak tooling, footprint and energy. It records ideas and evidence only, with no code and no template (M-1, D-S10).

**Izcal constraints that apply.** HGBA-2 P1 (security first) and M-1; HGBA-2 4.2 (page Wasm runs inside the JS engine because Wasm GC, JSPI and WebIDL need shared GC objects); Stage 1 design 5.2 (the DOM lives in a GC heap, with node headers, Rust-owned payloads, a prefinalizer that frees data-plane and semantic-plane slots, generational stable node IDs, and generated barriered setters), 5.3 (generated WebIDL bindings) and 5.4 (the data plane and the mutation log sit outside the GC heap and are keyed by `NodeId`); Stage 1 design 3.3 and HGBA-3 B1/DET-1 (GC-observable behaviour must be a pure function of inputs under simulation); the coding standard's CS-D3 TokenCell re-entrancy work, which was done against cppgc and must be redone against our own GC.

**Method note.** The web-search budget was used for the most design-relevant questions first. Most other findings come from direct reads of primary pages and papers (PDFs read in full text where noted). Items that could not be checked are graded Medium or Low and say so. Meta's Hermes "Hades" post could not be fetched (404) and is not used.

## 1. Collector algorithms: the state of the art, with measurements

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **Immix introduced the mark-region family.** It allocates into and reclaims contiguous 32 KB blocks of 128 B lines, and uses *opportunistic defragmentation*, which mixes marking and copying in one pass. The three classic tracers each give up one goal (space, fast reclamation or mutator locality); Immix keeps all three. | +7 to 25% total performance on average over 20 benchmarks against canonical collectors; as the mature space of a generational collector it matches or beats a tuned generational one (jbb2000 +5%) | [Blackburn & McKinley, PLDI 2008](https://www.steveblackburn.org/pubs/papers/immix-pldi-2008.pdf) | 2008-06 | High (abstract) |
| 1.2 | **LXR (Latency-critical ImmiX with Reference counting)** argues that short, regular stop-the-world (STW) collections are more efficient than concurrent evacuation. STW pauses do coalescing **reference counting** and judicious copying in an Immix heap; an occasional **concurrent trace** (SATB) finds cyclic garbage. It adds RC remembered sets, lazy decrements and survival-rate triggers. | Lucene in a tight heap: **7.8× the throughput and 10× better p99.99 latency than Shenandoah**. 17 workloads, moderate heap: **+4% throughput over G1, +43% over Shenandoah**. Shenandoah slows down 77% and 37% at 1.3× and 2× heaps. Field-logging write barrier costs **1.6%** on average (worst case h2o 4.6%) | [Zhao, Blackburn & McKinley, PLDI 2022](https://www.steveblackburn.org/pubs/papers/lxr-pldi-2022.pdf) (full text read) | 2022-06 | High |
| 1.3 | **Short pauses do not guarantee low latency.** On lusearch at a 1.3× heap, Shenandoah had lower median and p99 *pause* times than G1, but worse p99.9/p99.99 *request* latency, and **7× lower throughput**, because it could not keep up with a 9.5 GB/s allocation rate on 16 cores. Concurrent copying collectors (C4, Shenandoah, ZGC) need a loaded-value **read barrier** on every reference load. | Table 1 of the paper | [LXR, PLDI 2022](https://www.steveblackburn.org/pubs/papers/lxr-pldi-2022.pdf) | 2022-06 | High |
| 1.4 | **Production GCs cost more than their pause times show.** Using a "lower bound overhead" (LBO) method across five OpenJDK 17 collectors, GC adds **7–82% wall-clock time and 6–92% CPU cycles** over a distilled baseline. Concurrency and generous memory hide the cost. Newer low-pause collectors are much more expensive and sometimes give *worse* application latency than STW collectors. The authors collected RAPL energy readings but left them out for space. | 7–82% time, 6–92% cycles | [Cai, Blackburn, Bond & Maas, ISPASS 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.07880) (full text read) | 2022-05 | High |
| 1.5 | **Generational ZGC** (JDK 21, JEP 439) keeps pauses ≤1 ms (typically <1 ms) with colored pointers, load barriers and a new store barrier that maintains the remembered set. Non-generational ZGC was later made non-default and removed (JEPs 474, 490). | Cassandra: generational ZGC used **¼ of the heap for 4× the throughput** of non-generational ZGC, pauses still <1 ms | [JEP 439](https://openjdk.org/jeps/439) | 2023-09 (JDK 21) | High |
| 1.6 | **Barrier costs are measurable and small for writes, larger for reads.** On an i7-2600, the average mutator overhead was **0.9% for write barriers and 5.4% for read barriers**. The write barrier cost twice as much on in-order Atom. Newer benchmarks expose barrier overhead more than older ones did. | 0.9% write, 5.4% read (i7); read barrier 8.5% after optimization vs an earlier 15.9% | [Yang, Blackburn, Frampton & Hosking, ISMM 2012](https://www.steveblackburn.org/pubs/papers/barrier-ismm-2012.pdf) (abstract) | 2012-06 | High |
| 1.7 | **Conservative stack scanning costs little in itself; the collector built around it is what matters.** On 18 Java benchmarks, ambiguous stack and register roots falsely retained **<0.01%** of objects and pinned **0.03%**. Older conservative designs (mark-sweep, or mostly-copying with whole-page pinning) cost **12% and 45%** relative to generational collection. Conservative Immix and conservative RC with line-grain pinning come within **2–3%** of their exact versions; conservative RC Immix is slightly faster than a tuned exact generational collector. | <0.01% excess retention, 0.03% pinned, 2–3% gap | [Shahriyar, Blackburn & McKinley, OOPSLA 2014](https://www.steveblackburn.org/pubs/papers/consrc-oopsla-2014.pdf) (abstract and §1 read) | 2014-10 | High |

**Implications:** The best published results come from **mark-region heaps with RC or generational young-object reclamation, short STW pauses and a cheap field-logging write barrier**, not from concurrent evacuation with read barriers. Izcal should judge its GC by total cost (LBO-style time, cycles and energy) as well as pause percentiles, never by pauses alone.

## 2. Other runtimes: Go, OCaml 5, and the reference-counting revival

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Go's GC is concurrent, non-moving, non-generational mark-sweep.** One knob (`GOGC`) sets the heap target as live heap + (live heap + roots) × GOGC/100; doubling GOGC doubles the heap overhead and roughly halves GC CPU. The **pacer** takes 25% of CPU during marking and makes allocating goroutines *assist*. `GOMEMLIMIT` (Go 1.19+) is a soft limit, with GC CPU capped at about 50% so that thrashing slows a program by at most 2×. Pauses scale with GOMAXPROCS (stopping goroutines), not heap size. | 25% background mark CPU; ~50% cap under the memory limit | [Go GC guide](https://go.dev/doc/gc-guide) | page current 2026 | High |
| 2.2 | **Go's "Green Tea" GC scans pages, not objects**, so marking walks memory in order and uses caches better. AVX-512 (GFNI) processes page metadata. Experimental in Go 1.25; planned default in Go 1.26. Some workloads see no gain or regress (one object per page). | GC CPU **−10% to −40%** (−10% most common); ~10% more expected from vector code | [Go blog](https://go.dev/blog/greenteagc) | 2025-10-29 | High |
| 2.3 | **OCaml 5's multicore GC kept a stop-the-world parallel minor collector**, because it outperformed the concurrent minor collector (with domain-local minor heaps and a read barrier) "in almost all circumstances", and it does not break the C API. The major heap is mostly concurrent, with a short STW phase per cycle and safepoints added for it. | Qualitative in abstract; evaluation in §6 | [Sivaramakrishnan et al., ICFP 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11663) (full text read) | 2020-08 | High |
| 2.4 | **Perceus** (Koka) does precise, "garbage-free" reference counting with reuse analysis, so functional updates become in-place ("functional but in-place"). It was proved sound and garbage-free in a linear resource calculus. Cycles are not collected. | Claimed "competitive with other state-of-the-art memory collectors"; figures in the paper, not read | [Reinking, Xie, de Moura & Leijen, PLDI 2021](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/perceus-garbage-free-reference-counting-with-reuse/) | 2021-06 | Medium |
| 2.5 | **Lobster's compile-time reference counting** picks one owner per allocation and turns other uses into borrows. Cycles are not collected; a cycle report at exit is "largely sufficient" for its game-prototype programs. | Removes **~95%** of runtime RC operations | [Lobster docs](https://aardappel.github.io/lobster/memory_management.html) | n.d. | Medium (author's own figure) |

**Implications:** RC revivals work where the language controls aliasing (Koka, Lobster) or where tracing backs RC up for cycles (LXR). JavaScript and the DOM have cycles everywhere, so plain RC is ruled out; **RC as a young/mature reclamation mechanism under a tracing backstop (LXR) is a real candidate**. OCaml 5 and LXR both found that short STW parallel pauses beat concurrent designs that need read barriers.

**Wrapper design, compared.**

| Design | Who | Cost | Failure mode seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ref-counted DOM + separate cycle collector | Gecko | Two collectors to schedule; CC pauses up to seconds | Leaks when CC is slow or misses an edge; pauses |
| Ref-counted DOM + liveness heuristics for wrappers | Pre-2017 Blink (object grouping), WebKit (constraints) | Cheap tracing; heuristics | Premature wrapper collection, hidden retaining paths, leaks |
| Traced C++ DOM + separate JS heap, cross-component tracing | Blink 57+ (Oilpan + V8) | Two heaps, two allocators, a wrapper object per exposed node, cross-heap barriers | Complex coordination; conservative native stack (Section 5) |
| DOM object *is* the JS object (one heap) | Servo (reflectors) | All DOM state in one traced heap; Rust rooting discipline | Soundness rested on a lint |

**Implications:** Every engine that kept two memory managers for the DOM and JS paid with leaks, use-after-free or pauses. Izcal owns both sides, so it can make **one heap, one tracer, one barrier discipline** for JS objects, Wasm GC objects and DOM nodes, and decide per node whether a separate wrapper exists at all. The Stage 1 design's split (node object in the GC heap, a projected data plane outside it, keyed by generational IDs) fits this, because the data plane holds no GC pointers.

## 5. Rooting, safepoints and the conservative-stack question

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **V8 is migrating from precise roots (handles, stack maps) toward conservative stack scanning** (V8 bug 13257). Conservative roots remove HandleScope management and two bug classes (a handle outliving its scope; a raw pointer held across a GC point), but objects they point to **cannot move**, and interior pointers must be resolvable. | — | [Wingo 2023](https://wingolog.org/archives/2023/12/07/the-last-5-years-of-v8s-garbage-collector) | 2023-12-07 | Medium |
| 5.2 | **As of November 2025 V8's conservative stack scanning had not shipped.** V8 added **pinning** to its copying scavenger (pages with ambiguous incoming edges are quarantined), which also enables cheaper "direct handles". | "about a decade" to move the idea from Oilpan into V8 | [Wingo 2025](https://wingolog.org/archives/2025/11/13/the-last-couple-years-in-v8s-garbage-collector) | 2025-11-13 | Medium |
| 5.3 | **Security cost: a conservative collector is an address oracle.** Chromium issue 1144662 (November 2020) showed that Oilpan's conservative scan can test whether an object sits at a guessed address: put the guess on the stack (the PoC used Wasm to fill the stack), drop all real references, trigger GC, and see whether the object survived. Chrome closed it **WontFix**, reasoning that Spectre already defeats ASLR inside a renderer and that page script must be assumed able to read its own process's memory. | — | [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2021/02/26/chrome_aslr_bypass) | 2021-02-26 | Medium (press report of the bug thread) |
| 5.4 | **Ambiguous roots also make liveness depend on stale stack contents**, so what survives a GC depends on compiler, ABI and register allocation. SpiderMonkey instead enforces precise roots with a whole-program static hazard analysis (3.7); Nova and gc-arena enforce them with Rust's type system (Section 6). | — | Inference from 1.7, 3.7 and 5.1 | — | Medium (inference) |
| 5.5 | **Safepoints**: Go's pauses are dominated by stopping goroutines (2.1); OCaml 5 had to add safepoints beyond allocation points for its STW phases (2.3); V8 has a global safepoint that stops all background threads with heap access (source `src/heap/safepoint.h`). | — | [Go GC guide](https://go.dev/doc/gc-guide); [OCaml ICFP 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11663) | — | High (Go, OCaml); Medium (V8, search snippet of source) |

**Implications:** The evidence on conservative scanning is two-sided. It costs only 2–3% with a mark-region heap (1.7), and it removes whole classes of handle bugs, but it pins objects, it lets page script probe addresses (5.3), and it makes GC outcomes depend on stack garbage, which threatens **cross-platform determinism** (M0 found V8's precise-root GC hashes identical across macOS, Linux and Windows). An engine written in Rust from scratch can get precise roots from the type system for its own runtime code, and from compiler-emitted stack maps for JIT code, instead of retrofitting either. This is the design question with the most at stake in this strand.

## 6. Garbage collection in Rust: safe API designs

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | **gc-arena**: exact, incremental, cycle-collecting mark-sweep. Pointers `Gc<'gc, T>` carry a **generative ("branded") lifetime** so they cannot escape `Arena::mutate` or mix arenas. **Implications:** Two sound patterns exist: **collect only at points where the type system proves no unrooted pointer is live** (gc-arena's "mutation xor collection", Nova's scope tokens), and **root counting on the pointer** (rust-gc, slow). Neither has been shown at the scale of a JIT-compiling engine with a DOM. Izcal's GC API is a research item: the CS-D3 TokenCell work and Nova's scope tokens point the same way (a capability token proves where GC may happen), and the generational `NodeId` already planned for the DOM is the same idea Nova proposes for handle checks.

## 8. `WeakRef`, `FinalizationRegistry` and weak collections: what the spec allows

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8.1 | **ECMA-262 makes no promise that anything is ever collected.** Liveness is defined through a hypothetical "WeakRef-oblivious execution" and is undecidable; engines approximate it with reachability, with "significant implementation leeway". | — | [ECMA-262 §9.9](https://tc39.es/ecma262/#sec-weakref-processing-model) (text read) | living standard, read 2026-10-19 | High |
| 8.2 | **`deref()` keeps its target alive until the end of the current synchronous job** (`[[KeptAlive]]`, cleared by `ClearKeptObjects`, which hosts call when a synchronous run ends). Neither clearing nor cleanup may interrupt synchronous execution. | — | [ECMA-262 §9.9.1, §9.10](https://tc39.es/ecma262/#sec-weakref-processing-model) | — | High |
| 8.3 | **Clearing is atomic per non-live set**: an engine that empties one `WeakRef` must also empty every other `WeakRef`, registry cell and `WeakMap`/`WeakSet` entry that could otherwise observe the same value. Whether cleanup is enqueued is an **implementation-defined choice**, and callbacks may be omitted for any reason. Scheduling cleanup is the host's (`HostEnqueueFinalizationRegistryCleanupJob`). | — | [ECMA-262 §9.9.3–9.9.4](https://tc39.es/ecma262/#sec-weakref-processing-model) | — | High |
| 8.4 | **Escape analysis may observably empty a `WeakRef`** whose target's identity is never observed; rematerialization that empties a WeakRef to an *observed* object is prohibited. WeakMap values are not live just because they sit in the map (ephemeron semantics). | — | [ECMA-262 §9.9.2–9.9.3 notes](https://tc39.es/ecma262/#sec-weakref-processing-model) | — | High |

**Implications:** The spec gives Izcal full freedom over *when* weak targets clear and finalizers run, and makes the host schedule cleanup. That freedom is what allows Izcal to make GC-observable behaviour **deterministic by construction**: clear at defined points and enqueue cleanup through the substrate scheduler. Ephemerons are mandatory, and the JIT's escape analysis interacts with weak semantics in a way the spec already permits.

## 9. Determinism: GC under simulation and replay

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9.1 | **Izcal's own M0 probe**: with V8's stock platform, the GC-observable channel (finalization order, WeakRef liveness per step) diverged on every OS even with `--predictable --single-threaded` (848, 912 and 989 distinct hashes in 1,000 runs on macOS, Linux and Windows). The forked predictable platform made it deterministic and **bit-identical across the three OSes**; the worker-task mode changed results in release builds only. | 848/912/989 distinct vs 1 | `m0/determinism_crossplatform.md` (local) | 2026-10-14 | High |
| 9.2 | **Replay.io's "effective determinism"**: GC and JIT may differ between recording and replay as long as JS-visible state matches; finalizers are the exception, so the recorder **leaks finalizable objects** (keeps them alive). That works for recordings of a few minutes; the stated plan was to run finalizers at deterministic points. | "a few minutes or less" | [Replay blog (Hackett)](https://www.replay.io/blog/effective-determinism) | 2021-09-14 | High |
| 9.3 | **GC triggers in production engines depend on heap growth heuristics, page sizes, code size and concurrent threads**; M0 noted V8's triggers happened to match across OSes for its script, but this is not guaranteed for JIT-tiering or code-size differences. | — | `m0/determinism_crossplatform.md` | 2026-10-14 | High |

**Implications:** Determinism must be a GC design input, not a mode. The JS-observable surface is small and spec-bounded (Section 8): **when weak targets clear, the order of cleanup callbacks, and memory-pressure-driven behaviour**. If GC *triggers* count allocation in a platform-independent unit (for example, bytes in a canonical object size, not OS pages) and clearing/cleanup happen only at scheduler-defined points, then concurrent and parallel GC work can stay invisible. Conservative stack scanning conflicts with this goal (5.4).

## 10. Shared-memory JS and multi-threaded heaps

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10.1 | **Shared structs (TC39 Stage 2)**: sealed, fixed-layout objects with null prototypes, shareable across agents without copying; fields hold only primitives or other shared objects; shared fixed-length arrays; `Atomics.Mutex` (unlock tokens) and `Atomics.Condition`. | 10.2 | **The proposal names the hard GC parts**: sharing **strings** (engines mutate string representations in place — ropes, slices, flattening), and making mutexes **safe under moving GC** (pin them or implement them in user space). **Implications:** Leak tools today work from **snapshots and heuristics** on top of a GC that does not record why an object is alive. ## 13. Lessons for synthesis (ideas and evidence, never code)

| Project | What happened | Lesson | How Izcal could apply it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gecko CC | Two collectors (GC + CC); CC pauses from ms to seconds | Two memory managers for one object graph cost pauses and leaks | One tracer for JS, Wasm GC and DOM |
| Blink pre-Oilpan | RC DOM leaked through cycles; skipped counts caused UAF | RC for a cyclic, hostile-script-driven graph is unsafe | DOM nodes are GC objects (Stage 1 5.2 already) |
| Blink object grouping → unified heap | Heuristic wrapper liveness lost properties and hid leaks | Liveness must be traced, not inferred | Exact cross-type tracing; consider no separate wrapper |
| Servo reflectors | One GC, but rooting safety rested on a lint | Rooting safety must be in the type system, not a lint | Token/scope-based GC API (gc-arena, Nova lineage) |
| Riptide, Oilpan | Non-moving + conservative stacks; good pauses | Non-moving cores are viable; conservative roots leak addresses and pin | Precise roots where Rust and JIT stack maps allow; measure before choosing |
| V8 conservative migration | A decade to move CSS into V8; still unshipped Nov 2025 | Rooting is near-impossible to change later | Decide the rooting model before the first line of runtime code |
| LXR, OCaml 5 | STW RC/parallel beats concurrent evacuation with read barriers | Prefer short parallel pauses + cheap write barrier | Mark-region + RC/young reclamation + backup trace as the lead candidate |
| Shenandoah (LXR Table 1) | Low pauses, poor tail latency and throughput | Pauses are not latency | Targets on request/frame latency and LBO, not pause only |
| Replay.io | Finalizers broke replay; workaround leaks objects | Finalization is the determinism hazard | Clear weak refs and run cleanup only at scheduler-defined points |
| V8 sandbox | 60% of in-the-wild Chrome exploits were V8 | GC and JIT logic bugs become memory corruption | GC invariants verified (barrier completeness, heap verifier) plus a heap sandbox boundary |

## 14. Open questions and flagged claims

- **Open:** precise vs conservative roots for an engine whose runtime is Rust and whose JIT emits code. The OOPSLA 2014 numbers (2–3% cost) are for Java; there is no published browser-scale measurement of the security cost beyond the WontFix address-oracle bug.
- **Open:** can a mark-region + RC design (LXR-like) keep frame-time p99 low at browser heap sizes (tens to hundreds of MB, many small heaps per process)? LXR was measured on server JVM workloads only.
- **Open:** wrapper-per-node vs DOM-node-is-JS-object in a heap Izcal owns. No modern engine measured both.
- **Open:** a sound Rust API for a moving, incremental GC called from deep Rust call stacks. Nova's contravariant design is not yet sound by its author's own account.
- **Flagged (single-source / commentary):** 3.5, 3.6, 5.1, 5.2 rest on Andy Wingo's reading of V8 commits, not on V8 team publications. 5.3 rests on a press report of the Chromium bug thread. 4.5's telemetry figures come from search snippets of Bugzilla. 7.3 (shipping versions) and parts of 6.3 are from general knowledge and were not re-checked this session. 12.3 is vendor data.
