# Izcal content blocking: list production, governance and delivery

Compiled 19 October 2026. Primary sources were preferred (maintainers' policy pages and READMEs, vendor methodology posts, papers, court reporting, browser documentation).
Confidence: **High** = primary source read and the claim checked in its text; **Medium** = primary abstract or snippet, a search-engine summary of a primary page, a vendor claim without data, or a well-known fact recalled but not re-read this session; **Low** = secondary source only, or unverified.

**Method note.** This strand follows phases 2 and 3 of `.claude/skills/first-principles-spec/SKILL.md` and answers the frame note's list-curator user ("find what tracks, prove it, publish it, catch evasion") and NW-D26, NW-D17, NW-54 and Section 6.15 of `reports/Izcal networking.md`. The session's shared web-search budget ran out after about 25 searches. After that only known primary pages were fetched. Items not re-read this session are marked "(not re-read)" and graded Medium or Low. **M-1 part 3:** no blocker's or engine's source code, commits, diffs or code reviews were read or are linked. **Implications:** Every mature list is either copyleft or non-commercial, which confirms NW-D26. The lab use OD-L2 allows is safe for the GPL, CC BY-SA and MPL lists, but the three **NC-licensed datasets (Disconnect, Tracker Radar, TrackerDB)** need a legal opinion before even internal comparison, because Izcal's lists feed a product. Two production models exist: **hand curation from reports** (EasyList, uBO, AdGuard: tens to hundreds of thousands of commits, a handful of core people) and **measurement-derived classification** (Tracker Radar, Disconnect's pipeline, Privacy Badger, ITP). NW-D26 chose the second, which is the one with the smaller staff need and the published evidence.

**Implications:** Section 6.15 defines "third-party" by **site** (T vs F), not by entity. That is the stricter and more honest choice, and Izcal should keep it: an entity exemption is a policy decision that favours large conglomerates, and entity data is costly to maintain and is NC-licensed where it exists. Izcal still needs **its own entity data for explanation and grouping** ("Blocked here: 23 trackers from 9 companies"), built from WHOIS, certificate SANs, CNAME chains and corporate records, and published with evidence like any entry.

**Implications:** A browser vendor that ships its own list is a **bigger target than a volunteer list**: it is a company with assets in reach, and its list is on by default. Publishing per-entry evidence ("domain X set identifier Y on 412 sites") is a factual statement; it must be accurate, dated, reproducible and correctable, or it becomes a disparagement risk.

## Design ideas for Izcal's list operation (ideas only)

1. **Behaviour-first classification with a human gate.** Classify on Izcal's crawl by Privacy Badger-like and ITP-like signals (identifier-like cookies and storage in a third-party context, cross-site identifier parameters, fingerprinting API use attributed to the script, bounce redirects, CNAME aliasing), with every candidate reviewed before publication (6.15). Never learn on the user's device by default: Privacy Badger's and ITP's experience (1.20, 1.23) shows per-user learned state is a fingerprinting and history-sniffing surface. The **same list for everyone** is a privacy property, not just a convenience.
2. **An entry is a record, not a line.** Each published entry carries the rule, the category, the measured evidence (sites seen, cookies or APIs observed, crawl date, crawl IDs), the reviewer, the first and last seen dates, and its exceptions. The text list is compiled from the records. This gives DevTools its "blocked by rule R of list L, entry E, evidence X" (frame).
3. **Exceptions are entries too.** Allowlist entries carry their reason (breakage report ID, corpus diff). No payment, contract or partner relationship can create one; legal takedowns are published with the demand.
4. **No code-bearing rules in Izcal's lists or in user lists:** no `$rewrite`, no list-supplied scriptlets, `$redirect` only to built-in resources (5.1).
5. **Signed, identical, logged releases.** Sign each version offline; publish each version's hash to an append-only log; clients verify the signature and the log entry, so a targeted list is detectable (5.1). Ship **diffs** keyed by version with a checksum of the result (6.3), plus a full sync every few days.
6. **Uniform fetch schedule.** All clients fetch Izcal's default lists at wake-grid times, with no per-user query parameters; user-added lists are labelled as revealing the user's choice to their host, optionally via the OHTTP relay (6.7).
7. **A crawl that looks like a user.** Headed Izcal, realistic profiles, egress from several regions and networks; measure and publish the crawl's own block rate per CDN and per region (2.2, 2.3); compare crawl results against consented real-user breakage reports, never against user browsing.
8. **Sample beyond popularity lists.** Combine Tranco (recording list IDs) with a CT-derived per-region sample (2.4, 2.8); stratify by region and CDN.
9. **Own entity data**, built from WHOIS, certificate SANs, CNAME chains and corporate records, used to explain and group, **not** to exempt same-entity tracking (3.3).
10. **Published service levels.** 7-day† triage (NW-54), a public appeal path for site operators with a named reviewer, and a public evasion log (domain rotation, CNAME and first-party proxying).
11. **Hosting sized for hostile clients:** rate limits and a cache-friendly CDN, budgeted for several TB a day at scale (5.6).

## Questions for measurement

1. On Izcal's own crawl, what share of sites block or alter a **headed Izcal crawler** against a headless one, per CDN and per region? Does the tracker set seen differ (2.2, 2.3)?
2. What **coverage** does a behaviour-only classifier reach against EasyPrivacy, uBO and AdGuard tracking lists in the lab (GPL lists only until the NC question is answered), and which misses are behavioural blind spots versus crawl gaps?
3. How many **distinct third-party hosts** pass the "≥ 3 sites with identifier-like state" bar on a 100k-site crawl, and how many reviewer-hours does each entry need? This replaces the 3–6† staffing estimate.
4. How fast does a new tracker domain (rotation, CNAME cloaking) reach Izcal's list after first sighting, against the ~410-day lag reported for replica ad domains (4.7)?
5. What **corpus breakage** rate (NW-54's ≤ 1%†) does each list version produce, measured with and without blocking on the same pages?
6. Bandwidth and timing: what is the size of a typical daily diff, and does the uniform schedule hold across OSes and battery states (6.17)?
7. Is list choice observable to pages? Can a page tell which lists or exceptions a user has, beyond PRIV-2's allowance (1.23)?
8. Does a CT-derived sample find tracker hosts that Tranco top 1M crawls miss, and at what liveness-filtering cost?
