# Phase 3, batch 3: record of the owner's decisions of 21 October 2026

Written 21 October 2026. Most of the work lands on branch `spec/phase2c`; the security-scope amendments land on `spec/security-scope` and the coding-standard amendments on `spec/cs-amendments`, each in its own worktree and published when that branch merges. This file records what the owner decided on 21 October 2026 and where each decision is applied. It is a decision record, not a research note: it cites the documents it changes, and every change it names carries its own dated revision line in the document it lands in.

## 1. Every batch-3 recommendation is accepted

The owner accepted, in full and as written, every recommendation in the decision sections of the three batch-3 specifications:

- **`reports/Izcal storage.md` §9.3, SG-D1 to SG-D12** (spec now draft 0.3.1). Notable consequences: **SG-D1** answers the M-1 classification question the owner reserved — Izcal writes its own storage engine, a copy-on-write page forest with commit slots and a value log (SG-D2); SQLite (WAL) is refused, both because it is not MIT-licensable and because WebSQL carries the lesson, and no embedded store documents cross-process snapshot mapping or simulation-first determinism. **SG-D5** rejects Safari's 7-day idle deletion: eviction is whole-bucket and deterministic, and stale data is removed only by explicit clearing or targeted deletion, never by a default timer (idle deletion remains a user setting). **SG-D7** puts at-rest encryption on per-container AEAD keys unwrapped by the broker, with its scope stated honestly — theft and other users, not user-context malware. **SG-D8** records every session from a pinned copy-on-write start state on a scratch shelf served by a dedicated memory-only service instance, so live stores are never touched and replay needs no values-read rule. **SG-D10** sets the blob threshold at 256 KiB† into the value log, with renderer-held smaller blobs. **SG-D11** holds history and bookmarks in the storage service under the SG-D7 key discipline, schema owned by the shell (Q-SG-6). **SG-D12** confirms sessionStorage in the broker and stateless Web Locks, and states restart-revival as unimplemented policy rather than an omission. G153's sole plaintext exception is 6.7's broker-allocated, read-only snapshot staging region, authenticity-verified at publish.
- **`reports/Izcal WebCrypto.md` §9.4, WC-D1 to WC-D10** (spec now draft 0.3). Notable consequences: **WC-D1 closes Q-NW-14 and ledger G-18** — this spec owns `hg-webcrypto` and the `Crypto`/`SubtleCrypto` surface, while `hg-crypto` stays the networking spec's L2s crate under G36 and renderers link it. **WC-D2** grows `hg-crypto`: RSA private-key operations (blinded), ECDSA P-384 signing, ECDH P-384, Ed25519 signing, AES-CBC/CTR/KW and PBKDF2; pins `ctr`, `cbc`, `aes-kw` and `pbkdf2` (MIT-dual); and adds seven operations to the constant-time surface, with NW-50's taint check gaining riscv64 and per-link-configuration runs. **WC-D3** keeps WebCrypto in-renderer, on the invoking renderer's substrate pool, with key bytes in `hg-crypto` secret types; the per-site crypto service is rejected with a written trigger, and no new process kind is added. **WC-D4** draws entropy per call from the OS kernel interface, with no userspace DRBG and fail-closed behaviour. **WC-D6** sets custody: clone-wire records accepted by exactly two sinks (agent messages and IndexedDB; the history/navigation-state sink refuses them), key-bearing records sealed by the creating renderer under the partition's at-rest key so the storage service holds only ciphertext, and the IndexedDB path gated on the storage spec's seal/unseal interface. **WC-D8** records every random draw as a value, with internal draws (blinding, OAEP seeds, PSS salts, nonce hedging, keygen) in the sensitive class — encrypted-at-rest recordings only, redacted on export, because recorded internal draws are key-recovery material. **WC-D9** keeps post-quantum out of the web API in Stage 1, with a test-only, from-FIPS-203-prose ML-KEM oracle. **WC-D10** adds the `crypto` IIP domain, whose types structurally cannot hold key bytes.
- **`reports/Izcal extensions.md` §9.4, EX-D1 to EX-D16** (spec now draft 0.3). Notable consequences: **EX-D1** fixes the compatibility surface as three published rings (implemented / accepted-as-data / refused), each Ring A namespace under a published conformance contract. **EX-D2** supports MV3 only and refuses MV2 with a published migration mapping and tool. **EX-D3** puts one component host per extension origin per profile on the jitless embedded engine (JS-D10), content scripts in isolated worlds, and extension pages in own-origin renderers. **EX-D5** replaces install-time-all with capability tuples: install-time declaration is a ceiling, grants are runtime, per-origin, per-container, revocable, expiring, and prompts sit in the GR-D28 layer. **EX-D6** maps extension storage to the storage service, per (extension, profile), per (extension, container) in `split` mode, and stages `storage.sync` to Stage 3 as a published incompatibility. **EX-D7** adopts the content-blocking spec's three tiers, makes DNR JSON data and `webRequest` an observational adapter over the audited tier-3 path, and refuses blocking forms. **EX-D9** makes native messaging broker-mediated end to end, through one guarded door. **EX-D10** commits to an Izcal directory with published review decisions and a decision log, a signing service and a uniform update grid. **EX-D12** defaults resource-URL randomization on and measures extension enumeration. **EX-D16** routes extension edits through ED-D21 intents, with no input synthesis and no activation minting. EX-D3 also replaces Wasmtime with the component host in HGBA-2 W-1/X-3 and Stage 1 D-S3, and EX-D4 makes extension origins their own site keys (HGBA-2 I-1, `ext://<id>` in `hg-url`).

Each spec's decision section carries the stamp "**Owner decision (21 October 2026): every recommendation below is accepted.**", in the form `reports/Izcal JavaScript engine.md` §9.4 uses, and each has a dated revision line (0.2 → 0.3, a notable revision under `CONTRIBUTING.md`; storage's 0.3.1 adds the two receiver rows the WebCrypto and extensions specs require).

## 2. How the batch lands

Three points about where the decisions land, each recorded rather than silently resolved:

- **The storage decision table is §9.3, not §9.4.** The batch task named §9.4; the storage spec's §9.4 is its crate rules (G150–G159) and its decisions are §9.3 (SG-D1–SG-D12). The owner accepted §9.3 in full. No section was renumbered: moving the decision table above the crate rules would have broken the spec's own cross-references for no gain.
- **HGBA-2 2.3.4 is a revision of the frozen spec, not the 2.4.0 rewrite.** The batch-3 HGBA-2 rows — the storage service in the Section 3 diagram, the 4.6 persistence sentence (SG-D1, SG-D7) that retires the SQLite-in-WAL rule, the component host in 4.2/D5, W-1 and X-3 (EX-D3), and extension-origin site keys in I-1 (EX-D4, A-EX-10) — are local amendments and are applied in 2.3.4 with a dated revision line. The superseded-section rewrites queued as A-H2S, and the 2.4.0 version itself, still land in 2.4.0; no superseded decision changed. Nothing from WebCrypto targets HGBA-2: its amendments go to the networking, JavaScript, DOM, Stage 1, footprint, DevTools, security-scope, storage and coding-standard documents and to `CONTRIBUTING.md`.
- **The shell row is handed to the shell owner, not applied.** A-EX-12 (the Extensions page and its slots) is the one extensions row not applied in this batch: the shell surface is being rewritten as `reports/Izcal shell.md` (batch 4, started 21 October 2026), so the row — and Q-SG-6's dependency on the shell's settings UI — land with that spec. `reports/HGBA shell UI design.md` was not edited, and A-EX-12 stays open in the ledger.

## 4. What is left to later owners

- **A-EX-12, and Q-SG-6's settings-UI half, to the shell owner.** The Extensions page and slots land with `reports/Izcal shell.md` (batch 4). Q-SG-6 (history and bookmark schema ownership) also names the shell design: SG-D11 claims the stores, the schema and settings UI belong to the shell, and an A-UI-class row is pre-seeded in the storage spec's §9.5 for Stage 1 13.2.
- **Q-SG-7 to the security-scope owner.** Storage 6.14 yields if the security scope's SH-R6–R9 assign the picked-file byte transport elsewhere.
- **A-SG-12 pending the security-scope merge.** The storage-service threat model — assets, capabilities and key discipline per SG-D7, and the attribution-history asset named — is the storage row awaiting that merge; A-SG-13 is queued for HGBA-3 (§3).
- **Q-EX-2 resolves at EX-M2 by measurement.** Whether real extension workloads meet EX-4's latency and CPU budgets on the jitless embedded engine; a measured shortfall escalates to the owner as EX-D3's reversal trigger, never as a silent tier raise.
- **Q-EX-4 by later decision.** Witness cosigning for the directory's review decision log is a non-staff governance cost, put to the owner with the directory launch (EX-D10).
- **WebCrypto's six questions, by measurement or later decision.** Q-WC-1 (when the W3C registry admits ML-KEM and ML-DSA) is watched at each pin bump and follows the registry or an owner decision (WC-D9). Q-WC-2 (the storage seal/unseal interface) is accepted on both sides — the storage spec's 0.3.1 receiver rows state the requirement — and the wording is coordinated with G-19's owner before WC-M3. Q-WC-3 (whether corpus measurements ever justify X448) is reviewed at each corpus refresh against the dead-surface rule. Q-WC-4 (the PBKDF2 600 000†-iteration and GCM 96-bit† warning floors) is confirmed or adjusted at WC-M4 with the use-counter data. Q-WC-5 (Windows `ProcessPrng` on the oldest targets) is confirmed at WC-M0, with `BCryptGenRandom` the documented fallback. Q-WC-6 (reseed on restore) binds any future checkpoint/restore feature and is owned jointly with the substrate and footprint specs when one is proposed.
- **Two pre-written contingencies, recorded in the ledger, not applied.** DT-D2's worker-replay clause waits on a DevTools owner decision; WC-D3's key-service process kind waits on WC-D3's reversal trigger (measured exfiltration-class exploitation, or an owner P1 decision on E2EE custody).
