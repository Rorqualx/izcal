# Izcal compliance and security response: history and postmortems

Compiled 24 October 2026. Primary sources were preferred (regulation text on EUR-Lex and legislation.gov.uk, standards bodies' catalogue pages, vendors' policy pages and posts, maintainers' blog posts, mailing-list posts, government agency pages).
Confidence: **High** = primary source read and the claim checked in its text; **Medium** = primary abstract or snippet, or a vendor claim without data, or a secondary report that agrees with a primary date; **Low** = secondary source only.

**M-1 statement.** No third-party source code was opened, read, linked or cited. No code files, headers, commits, diffs, code reviews or repository data files were read. Exodus Intelligence's 2019 patch-gapping post includes exploit code, so it was not opened; secondary news coverage is cited instead. Only regulation text, standards catalogue pages, vendor policy and accessibility-report PDFs, maintainers' blog posts, mailing-list prose and news were read. No value in this note comes from code.

**Search budget.** About 30 web searches were used before the shared session budget ran out. Every priority-1 to priority-5 question got at least one search or a direct read of a known primary page. After the budget ran out, only known primary pages were read directly. Rows that rest on memory or on a search snippet alone are graded Medium or Low and are listed in "Claims I could not verify".

## 0. Chronology

| 2019–2023 | **Chrome patch gap** | Fixes land in public source before they ship. | An open repository makes every fix visible. | Attackers can turn a public fix into an exploit (n-day). | Stable updates went to every two weeks (gap from 35 to about 15 days), then weekly from Chrome 116 (August 2023). | Shipping cadence treated as a security control. |

**Implications:** Every durable norm since 2000 has three parts: an acknowledgement deadline, a fix deadline and a patch-adoption window. Izcal should publish all three in its own policy and measure itself against them. Multi-party cases fail when parties cannot talk to each other (Meltdown) or when notice is improvised (Heartbleed). So Izcal needs a written embargo procedure, including whom it notifies downstream (Linux distribution packagers) and which coordinator it uses, before the first binary. xz shows that a disclosure can bypass upstream entirely when upstream is suspect. Release integrity (UPD-R1–R5, a second release signer) is part of security response, not separate from it.

**Implications:** Browser vendors converged on three things: a short severity scale defined by browser-specific exploit conditions (Mozilla's), numbered advisories per release, and a shipping cadence chosen to close the patch gap. Izcal will be open source, so every fix is public when it lands. SEC-1 and SEC-2 must therefore be clocked from the time the fix lands, not only from the report. Either fixes land privately until release, or the release follows within days. The AI-report flood (curl, Chrome VRP) means intake needs filtering, labelling and a published rule for rejecting reports before any bounty exists. Apple's BSI shows that a mitigation channel between releases (UPD-R5) is now standard practice.

| 3.8 | The **Linux kernel became a CNA** in February 2024. Greg Kroah-Hartman: "the system overall is broken in many ways, but this change is a way for us to take more responsibility". heise counted about 3,375 kernel CVEs in the first months, about 100 of them withdrawn, and critics said many fix no real vulnerability. | ~3,375 CVEs | [LWN 978711](https://lwn.net/Articles/978711); [heise](https://heise.de/-9963850) | 2024 | Medium |

**Implications:** The CVE ID survived 27 years. Its supply chain did not hold: one contract almost lapsed, NVD enrichment collapsed, and third-party scores override the vendor's. A new vendor should be its own source of truth. That means becoming a CNA (under the MITRE Root or ENISA's Root), publishing its own machine-readable advisories with its own severity and affected-version data (Izcal's advisory format, CSAF/OSV-class), and mapping to CVE, EUVD and GCVE IDs rather than depending on any one of them. The kernel's experience shows a CNA must publish its assignment rule, or volume becomes a dispute. curl's experience shows a vendor that publishes CVSS invites rescoring, while one that does not publish it still gets rescored. Izcal should publish its own scale and say how it maps to CVSS.

**Implications:** Regulation has moved from voluntary pledges (CISA, 2024) to law with dates. CRA reporting has applied since 11 September 2026, and PLD liability for software and for missing security updates starts on 9 December 2026. Both laws use the same tests for commercial activity: a price, monetising other services through the platform, personal data as a condition of use, and donations beyond cost. So FI-D11 (plan as a CRA manufacturer) is the safe reading. Search-revenue deals, paid support or donations beyond cost would each point to "commercial". FI-D12 (opt-in telemetry only) keeps Izcal clear of the "personal data as a condition for use" test. Whether Izcal, or a foundation behind it, could be a steward rather than a manufacturer is a question for legal advice. PSTI likely does not reach a desktop browser, but its published-support-period rule matches CRA Art. 13 and is cheap to meet anyway.

**Implications:** Browser ACRs have converged on one scope: the browser UI plus "exposure of web content to OS accessibility APIs", with no claim for third-party content and with DevTools excluded. ## 6. Lessons for a new browser vendor

| Project | What happened | Lesson | How Izcal applies it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bugtraq, RFPolicy | Researchers published because vendors were silent. | A vendor that does not acknowledge reports loses control of the timeline. | Publish acknowledgement and status-update times (also a PSTI-style duty). |
| Project Zero 2020→2021 | Full 90 did not change fix times. Adoption mattered. | Measure fixes as installed, not shipped. | SEC targets clocked to installed fixes, using update telemetry only under FI-D12's opt-in. |
| Meltdown/Spectre | Siloed, uneven notice. | Coordination needs a written process and a shared channel. | A written embargo procedure naming downstream packagers and the coordinator. |
| Heartbleed | Ad hoc pre-notice. Some distributions were missed. | Keep a downstream contact list before you need it. | A packager notice list kept as part of the release process. |
| xz-utils | A single maintainer was taken over. Disclosure went around upstream. | Release integrity is a security-response issue. | A second release signer before the first binary (SEC-D4), and reproducible, attested releases. |
| Chrome patch gap | Public fixes became n-days. | Cadence is a control. | The SEC-1/SEC-2 clock starts when the fix lands. Ship emergency releases within days. |
| Apple RSR→BSI | User action killed RSR uptake. | Out-of-band fixes must install by default. | UPD-R5 mitigation channel on by default, with a published removal rule. |
| curl, Chrome VRP 2026 | AI report floods. | Money draws noise, and intake needs triage at scale. | Labelled intake, a published rejection rule, no bounty until triage is staffed (D-S8). |
| CVE/NVD 2024–2026 | Registry funding and enrichment failed. | Do not depend on one registry. | Be a CNA, publish machine-readable advisories, and map to CVE, EUVD and GCVE. |
| Linux kernel CNA | Volume dispute. | Publish your CVE assignment rule. | A written rule for which fixes get IDs. |
| curl CVSS | Rescoring by third parties. | Own the severity statement. | Own scale (Mozilla-style, browser-specific) plus a stated CVSS mapping. |
| CRA, PLD | Law with dates. Monetisation tests. | Commercial status turns on revenue and data. | FI-D11, FI-D12. Legal advice before EU distribution. |
| VPATs | Self-attested and stale. | Generate reports from tests per release. | A per-platform ACR from the accessibility-tree test suite. |

## Open questions for legal advice or black-box checking

1. **CRA status.** Would any planned revenue source (search-engine deals, paid support, donations above cost, enterprise builds) make Izcal's supply "commercial" under recital 15? Could a not-for-profit entity behind Izcal be a **steward** (Art. 3(14), Art. 24) instead of a manufacturer, given that it would also develop the product? Recital 18 settles only the not-for-profit case where "all earnings after costs" go to not-for-profit objectives.
2. **PSTI.** Is a stand-alone browser distributed by download a "relevant connectable product", or only software on an excepted computer (Schedule 3, para. 5)? On Android (Stage 2), is Izcal "software used for its intended purposes" of a smartphone? Note that smartphones are carved out of paragraphs 2 and 3 of Schedule 1.
3. **PLD.** From 9 December 2026, does an MIT-licensed browser distributed without charge but with optional donations fall under Art. 2(2)? Does Art. 11(2)(c), on lack of security updates, create liability for versions outside the published support period?
4. **CVE program after March 2026.** What are the current MITRE contract term and CVE Foundation role? Check cve.org and CISA directly.
5. **ENISA Root and the CRA.** Does ENISA's CVE Root role connect to the CRA single reporting platform (Art. 16)? ENISA's August 2026 page is silent on this.
6. **EN 301 549 V4.** Has it been published, and is it cited in the OJ as the EAA harmonised standard? Check ETSI and the OJ.
7. **Chromium VRP 2010 amounts and Chrome 94 start.** The post bodies did not render. Re-read them in a browser.
