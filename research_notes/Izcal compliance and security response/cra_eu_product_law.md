# Izcal compliance and security response: the CRA and EU product law, read from the primary text

Compiled 24 October 2026. Primary sources were preferred (the Official Journal text of Regulation (EU) 2024/2847, its delegated and implementing acts, Directive (EU) 2024/2853, Directive (EU) 2022/2555, European Commission and ENISA pages).
Confidence: **High** = primary source read and the claim checked in its text; **High-R** = the full operative text was read in a verbatim reproduction of the Official Journal (springlex.eu) and not on EUR-Lex itself; **Medium** = primary abstract or snippet, or a vendor claim without data; **Low** = secondary source only.

**How the text was read.** EUR-Lex answered every scripted request for the CRA with an empty HTTP 202 bot challenge, so the CRA articles, recitals and annexes were read in full from springlex.eu's reproduction of OJ L 2024/2847 of 20 November 2024. That site marks its corrigenda (for example the 2 July 2025 corrigendum to Art. 64(10)). Rows graded High-R should be compared once against the [EUR-Lex text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R2847) or the [ELI page](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/2847/oj/eng) before the spec cites them. Springlex does not print paragraph numbers. I numbered them from its paragraph anchors, and the cross-references inside the Regulation confirm the numbering: Art. 18(2) cites "Article 13(12), first subparagraph" for the technical documentation, and Art. 28(3) cites "Article 13(20)" for the simplified declaration. The revised Product Liability Directive was read in full on its [EUR-Lex ELI page](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/2853/oj/eng).

**M-1 statement.** No third-party source code was opened, read, linked or cited. No code files, headers, commits, diffs, code reviews or code-repository data files were read. That includes ETSI and CEN standards repositories and the code-host wiki that one search returned. **Search budget.** Five web searches ran before the shared budget ran out (the sixth was refused). ## 1. Scope and role: who is a manufacturer, and when is a browser "made available on the market"

**Implications:** The CRA reaches Izcal only through "commercial activity", and recital 15 names the triggers. FI-D11 (plan as manufacturer) is the safe default. Art. 24 still makes Art. 14 reporting apply under the steward route, so the 24 h / 72 h / 14-day runbook is needed whichever role legal advice confirms.

## 3. Annex I Part I: essential cybersecurity requirements for the product's properties

All items apply "On the basis of the cybersecurity risk assessment referred to in Article 13(2) and where applicable". Source for every row: [CRA Annex I](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/annex-1/), 2024-11-20, High-R.

| # | Item | Verbatim operative phrase | Design consequence for Izcal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | Part I(1) | "designed, developed and produced in such a way that they ensure an appropriate level of cybersecurity based on the risks" | The security scope's threat models become the Art. 13(2) risk assessment. |
| 3.2 | (2)(a) | "be made available on the market **without known exploitable vulnerabilities**" | Release gate: no open, known-exploitable issue in any shipped component (own code and SBOM matches) at publish time. |
| 3.3 | (2)(b) | "a **secure by default configuration** … including the possibility to **reset the product to its original state**" | A "reset to defaults" path for each profile. Defaults documented as part of conformity. |
| 3.4 | (2)(c) | "**automatic security updates** that are installed within an appropriate timeframe **enabled as a default setting**, with a **clear and easy-to-use opt-out mechanism**, through the **notification of available updates** to users, and the **option to temporarily postpone them**" | Auto-update on by default. One-click opt-out with instructions (Annex II(8)(e)). A postpone control and an update notification. Recital 56 adds "the possibility to approve the download and installation of the security updates as a final step". Admin policy may disable updates (recital 56: professional settings). |
| 3.5 | (2)(d) | "protection from unauthorised access by appropriate control mechanisms … and **report on possible unauthorised access**" | Profile and sync authentication. Visible reporting of sign-ins and new devices (Stage 2 sync). |
| 3.6 | (2)(e) | "protect the **confidentiality** of stored, transmitted or otherwise processed data … such as by encrypting relevant data at rest or in transit by state of the art mechanisms" | Profile secrets encrypted at rest. TLS everywhere. End-to-end encrypted sync (Stage 2). |
| 3.7 | (2)(f) | "protect the **integrity** of … data … commands, programs and configuration against any manipulation or modification not authorised by the user, and **report on corruptions**" | Signed binaries and data channels (UPD-R1 to R5). Integrity checks on profile stores, with a user-visible corruption report. |
| 3.8 | (2)(g) | "process only data … adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary in relation to the intended purpose … (**data minimisation**)" | Telemetry and crash records hold only what is needed (FI-11, FI-12). This applies to remote data processing too. |
| 3.9 | (2)(h) | "protect the **availability of essential and basic functions, also after an incident**, including through resilience and mitigation measures against denial-of-service attacks" | Recovery window and safe mode. Updater works when servers degrade. |
| 3.10 | (2)(i) | "**minimise the negative impact** by the products themselves or connected devices on the availability of services provided by other devices or networks" | Rate limits on retries, prefetch and update polling, with backoff and jitter. |
| 3.11 | (2)(j) | "**limit attack surfaces**, including external interfaces" | Process-model and capability-IPC evidence. Off-by-default for remote debugging and agent ports. |
| 3.12 | (2)(k) | "**reduce the impact of an incident** using appropriate **exploitation mitigation mechanisms** and techniques" | Sandboxing, CET/CFG, Hardened Runtime: list them in the technical documentation. |
| 3.13 | (2)(l) | "provide security related information by **recording and monitoring relevant internal activity**, including the access to or modification of data, services or functions, **with an opt-out mechanism for the user**" | A local security event log (permission grants, extension installs, policy changes, update events) that the user can switch off. It stays local unless the user opts in (FI-D12). |
| 3.14 | (2)(m) | "the possibility for users to **securely and easily remove on a permanent basis all data and settings** and, where such data can be transferred to other products or systems, ensure that this is done in a secure manner" | Profile wipe (including server-side sync data in Stage 2) and a secure profile export. |

**Implications:** Most Part I items are already intended in Izcal's security scope. The new design items are reset to defaults (2)(b), update postpone and notification (2)(c), a user-switchable local security log (2)(l), corruption reporting (2)(f), and full data removal including server-held data (2)(m).

## 4. Annex I Part II: vulnerability handling, and the SBOM

Source for rows 4.1–4.8: [CRA Annex I Part II](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/annex-1/), 2024-11-20, High-R.

| # | Item | Verbatim operative phrase | Design consequence for Izcal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | II(1) | "identify and document vulnerabilities and components …, including by drawing up a **software bill of materials in a commonly used and machine-readable format covering at the very least the top-level dependencies**" | SBOM generated per build and per channel from the lockfile and vendored data sets (fonts, lists, ICU-like data), in SPDX or CycloneDX. Izcal goes further than the minimum: transitive dependencies included (†, first-principles target). |
| 4.2 | II(2) | "address and remediate vulnerabilities **without delay**, including by providing security updates; **where technically feasible, new security updates shall be provided separately from functionality updates**" | A security-only update path: a patch release on the current stable branch without feature changes. | 4.3 | II(3) | "apply **effective and regular tests and reviews** of the security" | Fuzzing, external audits and red-team cadence recorded as test reports (Annex VII(6)). |
| 4.4 | II(4) | "once a security update has been made available, **share and publicly disclose information about fixed vulnerabilities**, including a description …, information allowing users to identify the product … affected, the impacts …, their **severity** and clear and accessible information helping users to remediate"; "in duly justified cases … they **may delay** making public information regarding a fixed vulnerability **until after users have been given the possibility to apply the relevant patch**" | Public advisories for every fixed vulnerability, with a severity scale (PO-16.6) and affected version ranges. | 4.5 | II(5) | "put in place and **enforce** a policy on **coordinated vulnerability disclosure**" | A published CVD policy. | 4.6 | II(6) | "facilitate the sharing of information about potential vulnerabilities in their product … as well as in **third-party components** …, including by providing a **contact address** for the reporting" | A security contact on the website and in the product (Art. 13(17), Annex II(2)). Upstream reporting for dependencies (Art. 13(6)). |
| 4.7 | II(7) | "mechanisms to **securely distribute updates** … to ensure that vulnerabilities are fixed or mitigated in a timely manner and, where applicable for security updates, in an **automatic manner**" | TUF-based updater (UPD-R1 to R5), described in the technical documentation (Annex VII(2)(b)). |
| 4.8 | II(8) | "where security updates are available …, they are **disseminated without delay** and … **free of charge, accompanied by advisory messages** providing users with the relevant information, including on potential action to be taken" | Security updates are never paywalled, including in any enterprise edition (except a tailor-made, contractually agreed business product). Release notes carry an advisory link. |
| 4.9 | **SBOM publication.** Recital 77: "Manufacturers **should not be obliged to make the SBOM public**." Annex VII(8): the SBOM goes into the technical documentation "further to a reasoned request from a market surveillance authority". Annex II(9): "**If the manufacturer decides to make available** the software bill of materials to the user, information on where [it] can be accessed." If Izcal uses Art. 32(5) (public technical documentation, 6.6), Annex VII(2)(b) lists "the software bill of materials" in the documentation, which suggests the SBOM becomes public too (open question L-7). | 4.10 | SBOM format: "The Commission may, by means of implementing acts …, specify the format and elements of the software bill of materials". | Art. 13(24) | [CRA Art. 13](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/cra/cra-regulation/article-13/) | 2024-11-20 | High-R | No such implementing act was found by 24 October 2026 (not searched; budget). Generate both SPDX and CycloneDX until one is fixed. |

**Implications:** Part II is mostly process, and process is cheap to design in now. The two engineering items are a security-only update path, required "where technically feasible", and an SBOM produced per build. A rolling release that bundles features with fixes would need a written justification that separation is not feasible.

## Open questions for legal advice or black-box checking

- **L-1 (establishment).** Where will the Izcal legal entity be established? This decides the coordinating CSIRT (Art. 14(7)), the languages of user information and declarations (Arts. 13(18), 28(2)), and whether an authorised representative is needed. Does a non-EU manufacturer that distributes only by direct download need an EU authorised representative or another EU-established economic operator under Regulation (EU) 2019/1020 as amended by CRA Art. 66? Not read.
- **L-2 (role).** Can an Izcal foundation that ships the end-user browser under its own name be a *steward* rather than a manufacturer, given Art. 3(14) "other than a manufacturer" and recital 19's "intended for commercial activities"? Or is a non-monetised Izcal simply outside the CRA, with no role at all?
- **L-3 (Nightly).** Is a permanent rolling Nightly or Beta channel "made available only for a limited period required for testing purposes" (Art. 4(3)), when each build lives for days but the channel exists indefinitely? Recital 37 says "for the time necessary to test it and gather feedback".
- **L-4 (Art. 14 and testing builds).** Does Art. 14 reporting apply to an actively exploited vulnerability that exists *only* in a testing build that Art. 4(3) lets through without compliance? Recital 37 asks for vulnerability handling "to the extent possible".
- **L-5 (product unit).** For a browser released every few weeks, what is "the product" whose support period runs and which is "placed on the market": each version, each major line, or the product name? Is each feature release a substantial modification (recital 39)? What does guidance C(2026) 5252 §§ on support periods say?
- **L-6 (OS drops).** If Izcal stops supporting an OS version during a support period, do users stuck on that OS "incur additional costs to adjust the hardware and software environment" (Art. 13(10)), so that security updates must continue for the last compatible version until the support period ends (by analogy with recital 40's smartphone sentence)?
- **L-7 (Art. 32(5) publication).** Does "the technical documentation … made available to the public" require publishing the full risk assessment, test reports and SBOM, or may security-sensitive parts be withheld? Does a module A self-assessment under Art. 32(5) still need any notified body involvement?
- **L-8 (funding).** For the chosen D-S8 option: is a default-search revenue share "monetis[ing] other services" (recital 15)? Does a paid edition make the free MIT edition commercial? How are "costs" measured for the donations test? Does recital 18's not-for-profit proviso override recital 15's signs?
- **L-9 (remote data processing and evidence).** Are update servers, crash ingestion, opt-in telemetry, and Stage 2 sync and push "remote data processing solutions" (Art. 3(2))? May update-server request geography be used to fill in Art. 14(2)(a)'s Member-State list and Art. 14(7)(d)'s "highest number of users" without breaching FI-D12 or the GDPR?
- **L-10 (NIS2).** At what size would end-to-end-encrypted sync be a "cloud computing service" under NIS2 Annex I? Would an Izcal-run DoH resolver make Izcal a "DNS service provider" in scope regardless of size (Art. 2(2))?
- **Black-box checks.** (a) Read EN 304 617's published clauses when ETSI releases them, and record its OJ citation date. (b) Check NANDO for CRA notified bodies. (c) Check whether an implementing act on the SBOM format (Art. 13(24)) or on notification formats (Art. 14(10)) has been adopted. (d) Confirm the SRP portal address on ENISA's own site.
