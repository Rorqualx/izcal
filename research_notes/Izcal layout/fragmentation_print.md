## 1. The fragmentation model (CSS Fragmentation 3 and 4)

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | Fragmentation 3 is still the 2018 Candidate Recommendation. The `region` and `avoid-region` values are at risk. | CR 2018-12-04; no later TR snapshot found | [css-break-3 CR](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/) | 2018-12-04 | High |
| 1.2 | **Core terms (§2).** A **fragmentainer** is a box (a page, column box or region) that holds part of a fragmented flow. A **fragmentation context** is an ordered series of fragmentainers with one block flow direction. A **box fragment** is the part of a box in exactly one fragmentainer. The **remaining fragmentainer extent** is the block-axis space left after the preceding content. | 4 core terms | [css-break-3 §2](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#fragmentation-model) | 2018-12-04 | High |
| 1.3 | **Parallel flows (§2.1).** Each formatting context fragments independently, for example table cells, flex items, and content overflowing a fixed-size box. The UA *may* balance unforced breaks across parallel flows. It *must not* do so to match a forced break. | 1 may, 1 must not | [css-break-3 §2.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#parallel-flows) | 2018-12-04 | High |
| 1.4 | **Three classes of break point (§4.1).** **Class A** is between siblings in the block axis: blocks, floats, table row groups and rows, and multicol column boxes. **Class B** is between line boxes. **Class C** is between a container's content edge and its child, and exists only when there is a non-zero gap. | 3 classes | [css-break-3 §4.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#possible-breaks) | 2018-12-04 | High |
| 1.5 | **Monolithic content** has no break points. It includes most replaced elements, a single line box, and scroll containers. A UA *may* also treat `overflow: hidden` with a non-auto block size, and inline-block or inline-table boxes, as monolithic. Engines differ on these "may" cases. | 3 "may" cases | [css-break-3 §4.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#monolithic) | 2018-12-04 | High |
| 1.6 | **Break controls (§3).** `break-before` and `break-after` take `auto`, `avoid`, `avoid-page`, `page`, `left`, `right`, `recto`, `verso`, `avoid-column`, `column`, `avoid-region` and `region`. `break-inside` takes `auto`, `avoid`, `avoid-page`, `avoid-column` and `avoid-region`. They do not apply to abspos boxes. A break value on the first child (before) or the last child (after) **propagates** to the parent, up to the nearest matching fragmentation context, without changing computed values. | 12 / 5 values | [css-break-3 §3](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#breaking-controls) | 2018-12-04 | High |
| 1.7 | **Forced breaks (§4.3).** They occur only at Class A points. A forced value overrides avoid. Among `left`, `right`, `recto` and `verso`, the value on the latest element wins. A change of named `page` value across a Class A point also forces a page break. A forced break with no matching fragmentation context does nothing. | — | [css-break-3 §4.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#forced-breaks) | 2018-12-04 | High |
| 1.8 | **Unforced breaks (§4.4).** Rule 1: avoid values on Class A points. Rule 2: `break-inside: avoid` on an ancestor. Rule 3: `orphans` and `widows` at Class B points. Rule 4: Class B and C breaks need `break-inside: auto` on every ancestor. If too few break points remain, the rules are relaxed in order: drop Rule 3 first, then Rules 1, 2 and 4, then truncate cloned decorations. The UA "must not break at the top of the page", so every fragmentainer makes progress. | 4 rules; 3 relaxation steps | [css-break-3 §4.4](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#unforced-breaks) | 2018-12-04 | High |
| 1.9 | **Optimality is undefined (§4.5; the relaxation sentence is in §4.4).** The guidelines are "break as few times as possible", fill fragmentainers evenly, and do not break inside replaced elements. The spec says these conflict. It "does not define whether content breaks at a particular allowed break", and when avoids are dropped it "does not suggest a precise algorithm". | 3 guidelines, 0 algorithms | [css-break-3 §4.5](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#best-breaks) | 2018-12-04 | High |
| 1.10 | `orphans` and `widows` take positive integers, with an initial value of **2**. Zero and negative values are invalid. They apply to block containers that establish an inline formatting context. | initial 2 | [css-break-3 §3.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#widows-orphans) | 2018-12-04 | High |
| 1.11 | **Margins at breaks (§5.2).** An unforced break truncates adjoining margins to 0. A forced break truncates the margin before the break but keeps the margin after it. Cloned margins are always 0. | — | [css-break-3 §5.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#break-margins) | 2018-12-04 | High |
| 1.12 | **`box-decoration-break` (§5.4).** `slice` (the initial value) lays the box out as if unbroken and slices it, so no border or padding is inserted at a break. `clone` wraps each fragment with its own border, padding and margin. UAs *should* also apply it to breaks caused by bidi and by display type. | initial `slice` | [css-break-3 §5.4](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#break-decoration) | 2018-12-04 | High |
| 1.13 | **Varying fragmentainer sizes (§5.3).** Layout runs per fragmentainer with that fragmentainer's size. Intrinsic sizes are kept for the whole element. Whether monolithic content keeps one size across fragmentainers is a UA "may". | — | [css-break-3 §5.1, §5.3](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#varying-size-boxes) | 2018-12-04 | High |
| 1.14 | **Abspos and transforms (§5.5).** An abspos box fragments in the flow of its containing block. UAs need not correctly place boxes whose block-start depends on content that spans a break. UAs with limited memory need not place abspos boxes on an already-rendered page. Fragmentation happens **before** relative positioning and transforms. Transforms apply per fragment, and page separation should happen last. | 2 "not required" clauses | [css-break-3 §5.5](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-break-3/#transforms) | 2018-12-04 | High |
| 1.15 | **Fragmentation 4** is "identical to Level 3 except for" `margin-break` (`auto`, `keep`, `discard`) and the new break values `always` and `all`. `always` breaks the nearest containing context. `all` breaks every containing context and is at risk. | ED 2026-04-14; 3 additions | [css-break-4](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-break-4/) | 2026-04-14 | High |
| 1.16 | Fragmentation 4 has 2 open issues. One asks whether `all` is needed. The other asks whether to add a second `keep` keyword that keeps the margin after the break, as **Antenna House** already does. | 2 issues | [css-break-4 issues index](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-break-4/#issues-index) | 2026-04-14 | High |
| 1.17 | **Fragmentation consumers outside print.** `continue: discard` makes a box a fragmentation container and discards content after the break. `line-clamp` sets `continue: collapse` and "fragments away" the content after the clamp point. Line clamping is therefore a fragmentation feature, not a paint feature. | 3 `continue` values | [css-overflow-4 `continue`](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-overflow-4/#propdef-continue) | ED fetched 2026-10-19 | High |

**Implications:** The spec defines legal break points and an order for relaxing rules. It leaves the optimisation function to the UA. Izcal must therefore choose a break-scoring function and write it into its own spec as a deterministic, documented algorithm, because no spec text will pin it down. The three break classes, monolithic content and the rule-relaxation order belong in the fragment-tree model from the start.

## 2. What the layout-mode specs say about fragmentation (and do not)

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Flexbox §10.** "The exact layout of a fragmented flex container is not defined in this level." In a row flex container, break values on items propagate to the **flex line**. Class A points are between flex lines and Class C points are at the content edges. In a column flex container, Class A points are between items. | 0 normative algorithms | [css-flexbox-1 §10](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-flexbox-1/#pagination) | ED 2026-05-08 | High |
| 2.2 | The non-normative flex sample algorithm should "pull up if more than **50%** of the fragment would have fit … and push otherwise". Its test of intent: a column flex container with `justify-content: start` and no flexible items paginates exactly like block flow. A continued container's available space is reduced by the content-box space used on earlier pages, clamped at 0. | 50% pull-up threshold | [css-flexbox-1 §10.1](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-flexbox-1/#pagination-algo) | ED 2026-05-08 | High |
| 2.3 | **Grid §13.** "The exact layout of a fragmented grid container is not defined." Item break values propagate to their **grid row**. A forced break inside an item "effectively increases the size of its contents" and does not break sibling items. The sample algorithm is described as "a rough draft … still needs to be severely cross-checked" against flexbox. | 1 rough-draft algorithm | [css-grid-2 §13](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-grid-2/#pagination) | ED fetched 2026-10-19 | High |
| 2.4 | **Tables §6.1.** UAs must try to keep a row whole if its cells span no later row and its height is "at least twice smaller than" the fragmentainer's height and width. Other rows are **freely fragmentable**. When a row is split, all remaining height goes to its cells, each cell breaks independently, and top borders are not repainted on continuations. | ½ fragmentainer threshold | [css-tables-3 §6.1](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-tables-3/#fragmentation) | ED 2026-10-08 | High |
| 2.5 | **Repeated headers (§6.2).** In paged media, UAs **must** repeat header and footer rows when four conditions hold: the page is the table's fragmentainer, the group has `break-inside: avoid`, the repetition uses at most a quarter of the page for headers and a quarter for footers, and no row would appear twice on that page. The UA stylesheet sets `thead, tfoot { break-inside: avoid }`. Repeating across columns is optional. | ≤ 1/4 + 1/4 of the page | [css-tables-3 §6.2](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-tables-3/#repeated-headers) | ED 2026-10-08 | High |
| 2.6 | **Abspos (§4.4).** An abspos box is placed "ignoring any fragmentation breaks (as if the flow were continuous)" and then broken across fragmentainers. If it resolves to an earlier page or one already printed, printers may put it on the current page, on a later page, or omit it. "User agents must not paginate the content of fixed-positioned boxes." | 3 permitted outcomes | [css-position-3 §4.4](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-position-3/#abspos-breaking) | ED 2026-05-14 | High |
| 2.7 | **Fixed boxes repeat on every page.** CSS 2.x says `position: fixed` boxes are rendered on every printed page. Issue #12481 (Chris Harrelson, 14 July 2025) reports that Chrome and Firefox do this and that the repeats cover content on pages 2+. It asks whether repetition should become opt-in. | issue #12481 | [csswg-drafts #12481 (list archive)](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-css-archive/2025Jul/0363.html) | 2025-07-14 | High |
| 2.8 | **Multicol balancing (§7).** With balancing, UAs "should try to minimize variations in column height" while honouring forced breaks, widows and orphans. `column-fill: balance` (the initial value) balances only the last fragment in a fragmented context. `balance-all` balances every fragment. No algorithm is given. | 3 values | [css-multicol-1 §7](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-multicol-1/#filling-columns) | ED 2026-08-04 | High |
| 2.9 | **Multicol overflow (§8.2).** In continuous media, columns that do not fit become **overflow columns** in the inline direction and can affect the container's height. In paged media, the extra columns move to the next page(s). The same content therefore lays out along two different axes depending on whether the outer context is fragmented. | 2 behaviours | [css-multicol-1 §8.2](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-multicol-1/#pagination-and-overflow-outside-multicol) | ED 2026-08-04 | High |
| 2.10 | **Page floats** (`float: top/bottom` relative to a page or column) are not in any shipped browser. The GCPM footnote section says how page floats sit above the footnote area is an open issue. | — | [css-gcpm-3 §2.4.1](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-gcpm-3/#footnote-area) | ED 2026-10-09 | High |

**Implications:** Flex, grid and table fragmentation are defined only by constraints, plus samples that call themselves drafts. The spec cannot be a byte-level oracle for these cases. Izcal's spec-literal reference engine (B5) needs one written-down Izcal algorithm per layout mode, and differential tests against other engines should be treated as signals, not ground truth.

## 3. How browsers implemented fragmentation (published prose)

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **Blink legacy model.** Content was laid out as "a tall strip whose width is the inline-size of a column or page". Lines that crossed an assumed boundary got a **pagination strut** that pushed them down. Most of the actual fragmentation happened in pre-paint and paint, by clipping and translating slices of the strip (the flow thread). | — | [RenderingNG deep-dive: LayoutNG block fragmentation (Morten Stenshorne)](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/chromium/renderingng-fragmentation) | last updated 2023-01-03 | High |
| 3.2 | **Failures of the legacy model.** Text shadows were clipped and painted at the top of the next column. Transforms and relative positioning were applied before fragmentation, against the spec's order. Box shadows were clipped at column edges. Tall monolithic content was "brutally" sliced. `break-before: avoid` between blocks was not supported. Table fragmentation was limited, and flex and grid had none. | 7 failure classes | same | 2023-01-03 | High |
| 3.3 | **LayoutNG model.** Layout produces immutable fragments. The constraint space carries the fragmentainer block size and the current block offset. A **break token** records what is needed to resume, and tokens form a tree that mirrors the boxes broken inside. A break *before* a node creates no fragment, but the parent still emits a "break-before" token. Layout returns to the fragmentation root (the multicol container, or the document when printing), which makes a new fragmentainer and descends again from the tokens. | — | same | 2023-01-03 | High |
| 3.4 | **Break scoring and early breaks.** Every candidate breakpoint gets a score, from "perfect" down to "last-resort", which is not a valid break but beats overflow. The best breakpoint so far is kept as an **early break**, possibly a chain through nested blocks. If a later point turns out worse, layout reruns once, up to the early break: "we only have to re-layout at most once per fragmentation flow". | ≤ 1 re-layout per flow | same | 2023-01-03 | High |
| 3.5 | Blink relaxes rules in spec order. It violates `widows`/`orphans` before `break-before: avoid`. The article says "Chromium is the first browser engine that fully supports this combination of features." | — | same | 2023-01-03 | High |
| 3.6 | **Out-of-flow fragments** become children of the fragmentainer rather than of their containing block fragment. The article calls this "a problem that had to be solved in order to co-exist with the legacy engine" and expects the code to simplify. Monolithic content taller than a column now overflows instead of being sliced. | — | same | 2023-01-03 | High |
| 3.7 | **Ship sequence.** LayoutNG shipped without fragmentation in 2019, with most fragmentation behind a flag by mid-2019. Core block fragmentation (blocks, inline, floats, out-of-flow) shipped in Chrome **102**. Flex and grid followed in **103**, tables in **106**, and printing in **108**. "As of Chrome 108, the legacy engine is no longer used to perform layout." Container queries shipped first, with a warning that they did not yet work in print. | 3+ years from flag to finished | same | 2023-01-03 | High |
| 3.8 | Chrome 102 reached stable around late May 2022, and 108 around late November 2022. So block fragmentation shipped about 3 years after the first LayoutNG work, and printing was the very last piece. | ~2022-05 / ~2022-11 | recollection of the Chrome release calendar | — | Low |
| 3.9 | LayoutNG's general design (Kilpatrick and Ishii): an immutable fragment tree, with the parent constraint space stored on each fragment as its cache key. It cites about 10 chained flex bugs over more than a year, hysteresis bugs (fixed in Chrome 93), and 2^n nested two-pass layout. The article itself does not discuss fragmentation. | ~10 bugs; 2^n | [LayoutNG (developer.chrome.com)](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/chromium/layoutng) | last updated 2021-10-08 | High |
| 3.10 | **Gecko continuations.** An element's first frame is the primary frame. Each fragment after it is a **continuation frame**. Reflow creates "fluid" continuations (next-in-flow) and bidi creates "non-fluid" ones. Reflow status is complete, incomplete, or **overflow-incomplete**: the frame is complete, but overflowing content goes into invisible **overflow containers** kept on a special list. Pages come from `nsPageSequenceFrame` and columns from `nsColumnSetFrame`. | 3 statuses | [Mozilla Layout Overview](https://firefox-source-docs.mozilla.org/layout/LayoutOverview.html) | fetched 2026-10-19 | High |
| 3.11 | Gecko's dynamic reflow **pushes** overflowing children to the next-in-flow and **pulls** them back when space frees up. The document says this can leave a parent with two children where one continues the other, or gaps in the chain, which it calls "incorrect". The repair strategy still had to be done "eventually" for absolutely positioned blocks. | — | same | fetched 2026-10-19 | High |
| 3.12 | **WebKit multicol is mainly used for pagination.** Hyatt: "far and away the most common use case for our multi-column layout implementation is for pagination of content" not written for columns, "most prominently iBooks on the iPad". Transforms and relative positioning can push content past any margin, so authors may have to "visually slice and accept poor vertical breaks". | — | [www-style, David Hyatt](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/www-style/2013Mar/0141.html) | 2013-03-06 | High |
| 3.13 | WebKit bug 38402 (2010) moved pagination and column breaks from paint time into layout. The "New Multicolumn" flow-thread work ran from 2012 to 2014. So WebKit, like legacy Blink, started with paint-time slicing. | 2010; 2012–2014 | search-result summary of [WebKit bug 38402](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=38402) and webkit-reviews posts (not opened) | 2010–2014 | Medium |
| 3.14 | **Multicol balancing cost in Gecko (bug 575614, opened ~2010, still open).** Balancing is a binary search over column height that reflows many lines on every probe. roc: "we're reflowing a lot of lines a lot." Proposed fixes: lay out in one column, divide by the column count and lay out again (2 passes); a break-at-first-opportunity-after-height mode (David Baron, 2 passes); or recording every break point so one layout run is enough ("more work"). The bug is unassigned, with a P3 performance impact. | binary search; 2-pass proposals | [Bugzilla 575614](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=575614) | ~2010–2022 | High |

**Implications:** Every engine that bolted fragmentation onto a continuous layout (legacy Blink and WebKit with paint-time slicing, Gecko with mutable continuations and push/pull) paid for it in bugs and in a multi-year rewrite. Blink's working design is immutable fragments, break tokens carried by the constraint space, scored breakpoints, and at most one re-layout per flow. Izcal's B5 `layout(node, constraint_space) → immutable fragment` already has this shape, so fragmentation should be part of the first version of the contract, not added later.

## 9. Design implications for Izcal (synthesis for the spec author)

1. **One layout contract for continuous and fragmented flow.** The constraint space carries an optional fragmentainer: block size, block offset, the fragmentation type (page, column or clamp), and a flag saying whether this is the first fragmentainer. The result is one immutable fragment plus an optional **break token**. Continuous layout is the case with no fragmentainer. This keeps B5's `layout(node, constraint_space) → fragment` and its cache key intact (3.3).
2. **Break tokens are data, not pointers into a mutable tree.** A token is a small record (node ID, consumed block size, child token list, per-mode state such as the flex line, grid row or table row index). It is serialisable and hashable, so it can be part of DET-1 hashes and the from-scratch reference check. Gecko's mutable continuations with push/pull (3.10, 3.11) are the anti-pattern.
3. **A documented break-scoring function.** The spec leaves optimality open (1.9). Izcal writes down its own score ordering (forced; perfect; violates orphans/widows; violates avoid; last resort), its tie-breaks, and its re-layout bound: at most one re-layout per flow, as in Blink (3.4). The DevTools "why did this break here?" query (frame row on developers) then answers from the scores.
4. **Fragmentainers are fragment-tree nodes with geometry.** Pages, columns, `::column` and wrapped column rows are addressable fragments. Scroll snapping, scroll markers, accessibility (7.3), hit testing and the PDF writer read them directly.
5. **Out-of-flow placement as a pass at the fragmentation root.** Abspos is placed as if continuous, then broken (2.6). Blink's re-parenting to the fragmentainer (3.6) is a hint that out-of-flow layout belongs to a deferred pass over the fragmentation context. That pass is one of B5's named "non-tree edges".
6. **Post-layout effects stay post-layout.** Transforms, relative offsets, shadows and clipping apply per fragment in paint and property trees (1.14). Fixed-position repetition per page is a paint-time instantiation of one laid-out fragment, not repeated layout.
7. **Per-mode fragmentation written as Izcal algorithms**, because flex, grid and tables have no normative algorithm (2.1–2.4). Start from the spec samples (for example flex's 50% pull-up rule) and record every choice as a G-rule with a spec anchor and a "UA-defined" tag.
8. **Print is a recorded environment.** Print media, page size, margins, scale and insets come in as one JS-D15 input. The printed fragment tree is reproducible under replay (6.6). The `beforeprint` snapshot is taken at a recorded point (6.1).
9. **Room for GCPM.** Design the page fragmentainer with reserved-area slots (a footnote area at the bottom, page floats at the top and bottom, margin boxes). Content extent can then shrink while a page is laid out without breaking the token model. Footnote layout is iterative per page; bound it with fuel and a stated fallback, such as deferring the footnote body per `footnote-policy: auto` (5.8).
10. **Footprint.** Most nodes are never fragmented. Break tokens and multi-fragment lists should live in a side table keyed by node, not in the per-node 360 B† layout+paint budget. Only the "has more fragments" bit belongs per node. Black-box measure how often real pages fragment on screen (multicol, line-clamp) before sizing it.
11. **Multicol balancing with a pass bound.** Choose a balancing algorithm with a provable upper bound on layout passes, and a fuel bound with deterministic fallback. Gecko's open-ended binary search is the cautionary case (3.14). Recording every break opportunity in one pass, as Baron suggested, is the candidate to evaluate.
