### Must-fix list

1. **"Print was 'fundamentally broken' in Chrome until … Chrome 108"** (Section 1, People browsing row, citing [GO 5.11]). The words do not appear in Chrome's block-fragmentation article or anywhere Chrome published (searched), and GO 5.11 itself says "fundamental engine gaps". Correct statement: in Chrome's legacy engine "fragmentation support was just something that got bolted on top (printing)", with clipped shadows, sliced monolithic content and transforms applied before fragmentation, until printing moved to LayoutNG in Chrome 108 ([RenderingNG deep-dive: block fragmentation](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/chromium/renderingng-fragmentation), Stenshorne, updated 2023-01-03). Drop the quotation marks or use that sentence.
2. **"found it slower at 4 threads than at 1 (221 ms against 264 ms)"** (summary paragraph). The pair is in the wrong order for the sentence, and the numbers are averages over pages. Correct statement: "the average total primary-layout time per page rose from 221 ms with 1 thread to 264 ms with 4, because layout was a median 7.8% of single-thread time and style 67%" ([Zambre et al., HiPC 2016, arXiv 2002.03850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.03850) §III: "increase in the average total times for Primary Layout … 221.39 ms, 242.56 ms, 263.73 ms").
3. **6.4 quotes LayoutNG's central diff as "complicated but well-contained, and easy to unit-test"**. That string is spliced from two sentences. The article says "This diffing logic is complicated, but well-contained" and, separately, "Fixes to this diffing code are typically simple, and easily unit-testable" ([LayoutNG deep-dive](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/chromium/layoutng)). Quote one sentence, or paraphrase without quotation marks. HP 2.11 carries the same splice.
4. **6.5.2 and LY-D16: "Izcal balances paragraphs of up to 10 lines, the spec's limit for a mandatory attempt"**. CSS Text 4 has no mandatory attempt. It says balance "should avoid changing—and in the case of 5 or fewer lines must not change—the number of line boxes", and "UAs may treat this value as auto if there are more than ten lines to balance" ([css-text-4 §5.4 `text-wrap-style`](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-text-4/#text-wrap-style)). Correct statement: "Izcal balances paragraphs of up to 10 lines, the count above which CSS Text 4 permits falling back to `auto`, and falls back to greedy above it." The decision stands; its stated basis does not.
5. **6.10: the scroll-anchoring suppression window "ends at the end of the event-loop task"**, and Izcal's chosen boundary "(a) the end of each task and microtask checkpoint". CSS Scroll Anchoring 1 §2.2.1 defines the end itself: the window "ends at the end of the current iteration of the HTML Processing Model event loop, or immediately before the next operation whose result or side effects would differ as a result of a change in the scroll position … whichever comes sooner" ([css-scroll-anchoring-1 §2.2.1](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-scroll-anchoring-1/#suppression-window)). What HTML lacks is only the integration ("should be incorporated into the HTML standard once the scroll anchoring API is stabilized"). Izcal's (a) ends the window earlier than the spec (an event-loop iteration contains a task, its microtask checkpoint and the rendering update), so adjustments would be applied at points the spec does not allow. Restate the spec's boundary; either adopt "end of the event-loop iteration, or entry of a forcing API, whichever is first" or record (a) as a deviation with a black-box comparison under Q-LY-14. LY-D13 and the decision-record design depend on it.
6. **9.3: "Total at full strength 48–60†"**. The rows sum to **48–59** (minimum 4+4+5+5+3+4+4+2+3+4+7+2+1 = 48; maximum 5+5+6+6+3+5+5+2+4+5+9+3+1 = 59). Either raise one row's top by one or state 48–59, and carry the change to the summary, 9.3's "six components … 226–292" (which becomes 226–291), 9.5's Stage 1 8.1–8.3 row and the Risks table.
7. **9.3: engineer-years ("about 20 in year one and 38 in year two") contradict the hiring ramp in the same section** ("18–22 engineers at LY-M0 …; about 36–40 by month 12; full strength by month 18–24"). A linear ramp from 18–22 to 36–40 over year one averages about 27–31 engineers; year two, from 36–40 to 48–60 by month 18–24, averages about 42–52. Recompute the totals (for example 29 + 47 + 3 × 48 ≈ 220 at 5 years and 31 + 52 + 4.5 × 60 ≈ 353 at 6.5 years, so about **220–350 engineer-years†**), or change the ramp so the year sums agree.

### Recommended (not blocking)

- **2.1 Interop row, "Interop 2025 finished at 95–97%"**: two metrics from two sources. Mozilla: "The overall Interop score started at 25, and it's now 95" ([Mozilla Hacks](https://hacks.mozilla.org/2026/02/launching-interop-2026/)); WebKit: from 29% of tests passing to "a 97% pass rate" ([WebKit, Interop 2025 review](https://webkit.org/blog/17808/interop-2025-review/)). Say which. SP 12.5 and GO 5.7 disagree for this reason.
- **Section 3 and 9.3: "about 22,000 files, a third of them CSS 2.1's legacy suite"**: SP 1.12's own per-directory counts sum to 24,534 under `css/` (25,081 with `html/rendering`), and CSS2's 9,307 is 37–42% of that, not a third. The counts are graded Medium in the note; say "about 25,000 files, over a third of them CSS 2.1's".
- **Section 1, "the most-viewed layout questions are size questions ('why don't flex items shrink past content size?' 210k views)"**: 209,598 views is right, but that question ranks about 60th by views among the top 100 flexbox questions; the most-viewed are centering and 100%-height questions at 1.7–2.8M views ([Stack Exchange API](https://api.stackexchange.com/2.3/questions/36247140?site=stackoverflow)). Use GO 1.11's 1.87M "Flexbox: center horizontally and vertically" as the example, or call 36247140 "a recurring size question".
- **2.1 sticky row, "2014–17 … unshipped"** (HP 6.4 "~Chrome 35, 2014"): Chrome's own post gives no removal version; it says sticky landed in WebKit "Four years ago" and was removed "One year later" (about 2013). Chrome 35 comes only from a third-party post. Say "removed from Blink about a year after it landed in WebKit, back in Chrome 56 (January 2017)".
- **2.1 Regions row**: the >10,000 lines, ~350,000 lines and 140+ files are Seidel's, as quoted; the cited I Programmer article (29 January 2014) reports removal as "highly likely", not done. Add a primary source for the removal itself or say "proposed for removal in January 2014".

- **6.17, "FreeDom found a Rust assertion in Firefox's style code"**: FreeDom's Table 5 lists bug 1625187 as "HTML/CSS Rust assertion"; "style code" is an inference. Say "a Rust assertion in Firefox (HTML/CSS)".

- **Header cross-references are stale**: the style spec is now revision 0.3.3, fonts and text 0.3.4 and networking 0.3.3 (all 19 October 2026). The cited IDs are unchanged, and 9.3's 178–232† engineers still matches the reconciliation (its people total is now 231–290†, including type-design staff).
- **6.2, "WebKit moved from 1/60 to 1/64 in 2012 for exactly this reason"**: right in substance. The wiki's reason is "to avoid loosing precision when converting between LayoutUnits and Lengths (which use floats internally) … and to avoid having to do integer division" ([WebKit wiki: LayoutUnit](https://trac.webkit.org/wiki/LayoutUnit)); zoom motivated subpixel layout as a whole, not the 1/64 choice (IP 7.3 and 4.4's "fixed point is also what Blink and WebKit chose for zoom" are right).
