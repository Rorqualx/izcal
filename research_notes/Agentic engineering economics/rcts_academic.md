# Agentic engineering economics: RCTs and academic evidence

Compiled 21 October 2026. Primary sources were preferred (papers, author pages, publisher metadata). Every row was checked by fetching the cited page; where a publisher blocked access, the row says so and the grade is lower.

Confidence: **High** = primary source read and the claim checked in its text; **Medium** = primary abstract or snippet, or a vendor claim without data; **Low** = secondary source only.

**Scope.** This note covers controlled and academic measurements of AI-assisted software work: RCTs, field experiments, the 2026 meta-analysis, and the academic quality/rework evidence. It does not cover surveys, vendor telemetry, DORA or GitClear (strand 2), or token prices and agent-hour costs (strand 3).

## 3. The 2026 meta-analysis: gains are real in the lab and vanish in the field

Maier, Gunzenhäuser, Schweisthal, Schneider and Feuerriegel, "A meta-analysis of the effect of generative AI on productivity and learning in programming," arXiv:2605.04779, 6 May 2026. Preprint; PRISMA 2020, preregistered, 23 studies and 27 effect sizes, English-language work from 2019 to 5 December 2025.

| # | Finding | Numbers | Source | Date | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **Pooled productivity effect is positive but moderate.** Productivity is measured by objective proxies only: task completion time (n = 9), commits (n = 4), lines of output code (n = 1), and merged pull requests. Subjective productivity measures were deliberately excluded. | 16 productivity effect sizes from 14 unique studies. **Hedges' g = 0.33**, 95% CI [0.09, 0.58], SE 0.13, **p = 0.008**. | [arXiv:2605.04779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04779), full text [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.04779) | 2026-05-06 | High (preprint) |
| 3.2 | **Setting explains the gain.** Laboratory studies show a large effect; enterprise studies and open-source studies are indistinguishable from zero. ## 5. Coverage: what the controlled evidence does not measure

| Study | Task horizon | Codebase | Population | Quality/review measured? | What it cannot tell us |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Peng 2023 | One ~2-hour task | Greenfield, single JavaScript server | ~70 (35 per arm; paper text ambiguous), mostly 25–34, India/Pakistan, ~6 years' experience | No; authors say quality not examined | Maintenance, large repositories, design, review, security |
| Cui 2024/26 | Field (duration not stated in abstract) | Enterprise repositories at three firms | 4,867 developers | No; completed-task counts only | Defect rates, review time, architecture, long-horizon design |
| Paradis 2024/25 | ≤3 hours | 474-line changelist in Google's monorepo | 96 Google engineers | No; quality and equity not examined | Multi-week work, unfamiliar large codebases, design |
| METR 2025 | ~2 hours per task | Mature, familiar OSS repositories (1M+ LOC) | 16 experienced open-source developers | Partial: acceptance and cleaning time; no defect rate | Less-experienced developers, greenfield, unfamiliar code, future tools |
| METR 2026 follow-up | ~2 hours per task | 143 repositories | 57 developers | No | A reliable effect size; selection-biased |
| Appelt and Glauben 2026 | Onboarding task (duration not stated) | Brownfield existing codebase | 24 developers | Correctness measured (not significant) | Long-horizon work, large teams, design, review |
| Maier et al. 2026 | Mixed (lab to field) | Mixed | 23 studies | Excluded subjective measures; no quality pooling | Long-horizon work, design/verification, quality |

**What is missing, in the terms that matter for Izcal:**

- **Long-horizon engineering.** Only Cui (field experiments) and Shan (27 weeks, field data) have horizons beyond a single task; neither measures architecture, design quality or the cost of revisiting earlier decisions.
- **Large unfamiliar codebases.** METR's repositories are large but familiar to their maintainers; Paradis's task is enterprise-grade but a bounded 474-line change; PACIS is brownfield onboarding but only 24 developers. I found no RCT in which an engineer works for weeks in an unfamiliar 1M+ LOC codebase.
- **Design and verification.** No RCT isolates design work or code review. The review evidence is observational (Xu: 6.5% more review; Asdaque: 4.52x more review comments), and the one randomized quality study (Perry) is about security, not review cost.
- **Quality in the speed RCTs.** Peng, Cui and Paradis either state that quality was not examined or report only task counts. A speedup measured without defects cannot be converted into an engineering-year saving on its own.
- **Agentic tools.** METR's follow-up is the only randomized agentic evidence and its authors call it unreliable; PACIS is a 24-developer controlled comparison. The 2026 agentic case is promising on speed and unproven on correctness and review.

**Implications:** None of the controlled studies covers multi-week design or verification work in a large unfamiliar codebase, which is most of Izcal's engine effort. Their effect sizes cannot be transferred to those activity classes without an explicit, marked assumption.

## 6. What this licenses for the C-6 translation (interpretation, not a spec target)

The ranges below are derived from the rows above. They are **interpretation**, not measured facts about Izcal, and should carry the † discipline until Izcal's own milestones measure them.

| Activity class | Evidence | Implied speed multiplier | Confidence | Caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Greenfield, self-contained implementation with a test oracle | Peng: 55.8% time reduction (160.89 → 71.17 min); lab meta g = 0.73 (standardized effect, not a percent) | **1.3x–2.3x** (point 2.26x; CI wide) | Low–Medium | One small, single-language task; conditional on completion; do not apply to engine work |
| Enterprise feature work (field) | Cui: +26.08% tasks (SE 10.3); Paradis: ~21% faster but n.s. | **1.1x–1.3x** | Medium | One pooled field estimate; no CI in Cui's abstract; no quality outcome |
| Maintenance and debugging in mature, familiar code, experienced developers | METR: +19% time, 95% CI [+2%, +39%] | **0.7x–1.0x** (point 0.84x) | High for early-2025 tools | 16 developers; not 2026 agents; the authors say the setting is not representative |
| Agentic work with 2026 tools | METR follow-up: point estimates 1.04x–1.22x, CIs include 1; PACIS: agent 61.7% faster than assistant in a 24-developer onboarding task, correctness n.s. | **0.9x–1.2x** (AI vs no AI; PACIS is an agent-vs-assistant increment, not a baseline effect) | Low | Selection bias; small samples; correctness not improved |
| Review, verification and security | No RCT shows compression; Xu: 6.5% more review; Asdaque: 4.52x review comments; Perry and Pearce: negative security | **1.0x or worse** | Medium | Observational review data; no controlled multiplier |
| Design and architecture | No controlled evidence | **1.0x** | — | Treat as incompressible until measured |

**Rules this evidence supports:**

1. **Do not use 55.8% as a project-wide factor.** It is a completer-conditioned lab result on one greenfield task, with an ambiguous per-group N and no quality measure.
2. **Do not use self-reported gains.** METR's developers believed they were 20% faster while measuring 19% slower; the perception gap is about 39–40 points.
3. **Do not assume quality comes free.** The only security RCT finds less secure code; the agentic PR evidence shows about 45% of merged PRs need changes; the benchmark finds about 40% vulnerable outputs.
4. **Use per-activity-class factors, not a flat multiplier.** The meta-analysis's lab/enterprise/open-source split (0.73 / 0.19 / 0.01) is the clearest quantitative statement that context dominates.
5. **Carry a reversal trigger.** Measure the first Izcal milestones by activity class; if implementation speedup falls below 1.1x or review load rises above 1.0x, restate the translation.

**Implications:** The evidence supports a conservative, activity-class-based translation: some compression in greenfield implementation, little or none in maintenance, and none in design, review or security. Booking a flat agentic multiplier would contradict the best-controlled evidence.

## 7. Confidence, gaps and unverified leads

**High-confidence core.** Peng 2023; Cui 2024/2026 (pooled estimate and experience heterogeneity only; no CI or per-site numbers in the abstract); Paradis 2024/2025; METR 2025; METR 2026 follow-up; Maier et al. 2026 meta-analysis; Perry 2022/2023; Pearce 2021/2022; Watanabe 2025/2026; Asdaque 2026; Appelt and Glauben 2026.

**Medium.** Shan et al. 2026 (253-developer RCT and 27-week field study; abstract checked, effect sizes paywalled); the negative search results for "Savage et al." and "Addepalli et al." (absence of evidence, not proof of absence); the normal-approximation confidence intervals computed from Cui's SE and from Paradis's log-time coefficient.

**Low and not used.** The following leads were found but not verified, so no row depends on them:

- "The reviewer's burden: Code review load on senior engineers after AI tool adoption" (PeerJ submission). Search indexing reports 5 enterprise projects, 651 MRs, +204% MR volume, +105% time-to-merge, senior review coverage 4.7% → 26.9%. The PeerJ page returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch; treat the numbers as unverified. [peerj.com/reviewer-match/145074](https://peerj.com/reviewer-match/145074).
- "Dear Diary: A Randomized Controlled Trial of Generative AI Coding Tools in the Workplace" (ICSE-SEIP 2025). Found in search; the [IEEE CSDL page](https://www.computer.org/csdl/proceedings-article/icse-seip/2025/368500a618/29kKQkqjepO) returned no abstract, so no numbers are used.
- A 2025 Aalto University pilot three-arm RCT at a Finnish consultancy (25 developers, 130 tasks) was surfaced by search with a reported 40.2% faster task completion and 26.2% higher code quality, but the [Aalto record](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/826ea826-a839-40c5-a543-e285c6932fb4/full) refused connection, so the numbers are unverified and unused.
- AIDev aggregate acceptance rates by agent (Codex ~82.6%, Cursor ~65.2%, Claude Code ~60.6%, Devin ~53.8%, Copilot ~43.3%). Found in a [secondary summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/agentic-pull-requests-prs); the primary paper was not read, so these are not used.

**Gaps.**

- I found no peer-reviewed meta-analysis of sufficient size; Maier et al. is a 2026 preprint with 16 productivity effect sizes and 59.3% higher-risk-of-bias estimates.
- I found no RCT of multi-week design or verification work, and no RCT of an unfamiliar 1M+ LOC codebase.
- No RCT in this note reports both a time saving and a defect rate for the same task.
- The agentic-tool evidence is one unreliable RCT follow-up and one 24-developer conference experiment; the 2026 industry data (strand 2) is broader but not controlled.
- The 2026 literature is moving quickly; the 2025 METR result is already described by its authors as "out of date" for 2026 tools.

**Implications:** The evidence base is thinnest at exactly the horizons that matter for C-6, so the first Izcal milestones should be designed to produce the missing per-activity-class measurements, not only to ship code.
