HGBA-2 2.3.0 to 2.3.2 exclude every library that does browser-engine work, which includes every library that decodes images, demuxes media, decodes or encodes audio and video, manages colour or runs an audio graph: libpng, libjpeg-turbo, giflib, libwebp, libavif, libjxl, dav1d, libaom, libvpx, OpenH264, FFmpeg, GStreamer, Skia's codecs, image-rs and zune as engine components, Symphonia, cubeb, Little CMS and their kind. Only OS interfaces remain: OS media frameworks, hardware decoders, audio and capture APIs, and the DRM modules of D-S7. So Izcal builds its own image and media stack. The record says four things. **First, image and media parsers are a lasting attack surface, and almost every exploit is one kind of bug.** About one in twenty in-the-wild 0-days in Project Zero's sheet (22 of 412, and 14 of 270 since 2021) is an image or media bug, and in every one with a published root cause (5 of 5 with a Project Zero or named-analyst root cause; a third-party analysis of CVE-2018-4990 fits the same class) a size, count or table shape read from the file disagreed with an allocation ([SE 1.1, 4.1][SE]); the hardest (libwebp, AppleAVD, WebRTC) were probably found by manual review, and fuzzing had missed them ([SE 4.7][SE]). **Second, memory-safe decoders written from scratch now beat the C libraries they replace**: Chromium's default PNG decoder has been a safe-Rust crate since M139 at about 1.9× libpng's speed, its GIF decoder is Wuffs, a memory-safe language with compile-time proofs, and Chrome 155 ships JPEG XL through a pure-Rust decoder ([SE 6.1–6.4][SE], [HP 4.10][HP]). **Third, no engine has ever shipped a media stack without a framework**: Firefox moved from GStreamer to FFmpeg for coverage (H.264 playback had depended on which GStreamer plug-ins users had installed), Ladybird moved its decoding and demuxing to FFmpeg in 2024–2025, and Servo's media work was built on GStreamer ([HP 8.2, 8.5, 8.6][HP], [GO 8.10, 8.11][GO]). Izcal's rule has no precedent, and its cost is large: the one public datum, porting only the C part of dav1d to Rust while keeping its 250,000 lines of assembly, took more than 20 person-months against an estimate of 7 ([GO 8.1–8.4][GO]). **Fourth, patents, not engineering, decide which codecs a browser may ship**: an independent browser that ships its own H.264 decoder owes the AVC pool up to $9.75 M a year, while the OS vendor's licence covers the OS decoder ([FC 9.1, 9.10][FC], [OS 4.1, 4.2][OS]).

**The central idea: one sealed, compute-only media process per renderer key (origin under FP-D8) does all parsing, and everything it parses is generated from a description of the specification.** Every container, header, metadata, colour-profile, playlist and caption parser is generated from a `.hgbits` description that carries the specification's value ranges and cross-field constraints, with proved bounds; entropy tables are built in a separate validating pass over a private copy; decoders allocate only from budgets sized from validated headers and cannot panic, recurse over input or loop unboundedly. Decoded output of Izcal's own decoders that a page can observe is a pure function of the input bytes, bit-identical on every OS and ISA, and the timing around it is a recorded B1 input. The renderer parses no image or media byte at all, and neither the encoded bytes nor the pixels and samples of opaque cross-origin media ever enter it. The decisions that matter most:

- **Formats (MD-D1, MD-D2).** Izcal decodes PNG and APNG, JPEG (the web's subset), GIF, WebP, AVIF still and sequences, BMP, ICO and CUR, and JPEG XL; AV1, VP9 and VP8; Opus, Vorbis, FLAC, MP3 and PCM. It does not expose HEIC, JPEG 2000, JPEG XR, Theora or arithmetic-coded JPEG.
- **H.264, AAC and HEVC (MD-D3, owner decision 19 October 2026: royalty-free).** Izcal pays no codec royalties: every codec Izcal ships in its own code is royalty-free. H.264, AAC and HEVC play through OS decoders on macOS and Windows, where the OS vendor holds the licence. Izcal builds its own H.264 and AAC-LC decoders and an H.264 Constrained Baseline encoder (WebRTC requires H.264 on every platform), keeps them ready, and ships none of them under a patent licence unless the owner takes one at the licence decision point before the first public release (AAC-LC may ship where its patents are confirmed expired, since it is then royalty-free); HEVC is decoded only by OS decoders and never by Izcal code. OpenH264 is not used.
- **Placement (MD-D5, MD-D10).** FP-D11 (b)'s decoder process becomes the **media process** per renderer key ((site, container), or origin where FP-D8 keys renderers by origin), at the strictest sandbox tier: every demuxer, decoder, encoder, ICC parser, WebVTT and HLS parser, and the rasterization of opaque cross-site SVG images run there. Hardware decoders and OS decoder sessions, which need device or kernel-driver access, run in a separate **hardware-decode process** per renderer key that runs no Izcal parser. A broker-launched **audio service** mixes and plays PCM and runs Izcal's own voice processing; a **capture service** owns cameras, microphones and screen capture; the graphics spec's **compositor GPU service** presents frames and owns display colour, and its **content GPU process** of each renderer key rasters page content, opaque images included.
- **Colour (MD-D8, MD-D9).** Images decode into one of two canonical working encodings (8-bit sRGB, or linear FP16 with extended BT.709 primaries); display conversion, HDR tone mapping and gain-map application happen only in the compositor GPU service or the OS compositor, so no web-readable pixel depends on the display. No ICC-authored file ships.
- **Determinism and privacy (MD-D11, MD-D12, MD-D14).** Codec capabilities are reported from a per-build policy table that claims hardware efficiency only where every device of the OS family has it; Web Audio renders bit-identically across machines, which removes the AudioContext fingerprint at its source.
- **Real-time audio (MD-D31).** AudioWorklet's place on a real-time thread depends on garbage-collection and threading guarantees the JS engine spec must add; the decision and its fallback are explicit.

## 1. The problem and who has it

Images are the largest part of most pages and the Largest Contentful Paint element on 76% of mobile and 85% of desktop pages ([Web Almanac 2025, Performance](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2025/performance)). The `<video>` element is on 6.7% of mobile and 7.7% of desktop pages ([Web Almanac 2024, Media](https://almanac.httparchive.org/en/2024/media)). Izcal must turn encoded images, media containers and audio and video bitstreams into colour-correct pixels and sample-accurate sound through the web's media APIs, without any library that does this work (M-1 part 1), with everything that ships available under MIT, and with the attack surface treated from first principles.

| Who | Their task | What fails today |
|---|---|---|
| **People reading and watching** | See images quickly at the right size and colour; play video smoothly with sound in sync, on battery, with captions; pay for content and have it play | Colour was retrofitted one surface at a time (Firefox's wide-gamut bug open for about 8 years, [CH 10.3][CH]); browsers differ by 38–70% in battery life for the same video ([MP 11.1, 11.2][MP], [GO 9.2][GO]); a hostile file is a zero-click exploit ([SE 2][SE]) |
| **Web developers** | Ship images and video that load fast and look right; know why an image is blurry, late or off-colour, why a frame dropped, why autoplay was refused | Half of pages send a largest image bigger than a phone can show; `sizes` is inaccurate enough to change the chosen candidate for 14% (mobile) to 20% (desktop) of `sizes` attributes ([GO 1.9, 1.10][GO]); DevTools show logs and counters, not causes ([GO 3][GO]) |
| **Real-time applications** | Video calls, audio production, media editing (WebRTC, AudioWorklet, WebCodecs) | Web Audio races between script and the audio thread were exploited twice in the wild ([SE 3.8, 3.9][SE]); latency and glitch behaviour differ per OS ([MP 9][MP]) |
| **Layout, paint, the GPU service, text and the semantic plane** | Intrinsic sizes, frames on the compositor's clock, pixels in a known colour space, captions and alt text | No engine states which stage converts colour ([CH 11.4][CH]) |
| **Izcal's engineers and agents** | Replay a page with media bit-exactly (B1); test every decoder against its specification | DET-1 excludes media and audio today ([H3][H3] DET-1); tools give up on `<video>` because output differs by machine ([GO 7.3][GO]) |

**Success, in measurable terms** ([frame][FR]): every exact-output decoder matches its conformance streams bit for bit and every tolerance decoder meets its specification's test and Izcal's own cross-ISA identity (MD-1, MD-2); no image or media byte is parsed outside the media process (MD-20); every container and header parser is generated with proved bounds (MD-21); decoded image memory meets FP-13 and playback energy is within a set margin of the OS's own player (MD-52, MD-54); A/V sync, dropped-frame and glitch targets hold (MD-7 to MD-9); media replays deterministically in simulation (MD-41); and codec exposure adds no identifying bits beyond the build and OS family (MD-30).

## 2. History: what came before and why it was built that way

### 2.1 Chronology

|---|---|---|---|---|

### 2.2 Technology mismatches: assumptions that no longer hold

| Assumption baked into older stacks | What changed | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Decoders run in-process and C libraries are trusted | Zero-click exploits through images (BLASTPASS, FORCEDENTRY) and codecs; Chrome and Firefox now require a memory-safe decoder for a new format | [SE 2][SE], [HP 4.5, 4.10][HP] |
| Images and video are 8-bit sRGB | P3 displays, HDR video and stills, `cICP` in PNG | [HP 14.2][HP], [CH 4][CH] |
| Decode at load, keep the full bitmap | Phones, lazy loading, `decode()`, downscale-on-decode | [FP 6.5][FP] |
| Decoders are single-threaded | AV1 and JPEG XL decoders are designed for threads; Firefox's JPEG XL performance depended on them | [HP 4.9][HP], [FC 11.9][FC] |
| The CPU decodes | Hardware decoders use under 9% of the energy of optimised software decoders | [MP 11.6][MP] |
| The page cannot see decode | MSE, WebCodecs and AudioWorklet expose segments, frames and samples to script, and with them decode speed and device timing | [MP 13][MP] |
| Frameworks give coverage | Coverage varied with installs (playback depended on which plug-ins were installed) and frameworks carry hundreds of parsers (FFmpeg's security page lists 518 CVE IDs) | [HP 8.2][HP], [SE 5.1][SE] |
| Colour conversion to the display is harmless | Reading the display profile back through `getImageData` was "a known fingerprinting technique" ([WHATWG 2014](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-whatwg-archive/2014May/0154.html)) | [CH 11.1][CH] |
| The browser's audio output is per-device and unobservable | AudioContext output differs by machine: about 5.4 bits of fingerprint | [SE 11.5][SE] |

## 3. Today: the landscape and its gaps

**Every engine leans on a library or the OS.** AOM says Chrome, Firefox, Edge and Safari use dav1d, though Safari plays AV1 video only with a hardware decoder ([FC 7.3, 7.6][FC]); Firefox relies on OS decoders for H.264 "to avoid patent concerns" ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Formats/Video_codecs)); every engine decodes HEVC only through the OS or hardware ([FC 7.2][FC]); Safari's new image formats arrive with OS releases (HEIC and JPEG XL with Safari 17), consistent with decoding through the OS image stack ([FC 7.9][FC]). No engine owns its whole stack.

**Attack surface, measured.** 22 of 412 in-the-wild 0-days are image or media bugs; 8 of them are in OS media frameworks a browser calls but cannot patch ([SE 1.1, 1.6][SE]). The root-cause classes are size or count trusted from the file (every published decoder root cause), cross-thread races between script and the real-time audio thread (two Web Audio 0-days plus at least four variants), and state across reinitialisation (WebRTC, MediaCodec glue, a libvpx encoder bug) ([SE 4][SE]). In Rust, the residual classes are panics, unbounded allocation, stack overflow, non-termination and unsound `unsafe` (the one Rust media near-miss in Android, CVE-2025-48530, was in `unsafe` code) ([SE 6.10, 7.5][SE]).

**What no tool shows** ([GO 3][GO]): decode time and decoded bytes per image; why an image is blurry (which `srcset` candidate, which DPR, downscale factor); why an image's colour looks as it does; why a frame dropped; A/V drift over time; MSE buffer state with evictions; why autoplay was refused; which codec, hardware or software, and why; energy per playback. The only standard health metric is a pair of counters, `droppedVideoFrames` and `totalVideoFrames` ([Media Playback Quality](https://w3c.github.io/media-playback-quality/)).

**What is unsolved for users.** Captions ignore OS preferences in one engine ([Bugzilla 1721736](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1721736)); no author control pauses an animated image ([csswg-drafts #1615](https://github.com/w3c/csswg-drafts/issues/1615)); one engine sends unlabelled images to a server for alt text while another runs a local model ([GO 6.1–6.4][GO]); browsers differ by tens of percent in playback energy on identical hardware ([MP 11.2, 11.3][MP]).

**What Izcal can expose that no one does** (`izcal-capabilities.md`, [GO 11][GO]): deterministic decode replay, because software decoder output is a pure function of bytes (B1); spec anchors in every decoder, so a corrupt-stream error names the clause it violated (ANC-1); one "why does this image look like this" explanation from candidate selection to display conversion; a cause for every dropped frame; per-site, per-element energy per playback (FP-17 to FP-20 for wakeups, FP-21 to FP-23 for energy); an autoplay decision with the rule that made it; bit-identical Web Audio, which removes a fingerprint instead of adding noise.

## 4. First principles

### 4.1 Users' real tasks

| Task | What it requires of the engine |
|---|---|
| See an image now, at the right size | Decode only what is near the viewport, at display size, before the frame that needs it; never block script or paint on a decode |
| See it in the right colour | One colour pipeline for CSS, images, video and canvas, tagged inputs honoured, untagged treated as sRGB (CSS Color 4 §3.5), display conversion at the end |
| Watch a video on battery | Hardware decode when it is cheaper, overlays and direct scanout, bursty fetching, no work for invisible frames |
| Hear sound in sync | Audio clock as the master; video presented against it within a tolerance users cannot detect |
| Talk in a call | Low-latency capture, echo cancellation, jitter buffering, mandatory codecs on every platform |
| Not be attacked by a file | No parser that trusts a number from the file; a crash contained to a process that holds nothing worth stealing |
| Not be tracked by media APIs | Capabilities and outputs that reveal no more than the build |
| Know why (developer) | Every decision the engine made recorded with its cause and its specification step |

### 4.2 Platform primitives

The specifications define the bitstreams and their decoding processes: PNG Third Edition, ITU-T T.81 and T.83, GIF89a, RFC 9649 (WebP), AOM's AVIF 1.2.0 over ISO/IEC 23008-12 and 23000-22, ISO/IEC 18181 (JPEG XL), the AV1 specification, the VP9 specification, RFC 6386 (VP8), ITU-T H.264, RFC 6716 and 8251 (Opus), the Vorbis I specification, RFC 9639 (FLAC), ISO/IEC 11172-3 and 13818-3 (MP3), ISO/IEC 14496-3 (AAC); containers ISO/IEC 14496-12, RFC 9559 (Matroska), RFC 3533 (Ogg), ITU-T H.222.0 (MPEG-2 TS), RFC 8216 (HLS); colour ICC.1:2022, ITU-T H.273, ITU-R BT.2100, BT.2390, BT.2408, ISO 21496-1, CSS Color 4 and CSS Color HDR; the web APIs in HTML, MSE 2, WebCodecs, Web Audio 1.1, Media Capture and Streams, EME 2, WebVTT, Picture-in-Picture and Autoplay Policy Detection ([FC 1–4][FC], [CH 1–8][CH], [MP 1–7][MP]).

Three facts about these primitives shape the design:

1. **AV1, VP9, VP8, H.264, PNG, FLAC and lossless WebP define exact output** ([FC 3.2–3.5, 4.3][FC]). Their decoders can be checked bit for bit, and a recording made with a hardware decoder can be replayed with a software one if both conform (for AV1 with film grain, only if both apply the grain process exactly; otherwise replay compares pre-grain frames and applies Izcal's own grain).
2. **JPEG, MP3 and AAC define output only within a tolerance; Vorbis sets no numeric tolerance; Opus partly** ([FC 1.2, 4.1, 4.4–4.6][FC]). Two conforming decoders differ. Izcal fixes its own arithmetic so that its output is identical everywhere, which is stricter than the specifications.
3. **ICC sets no CMM accuracy and no interpolation method** ([CH 1.3, 12.1][CH]); CSS Color 4 allows three gamut-mapping algorithms ([CH 7.1][CH]). Colour determinism must be Izcal's own contract.

OS primitives: full decoders (VideoToolbox sessions, Media Foundation transforms, Android MediaCodec) where the OS vendor holds the codec licence; low-level acceleration (Vulkan Video, D3D12 Video, VA-API, V4L2 stateless), where the application parses the bitstream and the hardware reconstructs ([OS 2.6, 3.5][OS], [MP 10.1, 10.2][MP]); zero-copy frame handles (IOSurface, DXGI shared handles, dmabuf); OS compositors that accept tagged FP16 surfaces with time-varying HDR headroom ([CH 9][CH]); OS audio with sanctioned real-time paths (MMCSS and the Real-Time Work Queue, audio workgroups, RTKit or the portal) ([MP 9.4–9.8][MP]); capture behind system pickers ([OS 10.7–10.9][OS]); CDMs inside OS protected processes ([OS 6.1, 6.2][OS]).

### 4.3 Izcal's principles that bind this design

P1 first: hostile input, I-1 (never two sites' data in one address space), I-2 (zero ambient authority). M-1: no library doing this work, MIT only, specifications as the source, other engines and codec libraries as black-box signals only, their source never evidence. B1 and JS-D15: timing-dependent decisions are recorded inputs. JS-D12: one implementation of each math function, no FMA contraction, cross-ISA identity. FP-D11 (b), FP-D13 (a) and FP-D19: decoders per (site, container), every codec in every build, no device-dependent web-observable values. D-S1: anti-fingerprinting by seeded readback noise and normalised discrete surfaces. D-S7: DRM through licensed components only.

### 4.4 Assumptions kept and broken

| Assumption | Keep or break | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A browser uses a media framework or codec libraries | **Break** | M-1 part 1; and frameworks bring the surface ([SE 5.1][SE]) |
| Decoders run in the renderer | **Break** (MD-D5) | FP-D11 (b); a decoder exploit should reach only pixels; opaque cross-origin data should not be in the renderer at all |
| Decoders run in a shared utility process | **Break** | I-1: a pooled process holds every site's in-flight media ([SE 12.2][SE]) |
| Parsers are hand-written and then fuzzed | **Break** (MD-D6) | Every published root cause is a trusted size; fuzzing missed the hardest bugs ([SE 4.1, 4.7][SE]); a generated parser with the specification's ranges would have rejected the AppleAVD input ([SE 3.4][SE]) |
| Performance needs hand-written assembly | **Break, measured** (MD-D7) | Safe Rust PNG beats libpng; JPEG XL in Chrome has no assembly ([SE 6.3][SE], [FC 11.11][FC]); AV1 is the open case and is measured |
| Decode output may differ by platform within the specification's tolerance | **Break** | B1 and PRIV-2; Izcal pins its own arithmetic |
| Convert colour to the display at decode | **Break** (MD-D8) | Fingerprinting and determinism; one decoded copy across monitors |
| Hardware decode when available, always | **Keep, bounded** (MD-D10, MD-D11) | Energy (under 9%, [MP 11.6][MP]); but it must not change web-observable pixels or expose hardware bits |
| Autoplay depends on the user's history with the site | **Break** (MD-D13) | A history-derived rule is browsing history and not a function of recorded inputs |
| Patent-encumbered codecs come from the OS | **Keep on macOS and Windows; extend on Linux** (MD-D3) | The OS vendor is the licensee there; Linux has no OS decoder, and WebRTC requires H.264 |
| DRM is the browser's own module | **Keep D-S7** | Licensed components only; never an unlicensed CDM |
| Media cannot be replayed deterministically | **Break** (MD-D12) | Software decode is a pure function; only timing needs recording |
| Developer tools show media as logs | **Break** | Causes, with the specification step (ANC-1) |

## 5. Lessons learned

Ideas and evidence only; no code and no template (M-1).

| Project | What happened | Lesson | How Izcal applies it |
|---|---|---|---|
| MNG | Removed for size, no maintainers, little use ([HP 2.2][HP]) | A format's cost to implementers decides adoption | Every format here is weighed on implementer cost, patents, a second vendor and attack surface (MD-D1) |
| Arithmetic JPEG | Never shipped: no second vendor ([HP 1.8][HP]) | A format only one engine decodes fragments the web | Izcal decodes the web's subset of T.81, not more (MD-D1) |
| JPEG 2000, JPEG XR, HEIC | Single-engine formats through OS codecs never spread; Safari dropped JPEG 2000 ([HP 3.5–3.7][HP]) | OS-only formats make the web differ by OS | No OS-only image format is exposed (MD-D1) |
| JPEG XL | Removed, then reinstated once a memory-safe decoder existed ([HP 4][HP]) | The deciding question became "is there a safe, fast decoder" | Izcal's own decoder; decode cost measured before shipping (MD-D2) |
| libwebp CVE-2023-4863 | Huffman tables sized from worst-case values that assume valid trees; fuzzing could not reach it ([Isosceles](https://blog.isosceles.com/the-webp-0day/)) | Build tables only after validating them; fuzzing is not assurance | Separate validating pass for every entropy table, proved capacity (G52) |
| AppleAVD CVE-2022-22675 | A field the specification bounds to 0..31 indexed a 32-element array with values up to 255 ([P0 RCA](https://googleprojectzero.github.io/0days-in-the-wild/0day-RCAs/2022/CVE-2022-22675.html)) | The specification's ranges are a free validator | Generated parsers carry every range (G51) |
| FORCEDENTRY | A ".gif" reached over 20 codecs through format sniffing, outside BlastDoor ([P0](https://projectzero.google/2021/12/a-deep-dive-into-nso-zero-click.html)) | Sniffing multiplies surface; routing must be total | One decoder per sniffed format, no fall-through; every image path through the media process, including fonts, favicons and notifications (MD-D5) |
| Stagefright, Android media | Integer overflows in MP4 handling; the answer was compute-only processes ([SE 3.2, 9.1, 9.2][SE]) | Contain decoders where an exploit gains nothing | The media process has no network, file or device access |
| BlastDoor | Exponential restart throttling to a 20-minute cap ([SE 9.3][SE]) | Make brute force slow | Restart throttling per (site, container) (MD-26) |
| Web Audio 0-days | Buffers shared between the main thread and the audio thread without proper locking ([SE 3.8, 3.9][SE]) | Real-time threads need an ownership model, not locks | No mutable buffer is shared between script and the render thread except by transfer (6.8) |
| WebRTC CVE-2022-2294, MediaCodec CVE-2020-6572 | State across reconfiguration ([SE 3.5, 3.10][SE]) | Codec objects need explicit state machines | Every codec and session object is a generated state machine tested under random control sequences (MD-22) |
| Firefox GStreamer → FFmpeg | Coverage then depended only on FFmpeg being installed ([HP 8.2][HP]) | Coverage of real content decides users' experience | The format list is set from measured web usage (MD-D1), and the Linux H.264 gap is named (MD-D3) |
| Ladybird, Servo | Both new engines relied on frameworks ([GO 8.10, 8.11][GO], [HP 8.5][HP]) | A no-library media stack is a multi-year, multi-team effort | Staffed as its own tracks with a parity date, not a side task (Section 9) |
| rav1d | More than 20 person-months against 7 for the C part only; about 5% slower ([GO 8.2–8.4][GO]) | SIMD is where AV1 decode time goes, and estimates run 3× low | The SIMD strategy is a decision with a measured reversal (MD-D7); estimates carry a 3× contingency for AV1 |
| Chrome Rust PNG, BMP | Shipped only with no regressions; faster than C ([SE 6.1–6.4][SE]) | Memory safety costs no speed for image formats | Speed targets relative to the fastest black-box decoder (MD-50) |
| Firefox colour management | Partial management (tagged only, v2 only, images not video) left bugs open for years ([CH 10.3][CH]) | One pipeline for every source, v2 and v4 from the start | MD-D8 |
| OpenH264 | Royalty coverage only for Cisco's binary ([OS 4.6][OS]) | A licence attached to a binary cannot be moved to Izcal's code | Not used; MD-D3 decides H.264 on its own terms |
| Widevine and independent browsers | Refused ([HP 10.3][HP], [OS 8.3][OS]) | DRM is a licensing relationship | D-S7 unchanged; native PlayReady and the protected-media host |
| Chrome's MEI autoplay | History-based, unpublished threshold ([MP 7.2][MP]) | A heuristic that depends on history is neither explainable nor replayable | Stateless rule (MD-D13) |
| Microsoft Edge 2016 test, Firefox 70 Core Animation | Edge ran 7 h 22 min and Chrome 4 h 20 min streaming the same video on identical laptops (Edge drew 2.07 W and Chrome 2.82 W in a mixed browsing lab test); Firefox 70's Core Animation change cut 28.4 W to 16.4 W from compositing alone ([MP 11.1–11.3][MP]) | The presentation path, not the decoder, often decides playback energy | Overlays and direct scanout are requirements on the GPU service; energy measured against the OS player (MD-D24) |

### 6.1 Processes and placement (MD-D5, MD-D10, MD-D20, MD-D29)

**The media process.** FP-D11 (b) placed image decoding in a decoder process per (site, container), never pooled (per renderer key after reconciliation 2, 6.0), exiting after 10 s† idle (FP-38). This spec extends that process to all media parsing and calls it the **media process**: every demuxer, image and audio and video decoder, every encoder, the ICC parser and colour conversion at decode, the WebVTT and HLS playlist parsers, RTP depacketizing and jitter buffers, and Clear Key decryption. It runs at the security scope's **strictest tier: compute plus IPC, no file system, no network, no devices and no window-server connection** ([SS][SS] 6.7); the broker grants it shared-memory regions and endpoints, as for every service (I-2). It holds the site's own media and the opaque cross-origin media that site embeds. This is the shape every platform converged on after an exploit wave: Android's mediaserver split and `media.swcodec` with "no access to device drivers", Apple's BlastDoor, Firefox's RDD process ([SE 9.1–9.3, 9.7][SE]).

| Option | What an exploit reaches | Cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Decoders in the renderer | The site's renderer, JS heap and JIT memory; opaque cross-origin content in the renderer for Spectre-class reads | None | Rejected: FP-D11 already chose (b), and G56 needs opaque data out of the renderer |
| (b) **Media process per (site, container)** | That site's media **plus the opaque media it embeds, which its renderer cannot see**; no device, file or network authority. The renderer→media endpoint is therefore an escalation boundary (IPC-R17): a compromised renderer that exploits its media process gains the opaque data G56 protects, and nothing else | One process per media-using (site, container); ≤ 2 MiB† idle (FP-38); one IPC hop per decode job | **Recommended** |
| (c) One pooled media utility process | Every site's in-flight media (I-1 violation, [SE 12.2][SE]) | Least memory | Rejected |
| (d) Separate image and media processes per (site, container) | As (b), split | A second process per site for no authority reduction | Rejected: same input trust, same authority |

**Bytes arrive sealed (IPC-R7).** The media process parses only memory no other process can write: encoded bytes arrive as a **sealed read-only region** (the security scope's IPC-R1 read-only kind, sealed by the broker or the network service before the grant) or are copied once into private memory before any parse. No parser or decoder reads a region another process can still write; in particular G52's validating pass and table build both read a private copy, so a peer cannot change code lengths between the passes (the double-fetch form of the libwebp bug, [SE 2.1][SE]). MD-22 includes an adversarial-peer case that mutates every granted region during decode.

**Opaque media never enters the renderer (G56).** Script cannot read the content of a no-cors cross-origin response (canvas tainting; Web Audio outputs silence for opaque `MediaElementAudioSourceNode` inputs). Izcal goes further: **neither the encoded bytes nor the decoded pixels or samples of an opaque response map into the renderer's address space.** The partition service, which knows a response is opaque when it computes the response's tainting, delivers its body on a broker-bound endpoint to the media process of the requesting (site, container); the renderer receives only an opaque handle and the typed header (intrinsic size, frame count, orientation, colour class, duration), which HTML already exposes. A service worker that forwards an opaque response passes the handle, never the body; the Cache API stores it in the storage service. Decoded opaque images go from the media process to the **content GPU process of the embedding renderer key** as regions that process fixed (FP 6.5), and opaque video frames to the compositor's video layers and, for script-visible uses, to that content GPU process; opaque audio goes to the audio service. Every raster of content that contains an opaque image (tiles, CSS filters and masks, canvas drawing, printing, tab capture) therefore happens where the pixels are, in the content GPU process, including its device-less CPU path (HGBA-2 P3); the graphics spec accepted this requirement ([GR 6.1, 6.15, 6.27][GR]). This closes Spectre-class reads of cross-origin image and media content from a compromised or speculating renderer, which per-site renderers alone do not, because every engine today decodes cross-origin images in the embedding page's renderer ([SE 12.1][SE]). Timing channels that the web platform exposes by design (`decode()` resolution, load and paint times) remain; they are enumerated and measured by MD-31 (b).

**Hardware decode runs in its own process (MD-D10).** Hardware decoders need device or kernel-driver access: a DRM render node or VA-API device on Linux, D3D kernel interfaces on Windows, IOKit user clients or OS decoder services for VideoToolbox, a D3D device for Media Foundation's hardware path. Giving that access to the media process, the process most likely to be compromised by input, would let every site's media process reach a GPU or decoder kernel driver directly; AppleAVD's H.264 bug was in such a kernel-side decoder ([SE 3.3][SE]). So while a (site, container) uses hardware decode, the broker launches a **hardware-decode process** for it: it holds a video-only device (decode queues only, no graphics or compute queues, no window) or an OS decoder session, **runs no Izcal parser**, and receives from the media process only typed picture parameters with slice data (split decode) or access units the media process has already validated (OS full decoders). Its sandbox grants are exactly those its OS path needs, recorded per OS by spikes MD-S1, MD-S2 and MD-S4 in the security scope's per-process index. Decoded pictures stay in GPU memory and are exported as OS handles to the GPU service, and to the media process for readback. It exits with the last hardware session. Section 6.9 gives the per-codec paths.

**Restart throttling.** After a crash, the broker restarts a (site, container)'s media or hardware-decode process at once the first time, then with a delay that doubles after the second crash in a 10-minute† window, capped at 20 minutes† and reset after an hour† without a crash, with the address-space layout re-randomised on every restart, as BlastDoor does ([SE 9.3][SE]). A crash shows a "media stopped for this site" label, never a browser-level error. Media in other sites is unaffected.

**Total routing.** Every image and media byte reaches a decoder only through a media process: page images, CSS images, CSS Color 5 `@color-profile` profiles (returned as a typed descriptor), notification images, images inside fonts (`sbix`, `CBDT`, as TX already decided, and rasters embedded in OT-SVG glyph documents, which TX's restricted glyph mode must route here or disallow), clipboard images pasted into a page, Media Session artwork, and camera frames that arrive compressed. **Favicons** are decoded once at fetch time in the media process of their (site, container) and stored as a size-capped bitmap (64 × 64 px† at the device's maximum DPR) in the profile, so the shell never decodes and history or bookmark views never start processes. **PDF** images (including JPEG 2000, JBIG2 and CCITT, which PDF needs and the web does not) are decoded by the same L2m crates inside the PDF process's own sandbox, which follows G50–G53 like a media process. FORCEDENTRY shows that one unrouted path defeats the design ([SE 9.4][SE]).

**SVG as an image (MD-D20).** SVG images run in the secure static or secure animated processing mode: no script, no external resources ([SVG 2 processing modes](https://www.w3.org/TR/SVG2/conform.html#processing-modes)). A same-origin or CORS-approved SVG image is rendered in the renderer by Izcal's SVG engine, like inline SVG; this is the one image format G50 lets the renderer parse, because it is a document format the renderer already parses for inline SVG. An **opaque cross-site SVG image** is rendered in the media process, by the scriptless SVG, XML, style, layout and text crates linked into it (mapped from the engine image) **through the SVG spec's scriptless document host** — **Q-MD-18 is answered yes (SV-D1, owner decision 20 October 2026): the DOM exists there without a JS agent heap**, and MD-S10 is now joined with the SVG spec's SV-S1 as one proof suite for the scriptless host in a non-renderer process — and returned as pixels like any other image. Fonts in such images are the Izcal font set and the OS tier only, reached through a read-only font endpoint, since SVG images load no external resources; **a `data:` font declared inside an opaque SVG image is parsed in the media process under the fonts and text spec's fuel** (R8, applied 20 October 2026). **Static-document keys include the request and credentials mode**, so two fetches of one URL under different modes hold different documents. The **opaque-image raster protocol is pull-only**: the renderer asks for a raster at a size, zoom and DPR and receives pixels, with **no next-change or environment-read signal** back to the renderer — an animated image's frames arrive as the media process produces them, and the renderer learns nothing about when the next frame will come. Re-raster latency is target **SV-43**, separate from MD-56. **Under the reduce-motion setting, SVG images show the t = 0 sample paused with a play control** (SV-D16, R8). The alternative, rendering all SVG images in the renderer as other engines do, puts cross-site SVG source in the renderer; it is MD-D20's fallback, recorded as a named exception to G56.

**The audio service (MD-D29).** One broker-launched process owns OS audio output and input devices: it mixes PCM streams from renderers (Web Audio) and media processes (media elements), resamples to the device rate, runs the voice-processing chain (MD-D16) and writes to the OS. It parses nothing: every input is PCM in fixed-format shared ring buffers with a typed header the service re-validates. It holds every site's output-ready PCM and every granted microphone stream, so, like the GPU service, it is a **shared-process exception to I-1**, recorded as such with a threat model in the security scope (Section 9.5); it is tier P (never panics on any input, coding standard CS-ER-3), keeps no recording of any stream, and exits after 30 s† with no stream open (a value this spec sets). The alternative, one OS stream per renderer, would give every renderer device access (I-2) and multiply real-time threads and OS streams.

**The capture service.** One broker-launched process opens cameras, microphones and screen capture through the OS's pickers and consent (ScreenCaptureKit's picker, Windows' `GraphicsCapturePicker` with its yellow border, the xdg-desktop-portal ScreenCast and Camera interfaces) ([OS 10.6–10.10][OS]) and delivers frames only to the endpoints the broker granted for that (site, container). It is the second recorded shared-process exception (it handles every site's granted capture). It parses no payload: many cameras deliver MJPEG or H.264, and a USB device can be hostile input, so compressed frames pass untouched to the requesting (site, container)'s media process, which decodes them under G50–G53.

**The CDM process (SEC-33) and the protected-media host (D-S7)** are unchanged; Section 6.11 restates how media reaches them.

**Specifications defined by code (MD-D4).** RFC 6386 says VP8's "bitstream is defined by the reference source code and not this narrative", and RFC 6716's normative part is the C decoder in its Appendix A, with conformance judged by the RFC's comparison tool ([FC 3.3, 4.1][FC]). **Constant tables (MD-D4, second part).** Decoders need the constant tables their specifications print: AV1's default CDFs and scan orders (AOM), VP9's probability tables (Google's WebM project), JPEG's example Huffman and quantisation tables (ITU-T T.81 Annex K), H.264's CABAC context tables (ITU-T), MP3's and AAC's tables (ISO/IEC 11172-3, 13818-3, 14496-3), JPEG XL's (ISO/IEC 18181), WebP lossless's (RFC 9649), FLAC's (RFC 9639), Vorbis's (Xiph.Org) and Opus's and VP8's (inside the RFCs' BSD-licensed code components). Where a table follows from a formula the specification states (window functions, quantiser curves, many scan orders), `hg-bitgen` generates it at build time from the formula and records that. The rest are standards data only if their publisher counts as a standards body under M-1 part 1's exception: ITU, ISO and the IETF clearly do; AOM, Google's WebM project and Xiph.Org are put to the owner; tables printed only inside RFC code components are copied from the RFC's prose tables where they exist, and otherwise put to the owner with the reversal route above.

### 6.3 Parsers: one description per format, generated and proved (MD-D6, MD-D17)

**`.hgbits` and `hg-bitgen`.** Every container box, header, metadata block, colour profile, playlist and caption file is described once in `.hgbits`, a declarative description language: fields with their bit widths and descriptors (the AV1 and VP9 specifications already write syntax as tables of `f(n)`, `su(n)`, `le(n)`, `leb128()`, `uvlc()`), the specification's value ranges (H.264's `cpb_cnt_minus1` in 0..31), cross-field constraints (a DNG's `SamplesPerPixel` must equal the JPEG SOF component count), length and count dependencies, and the `// spec:` anchor of every field. `hg-bitgen` (a tier T tool) generates the Rust parser, a structure-aware fuzzer and a serializer for tests, and emits Kani harnesses that prove, for each generated parser: every read in bounds, every arithmetic step checked, acceptance exactly of the described language, each byte read at most once, and no allocation. These are the properties EverParse proves for Hyper-V's 100+ message kinds without noticeable performance overhead ([SE 8.1, 8.2][SE]). The decision to build Izcal's own generator rather than use a published one follows M-1 (EverParse and Vest are evidence that the approach works). Four of the seven root-cause classes in the record (specification ranges, inconsistent fields, layout assumptions and size arithmetic) are rejected by construction; entropy-table construction (G52), cross-thread races (6.8) and lifecycle bugs (MD-22's state machines) need the other rules ([SE 8.7][SE]). A shared description language with the fonts spec's `.hgfont` is proposed as an amendment, not assumed (Q-MD-9).

**Entropy coding is an algorithm, not a format.** Huffman tables (JPEG, PNG's deflate through `hg-compress`, GIF's LZW, WebP lossless), arithmetic and range coders (AV1's multi-symbol coder, VP8 and VP9's boolean coder, H.264's CABAC, Opus's range coder) and ANS (JPEG XL) live in `hg-entropy`. Rule **G52**: a table is built only after a separate validating pass proves the code lengths form a complete or permitted prefix code and the table fits its fixed capacity; capacities are typed array lengths derived from the specification, and Kani proves every table write in bounds for every input that passes validation. Both passes read a private copy of the table definition, never a shared region (6.1). This is exactly the step libwebp got wrong ([SE 2.1][SE]).

**Decoder discipline (G53).** A decoder's input is always hostile and a panic is always an input bug, so every L2m crate is held to tier P's **outcome** (never panics on any input, [coding standard][CS] CS-ER-3) whatever process hosts it. Literal tier P lints would fight the codec specifications, which define saturating clips and modular steps (`Clip1`, `Clip3`, `Round2`) in every pixel and sample operation, and would put a checked operation in every inner loop against MD-50 and MD-51. So L2m gets its own **lint profile**, agreed with the security owner as a coding-standard amendment: saturating and modular operations are allowed where the specification defines them, marked by that operation's `// spec:` anchor instead of a `cs-allow` marker; hot loops use **proved-range integer newtypes** (Kani proves the range once, at construction from validated header values) instead of `checked_*` per sample; indexing is in bounds by construction (fixed-size arrays and iterators); everything else follows tier P. Spike **MD-S9** measures the profile's cost on the PNG and JPEG kernels at MD-M0. Allocation goes only through a **budget handle** obtained after header validation: the handle's size is computed from validated fields with checked arithmetic and charged against the (site, container)'s canonical media budget before any pixel or sample work. There is no recursion over input structure (ISO-BMFF box nesting, Matroska element trees and JPEG XL's modular trees use explicit bounded stacks, depth ≤ 32†), and every input-driven loop consumes **fuel**. These four rules turn the Rust failure classes, panic, out-of-memory, stack overflow and non-termination ([SE 7.5][SE]), into specified errors.

**Canonical limits (MD-D17).** The specifications permit bombs: PNG dimensions up to 2³¹−1, deflate at 1032:1, APNG with 2³¹−1 frames looping forever ([SE 10.1–10.3][SE]). Izcal's limits are canonical, identical on every device, following FP-D19's principle (an extension of FP-D19, which decides heap and buffer limits; listed in 9.5), and an image or stream beyond them is a decode error with the error event the specification defines:

| Limit | Value† | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Image width or height | ≤ 32,768 px | Larger than any display and CSS's practical use; AVIF Baseline's 8,192 × 4,352 fits easily ([FC 1.6][FC]) |
| Image area for script-observable decode (canvas, `createImageBitmap`, WebGL) | ≤ 2²⁸ px (268 M px, 1 GiB at 4 B/px) | Bounds a single readback; display-path decode of larger valid images uses downscaled decode instead |
| Decoded bytes held per (site, container) | FP-13's working-set rule, enforced by the media budget | FP 6.5 |
| Animated image frames | ≤ 2¹⁶ frames decoded per loop; ≤ 2 decoded frames held (FP 6.5) | An APNG or GIF CPU bomb costs at most the frame rate of the compositor |
| Media budget per (site, container) | CPU fuel per wall-clock second at background QoS when hidden; decode jobs beyond it are queued, never dropped silently | WebCodecs saturation semantics ([MP 4.1][MP]) |
| Compression ratio inside any container | ≤ 1032:1 per stream, as the format allows, but decoded size always checked against the validated dimensions | Size comes from validated headers, never from decompressed length |

These are set from first principles and checked against the corpus's distribution of real image dimensions and frame counts at MD-M1 (Q-MD-10): no corpus page may break.

### 6.4 Decoders and encoders: exactness, arithmetic, SIMD and threads (MD-D7)

**Output contract.** For exact-output formats, the decoder's output is the specification's output, bit for bit, checked against conformance streams (MD-1). For tolerance formats, Izcal defines one output: JPEG uses one integer IDCT meeting T.83's accuracy (the same on every ISA) and one chroma upsampling filter, chosen at MD-M0 by measuring on the corpus which filter minimises difference to the black-box majority of engines (M-1 part 2); Vorbis, MP3 and AAC are decoded in IEEE single precision with no FMA contraction and `hg-math`'s functions (JS-D12; `hg-math` gains f32 entry points defined as the f64 function rounded once to f32, a JS-spec amendment), with fixed rounding at output. Two further rules make float code identical across ISAs: **every float reduction has one canonical association order** (for example eight interleaved accumulators), defined by the scalar reference and matched by every ISA's kernel whatever its vector width, because AVX2's 8 lanes and NEON's 4 would otherwise sum in different orders; and **subnormals are flushed in software** at the points the contract names (node outputs, filter states), never through the FPU's flush-to-zero modes, which Rust and LLVM treat as undefined behaviour to change. Every one of **Izcal's own** decoders is therefore bit-identical across macOS, Linux and Windows and across x86-64 and AArch64 (G55, MD-40). Output of **OS decoders** (H.264, HEVC and AAC on macOS and Windows, HE-AAC everywhere) is not Izcal's and may differ by OS version; Section 6.15 bounds what that reveals.

**SIMD (MD-D7).** dav1d keeps about 250,000 lines of hand-written assembly ([GO 8.1][GO]); a safe-Rust fork replacing it with intrinsics reports 1.56× slower on one 4K AVIF still (a historical measurement, Low confidence) ([FC 11.4][FC]); Chrome's JPEG XL decoder uses a portable SIMD layer and no assembly ([FC 11.11][FC]); Rust 1.87 made most SIMD intrinsics callable without `unsafe` inside matching target-feature contexts ([Rust 1.87.0](https://blog.rust-lang.org/2025/05/15/Rust-1.87.0/)). Options: (a) Izcal-written Rust SIMD kernels per ISA (x86-64 with AVX2 and SSE4.1 baselines, AArch64 NEON) over **one `hg-simd` crate in L2s, owned by this spec and shared with the graphics spec's rasterizer** ([RN2 Q18][RN2]): calling a `#[target_feature]` function from a context without that feature, and the pointer-based load and store intrinsics, are `unsafe`, so `hg-simd` wraps them in per-ISA dispatch and slice-based load and store functions, the only `unsafe` in either spec's SIMD code, and the kernels in `hg-dsp` and `hg-raster` use that safe API; every `unsafe` block is inventoried (G5), Kani harnesses prove each kernel's index arithmetic, and every kernel has a scalar reference kernel and a bit-exact differential test (G54); (b) Izcal-written assembly per ISA; (c) scalar only. **Recommendation: (a)**, with the selected kernel set fixed per ISA so output never depends on CPU features (kernels are bit-exact against the scalar reference by test, so selection affects only speed). Reversal: if AV1 or VP9 misses MD-51 after the MD-M2 optimisation pass, hot kernels are written in Izcal's own assembly inside `hg-simd`, with the same reference and differential rule.

**Threads.** AV1, VP9 and JPEG XL decoders use tile, frame and row parallelism through the substrate's thread pool (the substrate is the only source of threads: Stage 1 G3; JS G13 for the engine); results are a pure function of input whatever the interleaving (phase purity, HGBA-3 B5), checked by running every conformance stream at 1, 2 and 8 threads with identical output (MD-40).

**Encoders.** Izcal needs encoders for `canvas.toBlob` and `toDataURL` (PNG, JPEG, WebP), MediaRecorder (WebM with VP8, VP9 or AV1 and Opus), WebCodecs encode, and WebRTC (VP8, VP9, AV1 real-time, Opus, G.711, and H.264 per MD-D3). Encoders run in the media process (CVE-2023-5217 was an encoder bug reached from a page, [SE 3.7][SE]), use the same discipline and are **deterministic**: identical input and settings give identical bytes on every machine, which makes canvas export a function of the canvas's pixels only. Export encodes in the media process of the key from the canvas's canonical pixels (the renderer's for origin-clean canvases) and adds **no noise**: under the one D-S1 amendment the graphics spec holds ([GR 6.15][GR]; owner decision OD-6, [RN2 Q14, Q22][RN2]), only pixels that were already noised (WebGL or WebGPU output drawn into the canvas) carry noise. PNG encoding uses `hg-compress`'s deflate encoder (NW owns it, reconciliation P5). OS hardware encoders are used for H.264 on macOS and Windows (VideoToolbox's low-latency mode, Media Foundation's encoder; [OS 9.1–9.4][OS]) because the OS vendor holds the licence; Izcal's own encoders serve everything else.

### 6.5 Colour management and HDR (MD-D8, MD-D9)

**One pipeline for every source.** CSS colours, images, video, canvas and WebGPU output meet in one model: every surface carries a **colour descriptor** (primaries, transfer function, matrix, range, and HDR metadata), and pixel conversion is defined once in `hg-colour`. CSS **computed** colour values stay the style spec's: they are computed in f64 through `hg-math`, as ST 6.9 already specifies, because they are serialised to script; `hg-colour` takes those values as its input for painting, with one documented conversion from the f64 computed value to the f32 pixel path (MD-D28). The history is unanimous that partial colour management is what failed ([CH 10][CH], [HP 5][HP]).

**Inputs to a descriptor.** A decoder produces a descriptor from, in precedence order, PNG's `cICP` > `iCCP` > `sRGB` > `cHRM`+`gAMA` ([CH 4.3][CH]); CICP code points in AVIF, JPEG XL and video bitstreams (ITU-T H.273); ICC profiles (v2 and v4.4 including `cicp`, never iccMAX, [CH 1.6][CH]) embedded in JPEG (APP2 chunks reassembled with gap, duplicate and count checks), PNG, WebP, AVIF and JPEG XL; and the defaults of CSS Color 4: untagged images are sRGB; untagged video is BT.601 below 720p, SMPTE ST 296 at 720p, BT.709 at 1080p, and BT.2020 at 4K and above for SDR video ([CSS Color 4 §3.5](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-4/#untagged)). A malformed profile means "untagged" ([CSS Color 4 §3.4](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-4/#tagged-images)), never a failure. The ICC parser is a generated `.hgbits` parser in the media process (Little CMS's crafted-profile CVEs, [CH 3.5][CH]).

**Canonical working encodings (MD-D8).** The media process converts every decoded image into one of two encodings:

| Class | When | Encoding | Bytes per pixel |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S** | The source is SDR and its gamut lies within sRGB (sRGB-tagged, untagged, or a profile whose primaries are within sRGB) | RGBA, 8 bits per channel, sRGB transfer (IEC 61966-2-1 piecewise curve), premultiplied | 4 |
| **W** | Wide gamut, more than 8 bits of SDR precision that matters (16-bit PNG, 10-bit AVIF), or HDR (PQ, HLG, gain-map base plus map) | RGBA half-float, **linear light, BT.709 primaries extended** (negative and above-1.0 values carry wide gamut and HDR), 1.0 = HDR reference white of 203 cd/m² ([BT.2408](https://www.itu.int/pub/R-REP-BT.2408)), premultiplied | 8 |

Class S covers most images: 87.7% carry no ICC profile (CICP-tagged images are counted there too), plus sRGB-tagged ones ([FC 6.4][FC]); class W covers at least 1 in 80 images (the Almanac's floor) plus HDR. The extended-linear BT.709 encoding is the model Windows' DWM composes in (scRGB FP16) and close to macOS's EDR model (1.0 = SDR white), so the GPU service hands OS compositors surfaces they take natively ([CH 9.1, 9.3][CH]). Gain-map images keep the base rendition in its class and the gain map as a second plane with its metadata (MD-D9). Video frames keep their decoded YUV planes and descriptor; conversion happens at presentation.

**Conversion contract.** ICC sets no accuracy or interpolation rule ([CH 1.3, 12.1][CH]), so `hg-colour` defines one, in **two forms with one definition** ([RN2 Q12][RN2]): per-pixel f32 at decode in the media process (G55), and tables built on the CPU with the same f32 contract and applied by integer lookup at raster in the renderer and the GPU processes (graphics G61); each (source, destination) conversion has exactly one form, named in `hg-colour`'s table of conversions, and a class W texel enters the graphics spec's S8 or E16 encodings through a table indexed by its half-float bit pattern ([GR 6.5][GR]). The f32 definition: matrix-shaper and parametric-curve profiles are evaluated in IEEE single precision with no FMA contraction and `hg-math`'s f32 `pow` (6.4); sampled curves use linear interpolation as ICC requires; CLUTs use **tetrahedral** interpolation in 32-bit fixed point; conversion to class S rounds half to even after dithering-free quantisation; transfer functions (sRGB, BT.709, PQ, HLG with its OOTF per BT.2100) are evaluated exactly as the standards' formulas in single precision. Accuracy: ΔE00 ≤ 0.5† against a float64 reference over the ICC test profiles and the corpus's profiles (MD-5). Gamut mapping for images is the profile's perceptual intent where it has one, otherwise relative colorimetric with values kept unclipped in class W until display; CSS colours are gamut-mapped by the style spec's computation with CSS Color 4's algorithm, one of the three it allows, chosen jointly with this spec so that canvas and display use the same one (CSS Color 4 requires one algorithm "wherever CSS mandates that gamut mapping be performed", [CH 7.1][CH]).

**Display conversion happens only at the end.** The compositor GPU service converts class S and W surfaces, video frames and canvases to the window's output descriptor, applies HDR tone mapping and gain maps, and hands the OS compositor tagged surfaces (FP16 extended-linear for HDR or wide-gamut windows, 8-bit sRGB otherwise), with the OS's **headroom** as a time-varying input from the HAL's colour-output description ([CH 9][CH]). No pixel a page can read (canvas `getImageData`, `toBlob`, `createImageBitmap`, WebGL and WebGPU uploads, WebCodecs `VideoFrame.copyTo`) passes through the display conversion; each is defined in the canvas's or frame's own colour space, as HTML requires ([CH 8.1, 11.2][CH]). So the display profile and headroom never reach script, which closes the 2014 fingerprinting technique ([CH 11.1][CH]), and one decoded copy serves every monitor (FP 6.5).

**Readback is canonical.** When a class W image is drawn into an sRGB unorm8 canvas, `hg-colour` converts with the CSS Color 4 rules for that canvas (gamut mapping for unorm8 backing stores, [CH 7.3][CH]); an HDR image drawn into an SDR canvas is tone-mapped with the canonical EETF at a fixed headroom of 1.0, never the display's. A video frame read back is converted from its YUV planes by `hg-colour`'s integer path on the CPU (or a GPU path proved bit-identical to it), never by whatever the display path used.

**HDR (MD-D9).** PQ and HLG video and stills are presented with HDR reference white at 203 cd/m² and tone-mapped with the **BT.2390 EETF** ([BT.2390](https://www.itu.int/pub/R-REP-BT.2390)), both of which CSS Color HDR cites ([CH 5.2, 5.3][CH]); on each OS the reference white is mapped to the OS convention (scRGB 1.0 = 80 cd/m² on Windows, scaled by the user's SDR white level; EDR 1.0 = SDR white on macOS; the Wayland image description's reference luminance). **Gain maps**: ISO 21496-1 metadata is canonical and Ultra HDR's XMP form is the fallback, preferring ISO when both are present ([CH 6.2][CH]); the gain-map weight uses the GPU service's live headroom and the element's `dynamic-range-limit` (Chrome 136, Safari 26, [CH 7.7][CH]); the published decode formula is applied in the GPU service ([CH 6.3][CH]). **Exposure**: `dynamic-range` and `color-gamut` media queries report the coarse buckets the specifications define, counted in PRIV-2; numeric headroom is never exposed, following CSS Color HDR's privacy text ([CH 13.2][CH]); Chrome's proposed headroom query (an extension of the Window Management API, on which Mozilla and WebKit have negative positions) is not implemented ([CH 13.3][CH]). **sRGB's curve**: Izcal treats sRGB content as the IEC 61966-2-1 piecewise curve, as CSS Color 4 defines it, and tags surfaces as sRGB to the OS; KWin's choice of pure gamma 2.2 in HDR mode ([CH 5.7][CH]) is recorded and measured per OS (Q-MD-6).

**No ICC-authored file ships (MD-D8).** ICC's v2 profiles may be copied but not altered with ICC's name, and the sRGB v4 profile may be passed on only unchanged ([CH 2.1, 2.2][CH]); neither is MIT. Every built-in colour space is constructed in code from the published parameters (IEC 61966-2-1; BT.709, BT.2020, BT.2100; SMPTE ST 2084; Display P3 as CSS Color 4 defines it), and where Izcal must write a profile (an encoder embedding one), `hg-colour` generates a minimal v4 profile from those parameters; a CC0 project shows such a profile fits in about 0.5 KB ([CH 2.5][CH]). Whether bare standard parameters are free of copyright is confirmed by the legal review (Q-MD-5).

### 6.6 Images: decode scheduling, downscaling, caches and animation (MD-D18, MD-D19)

**Header first.** When bytes arrive, they reach the media process as a sealed read-only region (6.1): from the renderer for same-origin and CORS-approved responses, and directly from the partition service for opaque ones. The media process runs the generated header parser and returns a typed **image header**: format, intrinsic size after Exif orientation (CSS Images 3 `image-orientation: from-image`), frame count and loop count, colour class, density-corrected size where the format carries it. Layout uses only this record. A header result is a decision point (JS-D15), logged when recording.

**Decode on demand (FP 6.5).** The renderer's `hg-imgpipe` schedules decodes by priority: images intersecting the viewport, then those within one viewport† ahead in the scroll direction, then images whose `decode()` promise is pending, then speculative work at background priority. A decode's result is applied at a frame boundary, at a recorded decision point; the page never waits on a decode on the main thread, and paint uses whatever is ready. `decoding=sync` is honoured by holding the frame that first paints the image for at most one frame† while its decode completes; `decoding=async` and `auto` never hold a frame. `img.decode()` resolves when the request is completely available and its decode is complete, at the decision point after it (HTML's algorithm, [MP 2.1][MP]). Progressive JPEG passes and interlaced PNG passes are displayed as they complete, but partial states are display-only: HTML gives script no access to a partially decoded image.

**Downscale only for display (MD-D18).** FP 6.5 decodes at display size × DPR, using scaled IDCTs for JPEG and streaming row decode into a downscaler elsewhere. A downscaled decode changes pixels, so it is used **only for display**. Every path whose pixels a page can read (canvas `drawImage` source pixels, `createImageBitmap`, WebGL and WebGPU uploads) decodes at intrinsic size, so web-observable results never depend on viewport, DPR or memory pressure (FP-D19). Those intrinsic-size decodes are charged to the page as FP 6.5's canvas readback is, outside FP-13's display-size cap (amendment).

**Caches.** Encoded bytes of same-origin and CORS-approved images are kept once per partition in the renderer's resource store (FP 6.5); encoded bytes of opaque images are kept once per partition in the media process, charged to the site (FP 6.5 amendment); otherwise the media process keeps no encoded copy after a job. Decoded pixels live in discardable shared regions fixed by the content GPU process of the embedding renderer key (FP 6.5), keyed by the renderer projection of the networking spec's partition key **(T, C, X, n)** plus the request URL, the decode size and the colour class ([RN P18][RN]); a cache hit is a decision point. Opaque images' regions are mapped by that content GPU process and the media process only (G56). The cache is class (a) derived data (FP 6.4, FP-D16 not applicable: no on-disk format); discarding and re-decoding gives identical pixels because decoding is pure (FP-12).

**Animated images (MD-D19).** GIF, APNG, animated WebP, AVIF image sequences and JPEG XL animation are decoded frame by frame, at most two decoded frames held (FP 6.5). Frame timing comes from the document's animation timeline on the substrate clock, so it replays. HTML and the formats leave minimum frame delays unstated; engines clamp very short GIF delays, and Izcal measures the shipping engines' clamp black-box at MD-M0 and records it as an interoperability observation (M-1 part 2) rather than taking a value from any engine's code. **Reduced motion**: when the OS's reduce-motion or "auto-play animated images" setting is on, animated images and muted, looping, autoplaying videos show their first frame with a play control, as Safari does for images ([GO 5.1][GO]); the user can play each one, and a per-site setting turns animation back on. The page sees `prefers-reduced-motion` as today and no other new signal. Izcal follows CSSWG issue 1615 so authors can opt specific images in when it is specified ([GO 5.4][GO]).

**Image observability for developers** (Section 6.17): for each image, the `srcset` candidate chosen and why (the `sizes` result, DPR, the candidates' widths), bytes, decode time and process, decoded bytes, downscale factor, source colour descriptor and class, and whether it was the LCP element and was lazy-loaded.

### 6.7 The media pipeline: element, clocks, MSE, HLS, WebCodecs and recording (MD-D21, MD-D22)

**Split of work.** The renderer's `hg-media` implements the web-visible algorithms, written from HTML, MSE 2, WebCodecs and MediaStream Recording with spec anchors: the media element's `readyState` and `networkState` machines, seeking, the official playback position sampled at stable states, the resource selection and fetch algorithms, `timeupdate` and `progress` cadence, MSE's coded frame processing, buffered-range and eviction algorithms, WebCodecs' control queues and saturation, and track lists. The media process demuxes and decodes and returns typed results: init-segment descriptions, coded-frame index entries (timestamps, durations, keyframe flags, sizes), decoded frame handles and PCM. Compressed frame data stays in the media process; the renderer holds only the index MSE needs.

**Clocks and A/V sync.** The audio device clock is the master. The audio service publishes, through a shared-memory timestamp block, the playout position of each stream in frames and the OS timestamp it corresponds to; the media process and the GPU service derive media time from it. The GPU service selects, at each composite, the decoded frame whose presentation interval contains the expected display time (from the OS's vsync timestamps), so frame selection happens where the frame is shown and needs no main-thread work. Target: audio within +15 ms (early) to −45 ms (late) of video at the speaker and screen, the tightest published receiver tolerance (ATSC IS-191, [MP 8.4][MP], Low confidence; BT.1359's detectability thresholds are +45/−125 ms, [MP 8.1][MP]), measured as MD-7. Video without audio uses the substrate's monotonic clock. **Script-visible frame reads** (`drawImage(video)`, `texImage2D(video)`, `new VideoFrame(video)`, `requestVideoFrameCallback` metadata) use the frame at the official playback position, as HTML defines the current frame, supplied by the media process; only on-screen presentation uses the compositor's selection. For an **origin-clean** source the media process sends that frame as canonical RGBA (6.5's integer YUV path, MD-32) to the renderer too, because the renderer is the authoritative raster of every origin-clean canvas ([GR 6.15][GR]), and the same pixels to the content GPU process for its display copy; hardware-decoded frames are read back to the media process first ([RN2 Q21][RN2]). A `VideoFrame` made from a WebGL or WebGPU canvas, or a `MediaStreamTrackProcessor` frame from such a canvas's stream, carries the graphics spec's noised pixels, and `VideoFrame.copyTo` of OS-decoded frames follows the one D-S1 rule ([RN2 Q14, Q23][RN2]). Playback rate with `preservesPitch` uses Izcal's own time-stretching (WSOLA-class, written from published papers), the output of which is part of the canonical audio contract.

**UA-chosen timing, fixed (MD-D12).** HTML leaves intervals to the UA: `timeupdate` every 15–250 ms, `progress` every 350 ± 200 ms, `stalled` after about 3 s, and the `HAVE_ENOUGH_DATA` estimate of download rate ([MP 1.1–1.5][MP]). Izcal fixes them: `timeupdate` at 250 ms† of media time (the spec's maximum, the fewest wakeups), `progress` at 350 ms† of wall time while bytes arrive, `stalled` at 3 s†, and `HAVE_ENOUGH_DATA` computed from the partition service's recorded throughput estimate for this (T, C), never a browser-wide one (which would be a cross-site channel), with a stated formula. Each is a deterministic function of recorded inputs.

**MSE quotas (MD-D22).** SourceBuffer quotas are canonical: 128 MiB† per video SourceBuffer and 16 MiB† per audio SourceBuffer (about 50 s of 4K AV1 at 20 Mbit/s and many minutes of audio), identical on every device; `QuotaExceededError` and the coded frame eviction algorithm are therefore functions of what the page appended, never of device memory. Memory pressure never evicts from a classic SourceBuffer silently; under pressure the media process's buffers are charged and reported like any other (FP 6.7).

**ManagedMediaSource and native HLS (MD-D21).** ManagedMediaSource ([MSE 2](https://www.w3.org/TR/media-source-2/)) lets the UA decide when to stream and when to evict; Safari ships it for power ([MP 3.5][MP]). Izcal implements it with a **deterministic, energy-driven policy**: `startstreaming` when buffered media ahead of the playback position falls below 10 s†, `endstreaming` when it exceeds 30 s† (so the radio idles between bursts), and eviction of ranges more than 30 s† behind the playback position when the buffer passes its quota, each a decision point. Native HLS (RFC 8216) is implemented with the playlist parser in the media process, ABR in `hg-media` using the same throughput estimate, and the same buffering policy; Safari ships it and Chrome rolled it out on desktop in late 2025 ([HP 9.3, 9.5][HP]).

**WebCodecs.** The same decoders and encoders, without a container. `hardwareAcceleration` and `optimizeForLatency` are hints; "prefer-software" always uses Izcal's own codec, so its output is exact and replayable; "prefer-hardware" may use the hardware path for playback-like work, but `VideoFrame.copyTo` always returns canonical pixels (6.5). The saturation maximum is a canonical constant per codec (4† decode requests in flight), so `decodeQueueSize` depends only on the page and on recorded decode-completion events. `configure()` with `prefer-hardware` never fails for a configuration the policy table supports: without hardware it runs Izcal's own codec, which the hint permits, so the outcome reveals nothing (MD-30); `decoderImplementation` reports one constant per OS family; Media Capabilities `encodingInfo` answers from the same table as `decodingInfo`. Codec reclamation follows WebCodecs' rule (no progress for 10 s, or background) ([SE 10.5][SE]). Every codec object is a generated state machine; WebCodecs' own security text asks for fuzzing of random control sequences, which MD-22 requires ([SE 9.9][SE]).

**MediaRecorder** produces WebM (VP8, VP9 or AV1 with Opus) on every platform, and MP4 (H.264 with AAC) where MD-D3's licences allow; chunk boundaries for `timeslice` are computed in media time, so they are deterministic given the recorded input frames.

### 6.8 The audio stack (MD-D14, MD-D15, MD-D16, MD-D31)

**Web Audio graph in the renderer.** `hg-webaudio` implements Web Audio 1.1 ([W3C WD 22 September 2026](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.1/)): the graph is rendered by quantum (128 frames, 2.67 ms at 48 kHz) on a dedicated render thread in the renderer, and the rendered PCM goes to the audio service through a lock-free single-producer ring buffer. **Ownership rule** (from CVE-2019-13720 and CVE-2021-21166, [SE 3.8, 3.9][SE]): no mutable buffer is shared between the script thread and the render thread. Script-visible `AudioBuffer`s are copied or transferred into render-owned storage at a quantum boundary; node parameter changes travel as messages applied at quantum boundaries; `ScriptProcessorNode` gets fresh buffers per event. This is enforced by types: render-thread state is not `Send` to the script thread.

**AudioWorklet and real-time scheduling (MD-D31).** AudioWorklet runs page JavaScript on the render thread, in an agent of Izcal's JS engine. A real-time thread can host it only if the engine guarantees, for that agent: a collection pause well under a quantum (≤ 0.5 ms†) with no safepoint shared with the process's other agents; allocation without process-wide locks (priority inversion); no code installation, tier-up or W^X toggling on that thread (compilation elsewhere, JS-D15's install points outside the quantum); and joining the OS's real-time mechanism (an audio workgroup on macOS, MMCSS's "Audio" or "Pro Audio" task on Windows, RTKit or the xdg Realtime portal on Linux, [MP 9.4–9.8][MP]) from a sandboxed renderer through the broker and the substrate. The JS spec states none of these today (HGBA-2's GC target, p99 ≤ 4 ms on the main thread, is far above a quantum). Options: (a) the worklet agent on a real-time thread, with these requirements written into the JS spec as targets; (b) the worklet on a high-priority, non-real-time thread with one extra quantum of output buffering; (c) worklets on the real-time thread only when the graph has no AudioWorkletNode, otherwise (b). **Recommendation: (a), designed from the start, with (b) as the shipped behaviour until spike MD-S8** (a real-time thread with a JS agent in a sandboxed renderer on each OS) and the JS spec's worklet targets pass; MD-9 and MD-10 state their thresholds per option. Graphs without AudioWorklet run on a real-time thread from the first release, since they run no page code.

**Bit-identical rendering (MD-D14).** Web Audio specifies float32 processing but not the math library, FFT, reduction order or denormal handling, which is why AudioContext output differs by machine (5.4 bits, [SE 11.5][SE]). Izcal renders every node in IEEE single precision with no FMA contraction, `hg-math`'s f32 functions (6.4), one FFT implementation and one canonical reduction order in `hg-dsp`, subnormals flushed in software at node outputs and filter states (6.4), and its own fixed choices where the specification leaves them open (the `DynamicsCompressorNode` curve, the `PannerNode` HRTF data, oscillator band-limiting). An `OfflineAudioContext` render is then bit-identical across OS and ISA (MD-30), which removes the AudioContext fingerprint at its source instead of adding noise; D-S1's seeded noise on audio readback becomes unnecessary for Izcal's own rendering and is proposed for removal once MD-30 passes, as part of **one D-S1 amendment** with the fonts spec's `measureText` (TX-D4) and the graphics spec's canvas rule (GR-D12), whose text the graphics spec holds (owner decision OD-6, [RN2 Q14][RN2]). The exception is audio decoded by an **OS** decoder (AAC and HE-AAC on macOS and Windows, which `decodeAudioData` and CORS media routed into Web Audio can reach): its samples can differ by OS version, so D-S1's noise stays on buffers derived from OS-decoded audio until `hg-aac` replaces the OS decoder there (MD-D3), and the privacy mode reports those formats unsupported to Web Audio.

**Device facts are normalised.** The default `AudioContext.sampleRate` is **48,000 Hz on every device**, not the device's preferred rate, and the audio service resamples; `renderSizeHint: "hardware"` returns 128 frames everywhere (Web Audio 1.1 flags "hardware" as a fingerprinting risk, [MP 5.2][MP]); `baseLatency` and `outputLatency` are reported in steps of 10 ms†; `playbackStats` updates at most once per second and only while the document is visible or holds microphone permission, as Web Audio 1.1 requires; Izcal applies the visibility condition only ([MP 5.6][MP]). Each value that comes from the device (latency, sink changes, device errors) is a recorded B1 input.

**Output devices.** `AudioContext.setSinkId()`, `HTMLMediaElement.setSinkId()` and `selectAudioOutput()` follow the Audio Output Devices API: device identifiers are per (site, container) and per origin, generated by the broker and cleared with site data; `selectAudioOutput()` needs transient activation and shows the broker's chooser; `setSinkId()` needs the `speaker-selection` permissions-policy feature ([MP 6.3][MP]); the audio service routes the stream; a sink change is a recorded input and fires `sinkchange`.

**HRTF data (MD-D15).** `PannerNode`'s HRTF model needs measured head-related impulse responses, and Web Audio does not define the data set. Whether any published measured set is available under MIT was not researched (flagged, 11.2). Options: (a) Izcal commissions its own measurement of a dummy head in an anechoic chamber and publishes the data under MIT; (b) an analytic spherical-head model (published as equations, no data); (c) a third-party data set, only if its licence is MIT. **Recommendation: (a), with (b) until (a) is delivered**; cost is a contracted acoustics lab plus about 0.25 engineer-years† (a placeholder). The data is part of FP-D13's data set.

**The resampler.** One arbitrary-ratio band-limited resampler (windowed-sinc interpolation from a filter table, Julius O. Smith's published method, [MP 9.12][MP]) in `hg-dsp`, used for device-rate conversion in the audio service, `AudioBufferSourceNode` playback-rate changes and media playback; quality targets (passband ripple, stopband attenuation, aliasing) are Izcal's own and published with a test set (MD-9b).

**The audio service and OS output.** Core Audio on macOS, WASAPI shared mode on Windows (with `IAudioClient3` small buffers only while an "interactive" context or a WebRTC call is active, because "low latency means higher power consumption", [MP 9.3][MP]), and PipeWire (falling back to PulseAudio's protocol, then ALSA) on Linux. Media playback uses about 10 ms† buffers; the audio service holds no device open when no stream plays. Whether WASAPI and PipeWire work from a sandboxed process is measured (MD-S3, [OS Q10][OS]). **OS callbacks and OS-created threads**: Core Audio IOProcs, WASAPI event threads, PipeWire loops, VideoToolbox and Media Foundation callbacks all run on threads the OS creates. Stage 1 G3 lets only `hg-substrate-os` touch threads and ambient OS effects, so `hg-substrate-os` gains two interfaces: a **real-time thread** request (which joins the OS mechanism above) and an **OS-callback bridge** that turns each OS callback into an event on a substrate sequence, recorded under B1; `hg-media-os`, `hg-audio-service` and `hg-capture-service` reach the OS only through them and are registered in the `unsafe` inventory with owners (coding-standard amendment). The graphics spec's presentation and timing callbacks use the same bridge, so the substrate interface has one owner (Stage 1) and two users ([RN2 Q17][RN2]).

**Voice processing (MD-D16).** WebRTC calls and `getUserMedia` with `echoCancellation`, `noiseSuppression` and `autoGainControl` need echo cancellation against what the speakers play. The audio service sees the microphone and Izcal's own mixed playout, so `hg-voice` (Izcal's own acoustic echo canceller, noise suppressor and gain control, written from published papers) runs there. Izcal's mix is not everything the speakers play: the protected-media host (WebView2, WKWebView), notifications and other applications play through the OS, which the OS voice-processing units cancel against. Options: (a) `hg-voice` everywhere, with a **system-loopback reference** where the OS grants one (WASAPI loopback capture on Windows, a system audio tap on macOS, the PipeWire monitor of the default sink on Linux), so it cancels against system output too; (b) the OS voice-processing units on macOS and Windows (macOS's voice-processing I/O unit, Windows' communications processing), which are OS interfaces M-1 allows, with `hg-voice` on Linux; (c) `hg-voice` against Izcal's mix only. **Recommendation: (a)**: one behaviour on every OS, testable in simulation, and Linux needs it anyway. Reversal: if `hg-voice` misses MD-11's thresholds, including double-talk with host and system audio playing, at MD-M2, then (b) on macOS and Windows while the gap closes.

### 6.9 Hardware decode and OS media frameworks (MD-D10)

**When.** Hardware decoders use under 9% of the energy of optimised software decoders, but Media Capabilities warns that hardware is not automatically efficient at low resolution ([MP 11.6, 4.5][MP]). Izcal uses hardware decode for media-element and MSE playback at or above 480p† when the device has it for that codec and profile, and software otherwise; WebCodecs follows its hint (6.7). The choice per playback is a recorded B1 input and is shown in DevTools.

**Where (MD-D10).** Options: (a) the media process opens a video-only device itself; (b) the GPU service submits decode work for every media process, so slice data reaches the driver inside the shared GPU service; (c) **a hardware-decode process per (site, container)** that holds the device or OS decoder session, runs no Izcal parser, and receives only typed picture parameters with slice data, or pre-validated access units, from the media process (6.1). **Recommendation: (c)**. (a) gives kernel-driver access to the process most likely to be compromised by input, against the security scope's strictest tier; (b) puts a driver bug triggered by slice data in the one process that holds every site's pixels; (c) keeps the parser at the strictest tier and confines the driver surface to one site. Cost: one more process per (site, container) while hardware decode is in use, with per-process device memory measured by MD-S4 against FP-38; one IPC hop per access unit. Reversal: if MD-S4 measures more than 8 MiB† charged per hardware-decode process, or an OS forbids a video-only device outside a GPU process, then (b) on that OS, where (b) now means the **content GPU process of the same renderer key** (graphics GR-D2), which holds only that key's data, re-validating every typed parameter against the specification's ranges. (c) stays the recommendation even though GR-D2 removed (b)'s shared-process objection: the content GPU process parses display lists and shaders from a possibly compromised renderer, and adding OS decoder grants to it would widen the sandbox of a parsing process, against P1 ([RN2 Q9][RN2]).

**How, per codec class.**

| Codec | Path | Process |
|---|---|---|
| AV1, VP9 | **Split decode**: the media process parses every header with its generated parser and produces typed picture parameters; a video-only device (Vulkan Video, D3D12 Video or VA-API decode queues, [MP 10.1, 10.2][MP], [OS 2.6, 3.5][OS]) reconstructs; decoded pictures stay in GPU memory and are exported as OS handles (dmabuf, DXGI shared handles) | Hardware-decode process |
| AV1 on macOS (M3 and later), VP9 on macOS 11 and later | VideoToolbox full decoder (macOS offers no low-level API); VP9 needs `VTRegisterSupplementalVideoDecoderIfAvailable` ([OS 1.4, 1.5][OS]); output as IOSurface-backed buffers | Hardware-decode process, with the grants MD-S1 records |
| H.264, HEVC; AAC and HE-AAC | OS full decoders only (VideoToolbox and AudioToolbox, Media Foundation transforms), never a low-level API with Izcal's parser (G57) | Hardware-decode process (for software-only OS audio decoders too, since they are OS code Izcal cannot patch), with the grants MD-S1 and MD-S2 record |

**Validate before the OS sees it (G57).** Every bitstream handed to an OS decoder or a device first passes Izcal's own generated header parser and the specification's ranges in the media process: H.264 SPS, PPS and slice headers, HEVC VPS, SPS and PPS, AAC AudioSpecificConfig, AV1 and VP9 headers. The AppleAVD bug, a range the specification bounds, would have been rejected ([SE 3.4][SE]). Eight of the 22 in-the-wild media bugs were in OS frameworks a browser cannot patch ([SE 1.6][SE]); validation and per-site confinement are what Izcal can do.

**Hardware output must not change web-observable pixels.** Exact-output codecs decoded by conformant hardware give the same YUV as software, but hardware decoders differ on corner cases and on film grain, which AV1 makes normative and which some hardware applies in a post-process or not at all. So: (1) when a codec and profile is first used on a device, the hardware path is checked **asynchronously** against Izcal's software decoder on a set of conformance streams including film-grain streams, with software decode used meanwhile, and the device lab repeats the check nightly (MD-42); a mismatch disables that path for that codec on that device and records why; (2) film grain is applied by Izcal (in software, or in the GPU service by a shader proved bit-identical to `hg-dsp`'s reference) whenever the hardware cannot match; (3) the hardware path records **frame hashes** while a recording is on, so a replay that substitutes the software decoder detects any mismatch and marks the replay "not exact" from that frame (6.13). Readback paths (6.5) use canonical conversion either way. **Device loss** follows HGBA-2 5.5: a lost or busy hardware decoder (VideoToolbox names "busy" as a reason, [OS 1.2][OS]) falls back to software at the next keyframe for royalty-free codecs, or reports a decode error for OS-only codecs, as a recorded decision.

### 6.10 Capture and WebRTC media (MD-D16, MD-D30)

**Capture.** `getUserMedia` and `getDisplayMedia` go through the broker's permission model and the OS pickers (6.1). A capture of a screen, another application's window or a browser window as a whole is the capture service's; a capture of an Izcal tab or element is composed by the graphics spec's compositor (GR 6.27); both deliver frames to the media process of the capturing key ([RN2 Q26][RN2]). Before any capture grant, `enumerateDevices` exposes at most one device of each kind with empty identifiers, the specification's two-bit floor ([OS 10.5][OS]). Captured frames are recorded in a B1 recording only by hash unless the user opts in to recording capture content (6.13).

**WebRTC media.** The networking spec owns ICE, DTLS, SRTP and SCTP in the partition service; "decoded media never enters it" ([NW 6.12][NW]). This spec owns everything above SRTP: the partition service hands decrypted RTP and RTCP packets to the media process of the page's (site, container) through a typed endpoint; the media process depacketizes (RTP payload formats for VP8, VP9, AV1, H.264 and Opus, each a generated parser), runs the **jitter buffers** (audio with time-stretching concealment, video with frame reordering and keyframe requests), decodes, and sends frames to the GPU service and PCM to the audio service. Sending runs the reverse path: capture service → media process (encode, packetize, FEC and retransmission) → partition service. Encoded transforms (`RTCRtpScriptTransform`) route encoded frames to the page's worker and back. WebRTC statistics (`jitterBufferDelay`, `concealedSamples`, `freezeCount` with its published definition, [MP 8.7][MP]) are computed from recorded packet arrival times, so they replay. `decoderImplementation` and `powerEfficientDecoder` are exposed only as the policy table of MD-D11 allows. While the page is hidden, received video is not decoded (the call stays live, a declared reason in FP 6.8, and audio continues); on return the media process requests a keyframe, as a recorded decision.

**Codecs for WebRTC (MD-D30).** Izcal's own VP8 (mandatory), VP9 and AV1 real-time encoders and decoders, Opus (mandatory) and G.711 (mandatory); H.264 Constrained Baseline through OS encoders and decoders on macOS and Windows and through Izcal's own on Linux (MD-D3). Stage 1 M7's requirement of post-quantum DTLS is the networking spec's.

### 6.11 EME and DRM (D-S7 restated)

D-S7 is unchanged and this spec only places its parts. **Clear Key** (`org.w3.clearkey`, which every EME implementation must support, [MP 12.2][MP]) is Izcal's own: the renderer runs EME's session algorithms from the EME 2 working draft; licence messages are JSON parsed by a generated parser in the media process; decryption (`cenc` AES-CTR, `cbcs` AES-CBC pattern) uses `hg-crypto` in the media process, so keys never enter the renderer. **Native PlayReady on Windows** (M7): EME sessions are relayed to the CDM process per (site, container) (SEC-33), which hosts `IMFContentDecryptionModule` (Windows 10 2004 and later); the encrypted samples go from the media process to the Windows Protected Media Path, and Izcal receives only a presentable surface: "The application ... does not have access to content after it is passed to the PMP" ([OS 6.1, 6.2][OS]). HDCP is enforced by the OS ([OS 6.3][OS]); EME 2's `getStatusForPolicy` HDCP answer is a recorded input reported in PRIV-2. **Protected media mode** stays the fallback on Windows and macOS; the DRM-1 and DRM-2 spikes found WebView2 granting Widevine at software level only, PlayReady up to SL3000, and WKWebView granting FairPlay ([OS 7.1, 7.2][OS]); the signed-in playback tests D-S7 still lacks are listed as MD-S5. Linux remains Clear Key only. Protected frames are never readable, never recorded and never shown in DevTools; a recording marks them as opaque spans.

**What `requestMediaKeySystemAccess` answers (MD-D32).** D-S7 switches a tab to the protected-media host only on actual encrypted playback, never on a capability probe. But a site that probes for `com.widevine.alpha` first and sees "unsupported" may never create `MediaKeys`, so the switch would never fire. Answers come from the policy table per OS family (6.15): Clear Key everywhere; PlayReady on Windows once the native path ships; every other key system unsupported, truthfully. Options for sites that stop at the probe: (a) a **user-initiated affordance**: when the top-level document probes a key system Izcal lacks and then attaches media (a `src`, MSE or a `<video>` that fails to start) without generating a licence request, the shell offers "This site may need protected playback: open in Windows' (or macOS's) media engine", and switches only when the user clicks; (b) answer "supported" for key systems the host supports, so the site proceeds and the real licence request triggers the switch; (c) nothing. **Recommendation: (a)**, decided with the shell owner: (b) would make every capability probe report the host's key systems, a fingerprinting surface and a false answer in Izcal's own engine; (a) keeps D-S7's "never on a probe" rule for automatic switching and costs one shell affordance. Reversal: if the deferred playback tests (MD-S5) show that most services stop at the probe and users do not find the affordance, revisit (b) for the top-level document only.

### 6.12 Captions, Picture-in-Picture, Media Session and autoplay (MD-D13, MD-D25)

**WebVTT.** The WebVTT file is parsed by a generated parser in the media process (G50); the renderer receives typed cues (timing, settings, a typed cue-text tree) and creates `VTTCue` objects and cue boxes, laid out by the layout spec's rules and styled with `::cue`. Cue `enter`, `exit` and `cuechange` follow HTML's "time marches on" on the media clock ([MP 6.7][MP]). **Generated captions and image descriptions (MD-D25, revision 0.4.2).** Generated captions and image descriptions are **behind the accessibility model grant** (B10's separate setting, ML-D4), off by default, opt-in, computed on device by the ML service (ML-D5), marked as generated, never sent off the device and never exposed to the page. They are not AI-switch features and are not governed by B10's AI surface rules; the grant, its model set S_A and its deletion rule are the ML service spec's.

**OS caption preferences** (font, size, colours, background) are applied by default to captions the page has not styled, as Chrome, Safari and Edge do and Firefox does not ([GO 4.1][GO]); they are applied in rendering and are not observable through `getComputedStyle` on cue content, so they add no fingerprinting bits (the user-preference trade-off of R0 accessibility preferences, D-S1, is respected by not altering them). **Text descriptions** (`kind=descriptions`) are voiced through the semantic plane when the user enables them (B8). **Generated captions and image descriptions** (MD-D25) are AI features: off by default (B10), opt-in, computed on device by the ML service, marked as generated in the semantic plane's repair overlay, never sent off the device and never exposed to the page; Chrome's server-side image descriptions are the design Izcal does not take ([GO 6.1][GO]).

**Picture-in-Picture.** Video PiP is a shell-owned OS window whose content is a GPU-service surface of the element's frames. **Document PiP** is different: a new top-level browsing context in the opener's renderer, in its own shell-owned window with the restrictions of the Document Picture-in-Picture report (transient activation, one window, closes with its opener, no navigation, [MP 6.6][MP]). Window sizes are OS facts reported as recorded inputs. A playing PiP video is "output the user perceives", a declared reason in FP 6.8.

**Native controls and fullscreen.** `<video controls>` and `<audio controls>` get a user-agent shadow tree built by `hg-media` and styled by the UA sheet: play and pause, seek with keyboard steps, volume and mute, the captions and audio-track menu, playback rate, Picture-in-Picture and fullscreen. Every control is exposed through the semantic plane (B8) with roles and names, reachable by keyboard and screen reader, follows forced-colours and reduced-motion preferences, and is tested by MD-12. The Fullscreen API's element fullscreen promotes a video layer to direct scanout where the OS allows (6.16), and the exit affordance and its security message are the shell's (the shell UI design owns their look).

**Media Session** metadata and actions reach the OS media controls through the broker; artwork images are decoded in the media process of the page's (site, container) before the broker passes the pixels on.

**Autoplay (MD-D13).** Chrome's Media Engagement Index depends on the user's history with each origin and has an unpublished threshold; Safari uses an inference engine; Firefox a gesture rule ([MP 7.1–7.5][MP]). Izcal's rule is **stateless**: a media element or `AudioContext` may start audible playback only with transient user activation in the document (or delegated by permissions policy from the top-level document), or when the user has set "allow audio and video" for the site in the site panel; muted or audio-less media may autoplay; a `video` autoplaying through its `autoplay` attribute is paused when it stops intersecting the viewport (HTML, [MP 1.9][MP]), and Izcal extends the same pause to muted media started by `play()` as its own UA policy; an `AudioContext` created without activation starts `suspended`. `navigator.getAutoplayPolicy()` reports the result. The decision is a pure function of recorded inputs, the user's setting is engine configuration recorded in a recording, and DevTools names the rule that decided. No engagement history is stored.

### 6.13 Determinism by construction (MD-D12)

DET-1 excludes "GPU-dependent output, media and audio pipelines" today ([H3][H3]); Stage 1 Section 3 calls media out of scope for simulation in Stage A. This spec brings the software media path into scope.

| Part | Deterministic how |
|---|---|
| Header parsing, demuxing, Izcal's software decode and encode, colour conversion, Web Audio rendering, resampling, time-stretching | Pure functions of input bytes and canonical parameters, bit-identical across OS, ISA and thread count (G55, MD-40) |
| Arrival of bytes | Network recording (NW-D2) joined to the renderer's data-pipe reads (reconciliation P20) |
| Decode completion, cache hits, header results, frame selection, hardware-or-software choice, autoplay decisions, MSE evictions under ManagedMediaSource, WebCodecs output timing | Decision points (JS-D15), logged when recording; per-frame entries (frame selection, cache hits) are batched per composited frame, and their log volume per minute of 1080p60 playback is measured at MD-M2 as media's share of **one** whole-renderer recording budget (HGBA-3 DET-1, JS-52: ≤ 1%† of p95 frame time), measured jointly with the engine, layout and graphics ([RN2 Q15][RN2]) |
| Audio device clock, output latency, vsync timestamps, sink changes, PiP window size, HDCP status | Recorded B1 inputs |
| Hardware decoder output | Frame hashes recorded on the hardware path. Exact-output codecs: replay uses Izcal's software decoder and marks the replay "not exact" from the first frame whose hash differs. OS-only codecs (H.264, HEVC and AAC on macOS and Windows): replay needs the same OS decoder, or uses `hg-h264` and `hg-aac` in **test-only replay builds** that never ship (subject to the legal review, Q-MD-3), or runs marked "not exact", as the fonts spec does for OS fonts (TX-D18) |
| Captured camera, microphone and screen content | Hashes only by default; full content only if the user opts in for that recording; otherwise replay substitutes recorded-shape synthetic frames, marked "not exact" |
| Protected (DRM) frames | Never recorded; opaque spans |
| WebRTC media payloads | Not recorded (NW 6.12 records only sizes and timing); replay of received media marked "not exact" |

**Simulation mode** runs a virtual audio device whose render callbacks are driven by the substrate's virtual clock, a virtual display clock, and software decoders only. A recorded playback then replays with identical JS-observable media events, identical frame hashes and identical audio hashes (MD-41), which the tools that today abandon `<video>` for being non-deterministic cannot do ([GO 7.3][GO]).

### 6.14 Security

**Threat model.** ATK-1 (malicious content) and ATK-3 (hostile input to a privileged parser) through every image, media, colour-profile, caption and playlist byte; peers in WebRTC; licence servers through EME; OS media frameworks Izcal cannot patch. The record's bug classes and Izcal's answer to each:

| Class (record) | Izcal's mechanism | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Size or count from the file trusted (5 of 5 published root causes, [SE 4.1][SE]) | Generated `.hgbits` parsers with specification ranges and cross-field constraints; allocation only from validated headers (G51, G53) | MD-21, MD-23 |
| Entropy table built before validation (libwebp) | Separate validating pass, proved capacities (G52) | MD-21 |
| Integer overflow in size arithmetic | Checked arithmetic in every L2m crate (tier P lints) | MD-23 |
| Races between script and real-time threads (Web Audio) | Ownership transfer only; render state not `Send` (6.8) | MD-22 thread-interleaving fuzzer |
| State across reconfiguration (WebRTC, MediaCodec, encoders) | Generated state machines for every codec and session object | MD-22 control-sequence fuzzer |
| Format sniffing reaching unexpected decoders (FORCEDENTRY) | One decoder per format chosen by the MIME Sniffing standard's rules; no fall-through to "any codec" | MD-20 |
| Panics, OOM, stack overflow, infinite loops (Rust media advisories) | No panic paths, budgets, no recursion over input, fuel (G53) | MD-23 |
| Unsound `unsafe` (the one Rust near-miss, CrabbyAVIF) | `unsafe` only in `hg-simd`'s dispatch and load and store wrappers (L2s), each block inventoried, index arithmetic proved by Kani; kernels safe Rust over its API, with the scalar differential as the main oracle (G54) | MD-25 |
| Decompression and CPU bombs | Canonical limits (6.3) | MD-24 |
| Bugs in OS media frameworks (8 of 22) | Validate before the OS sees it; OS decoders only in the per-site hardware-decode process (G57) | MD-20 |
| Driver bugs from slice data, and kernel-driver access from a compromised parser | Devices and OS decoder sessions only in the hardware-decode process, which parses nothing; the media process stays at the strictest tier (MD-D10) | MD-S1, MD-S2, MD-S4 |
| A peer rewriting shared bytes during parsing (double fetch) | Sealed read-only regions or a private copy before any parse (6.1, G51) | MD-22's adversarial-peer case |
| Brute-forcing an exploit by repeated crashes | Restart throttling (6.1) | MD-26 |
| Cross-origin pixel leaks through timing (Stone 2013, GPU.zip) | Opaque pixels never in the renderer (G56); the graphics spec keeps content-dependent filter costs off opaque surfaces (amendment) | MD-31 |
| ORB's subsequent range requests | The "subsequent range" marker set only by the network partition service, never by the renderer, as ORB's explainer warns ([SE 11.4][SE]) | NW amendment |

**Fuzzing** (MD-22): one structure-aware target per generated parser, generated from its description; one per decoder that **builds valid intermediate structures and then one invalid one** (the libwebp chain, [SE 2.2][SE]) rather than flipping bits; a control-sequence target for the media element, MSE and WebCodecs; a thread-interleaving target for the audio graph under the substrate's deterministic scheduler; differential targets against conformance streams and black-box oracles; and a regression corpus of every root cause in the record. Fuzzing is necessary but not the assurance; the proofs and the review are (MD-25's external audit).

**A "lockdown" profile.** HGBA-2's Hardened profile (S-4a) gains a media setting that disables JPEG XL, BMP, Web Audio and WebCodecs and limits video to AV1 and VP9 software decode, as Lockdown Mode drops formats for high-risk users ([SE 9.5, 9.6][SE]).

The cost of MD-D11 falls on devices that do have newer hardware: a device with AV1 hardware is told `powerEfficient: false` for AV1 when other devices of its OS family lack it, so a site may choose VP9 or H.264 and spend more bandwidth. The reversal trigger is measured on those devices: if, on the corpus's adaptive-streaming sites, the policy answers raise delivered bytes by more than 20%† or energy by more than 5%† on reference devices **with** AV1 hardware, a coarse hardware class per OS family ("AV1 hardware decode: yes or no", one bit counted in PRIV-2) replaces the single answer for `powerEfficient` only.

### 6.16 Energy and footprint (MD-D23, MD-D24)

**There is no playback energy budget in FP today** ([FR][FR]). Published numbers show why there should be: for the same stream on identical Surface Books, Edge ran 7 h 22 min and Chrome 4 h 20 min in Microsoft's 2016 test (in its mixed browsing lab test Edge drew 2.07 W and Chrome 2.82 W); Firefox's move to Core Animation cut 720p60 YouTube from 28.4 W to 16.4 W; Apple claims 5 more hours of 1080p streaming for Safari than Chrome on one MacBook Air; community measurements on Linux put browsers 1–2 W above mpv ([MP 11.1–11.4][MP], [GO 9.1–9.5][GO]).

**Metric (MD-D24).** FP's owner-accepted rule is that memory and energy thresholds are **absolute**, with comparisons published as information, never as the threshold (FP Section 8). MD-54 follows it: whole-system energy above the idle baseline, per minute of playback, per (clip class, device, decode path), measured with FP 6.13's platform meters under an exclusive rig booking. The absolute baseline per (clip class, device, path) is set at MD-M2 from measurement†, and from then on a release regresses if its mean exceeds the previous release's by more than the 95% confidence interval of run-to-run noise, exactly as FP-22 gates energy. The **ratio to the OS's own player** playing the same file (QuickTime Player on macOS, Media Player on Windows; on Linux mpv, like with like: hardware path against mpv's hardware path, never Izcal's software H.264 against it) is published beside it as information and used as a **diagnostic**: a ratio above 1.10† names the presentation or decode path as the place to look, without being a threshold.

**Mechanisms.**

| Mechanism | Owner |
|---|---|
| Hardware decode at ≥ 480p† where present (6.9) | This spec |
| Overlay and direct-scanout promotion of video layers (macOS CALayer, Windows multiplane overlays, Wayland subsurfaces with dmabuf), zero copies from decoder to scanout | Graphics spec (GR-D26), staffed there; measured by this spec's MD-54 and MD-S6, which replace the graphics spec's GR-41 ([RN2 Q27][RN2]), because no primary published figure exists ([MP 14 Q2][MP]) |
| Composites only when a new frame is due; no main-thread work per frame (frame selection in the GPU service) | This spec and graphics |
| Burst fetching: ManagedMediaSource and native HLS buffer to 30 s† then idle the radio (6.7) | This spec |
| 10 ms† audio buffers for media; small buffers only for interactive contexts and calls | This spec |
| **Hidden tabs (MD-D23)**: a hidden page playing audible video keeps decoding audio and **suspends video decode** (resumed at the next keyframe when shown, as a recorded decision); hidden WebRTC receive video is not decoded (6.10); hidden muted media is **suspended without changing `paused`** and without firing events (a decision point), and the page is frozen under FP-D4; offscreen muted autoplay is paused (6.12) | This spec, amending FP 6.8's "media pipeline as needed" |
| Decoder and media-process wakeups aligned to frame and audio periods; no timers in a paused pipeline | This spec, FP-18 |

**Footprint.** The media process: ≤ 2 MiB† idle (FP-38). While decoding, each video stream is charged from the codec's reference model, not a flat cap: AV1 and VP9 keep 8 reference frames, so a stream's frame storage is (8 references + 1 being decoded + F frames in flight for frame threading) × the frame's bytes at its coded size and bit depth (at 1080p 4:2:0, 1920 × 1080 × 1.5 B ≈ 2.97 MiB per 8-bit frame and twice that at 10-bit; at 4K, ≈ 11.9 MiB per 8-bit frame), plus a fixed per-stream overhead† measured at MD-M0 (MD-53). Frames on the hardware path are GPU memory, charged to the GPU ledger's "video" kind (FP-14) and to the hardware-decode process's site. MSE buffers are charged on their own line. The audio service: ≤ 4 MiB† idle with a stream open, 0 when exited. The capture service: ≤ 6 MiB† plus frame buffers while capturing. The **CDM process** (Windows, only during EME playback) hosts Microsoft's PlayReady code, which Izcal cannot size: its memory is reported as an attributed OS-code line, as the fonts reconciliation did for third-party IME DLLs (P13), and measured by MD-S5 ([RN2 Section 3][RN2]). Class W images take 8 B/px, so FP-13's "4 B per pixel" needs a W-class exception, and intrinsic-size decodes for readback a readback exception (amendments). Codec tables and the HRTF data join FP-D13's data set (FP-25).

### 6.18 Crate layout and rules

A new sub-layer **L2m** (media formats and codecs), a sibling of L2e, L2p and L2t: L2m crates depend only on L1, L2, L2s (`hg-compress`, `hg-math`, `hg-crypto`, `hg-colour`) and other L2m crates, never on L2e, L2p or L2t; they are sans-IO and reach threads only through the substrate. `hg-colour` is in L2s because both GPU process kinds (tier P), canvas and the media process need one pixel contract; `hg-simd` moves to L2s for the same reason, shared with the graphics spec's `hg-raster` ([RN2 Q18][RN2]). Clear Key needs AES-CTR and the `cbcs` AES-CBC pattern from `hg-crypto`, which NW owns under G36 (amendment).

| Layer | Crate | Scope | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| tools | `hg-bitgen` | `.hgbits` → parser, fuzzer, serializer, Kani harnesses, anchors; build-time generation of formula-defined tables | T |
| L2s | `hg-colour` | Descriptors, transfer functions, matrices, tetrahedral CLUT evaluation, image gamut mapping, EETF, gain-map maths, minimal profile writer | P |
| L2m | `hg-bits` | Generated-parser runtime: bit reader over sealed or private memory, budget handles, fuel | L2m profile |
| L2m | `hg-entropy` | Validated Huffman, LZW, range, boolean, multi-symbol, CABAC and ANS decoders | L2m profile |
| L2m | `hg-dsp` | Transforms (DCT, ADST, MDCT), loop filters, interpolation, FFT, resampler, time-stretch, scalar reference kernels with canonical reduction order, and the per-ISA SIMD kernels over `hg-simd`'s safe API | L2m profile |
| L2s | `hg-simd` | Per-ISA dispatch and slice-based SIMD load and store wrappers with a safe API, used by `hg-dsp` (L2m) and the graphics spec's `hg-raster` (L2g); the only `unsafe` in either spec's SIMD code, every block inventoried (RN2 Q18) | P, audited |

## 7. M-1 compliance

**Built new, by Izcal, from the specifications.** Everything in Section 6.18: the `.hgbits` language and `hg-bitgen`; every container, header, colour-profile, metadata, playlist, caption and RTP-payload parser generated from it; the entropy decoders; every image, audio and video decoder and encoder named in 6.2 and 6.4; the transforms, filters, FFT, resampler and time-stretcher; the SIMD kernels; the colour pipeline, the CMM contract, tone mapping and gain-map application; the media element, MSE, ManagedMediaSource, HLS, WebCodecs, MediaRecorder and EME algorithms; the Web Audio graph; the audio service, the voice-processing chain and the capture service; the jitter buffers; the decode scheduler and caches; the HRTF data (MD-D15). No code, design template or internal API shape is taken from libpng, libjpeg or libjpeg-turbo, giflib, libwebp, libavif, libjxl or jxl-rs, dav1d or rav1d, libaom, libgav1, libvpx, OpenH264, libopus, libvorbis, libFLAC, FFmpeg, GStreamer, Skia, Wuffs, image-rs, zune, Symphonia, cubeb, Little CMS, qcms, moxcms, CrabbyAvif, WebRTC's or any browser's media code. They appear here only as published evidence (M-1 part 3).

**M-1 part 3, checked where the design resembles existing systems.** Several choices match what others converged on: decoders in a sandboxed process, the audio clock as master, a shared audio output service, hardware decode for playback, frame selection at composition, canonical working colour spaces in linear FP16. Each is derived here from Izcal's own requirements (I-1, I-2, B1, FP budgets) and cited as evidence that it works, from prose: Android's and Apple's published hardening posts, Microsoft's documentation, Mozilla's wiki and Chromium's prose design documents. Prose pages hosted in source repositories are a grey zone and are listed here: Chromium's audio-service README and Rule of 2 ([SE 9.8][SE], [MP 9.11][MP]) carry no value into this spec (placement evidence only); the ORB explainer supplies the subsequent-range marker rule; the Compact-ICC-Profiles README supplies the 0.5 KB profile size; WebRTC's NetEq design document is not used; the Wuffs and servo/media READMEs are no longer the source of any claim (Chrome's Wuffs GIF decoder is cited from Microsoft's prose post, [SE 6.4][SE], and Servo's GStreamer use from its developer's blog, [HP 8.5][HP]); the rav1d-safe crate page's 1.56× figure is Low confidence and only motivates spike MD-S7, never a threshold. No constant here was read from a codec library: limits, buffer sizes and thresholds are argued from first principles (†) or from specifications.

**Dependencies used unmodified: generic infrastructure, every one available under MIT** (HGBA-2 2.3.2 M-1 part 1; [licence audit][LA] class 1): the Rust standard library (MIT OR Apache-2.0), including `core::arch` intrinsics; `smallvec`, `bitflags`, `thiserror`, `hashbrown` and `rustc-hash` (each MIT OR Apache-2.0), with `hashbrown` taken with `default-features = false` so `foldhash` (Zlib) never enters the graph; `serde` and `serde_json` only in `hg-bitgen`. **OS interfaces** (M-1 part 1's exception; licence audit class 5): VideoToolbox, AudioToolbox, AVFoundation's content-key and capture APIs, Core Audio, ScreenCaptureKit and IOSurface on macOS; Media Foundation transforms and `IMFContentDecryptionModule`, Direct3D 11 and 12 video, WASAPI, Windows.Graphics.Capture and DXGI shared handles on Windows; Vulkan Video, VA-API, V4L2, PipeWire, the PulseAudio protocol, ALSA, xdg-desktop-portal and dmabuf on Linux; Android MediaCodec in Stage 2; the WebView2 and WKWebView runtimes and the OS DRM modules (D-S7). MIT licenses Izcal's copyright in its code; the AOM licence grants patent rights in AV1; neither alters the other. **Not shipped**: any ICC-authored profile, any codec library, any third-party HRTF set, any OS codec package.

## 9. Milestones and staffing

### 9.1 What is staged, and why

The owner allows staging only for correctness, security or measurement. The rule is the JS and fonts specs': **design everything from the start, build in parallel, ship each part when its gates pass.**
