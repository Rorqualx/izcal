**The problem.** The record already promises on-device image descriptions and captions (MD-D25), model-based accessibility repairs (B10's R4), OCR for image-only PDFs (the PDF spec), and an AI switch whose "off" is defined by what does not happen (B10, AG-0). It owns none of the things those promises require: no process, no runtime, no models, no gate semantics, no size or energy budget, and no licence position for model weights. Ledger gap G-15 records the hole; MD 9.3 counts the caption and description models as "not counted here"; the Stage 1 process map has an ML service row that no spec defines. The gap is not paperwork. A browser that loads a model is running a dependency whose training data, licence, numerics, failure modes and memory behaviour are all new attack and trust surfaces, and every one of them is invisible in today's engines.

**The central idea: a model is a licensed data set executed by Izcal's own arithmetic.** Izcal ships no third-party inference framework, and its three Stage 1 weight sets are its own. The runtime — graph IR, kernels, quantisation, tokenizer, scheduler — is written from published papers and Izcal's measurements (M-1 part 1). The weights are trained by Izcal on commissioned or MIT-licensed data, released under MIT, and accompanied by a per-data-set ledger (ML-D3). A model arrives in a declarative, signed container that executes no code (ML-D7). Inference runs in one sandboxed service process with no network capability, launched only by a settings grant and exited when idle (ML-D5). Every inference output is recorded as a value, so replay never runs a model and non-deterministic fast paths cannot make a recording diverge (ML-D8). The second idea is that **the AI switch is not the accessibility switch**: B10 already separates the accessibility model's opt-in from the AI switch, and this spec makes the separation exact — two grants, each with its own model set, neither enabling the other (ML-D4). The third is that **a small model is enough for Stage 1**: the shipping proof is a 182M-parameter encoder-decoder under 200 MB that produces usable alt text on a laptop CPU ([MA 1.14][MA]), not a multi-billion-parameter vision-language model.

**The decisions that matter most:** (1) **ML-D1** — this spec owns the ML service, the runtime, the Stage 1 models and the gate semantics; ledger G-15 closes; HGBA-2 4.5 gains a replacement anchor to this spec; (2) **ML-D3** — Izcal's policy is to train and ship its own weights for all three Stage 1 models, MIT-licensed, with a D0–D5 data ledger; third-party MIT weight sets (Florence-2, Donut, Moonshine) remain a fallback only if the owner accepts an undocumented training-data chain, and non-MIT sets cannot ship at all — Firefox's Apache-2.0 model is out and Whisper's Apache/MIT conflict keeps it out ([DL 2.1–2.21][DL], [MA 6.1, 6.19][MA]); (3) **ML-D4** — two independent grants, AI and accessibility, with B10's off-state semantics made testable per grant; (4) **ML-D5** — one shared, sandboxed, network-less service, built always for the accessibility path, with generative task modules behind the `ai` feature, an explicit G4 exception, and the fourth I-1 shared-process exception the HGBA-2 owner must grant (conditional, like OD-8); (5) **ML-D6** — two numeric modes: a canonical reproducible mode and a fast mode, with integer kernels exact by construction and float order fixed where reproducibility is claimed; (6) **ML-D8** — outputs are recorded values and replay never invokes the runtime; (7) **ML-D9** — CPU-first, no NPU in Stage 1, and the NPU admitted later only where R-3's 1.5× end-to-end gate is measured per task and device. The work is **14–20.5† engineers plus 0.5–1† non-engineering** over **24–36 months†** of the Stage 1 window (30–36† for all three models; Section 9.3), additive to the media, accessibility and PDF tracks.

## 1. The problem and who has it

| User | Task, in their words | What fails today |
|---|---|---|
| **Screen-reader users** | "Tell me what this image is; tell me whether this description came from the page or from a machine" | 53.1% of the 2026 WebAIM Million's home pages are missing alt text and 16.2% of home-page images have none; counting questionable and repetitive text, more than one in four images fails ([MA 1.26][MA], [GO 1.1][GO]); the shipping machine descriptions that exist are terse, omit provenance and aberrations, and are heard as a single run-on sentence ([MA 1.24–1.25][MA]) |
| **People reading image-only PDFs** | "Read this scanned page to me, in reading order" | An image-only page has no text layer; Google estimates 12% of web PDFs are image-only, and a 2023 Luxembourg sample found 52% untagged ([GO 1.5][GO]); without on-device OCR the content is unavailable to assistive technology, and server-side OCR sends the document off the device (the PDF spec's rule) |
| **Deaf and hard-of-hearing viewers** | "Caption this video; it has no captions" | 430 million people have disabling hearing loss ([GO 1.2][GO]); captions depend on the page author, and live captioning in other browsers is a separate, undocumented on-device model with no provenance, no language statement and no recording or privacy contract |
| **Privacy-conscious people** | "Nothing I read or watch should leave this machine, and nothing should be downloaded behind my back" | Chrome's server-side descriptions and its silent multi-gigabyte model download are the two failure modes in this class; no engine publishes what models it has, their sizes, or what a switch actually disables ([GO 2.1–2.8][GO]) |
| **People who turn AI off** | "Off should mean off — no model process, no model file, no download, no AI UI" | B10 defines this and AG-0 tests it, but no engine defines a second accessibility model's boundary or proves the two settings are independent |
| **Browser engineers and reviewers** | "One runtime, one container format, one loader, three ISAs, auditable" | Shipping an inference framework (ONNX Runtime, OpenVINO, TFLite, Core ML, Windows ML) would add an unauditable binary dependency to the most privileged new process; their model parsers are a live CVE source ([IK 7.7][IK]) |
| **Izcal itself** | "A licence position for weights and data that survives an audit" | Model repositories mix code and weight licences; the same model can be MIT in one field and Apache-2.0 in another ([MA 3.7][MA]); training data licences are usually undocumented, so an MIT weight file proves nothing about the data behind it |

**What Izcal's specs already decide** (each item links to its source; Section 9.5 proposes the amendments):

- **[M-1][H2] (2.3.3).** Web-visible and platform behaviour is implemented from specs and published papers with anchors; no third-party project's source code is evidence; dependencies are generic infrastructure, MIT-only; correctness comes from vectors, differential fuzzing against black-box peers, and measurement.
- **MIT only (2.3.2).** Every shipped dependency and data set, model weights included, is MIT; anything else Izcal builds or trains itself. The licence audit's classes 1–5 are extended by class 6 in Section 9.5 for weights and their data.
- **[B10][H3].** AI is off by default: no agent or model processes, no models, no AI network traffic, no agent computation, inert web surface, no AI entry points, off survives updates. A security classifier under 50 MB may ship in the package with an ML-inventory entry. R4 model-based repairs sit behind a **separate accessibility setting**, off by default, downloading a local model at that moment; turning on accessibility enables no agent features and turning on AI does not enable the accessibility model.
- **[AG-0][H3].** With default settings: zero agent or model-host processes, zero model files outside the published inventory, zero AI endpoints, no AI UI, within 1%† of a build with the agent crates compiled out, over a 24-hour† idle period and a fresh-install and upgrade scenario.
- **[MD-D25][MD].** Generated captions and image descriptions are on-device, opt-in, marked generated, never sent off the device and never exposed to the page; if the size or quality bound cannot be met, the feature is **not offered**, never server-side. MD 6.12 says the ML service computes them; speech recognition is "an ML service feature under B10".
- **[PDF][PD].** Image-only pages (about 12% of web PDFs by Google's estimate) need OCR; the page raster comes from the content GPU process and recognised text runs come back; labelled as recognised; on-device, under the accessibility-model setting; "whose model the future ML spec owns (G-15, the ML service, extended here to OCR)".
- **[AX] R4 and Q-10.** B10's R4 repairs are recorded in the repair overlay as machine-generated and disclosed by the Q-10 mechanism (suffix, object attribute, or both, chosen by A11Y-3, [AX 6.10][AX]); the models for R4 and MD-D25 are counted by the ML spec, not by the accessibility track ([AX 9.3][AX]).
- **[ED-D9][ED].** Grammar is never a learned model unless AI is on and the person chooses it; **[AN-D12][AN]** compiles agent-facing parts only with feature `ai`.
- **[DM-D18][DM].** Observability has three cost classes: always counters, DevTools-attached journals, and recording-derivable facts.
- **[DT-D1, DT-D2][DT].** IIP domains are generated from `.hgidl`; recordings are off by default, user-initiated, start at process launch in the deterministic configuration, and exclude audio and GPU state.
- **[FP][FP].** Process kinds and threads are budgeted; clean file-backed pages are shared and uncharged; FP-25 bounds the installer; FP-22 is an absolute energy gate; FP-D13 forbids page-triggered downloads of web-observable data.
- **Stage 1 [1, 2.1][S1].** The ML service is 0 or 1 processes, launched "only if the AI switch or the accessibility-model setting is on"; `hg-ml-main` is listed as built only with feature `ai`; G4 forbids default-feature `hg-*-main` binaries from reaching `hg-ml*`; G9's anchor list does not yet include an ML crate.

**What success means, measurably.** Descriptions that a screen-reader user judges adequate as often as Firefox's shipping model, with a false-description rate under 5%† (ML-1, ML-2); OCR character error at or below the best shipping engine's same-lab rate on the document corpus (ML-3); captions at or below the best shipping on-device recogniser's word error plus 1 point† (ML-4); bit-identical outputs in canonical mode across x86-64, AArch64 and riscv64 (ML-5); 1000 of 1000 recordings replayed without loading a model (ML-7); zero network capability in the service (ML-8); both grants off reproducing AG-0's conditions with model files included (ML-9); zero bytes of user-downloadable weights in the installer (ML-16); no commissioned canary recoverable from the weights (ML-23); and every shipped weight and data set carrying an MIT-compatible licence with a ledger row (ML-20).

### 2.2 Technology mismatches: assumptions that no longer hold

- **"ML means a server."** Small models now run at useful quality on a laptop CPU: the shipping proof is 182M parameters and under 200 MB ([MA 1.14][MA]). Sending images, documents or audio off the device is a choice, not a requirement.
- **"A model is a file like any other."** A model file is a parser input with cross-field invariants, an attack surface with a CVE history, and a licence whose provenance is its training data ([IK 7.7][IK], [MA §6][MA]). Treating it as opaque bytes is the vulnerability.
- **"The NPU is the fast path."** Vendor TOPS do not predict latency; fixed dispatch cost makes small per-token work slower on the NPU, and energy wins depend on host polling ([IK 6.15–6.16][IK]). The CPU is the baseline every accelerator must beat end to end.
- **"Quantisation is free."** int8 with per-channel weights is close to floating point; 4-bit post-training collapses on small CNNs and 3-bit is not viable at 125–560M ([IK 1.9, 4.9][IK]). Compression choices are per-model measurements.
- **"IEEE 754 gives reproducibility."** It fixes each operation, not the reduction order; reproducibility is an opt-in attribute, and published reproducible-summation costs range from 1.2× to 8× on a dot product, with the high end coming from indexed-accumulator arithmetic and the low end from a same-core parallel run ([IK 1.3, 1.14, 1.16][IK]). A deterministic contract must be designed and paid for.
- **"Off is a UI state."** B10 already defines off by what does not happen; the engineering consequence is that the model host, the model files, the download path and the runtime must all be absent or gated, and each gate must be testable on its own.

## 3. Today: the landscape and its gaps

**What ships.** Chrome ships generative task APIs backed by a downloaded model, with a documented hardware floor (more than 4 GB VRAM, or 16 GB RAM and four cores, plus 22 GB free profile storage), desktop-only and five-language, and with NPU behaviour that is not documented ([H2 4.5][H2], [HP 3.6–3.7][HP]). Edge shipped the same class of API on a downloaded model. Chrome's PDF viewer and Reading Mode use an on-device OCR component; Google's own ChromeOS.dev page states 77 languages and seven additional scripts, but publishes no per-language error rate ([GO 2.4, 6.1][GO], [MA 2.3, 7.1][MA]). Chrome Live Caption runs on-device with downloadable language packs. Firefox ships local alt-text generation in its PDF editor and describes the output as a first draft the user validates ([MA 1.14][MA]). Windows 11 Live Captions has run on-device since the 2022 update, with language packs and up to five installed at once ([HP 1.8–1.9][HP]). Safari exposes Apple Intelligence features through OS frameworks whose placement is undocumented ([IK 6.3][IK]).

**Two recent retractions matter.** Edge launched its Prompt API on a ~3.8B Phi-4-mini with a reported ~5.5 GB VRAM and ~20 GB disk floor, then replaced it about a year later with a smaller model chosen for CPU reach ([HP 6.3–6.6][HP]). WebNN is a Candidate Recommendation Draft dated 10 October 2026, still requiring two independent implementations; Chrome reached origin trial in 2026 and Safari and Firefox do not implement it ([HP 9.2–9.3][HP]).

**The runtimes.** The shippable inference stacks are ONNX Runtime (MIT), OpenVINO (Apache-2.0), TFLite/LiteRT (Apache-2.0), Core ML and Windows ML (OS components) ([MA 6.14–6.22][MA]). Only one is MIT, and none is a specification. Their model parsers are the CVE class above ([IK 7.7][IK]). Using any as the Stage 1 runtime would put an unauditable binary in the most privileged new process and would decide Izcal's numerics and failure behaviour by vendor release; the OS components remain the only permitted NPU path later, under ML-D9, and are not the Stage 1 runtime.

**The gaps Izcal can own.**

1. **No browser specifies its ML.** No engine publishes a model container, a numeric contract, a determinism story, a per-model licence ledger, a data provenance record, or a testable definition of what each switch disables. The features ship; the contract does not.
2. **Accessibility descriptions are treated as a feature, not a safety surface.** The CHI 2024 evidence says users need provenance, uncertainty and context, and that fluent but wrong descriptions are harmful ([MA 1.24–1.25][MA]). Chrome prefixes its server descriptions with "Appears to be…", which signals uncertainty, but no shipping implementation publishes a false-description rate or marks provenance in the accessibility tree ([HP 2.1][HP]).
3. **The privacy story stops at "on-device".** It does not say what is retained, what is recorded, what a model download reveals, or whether another site's page can influence the model's inputs.
4. **Model licences are unresolved at the weight level.** The strongest small models are Apache-2.0, Gemma-licensed, community-licensed or dual-labelled; the MIT-licensed ones are few (BLIP-2, GIT, Florence-2, Donut, Moonshine), and none comes with an MIT-licensed or commissioned training-data provenance ([MA §6][MA], [DL 2.1–2.21][DL]).
5. **NPU access is opaque and unmeasured.** The OS runtimes decide placement, do not document it, and the published small-model measurements contradict the TOPS claims ([IK 6.17][IK]).
6. **No open evaluation for accessibility quality.** CIDEr, SPICE and CLIPScore measure reference overlap or image-text similarity, not whether a description serves a reader's task; CHAIR measures object hallucination only; no browser publishes a false-description or adequacy rate ([MA 1.17–1.21][MA], [GO 7.1–7.3][GO]).
7. **The model supply chain is treated as trusted because the feature is local.** Model hubs host malicious pickled weights and their scanners are advisory (about 100 malicious models found, one a reverse shell, one sample reported to bypass the scan), the loaders are a live parser-CVE class (18 CVEs and 211 findings across 71 projects, with a 255-byte ONNX reproducer), and a package compromise below the model shipped a miner for four releases ([SR 2.1, 2.5, 2.12, 8.2–8.3][SR]). No browser publishes a model-signing chain, a validated container contract or a parser audit, so "on-device" is doing security work it cannot do.

## 4. First principles

### 4.1 The users' real tasks

- A screen-reader user needs a description that is **true, short by default, available in a longer form when the image carries content, and marked as machine-generated**. The task is comprehension, not captioning.
- A person reading a scanned PDF needs the page's text **in reading order**, labelled as recognised, without the page leaving the device.
- A deaf viewer needs captions that arrive **while the audio is still relevant**, with a stated language and a visible indication that they are generated.
- A privacy-conscious person needs the default state to have **no model, no process, no download, and no network path**, and needs each opt-in to say what it will fetch and what it enables.
- An assistive-technology user needs the description to reach the accessibility tree and the repair overlay, **never the page**.
- Izcal's reviewers need one runtime, one container format, one loader, one gate implementation, and a licence ledger that can be audited file by file.

### 4.2 The platform's actual primitives

- **Compute is CPU first.** Every target has vector integer and float units; the NPU is reachable only through an OS runtime that decides placement; the GPU is a shared resource with its own scheduler and is not needed for models of this size.
- **Memory bandwidth, not peak FLOPs, bounds batch-1 inference.** A 0.2–0.6B model reads its weights for every token; the kernel's job is to stream them ([IK 2.21, 5.4][IK]).
- **Integer arithmetic is exact and associative modulo 2³².** int8 with int32 accumulation is the cheapest route to bit-identical results across ISAs ([IK 1.18][IK]).
- **Float arithmetic is order-dependent.** Reproducibility must be defined per operator, with a fixed reduction tree and no contraction, and its cost measured ([IK 1.14–1.17][IK]).
- **A file is not a program.** A declarative container with validated invariants can be safe; a container that executes code or trusts lengths cannot ([IK 7.6, 7.12][IK]).
- **Text is the cheapest output.** Descriptions, OCR text and captions are small; they record, compare and disclose far more easily than images or audio.

### 4.3 Izcal's principles that bind this design

- **P1, one process per trust domain.** The ML service is one process, sandboxed, with no ambient authority and no network; page content reaches it only through typed IPC.
- **A shared process is a cross-site boundary.** Site isolation exists because cache state and speculative execution cross process boundaries; one ML service for every tab is therefore page-invisible, stateless after a job, cache-free across sites and empty of capabilities, or it is a disclosure channel ([SR 1.1–1.9][SR]).
- **P3/P4, no blocking the page and no ambient threads.** Inference runs off the script thread, in its own process, under a user grant; Stage 1 has **no web-facing inference request path** — a page cannot ask for inference, and the only callers are browser-owned features acting under a grant — which also removes the API timing channel a page could use to detect a grant.
- **B10's off-state.** Every element of the off-state is a test: no process, no file, no download, no request, no UI. The accessibility grant is a second, independent off-state with the same discipline.
- **M-1 parts 1–4.** Izcal writes the runtime from papers; no framework ships; no engine source is read; every operator and kernel carries its paper anchor; black-box peers are test oracles only.
- **MIT-only.** Weights are data sets, and a data set's licence is only as good as its provenance; Izcal therefore trains what it ships and records the data (ML-D3).
- **FP.** Clean file-backed weight pages are uncharged and shared; the service exits when idle; no user model is in the installer; energy is an absolute gate.

### 4.4 Assumptions kept and broken

| Assumption | Verdict | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| A browser needs a multi-billion-parameter model for useful descriptions | **Break** | 182M parameters under 200 MB shipped usable quality ([MA 1.14][MA]); Stage 1 targets the 180–200M class |
| On-device means private | **Break** | On-device still leaves recordings, caches, model downloads and cross-site shared services; each needs a rule (6.9) |
| A permissive licence on a weight file covers the model | **Break** | The training data is the unresolved right; Izcal trains its own and records the ledger (6.7) |
| IEEE 754 arithmetic is reproducible | **Break** | Reduction order, FMA contraction and transcendentals differ; a declared canonical mode is required (6.4) |
| The NPU is the energy-efficient choice | **Keep, conditionally** | Measured wins exist but so do 3.6× slower decode and 2–4× energy; R-3's gate is the only admission (6.10) |
| A model file can be treated as opaque data | **Break** | It is a parser boundary with a CVE history; the container is validated before mapping (6.6) |
| AI off and accessibility off are the same setting | **Break** | B10 separates them; this spec makes them independent and testable (6.3) |

## 5. Lessons learned

- **The trust failure is the download, not the feature.** Chrome's silent, reported ~4 GB model download and its re-download after deletion did more damage than the feature it enabled; B10's rules exist because of it. Every model Izcal downloads is at an explicit user action, shown with its size, and removable in one step ([HP 3.8–3.10][HP]).
- **A single "block AI" switch is simple; two settings are honest.** Firefox's 2026 control disables every AI feature at once, which is easy to explain but cannot express B10's separation of the accessibility model from the AI switch. Izcal's 2×2 matrix is the version that can be tested.
- **A privacy sentence in UI copy is treated as a promise.** Chrome deleted its on-device privacy sentence in 148, and the removal was read as a retreat even though Google said behaviour had not changed ([HP 3.10][HP]). Izcal's privacy claims are spec text with a test attached, and the settings copy is generated from the same rule.
- **A model chosen for reach beats one chosen for a silicon class.** Edge's ~3.8B Phi-4-mini was gated to a small hardware set and replaced by a smaller CPU model about a year later ([HP 6.3–6.6][HP]). The portable CPU path is the base case, not the fallback.
- **Server-side accessibility descriptions are the wrong trade.** They work only online, send page images to a third party, and cannot be audited by the user. MD-D25's (b) is a better failure than (c).
- **A fluent description can be worse than none.** The CHI study's screen-reader participants needed provenance, aberrations and creator intent; a terse one-sentence caption missed all three ([MA 1.24–1.25][MA]). Marking and uncertainty are part of the product, not metadata polish.
- **Accessibility claims must match measured capability.** The FTC's accessiBe order is the boundary: generated text is an aid to the user, never a statement that a page is compliant.
- **Small models need per-channel quantisation and per-model measurement.** Per-tensor int8 destroyed MobileNet (0.1% top-1), while per-channel recovered 70.3% ([IK 1.8][IK]); 4-bit PTQ collapsed on the same class and 3-bit was not viable ([IK 1.9][IK]).
- **The NPU is not automatically faster or greener.** Fixed dispatch made a 0.6B model 3.6× slower per token, and host polling took up to 30% of system energy ([IK 6.7, 6.10][IK]). Measure end to end per task and device.
- **Model files are a security boundary.** The 2026 fuzzing results — 18 CVEs, 211 findings, a 255-byte out-of-bounds reproducer — are the cost of trusting lengths and cross-field invariants ([IK 7.7–7.9][IK]). The bug is rarely one bad length: it is a weight rank and a dilation count that disagree, which byte-level fuzzing does not construct and semantic mutation does ([SR 2.2–2.4][SR]).
- **A shared process is a cross-site boundary.** Site isolation's answer to a compromised renderer is one process per site, at 10–13% desktop memory cost, because cache state and speculative execution cross process boundaries ([SR 1.1–1.5][SR]). An ML service shared by every tab therefore has to earn trust with an empty capability set, no per-site state, no cross-site cache and no page-requested inference; a page that can request or read inference gets a grant, model and timing oracle even when no output is returned ([SR 1.7–1.8][SR]).
- **The model supply chain fails below the model.** Hubs host malicious weights and their scan badges are advisory; a build script can be compromised while the public repository stays clean; AI infrastructure exposed without authentication is exploited at scale ([SR 8.2–8.5][SR]). The attack arrives through a dependency, a build step or a download endpoint, not through the arithmetic, so signing the manifest and hardening the build path matter as much as writing the kernels.
- **Determinism is a numeric contract, not a flag.** Integer exactness buys it cheaply; float reproducibility costs between 1.2× and 8× on a dot product in the published range, and canonical mode's own per-operator cost is measured at ML-M0's determinism probe and published, so no fixed factor is claimed until then ([IK 1.16, 1.18][IK]).
- **A model's licence is only as good as its data.** A few strong small captioners are MIT at the weights (BLIP-2, GIT, Florence-2), but none documents an MIT-compatible training corpus. Training our own on a recorded ledger is the only defensible route to shipping.

New crates: **`hg-ml`** (L4, tier P: graph IR, planner, kernels, quantisation, tokenizer), **`hg-ml-service`** (L5, tier P: lifecycle, queue, grants, sandbox manifest), **`hg-ml-loader`** (L4, tier P: container parser and validator), **`hg-ml-tasks`** (L5: the task front ends), **`hg-ml-observe`** (L4: journal and IIP types), and **`hg-ml-api`** (L2: the generated IDL types). `hg-ml-main` (L6) is the binary. No new process kind: the Stage 1 map's ML service row becomes this process. No thread at rest: the service exits when idle (6.10).

### 6.2 The service process: one sandbox, no network, no page (ML-D5)

**One service, shared across sites.** Weights are the dominant cost of on-device ML, so one process serves every tab and profile container. The service holds no per-site state after a job completes: one job at a time, FIFO by user action, inputs passed as read-only handles and dropped at completion, outputs returned to the requesting caller only. The one exception is a caption session's decoder state, which spans that session's windows, is bounded per session, is never shared between sessions or sites, and is dropped when captions stop (6.5). Jobs carry a profile token; model handles, arenas and caption sessions are keyed by profile, and no output or state crosses one. There is **no content-addressed cache shared across sites**; description and OCR caches live in the requesting renderer's document and die with it, because a cache keyed by image bytes is a cross-site membership oracle. The service's memory is charged to the browser baseline, not to a site.

**Sandbox.** The strictest tier, with an empty capability set: no sockets, no DNS, no filesystem writes, no process creation, no ambient handles. It reads only the model store (read-only, per-model file handles passed by the broker) and receives shared-memory read-only handles for inputs. A path string in an IPC message is an ambient-authority request and the IDL rejects it ([SR 7.10][SR]). It is reached only through the broker's `hg-ml-api` endpoint; a renderer never holds an ML endpoint directly. The sandbox uses each target's native mechanism — seccomp-bpf plus Landlock on Linux, a per-process sandbox profile applied at launch on macOS, an AppContainer plus a job object on Windows — because none of them covers the whole contract alone: seccomp filters syscalls but cannot inspect pointer arguments, and AppContainer does not bound memory ([SR 7.1–7.7][SR]). On macOS the mechanism is the profile language, **not** App Sandbox entitlements, which confine the app bundle rather than one child process; the security note read Apple's App Sandbox guide but not the profile language, so the profile is specified with the security-scope owner at ML-M0 and carries no claim until then ([SR 7.6][SR], Q-ML-15). Resource limits are a second, separate layer: `memory.max` with group OOM on Linux cgroups v2 and working-set, CPU-rate and kill-on-close limits on a Windows job object, so an unbounded job kills the service and not the browser ([SR 5.3–5.5, 7.5][SR]). The sandbox manifest is part of the process's launch, a static check proves the binary imports no network type (G181), and the sandbox is **tested by denial** — a connect, an open-for-write and a process creation are each attempted and must fail, with a syscall trace over a real workload — because a manifest that says "no network" proves nothing ([SR 7.12][SR]; ML-8).

**Lifecycle.** Launched on demand by the first authorised job, exits after 30 s† idle, and never launches with both grants off. A job that has not started within its budget is cancelled and the caller gets a reason code; a crashed service takes no page down (R-2's analogue; ML-22). The process is a trusted process in the threat model: it sees cross-site content, so it is hardened like the broker — no web-format parsing beyond the declared input decoders, no page-controlled allocation sizes, and all input bounds checked at the IPC boundary.

**Build story and the G4 conflict.** Stage 1's table lists `hg-ml-main` as "built only with feature `ai`", but B10 requires the accessibility model to work with AI off. The resolution (ML-D5): **`hg-ml-main` and the accessibility task modules are built in every shipped binary; only generative and agent-facing task modules are behind feature `ai`.** "Generative" here means the Stage 2 text-generation task APIs, not ML-ASR's acoustic decoder, which is an accessibility task and is built always. G4 keeps its purpose — no default-feature binary reaches agent code — and gains one named exception: `hg-ml-main` may link `hg-ml*`, and nothing else may. The Stage 1 process map, build line and G4 are amended in Section 9.5.

**Placement against other specs.** One process for all sites is an exception to HGBA-2's I-1, which keys processes to (site, container). OD-8 is the **procedure** for such an exception — one owner-decided list, each entry with a threat model and its own conditions — but the ML service is **not** in that list and does not meet its defining criterion: OD-8's three services each parse no page payload, while the ML service consumes decoded images, page rasters and PCM audio. It therefore cannot inherit the existing exception. Section 9.5 proposes a **fourth I-1 exception** for the ML service, with the conditions above (empty capability set, no per-site state, no content-keyed cache, one job at a time, no page-facing request path) and its threat model in the security scope; **ML-D5 is conditional on the HGBA-2 owner's decision**, exactly as OD-8 was. The revisit trigger stands: if a future page-facing ML API arrives, the service splits per site or the API does not ship (ML-D5's reversal).

### 6.3 The two gates: AI and accessibility (ML-D4)

B10 already defines both settings; this spec turns them into exact, testable semantics. There are **two grants**, each with a fixed model set, and neither implies the other:

| Grant | Setting | Default | Authorises | Never authorises |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Accessibility model** | `accessibility.model` (the setting B10 requires) | Off | The Stage 1 model set **S_A**: image description, OCR and generated captions (caption membership is Q-ML-1; if the owner refuses it, ML-ASR moves under the AI grant); their download at enable time; the ML service for those tasks | Agent features, AI task APIs, AI network traffic, agent computation, any AI UI entry point |
| **AI** | `ai.enabled` (the AI switch) | Off | Stage 2: task APIs (translate, summarise, prompt), grammar/writing models **when the person chooses them (ED-D9's condition)**, agent-facing inference, the agent host's ML requests | Any accessibility task, the accessibility model set, the accessibility UI |

State matrix (Stage 1; Stage 2 rows are marked and describe **no Stage 1 binary** — they exist so the gate semantics are complete):

| `ai.enabled` | `accessibility.model` | `hg-ml-main` | Model files on disk | Downloads | Tasks that can run |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| off | off | never launched | none | none | none |
| off | on | launched on demand | only the S_A models the user selected | only at the moment of enabling | image description, OCR, captions |
| on | off | launched on demand | only Stage 2 S_AI models selected (Stage 2) | only at the moment of enabling | task APIs, grammar, agent inference (Stage 2) |
| on | on | shared | both selected sets | at each enable | both, still independently |

The AI-grant rows do not show B10's "keep" option: in Stage 2 a user may keep AI-grant models when switching AI off, and that is a Stage 2 row; the accessibility grant is unconditional as above.

**Semantics, stated so they can be tested.**

- **Enabling is explicit and sized.** Turning a grant on shows every model in its set with its download size and purpose; the user selects which to fetch (image description selected by default for the accessibility grant, because it is the R4 need). The download starts **at that moment**, from Izcal's update service, signed and hash-verified, never from a page, never prefetched, never on a timer (ML-11; B10's wording).
- **A task whose model was not fetched** asks the user once, in a browser-owned surface, and fetches only on that explicit action. It never fetches silently on first use.
- **Disabling stops, unloads and deletes.** Turning a grant off stops the service, unloads the models, and deletes that profile's model files for the grant's set; the two Stage 1 sets are disjoint, and a model used by both grants would be reference-counted rather than deleted under one; a deletion that fails at that moment is completed at the next launch and surfaced in the model manager. Deletion is unconditional for the accessibility grant: B10's "unless the user chooses to keep them" belongs to the AI switch's models, remains available for AI-grant models in Stage 2, and cannot apply here because ML-9 needs the exact default state. The default state is restored exactly, which is what makes AG-0 testable after an opt-in cycle (ML-9). The model store is **per profile**, not per installation: the disk cost is duplicated across profiles, but a profile's disable deletes its own files with no cross-profile side effect and no profile can observe another profile's choice (6.10).
- **Independence is a test matrix, not a sentence.** Turning accessibility on enables no agent feature and starts no agent host; turning AI on enables no accessibility task and downloads no S_A model (ML-10). Settings migrations and updates carry both states unchanged and never re-prompt (B10's "off survives updates").
- **Captions are a two-setting feature.** Captions run only when the accessibility grant is on **and** the user's media captions setting is on; the captions setting alone launches no service, downloads no model and enables no other task (MD 6.12's captions UI owns the second setting).
- **Enterprise policy may force off and lock either grant**, showing "managed" in the setting, which is B10's administrator rule; this spec adds no force-on, so an administrator cannot push a model download onto a user's device.
- **Page invisibility.** No web API reports either grant, and no web API can request inference in Stage 1. The only callers are browser-owned: captions follow the user's captions setting for the media a page plays, and the PDF viewer runs OCR on an image-only page only when the user asks in the viewer or an assistive technology reads the page — **an embedded PDF alone causes no OCR**. Both are grant-authorised, browser-owned and cannot be toggled or invoked by page script, so there is no API through which a page can select an input, read an output or query a grant (ML-12). One residual is **not** neutral and is stated as such: a page that plays audio while captions are on causes caption windows and can observe the contention it caused, which is a **disclosed accessibility-signal leak** — it reveals that the captions setting and the accessibility grant are on, and the page controls the signal's cadence. The mitigation is work shaping: a fixed one-window-per-second cadence, content-independent compute per window, no per-word or output-dependent work, and no caption state in the DOM or accessibility tree. The leak is measured at ML-M3 and must be accepted by the accessibility owner before captions ship (Q-ML-14); it is named in 6.9, ML-12 and the accessibility-spec amendment (9.5).
- **UI surfaces.** With both grants off, the only place ML appears is the settings page holding the two switches (B10). The model manager lives under those settings, lists installed models with size and purpose, and offers removal. There is no AI button, chip or suggestion anywhere else.

### 6.4 The inference stack Izcal writes (ML-D6)

**Scope.** `hg-ml` implements a graph IR, a planner, an arena allocator, a kernel set, quantisation and a tokenizer. It is written from published papers and Izcal's measurements; no framework source is read, and no framework is linked (M-1). ONNX's prose IR is a checklist of what a graph must carry, nothing more; its licence is Apache-2.0 and it is not shipped ([IK 7.4][IK]).

**Graph and operator set.** A graph is an acyclic SSA list of nodes over typed tensors; each node names an operator from a closed allowlist, its attributes, its quantisation metadata (scale, zero point, granularity, packing order, accumulator type) and its `// spec:` anchor. The Stage 1 operator set is deliberately small: dense/GEMM and GEMV, embedding gather, depthwise and 3×3 convolution (GEMM-lowered; im2col is the fallback lowering, not the primary path), layer norm and RMS norm, softmax (online, one pass), GELU (fixed exact or tanh form per model), SiLU, elementwise arithmetic, reshape/transpose/concat/slice, resize, argmax and CTC greedy decode. Winograd and FFT convolution are rejected: the 3×3 filters dominate and Winograd's accuracy cost is real ([IK 2.8–2.9][IK]). Fusion is first-order and expressed in the IR (norm+activation epilogues, quantise/dequantise around integer blocks), so the canonical mode can fuse without changing reduction order ([IK 2.17][IK]).

**Kernels and ISAs.** The micro-kernel is a packed-weight GEMM/GEMV in the Goto–van de Geijn anatomy ([IK 2.1][IK]), with per-ISA back ends: x86-64 at an AVX2 tier and an AVX-512/VNNI tier selected per generation (AVX-512 is a win on Ice Lake and Sapphire Rapids, a frequency risk on Skylake-X and unnecessary on AMD where it is two 256-bit halves, [IK 3.2–3.3][IK]); AArch64 with NEON plus dotprod/i8mm/BF16, and SVE/SVE2 compiled vector-length-agnostically or specialised per width ([IK 3.5–3.6][IK]); riscv64 with RVV 1.0, tuned against the core rather than translated from NEON ([IK 3.10][IK]). Kernel selection happens once per graph node at plan time, never per element ([IK 3.12][IK]). Attention is tiled and never materialises the N×N score matrix, the FlashAttention lesson re-derived for CPU caches; the GPU tiling constants do not transfer ([IK 2.4, 2.7][IK]). The scheduler runs one job at a time; prefill may fan out across a bounded pool, decode stays small-thread and bandwidth-first ([IK 5.14–5.15][IK]). ASR is a sequence of bounded windows (≤ 1 s†, or ≤ 500 ms† on the latency path, because a final cue cannot precede its window's end), each its own job, so a caption stream cannot monopolise the queue; a description or OCR request waits at most one window. Work stealing is the pool's model if a pool is used ([IK 5.8][IK]).

**Numeric modes (the core of ML-D6).** IEEE 754-2019 fixes each operation and its rounding, but not a reduction order across a vector or a thread count, so a reproducibility contract has to say more than "binary32" ([SS 2.1][SS], [IK 1.3][IK]). Two declared modes:

| Mode | Rules | Used by | Contract |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Canonical** | IEEE 754 binary32, round-to-nearest-even; no FMA contraction; a fixed reduction tree per operator, independent of thread count; Izcal's own transcendentals; integer operators exact and order-independent | Deterministic configuration, recordings, tests, conformance | Bit-identical across x86-64, AArch64 and riscv64, and across thread counts; the published claim |
| **Fast** | FMA, ISA-specific reduction trees, work stealing, vendor-equivalent transcendentals, int8 where the model tolerates it | Default interactive production | Reproducible on one binary and thread count only; never claimed cross-ISA; never recorded, so it cannot enter a replay (6.8) |

Integer kernels are exact in both modes: int8 weights with int32 accumulation and a fixed dequantisation order are bit-identical across ISAs at no accuracy cost ([IK 1.18][IK]). The canonical float path pays the reproducibility price only where a float operator remains; the published 1.2–8× range for reproducible summation ([IK 1.16][IK]), with canonical mode's own per-operator cost measured at ML-M0 and no fixed factor claimed before it, is why the hot path is integer-first and why the default interactive mode is fast. Every record carries `numeric_mode` as a mandatory field, and the simulator refuses a record with a missing or unknown mode (the worker-task-mode precedent in Stage 1 3.3).

**Quantisation.** The shipping recipe is int8 weights with per-channel symmetric scales and int8 activations where the model tolerates them; FP16/BF16 for norms, softmax and KV state; int32 accumulators. Per-tensor int8 weights are forbidden for convolutional models because they collapse small CNNs (MobileNet-v1 0.1% versus 70.3% per-channel, [IK 1.8][IK]). Four-bit weight-only is a per-model option admitted only with a measured quality delta and, below 1B parameters, quantisation-aware training; 4-bit activations are not used for sub-2B models ([IK 4.6, 4.9][IK]). The loader treats quantisation metadata as first-class validated data: the same weights read with the wrong granularity must fail validation, not produce garbage (6.6).

**Tokenizer.** Izcal trains its own byte-level BPE tokenizer from the BPE paper on its own cleared corpus ([MA 4.11, 4.15][MA]). No tokenizer crate ships (SentencePiece is Apache-2.0, a licence string recorded at Low confidence and verified at implementation, and is not evidence for design beyond its paper); the merges and the corpus are part of the model card and the data ledger.

**Accelerators (ML-D9).** Stage 1 is CPU-only. No Stage 1 task has a measured 1.5× end-to-end win over the CPU, the OS ML runtimes place work opaquely, and canonical mode must stay CPU. A later NPU path is permitted only through an OS ML runtime (Windows ML, Core ML) and only for a task and device where R-3's 1.5× latency or energy gate is measured against the CPU and, where a GPU path exists for the task, against the GPU too, exactly as HGBA-2 R-3 requires; Stage 1's tasks are CPU-only, so the CPU comparison applies first. It is a separate execution provider, never the canonical mode, and its outputs are recorded as values like any fast-mode job. The GPU is not used in Stage 1; a Stage 2 model that needs it enters through the GPU service's existing scheduling contract (Q-ML-7). Vendor TOPS figures are not inputs to scheduling ([IK 6.14–6.16][IK]).

**Why not an off-the-shelf runtime.** ONNX Runtime is MIT but is an inference framework doing browser-engine work, which M-1 part 1 reserves to Izcal, and it ships a large C++ parser with the CVE class above; OpenVINO and TFLite are Apache-2.0 and fail the licence rule outright; Core ML and Windows ML are opaque OS components that would decide numerics and placement ([IK 6.17][IK], [MA 6.14–6.22][MA]). None provides the determinism contract, the licence ledger or the container safety this spec requires, and all of them would put non-MIT or unauditable code in the service.

### 6.5 The model set: Stage 1 and Stage 2 (ML-D2)

| Model | Task | Architecture | Size budget (int8) | Stage | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ML-IMG** | Image description for MD-D25 and R4; short default, longer on request | ViT-style encoder (patch 14–16, 224–384 px) plus a small autoregressive decoder, 180–195M parameters | ≤ 200 MB† on disk | 1 | Text, confidence, model id, generated flag; never to the page |
| **ML-OCR** | OCR for image-only PDF pages and Reading Mode | DBNet-family detector plus CRNN/CTC or a small attention recogniser, per-script packs | ≤ 80 MB† per script pack, Latin first | 1 | Text runs with boxes, confidence, reading order, "recognised" label |
| **ML-ASR** | Generated captions for media (MD-D25) | Streaming encoder-decoder or CTC, 27–74M parameters, language id | ≤ 150 MB† | 1 (ML-M3; Stage 2 if Stage 1 exits before month 30†) | Interim and final cues with timestamps, confidence, generated flag |
| **Stage 2 tasks** | Translate, summarise, prompt, grammar and writing assistance, agent inference | To be specified with the task APIs | — | 2, `ai` feature and AI grant | Task-specific |

**Why this set.** Stage 1 is defined by the promises already in the record: MD-D25's descriptions and captions, R4's generated labels, and PDF's OCR. Nothing else is Stage 1. HGBA-2 4.5's translate, summarise and prompt APIs are **Stage 2**, because they are generative, page-faceable and belong behind the AI grant; WebNN stays behind a flag per HGBA-2 and is not shipped. The 182M/200 MB point is the shipping proof for ML-IMG ([MA 1.14][MA]) — Firefox ran it on ONNX Runtime and Transformers.js in a separate process, a stack Izcal does not ship; the size and quality are the evidence, not the stack ([HP 4.3][HP]); the OCR pipeline split is the mature one ([MA 2.1–2.11][MA]); the 27–74M class is the realistic streaming point for ML-ASR ([MA 3.5, 3.10][MA], [GO 6.7][GO]). The short-default and longer-on-request shape follows WCAG 2.2 SC 1.1.1's equivalent-purpose test, which sets no character limit and no quality metric; the adequacy bar is Izcal's own ([SS 1.1][SS]).

**Language scope is measured, not claimed.** Stage 1 ships English first for all three tasks, and Latin script first for OCR. The small-model evidence is blunt: Moonshine is English-only, and Whisper's 39M tiny model scores 29.6% word error on Common Voice 5.1 English against 9.0% for large-v2 ([GO 6.2–6.3][GO]); OCR vendors claim 50 to more than 100 languages (Chrome 77, PaddleOCR-VL 111, Tesseract more than 100) with no per-language character-error rate ([GO 6.1, 6.7][GO]). A language or script pack is added only when its measured CER or WER on a fixed, consented test set clears the same gate as English (ML-3, ML-4), and the model card states the Unicode and BCP 47 versions it was evaluated on ([GO 6.8][GO], [SS 2.8–2.9][SS]). No language count appears in marketing.

**One model at a time.** The service runs one job at a time in Stage 1. There is no batching, no concurrent sequence set and no page-requested queue; a second request waits, and the UI shows it waiting. This bounds memory and removes a scheduling side channel.

### 6.6 The model container and loader (ML-D7)

`.hgmodel` is Izcal's own declarative container. It has a fixed magic and version, a TLV manifest, and a tensor data region; it contains **no code, no pickles, no external file references and no dynamic linking** ([IK 7.6][IK], [SR 2.5][SR]). The manifest carries: model id, version, task, architecture graph, operator allowlist and opset, tensor table (dtype, rank, shape, strides, offset, length, quantisation metadata), tokenizer and vocabulary, training record digest, model-card digest, licence (`MIT`), data-ledger digest, and a detached Ed25519 signature block over the manifest, one signature per signing key, meeting the signing threshold. Tensors are page-aligned; the whole file and each tensor carry SHA-256 digests, computed with `hg-crypto` (no new hash dependency).

**Validation before mapping.** The loader reads a bounded prefix through a checked reader, validates the manifest, and only then maps the tensor region read-only. Mapping first and validating lazily has already exposed the address space to a hostile length, and a mapping past the end of a truncated file raises SIGBUS ([SR 2.7][SR]). The checks are the cross-field invariants that the CVE history says are missed: magic and version; header length against file length; tensor count against a bound; dtype/rank/dimension product against checked overflow ([SR 2.8–2.9][SR]); offset plus length inside the data region; alignment and declared strides; quantisation scale and zero-point counts and zero-point range; shared ranges only if declared; checksums; operator/opset allowlist; graph topology and SSA ([IK 7.12–7.14][IK]). A file that fails any check is refused with a reason code, never partially mapped. The format has no pickle and no code path on load, which is the property safetensors has and pickle does not ([SR 2.5–2.6][SR]), and a well-formed container of any format can still be hostile, because a format cannot guarantee safe loading on its own ([SR 2.10][SR]). The parser is tier P, has no `unsafe` outside a reviewed module, and is fuzzed with a **semantic mutator**, not only byte mutation, because the bugs live in cross-field relationships ([IK 7.14][IK], [SR 2.3–2.4][SR]).

**Signing and the store.** The signed model manifest names **every file by hash**, not a weight blob alone: a signature over a manifest with an incomplete file list authenticates nothing about the omitted files ([SR 3.10][SR]). Model manifests are signed under the browser's update root by a **dedicated model-signing role with a 2-of-3 threshold** rooted in an offline key: one key held offline in the release ceremony, one in the build HSM, one by the security lead. Rotation is signed under both the old and the new threshold, and a compromised key is revoked by a manifest signed by the other two, with an advisory and a re-verification of the affected models; a single build-machine key cannot substitute a model ([SR 3.1–3.2, 3.9][SR], with custody confirmed by the security-scope owner at ML-M0, Q-ML-15). Verification copies the update framework's five defences rather than inventing them: reject a version at or below the installed one (rollback), require a current timestamp (freeze), bind the model list to the signed snapshot (mix-and-match), cap every download by its declared length (endless data), and keep the root offline (key compromise) ([SR 3.2–3.3][SR]). Verification **fails closed before the loader sees the file**: a failure leaves no partial file, surfaces a user-visible error and is never retried in a loop ([SR 3.11][SR]). The model id, version, hash and size are also listed in the signed browser manifest, so a file that is not in the manifest never loads. The build records its provenance (SLSA-style build levels and per-step hashes) so a compromised build step is detectable ([SR 3.4–3.5][SR]). The store is content-addressed and immutable; a model is deleted only through the grant's disable path or the model manager. Truncation or replacement after validation is out of the threat model (a local writer can replace the store anyway), but every load re-verifies the hash before use.

**Why not an existing format.** ONNX has no mandatory checksum and an optional SHA1 for external data ([IK 7.2–7.3][IK]); safetensors is safe in principle but its offsets and shapes still need validation ([IK 7.5][IK]); both are third-party formats with their own compatibility surface. No ISO, W3C or IETF standard defines a signed, declarative model container with per-tensor hashes and quantisation metadata, so there is no standard to adopt ([SS 2.6][SS]). Izcal's container is smaller than the subset it would have to validate anyway.

### 6.7 Training, data governance and licences (ML-D3)

**The rule has two limbs, and only the second is hard.** A shipped weight must be MIT (or dual-licensed with MIT taken); training data must be Izcal-commissioned or MIT-licensed, with the licence recorded. The strands show the first limb is satisfiable off the shelf for captioning and document OCR — BLIP-2, GIT, Florence-2 and Donut are MIT, and Moonshine is MIT for ASR — but the second is not: no inspected model card documents an MIT-cleared or commissioned training corpus, and Whisper's distribution card says Apache-2.0 while its README says the weights are MIT ([DL 2.1–2.21][DL], [MA 6.1][MA]). Izcal's **shipping policy** is therefore to train and ship its own weights for ML-IMG, ML-OCR and ML-ASR, releasing them under MIT with the copyright held by the project. A third-party MIT weight set is a fallback only if the owner accepts an undocumented data chain (Q-ML-11); a non-MIT set cannot ship at all. Firefox's 182M model (Apache-2.0) is a test oracle, not a candidate.

**The D-class ledger.** Every data item gets a class before it can reach the trainer. **D0** commissioned: own photography, recordings and synthetic renders, or work-for-hire with signed releases. **D1** MIT or dual-licensed with MIT as one choice. **D2** CC0 1.0 or public domain, allowed only if the owner answers Q-ML-9 yes (recommended: yes, as a named exception recorded in the ledger, mirroring OD-L6). **D3** CC BY, evaluation-only unless Q-ML-10 says otherwise (recommended: no, because CC BY is not MIT). **D4** research, non-commercial or no licence, evaluation-only. **D5** unknown or conflicting, quarantined. The per-item record carries `item_id` (SHA-256 of the bytes), source and retrieval date, SPDX licence expression ([SS 2.7][SS]) and a hash of the stored licence text, D-class, consent and release ids, subject flags (face, minor, medical, biometric), an annotator record without raw worker identity, PII flags and a retention date, and review status. Each data set gets a signed manifest; each model gets a manifest with the weight hash, the training manifest hash, evaluation sets and their licences, and the model-card and Article 53 summary digests ([DL §9][DL]). The ledger ships with the model card and is an audit artefact; a build fails if any shipped model's ledger digest is missing or mismatched (ML-20).

**What may be training data.** Commissioned photography with commissioned captions; Izcal's own renderer with its own fonts for synthetic printed-text pages; commissioned handwriting and form scans with releases; commissioned read speech with releases and transcription; Project Gutenberg text (US public domain, after stripping the licence and checking non-US status) and US federal documents, both D2 and therefore only with Q-ML-9. **What may not:** COCO, Visual Genome, Flickr30k, Open Images, LAION, DataComp, PixelProse, RedCaps and Conceptual Captions at the image level — their image licences are CC BY, research-only or unstated, and none is MIT; annotating an image does not license it (COCO's annotations are CC BY 4.0 over Flickr images of mixed licence, Open Images' images are CC BY 2.0 with per-image verification required, and Flickr30k is research-only) ([DL 3.1, 3.4, 3.7][DL]); IAM (non-commercial); SROIE, DocVQA and the veraPDF corpus (no licence); Common Crawl and Wikipedia for training (a limited licence and CC BY-SA respectively); YouTube or podcast audio. These sets remain usable as **evaluation** data under the test-only exception (D4), and only as that ([DL 3.1–5.13][DL]).

**LAION is excluded twice.** It licenses metadata, not images, and it was taken down on 19 December 2023 after 1,008 suspected CSAM links were reported; Re-LAION-5B removed 2,236 links on 30 August 2024 ([DL 3.9][DL]). A browser vendor cannot defend weights trained on it, and Izcal does not train on it at all.

**Costs are a funded line, at a scale the quality bar implies.** Commissioned captions are cheap per item but scale: the first 10,000 images with five captions each, at 30 captions per hour, is about 1,667 annotator-hours, roughly $13k–$20k at $8–$12/hour before photography and review ([DL 8.7][DL]) — a first batch, not a corpus. The **pilot** is 25,000 images with five captions each (125,000 captions, about 4,167 annotator-hours, $33k–$50k before photography and review); the **funded plan** is 50,000–100,000 images (250,000–500,000 captions, about $67k–$200k before photography and review), because ML-1's comparator was trained on COCO and Flickr30k-scale data and a 10,000-image model is not a credible attempt at its bar ([MA 1.16][MA]). The pilot passes only if its measured false-description rate is within 2 points of ML-2's threshold; if it is not, the corpus plan and ML-D3 return to the owner with the measured learning curve, and the fallback is Q-ML-11's third-party MIT weights only with the explicit provenance exception. A 1,000-hour read-speech corpus at $250 per finished hour, plus transcription and 20% QC, is roughly $370k† ([DL 8.7][DL]). These are planning estimates, not quotes; the first 1,000 items replace them with measured rates, and the budget is Q-ML-12.

**The governance documents.** Each model ships a card following the model-card and Datasheet literature (intended use and limits, languages, training-data summary with the ledger digest, evaluation results per group where the data allows, known failure modes, quantisation recipe, tokenizer corpus, date) and a Data Statement for each speech or text corpus ([MA 5.1–5.2][MA], [DL 7.1–7.2][DL]). The EU AI Act Article 53 training-content summary, where it applies, is generated from the same manifest so the public disclosure cannot drift from the internal record; Article 53 binds providers of general-purpose models, whether these task-specific models are in scope is analysed with Q-ML-5 at ML-M0, and the summary is published regardless as voluntary disclosure, with the card stating which duties Izcal assumes and why (the AI Office template was published on 24 July 2025) ([DL 6.6][DL]). Contributor terms follow the Dataset Providers Alliance's opt-in principle ([DL 7.3][DL]). The card states what the model is **not** for: it is not a claim that a page is accessible, and its output is a first draft. The governance process maps to NIST AI RMF 1.0's govern, map, measure and manage functions without claiming certification ([SS 3.4][SS]).

**Training-data leakage is tested, not assumed.** An inference-only service exposes no gradients or parameters, but the weights can still reproduce what they were trained on: sequence models have emitted credit-card numbers and verbatim text from single documents ([SR 4.5–4.6][SR]). The probe is defined per task, because a captioner or a recogniser that reproduces its own input is doing its job, not leaking. For **ML-IMG**, the harness feeds 1,000 images that are unrelated to the canaries and 1,000 perturbed non-canary training images, and measures whether any of 500 unique commissioned strings (names, numbers, rare caption phrases) or 200 unique canary descriptors is emitted verbatim; feeding the canary image and receiving its own caption is the task and is not counted. For **ML-ASR**, the harness feeds silence and 50 hours† of unrelated audio and measures whether any canary transcript string is emitted. Both use greedy and sampled decoding, and the card publishes the probe, the sampling budget and the result; a recoverable canary is a failed training run, retrained with the duplicate or the unique item removed. This is cheap for a small model and is the only evidence that the commissioned-data promise survives into the weights (ML-23).

**No user data, ever.** The models never train on browsing data, page content, images a user viewed or accessibility repairs; those are inference-only. Training on user data would need a separate spec, explicit separate consent, and a cross-site data flow that P1 and B10 forbid ([DL 7.8][DL]).

**Training toolchain.** Training runs in Izcal's lab. The tools are not shipped, are not linked into any binary, and are not evidence for the runtime's design (the M-1 part 3 rule applies to design evidence, not to lab machinery); the toolchain and its versions are recorded in the model card. The preference is MIT tooling; a non-MIT lab tool is recorded with its licence in the card and never reaches a shipped artefact. Training compute is a budget item, not an engineering afterthought (Section 10's unbudgeted-compute risk; Q-ML-4, Q-ML-12).

**Evaluation governance (ML-D12).** ML-IMG is evaluated on an Izcal-held-out set with screen-reader participants and on automatic metrics as regression signals only; the accessibility-quality rubric scores correctness, omission, uncertainty and provenance, because CIDEr, SPICE and CLIPScore do not measure task adequacy and CHAIR measures object hallucination only ([MA 1.17–1.21][MA], [GO 7.1–7.3][GO]). No public adequacy benchmark exists, so the rubric and the false-description rate are published as Izcal's own instruments, with the test set's licence and the model version attached ([GO 7.6, 7.8][GO]). ML-OCR is scored by CER per script and document class, with reading order checked separately ([MA 2.12, 2.18][MA]). ML-ASR is scored by WER on clean, noisy and accented sets, plus real-time factor and first-cue latency. Every model is also run against a **black-box comparator** in the same lab on the same corpus — Firefox's generation path, Chrome's OCR path, Tesseract, Whisper — and the result is published, with the caveat that these are black-box measurements of shipped binaries, never source reads.

### 6.8 Determinism and recording (ML-D8)

**Inference is a recorded input/output pair.** During a user-initiated recording, each job writes one `ml.job` record: job id, task, model id/version/hash, the grant that authorised it, input **hashes** (never pixels or audio), output values (text, boxes, cues, confidence), `numeric_mode`, device class and a duration bucket. Replay serves the recorded output and **never invokes the runtime or loads a model**, so a replay works after the model is deleted and a fast-mode run cannot make a recording diverge, because a recording never runs one. This is the strongest form of the brief's requirement: non-deterministic paths are not merely quarantined from replay, they are irrelevant to it.

**Recording a shared service.** The ML service outlives any one recording, so DT-D1's "starts at process launch" is applied to the **requesting document's** recording session, not to the service. Each job carries the requesting document's session token; the service emits its `ml.job` record through the broker only to that session's journal, and only while that session's recording is active. Jobs from a tab that is not recording leave only counters. A recording started after the service has been running captures every subsequent job of its own tab, which is all replay needs: replay never asks the service for earlier work. A job shared by construction — none exists in Stage 1, because each job has one requesting document — would have to name every recording session it is written to, and is forbidden precisely to avoid that ambiguity (G185).

**What recordings never contain.** Raw images, raw audio, model weights, activations, intermediate tensors and any input content. DT-D2 already excludes audio; this spec extends the exclusion to every ML input and to model internals. The output text is page-derived content under the existing recording rules (off by default, user-initiated per tab, encrypted at rest), and it is redactable like any page text.

**Where determinism is still required.** (a) The deterministic configuration always selects canonical mode; a job in that configuration that would select fast mode is a test failure. (b) Canonical mode is bit-identical across the three ISAs and across thread counts, tested at 1000 runs per fixture (ML-5). (c) A record produced under the deterministic configuration always carries `canonical`; `fast` is reserved for a future non-deterministic recording configuration, and the simulator rejects a `fast` record until that configuration is specified. Recomputation tests run only on canonical fixtures. (d) A recording with a missing or unknown `numeric_mode` is refused by the simulator, following Stage 1's worker-task-mode rule.

**What the recording does not capture.** ASR jobs record outputs only and are flagged `input_unavailable`, because audio is never recorded. OCR and image-description inputs are content hashes; the replay does not need to reconstruct the raster because it never recomputes. This is a deliberate loss of re-execution fidelity in exchange for a guarantee that replay cannot diverge.

### 6.9 Privacy and security (ML-D4, ML-D5, ML-D7, ML-D8)

**Privacy rules.**

- **Nothing leaves the device.** The service has no network capability; model downloads go through the update service, not the service; no telemetry; no "diagnostics" that include text. A service crash records a reason code, job id and model id, and its crash path excludes heap contents and shared-input mappings, so a minidump cannot carry an image, audio or OCR text (the security scope's crash-report rule is amended in 9.5).
- **No retention.** Input buffers are dropped and zeroed after the job; no input is written to disk; no output is stored outside the requesting document's own state and the user's recording.
- **No cross-site state.** One job at a time, no shared output cache, no model state carried between jobs beyond immutable weights and a caption session's own decoder state — bounded per session (6.2), keyed by profile, never shared between sites, and dropped when captions stop. A per-document description cache lives in the renderer and dies with the document. This is a security rule, not only a privacy preference: a cache keyed by input bytes is a cross-site membership oracle ("this site's image was described before"), and shared allocator state is what a later job could use to infer an earlier one ([SR 1.7, 1.9, 4.9][SR]).
- **The weights are the residual.** The published privacy attacks — membership inference, model inversion from confidence values, model extraction from a prediction API, and verbatim memorisation — all need query access to the model ([SR 4.1–4.7][SR]). Stage 1 removes that access structurally: no page-facing API, no confidence values returned to a page, no retention after a job. It does not make the weights private. The data ledger's consent and quarantine rules and the leakage check (6.7) are the controls for the part the service cannot remove ([SR 4.10–4.11][SR]).
- **Input minimisation.** ML-IMG receives the decoded image at the size the task needs, not the full page; ML-OCR receives the page raster the PDF viewer already produced; ML-ASR receives the audio the media pipeline is already decoding, only while captions are on.
- **Audio and captions.** Captions stop when the tab is hidden or playback stops; no always-on listening, no wake-word path, and no microphone capture in Stage 1 (media captions use the page's audio, not the microphone). A page cannot turn captions on; while the user leaves them on, a playing stream costs at most one bounded window per second, and looping media cannot exceed that rate. That rate is also the disclosed grant-state signal named in 6.3 and ML-12, and the work shaping is what keeps it a constant-rate marker rather than a content-dependent one.
- **The model manager is the disclosure.** Installed models, sizes, purpose, grant and last use are visible; a user can delete any model at any time.
- **Downloads are not a profile.** Model downloads use the same update channel as browser updates: no account, no unique identifier and no per-user log of which model was chosen; the update service sees a request for a signed public file, and no page ever sees the request.

**Security rules.**

- **The parser is the boundary.** The `.hgmodel` loader is tier P, bounded, fuzzed semantically, and validates every cross-field invariant before mapping (6.6). No model can execute code. The CVE census shows that the bug is typically two fields that disagree, not one bad length, and that byte-level fuzzing alone does not reach it ([SR 2.1–2.4][SR]).
- **The service is hardened like the broker.** No web formats beyond the declared input decoders, no page-controlled allocation sizes, no ambient handles, no writable files, strict IPC types generated from IDL, and a Kani harness per message (the IPC rule). The sandbox is tested by denial on each OS, not by its configuration ([SR 7.12][SR]).
- **Supply chain.** Weights are built by Izcal's pipeline under a per-step provenance record, signed through a threshold model-signing role under an offline root, named by hash in the signed browser manifest, and listed in the model card; a build fails on an unsigned or unlisted model ([SR 3.1–3.5, 3.10][SR]). A model from a hub is untrusted input regardless of its scan badge: Izcal loads only a model it has rebuilt and re-signed into its own manifest after verification, never a hub artefact directly; a model update is an audit event (a new data ledger digest and card) ([SR 2.12, 8.3][SR]).
- **Input decoding.** Images arrive as decoded surfaces from the existing image pipeline (MD's decoders), not as compressed bytes for the ML service to parse; audio arrives as PCM from the media pipeline. The service does not grow a second image or audio decoder.
- **Failure.** A malformed model, a failed download, a corrupt file or a crashed service produces a reason-coded failure to the caller and an entry in the model manager; nothing retries silently, nothing falls back to a different model, and no page sees an error that differs from "the feature is unavailable".
- **Denial of service is a browser outage.** Because the service is shared, an unbounded job is a browser-wide failure. Dimension products, sample counts and input durations are checked at the IPC boundary with checked arithmetic, not trusted from a header ([SR 5.7][SR]); every job has a deadline and the supervisor kills the process group on cancel or overrun, so cancellation kills work rather than asking it to stop ([SR 5.8][SR]).
- **Stage 2: prompt injection is not a prompt problem.** Stage 1 has no structure that turns page content into an instruction, because no page can invoke a model; that is the cheapest injection defence available and is not traded away ([SR 6.6][SR]). If Stage 2 adds a page-facing or agent-facing task, the defence is code the model cannot rewrite: page content enters as labelled data, tool calls are checked against a capability policy, outputs never return to the page, and high-risk actions need human approval ([SR 6.1–6.3, 6.8][SR]).
- **Threat model.** The service is a trusted process that sees cross-site content; a compromise is a **cross-site disclosure event**, not a single-site bug. It sits outside site isolation by design — the browser's answer to the same problem is one process per site at 10–13% desktop memory cost — so it earns trust with an empty capability set, no per-site state, no content-keyed cache, one job at a time, fresh activation arenas, and no page-facing endpoint ([SR 1.1–1.2, 1.7–1.9][SR]). Removing page-requested inference removes the timing oracle a page would otherwise have for model presence, job duration and task selection. One grant-state oracle remains and is not described as neutral: a page that plays audio while captions are on can detect the caption windows it caused, so it learns that the accessibility grant and the captions setting are on. That is a **disclosed accessibility-signal leak**, mitigated by constant-rate work shaping (one bounded window per second, content-independent compute, no caption state in the DOM or accessibility tree), bounded by ML-12, measured at ML-M3 and accepted by the accessibility owner before captions ship (Q-ML-14). Apart from that signal and general CPU contention that any background work produces — a page can observe that the browser is busy, not what it is doing — any page-visible latency difference with grants on and off is a fingerprinting bug ([SR 1.8][SR]; ML-12).

### 6.10 Footprint and energy (ML-D10)

| Resource | Budget | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Installer | **0 bytes of user-downloadable model weights** (the B10 security-classifier inventory is empty at 3.1.0); the runtime and service code ≤ 4 MiB† compressed, **inside FP-25's ≤ 50 MiB† budget**; if FP-M2 finds no headroom the allocation is re-cut against other lines, and FP-25 is not raised silently | No user model is shipped; every model is a user-requested download (FP-D13 is not engaged because models are not web-observable data, and the download is never page-triggered); the runtime is small because it is a scalar/vector kernel library, not a framework |
| ML service while running | ≤ 4 MiB† charged base (code, stacks, queue) | It exits when idle, so there is no FP-1 line; the footprint spec gains one conditional row |
| Activations | ≤ 96 MiB† image description, ≤ 64 MiB† OCR, ≤ 128 MiB† ASR streaming, ≤ 192 MiB† total (the largest task's 128 MiB plus 64 MiB† arena slack and the service's base; one job at a time, so the per-task figures never sum) | Arena by liveness; bounded by model size and image/page limits |
| Model weights | Mapped read-only, clean, shared; **uncharged** per FP's charged-memory definition | The same weights serve every site and every process; they are file-backed and evictable |
| Disk after opt-in | ML-IMG ≤ 200 MB† + ML-OCR ≤ 80 MB†/script + ML-ASR ≤ 150 MB† | Shown in the model manager before download; removed on disable |
| Threads | 0 at rest; ≤ min(4, performance cores)† while running | Small-model decode has little intra-op parallelism; oversubscription adds sync cost ([IK 5.14][IK]) |
| Energy | Absolute baseline per task on the reference devices at ML-M3, then no increase beyond the measured noise band (FP-22 style) | GPU/NPU comparisons and other browsers are published as information only |
| Startup | Service launch to first token, model resident, ≤ 500 ms† p95; model load from page cache ≤ 300 ms†, cold ≤ 2 s† | Weights are mmap'd; the first job pays the page-in |

The weight-mapping rule is the footprint design's key lever: a 200 MB model that is mmap'd read-only, clean and shared costs the browser's charged memory almost nothing, while the OS page cache holds it once. The spec is explicit that this is **not** a claim that the model is free: it occupies disk and page cache, it can be evicted, and the first token after eviction pays the fault cost (ML-18). The targets measure both.

**The store is per profile.** Models are immutable public data, but a shared store would let one profile's disable delete another profile's model and would reveal across profiles that someone enabled the feature. Each profile therefore keeps its own copy under its profile directory, the model manager reports that profile's disk use, and the installer still contains no user model weights. The duplication is the accepted cost: an opt-in feature that a user can turn off cleanly, with no cross-profile state (ML-9, ML-13). If two profiles enable the same model, the copies are duplicate disk and page cache; that is the price of the rule, and the model manager reports it. Cross-profile invisibility covers the store and the model manager; the shared service makes ML **activity** observable across profiles through the general contention residual named in 6.9, which is accepted for the same reasons and stated rather than implied. A future shared store would need a reference count and a per-profile disclosure rule, and is not Stage 1.

**Distribution is a real cost, carried in 11.2 rather than a budget line.** The full 430 MB† S_A set to 100 million opting-in devices is about 43 PB, roughly $0.4M–$3.7M per distribution round at retail egress prices; the derivation and its confidence are in 11.2, and the figure is replaced by the update service's measured egress before any budget depends on it ([GO 3.4][GO]).

| Alternative | Why not |
|---|---|
| Ship ONNX Runtime / OpenVINO / TFLite | Non-MIT (OpenVINO, TFLite) or a large unauditable parser (ONNX Runtime); none gives a numeric contract, data ledger or container safety; all decide numerics and threading outside Izcal's control |
| Use Core ML / Windows ML as the only runtime | Opaque placement and formats, OS-version coupling, no CPU-independent determinism, no licence ledger; they remain the **only** NPU path later (ML-D9), not the Stage 1 runtime |
| Ship Firefox's distilvit or Florence-2 weights | distilvit is Apache-2.0 and cannot ship. Florence-2's weights are MIT, but its training data is not documented as MIT-cleared or commissioned; under ML-D3 it is a fallback only with an explicit owner exception (Q-ML-11), not the plan ([MA 6.10, 6.19][MA], [DL 2.7][DL]) |
| A multi-billion-parameter VLM | Hundreds of megabytes to gigabytes, slower on CPU, no measured accessibility benefit; the 182M class is the shipping proof ([MA 1.14–1.15][MA]) |
| A per-site ML process | Multiplies the model's memory by the number of sites, or forces a shared read-only mapping with per-site state anyway; no page-facing surface exists to justify it |
| Server-side descriptions or OCR | Forbidden by MD-D25 and the PDF spec's on-device rule; also the privacy failure the feature exists to avoid |
| A page-facing inference API in Stage 1 | Would require per-site isolation, a permission model, timing-channel analysis and a much larger security surface; deferred to Stage 2 under the AI grant |
| 4-bit weights everywhere | Measured collapse on small CNNs and a 30% perplexity rise for 4-bit activations at 1B ([IK 1.9, 4.6][IK]); per-model measurement only |

## 7. M-1 compliance

**Built new (Izcal's own, anchored).** The graph IR, planner, arena, kernel set, quantisation, tokenizer, container format, loader, service, scheduler and observation types are Izcal's, each anchored to the published paper or specification that defines its behaviour: Goto–van de Geijn for GEMM, Jacob et al. for integer quantisation, Krishnamoorthi for per-channel scales, Dao et al. for tiled attention, Lavin–Gray for the Winograd rejection, Milakov–Gimelshein for online softmax, Blumofe–Leiserson for work stealing, Sennrich et al. for BPE, and the IEEE 754-2019 text for the float contract. No framework, engine or library source is read; black-box comparators are shipped binaries measured from outside ([IK §7][IK], [MA §6][MA]).

**Pinned MIT dependencies.** `memmap2` (MIT OR Apache-2.0, MIT elected) for read-only mapping; `bytemuck` (MIT OR Apache-2.0 OR Zlib, MIT elected) for checked casts. Hashes and signature verification come from `hg-crypto` (the networking spec's crate; no new cryptography); transcendentals come from `hg-math` (the JavaScript engine spec's scalar math crate) or Izcal's canonical routines in `hg-ml`, never the platform libm, so the ML runtime and the JS engine share one rounding contract. No BLAS, no `ndarray`, no tokenizer crate, no compression crate. Each pin is resolved alone with `cargo tree`; the licence audit's OD-L8 requires the MIT-only `cargo-deny` allow list, and both checks run in CI.

**Training toolchain.** Not shipped, not linked, not evidence for runtime design. The model card records it. The shipped artefact is weights plus Izcal's runtime; the weights' licence is MIT and their data ledger is the provenance record (ML-D3).

**Patents and royalties.** Nothing Izcal ships in its own code is patent-encumbered: the `.hgmodel` container, the graph IR, the kernels and the loader are Izcal's own or implement published algorithms, and the MIT rule keeps every third-party weight set out unless Q-ML-11 is answered yes. MIT is a copyright licence with no express patent grant, so the licence check is necessary but not sufficient. A freedom-to-operate review of the Stage 1 operator set and of the published techniques the kernels rely on is a ML-M0 deliverable ([IK §7][IK]); an operator found encumbered is replaced by a published alternative, or licensed only on an explicit owner decision before the first release, following MD-D3's precedent. OS ML runtimes, if Stage 2 adopts one, are OS interfaces carrying the OS vendor's patent position, as OS decoders do in the media stack.

**Anchors and oracles.** Every operator and kernel carries `// spec:` anchors (G189). Oracles: operator-level reference tests against Izcal's canonical interpreter, differential testing of fast kernels against canonical, quantised-model parity against the float model, black-box peers for quality only (Firefox's alt-text path, Chrome's OCR path, Tesseract, Whisper), and the semantic fuzz corpus for the loader, run under address sanitizers in continuous fuzzing, because a parser's bugs must be found before an attacker finds them ([SR 7.8–7.9][SR]). The eval corpora are test-only and their licences are recorded in the data ledger's evaluation rows.

**Evidence discipline.** Papers, standards prose, model cards, licence files, vendor documentation, published measurements and Izcal's own benchmarks are the evidence classes used throughout ([HP][HP], [MA][MA], [IK][IK], [DL][DL], [SR][SR], [GO][GO], [SS][SS]). No repository source file was opened; licence files and model cards were read as data. Values marked † are set from first principles by this document.

## 9. Milestones and staffing

### 9.1 What is staged, and why

Four stagings, each for a correctness, licence or evidence reason, not a date: (1) **the model set is staged by promise**: descriptions (MD-D25, R4) first, OCR (PDF) second, captions (MD-D25) third, because that is the order of the record's existing commitments and because captioning has the tightest latency budget; (2) **Stage 2 tasks are behind the AI grant and the `ai` feature**, so the Stage 1 binary contains no Stage 2 task code (B10); (3) **the NPU is not built in Stage 1**, because no Stage 1 task has a measured 1.5× win and the OS runtimes are opaque (ML-D9); (4) **4-bit weights are per-model and evidence-gated**, never a default (6.4). No model is staged by convenience: each Stage 1 model is built and evaluated before its feature is offered.

## 11. Open questions and flagged claims

### 11.1 Open questions

- **Q-ML-1.** Does B10's accessibility setting legitimately cover **ASR captions**, or is captioning a general feature that belongs under the AI switch? This spec puts it in S_A because captions are an accessibility function and B10's R0–R4 list does not mention them; the owner should confirm the boundary, and the answer changes only which grant authorises ML-ASR, not the model.
- **Q-ML-2.** What is the acceptable first-cue delay for generated captions? No published threshold was found ([MA 3.8][MA]); ML-4's 1.0 s† is an Izcal measurement question, set with deaf and hard-of-hearing participants at ML-M3.
- **Q-ML-3.** What exact structure do recognised text runs take for PDF/UA? PDF/UA-2's ISO number and date were unverified in this pass ([MA 7.3][MA]); PD-D11's text-run schema is the integration point and is settled with the PDF spec's owner at ML-M2.
- **Q-ML-4.** Where does training compute come from, and at what cost? The models are Izcal-trained (ML-D3), so compute and annotation are a funded line, not an engineering detail. The plan's funding decision is still deferred; this spec's milestones assume it is resolved before ML-M1.
- **Q-ML-5.** Is Izcal a "provider" under the EU AI Act for generated descriptions and captions, and what machine-readable marking satisfies Article 50(2)? The legal analysis is due at ML-M0; the design already carries provenance structurally ([MA 5.4][MA]).
- **Q-ML-6.** Speaker diarisation and speaker labels for captions: no verified latency or comprehension evidence was found ([MA 3.9][MA]); Stage 1 captions carry none, and the question is reopened with user evidence.
- **Q-ML-7.** GPU placement for Stage 2 models: the Stage 1 models do not need it, and the GPU service's scheduling contract would have to grow an inference queue. Revisit at ML-M5 with measurements.
- **Q-ML-8.** Does a future page-facing ML API (Stage 2) keep the shared service, or split per site? ML-D5's reversal trigger states the options; the security scope and the DOM spec own the answer with this spec. Whatever the answer, a page-facing API must not return confidence values, must rate-limit per site, and must be isolated per site or not ship, because it restores the query access the published privacy attacks need ([SR 4.11, 6.7][SR]).
- **Q-ML-9 (the licensing strand's Q-D1).** Do CC0 1.0 and public domain count as allowed training data? They impose no conditions but are not MIT. Recommendation: **yes**, as a named exception recorded in the ledger, mirroring OD-L6. This is the decision that admits Common Voice and Project Gutenberg; without it, speech training is commission-only.
- **Q-ML-10 (the licensing strand's Q-D2).** Does CC BY 4.0 count for training? Recommendation: **no**, evaluation only. Answering yes would admit LibriSpeech, FLEURS, MLS, COCO annotations and Open Images annotations, each with an attribution ledger; it would not solve the image-level problem, because the images are not licensed by the annotations.
- **Q-ML-11 (the licensing strand's Q-D4).** May Izcal ship a third-party MIT weight whose training data is undocumented (Florence-2, Donut, BLIP-2, GIT, Moonshine)? Under the letter of the MIT rule the shipped artifact is MIT; the recommendation is **no by default**, because the data chain is the right the rule exists to protect. If yes, the card records the data statement as undocumented and the model is measured against the Stage 1 quality bars.
- **Q-ML-12.** What is the commissioned-data budget? 6.7's plan is $67k–$200k for 50,000–100,000 images with five captions each, roughly $370k† for 1,000 hours of speech, and training compute besides. The owner must answer with a funded corpus plan before ML-M1, because **ML-D3 is conditional on it**; without an answer the track starts with evaluation-only work and no training run, rather than stalling after a pilot.
- **Q-ML-13.** Does downloading a model at the user's explicit enable action satisfy ePrivacy Article 5(3)'s consent requirement for storing information on terminal equipment, or does it need a separate consent record? The EDPB's 2024 guidelines cover the technical scope of Article 5(3), but no regulator has decided a browser model download, and the Chrome case produced an allegation and a denial, not a ruling ([GO 4.6][GO], Low). The legal analysis is due with Q-ML-5 at ML-M0; the design already downloads only at an explicit, sized, user-initiated action, which is the strongest factual position available.
- **Q-ML-14.** Does the accessibility owner accept the **caption grant-state signal** — a page that plays audio can detect that captions are on from the contention of caption windows — with the constant-rate work shaping and the ML-M3 detectability measurement in ML-12? The alternative is not to offer generated captions, because no design that does work while media plays can be invisible to the page that plays it without constant dummy work. The answer is required before captions ship; it is the one accessibility-signal leak in this spec.
- **Q-ML-15.** Who holds the three model-signing keys, and is the macOS per-process sandbox profile the right mechanism? Both are specified with the security-scope owner at ML-M0: 2-of-3 custody (offline ceremony, build HSM, security lead) and rotation/revocation by the other two keys; on macOS the profile language rather than App Sandbox entitlements, whose confinement is to the app bundle. Until confirmed, the signing and sandbox claims take no stronger form than 6.2 and 6.6 state.

### 11.2 Claims that are single-source, unverified or conflicting

- **Chrome's 77-language on-device OCR and its 1–2 s/page latency** are a vendor claim with no per-language character-error rate and a secondary latency report: Google's ChromeOS.dev page states the language count and seven scripts, and the latency is not published at all ([GO 2.4, 6.1][GO], [MA 2.3, 2.16, 7.1][MA], Low). ML-3's comparator uses Chrome's shipped path in the same lab, not the published figures.
- **Whisper's weight licence conflicts** between the repository's MIT LICENSE and the Hugging Face card's Apache-2.0 ([MA 3.7, 6.1][MA]). The conflict is a reason to treat Whisper as a test-only oracle, not a candidate.
- **Firefox's "about 2 seconds"** is Mozilla's own statement without a device specification ([MA 1.14][MA], Medium); ML-18's latency target is an Izcal measurement on named reference devices, not a copy of it.
- **Core ML's placement** is undocumented in Apple's public pages; "Core ML makes its own placement" is architectural inference from the API shape, not a quoted document ([IK 6.3][IK], Low).
- **The ASSETS 2021 "Minimum Viable Descriptions" details** were read only from a search snippet; the study's content is used as direction, not as a measured requirement ([MA 1.28][MA], Low).
- **Chrome Live Caption's model size, energy use and language-pack count** are undocumented; only the launch language (English) and the on-device, offline behaviour are sourced ([HP 3.1–3.2][HP], Medium). They are context, not targets.
- **The NPU energy and dispatch figures** come from individual published measurements on specific devices ([IK 5.5, 6.7, 6.10][IK]); they justify the gate, not a prediction for any Izcal target.
- **The 30–36-month† three-model duration** is a placeholder derived from the model-training sequence, not a measured velocity, and it is bounded by Stage 1's 24–36-month† window; the ML-M1 review replaces it (9.3).
- **COCO's annotation licence** (CC BY 4.0) and the Flickr image licences behind it are Medium: the terms page did not render and the wording is reproduced by secondary sources ([DL 3.1][DL]). Flickr30k's research-only terms are High ([DL 3.4][DL]). The data ledger records both before any use, and evaluation-only use is D4 either way.
