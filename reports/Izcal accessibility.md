Izcal has no producer for the accessibility tree that every other spec consumes: HGBA-3 B8 and Stage 1 5.8 planned to feed the operating systems through AccessKit, and the owner-accepted JS-D11 makes that engine work Izcal builds itself (ledger G-10, G-17). The history is unusually clear. **Every operating-system accessibility API is synchronous and pull-based, and every multi-process browser that tried to answer those pulls by asking its renderers paid for it**: Chrome's first design was "a dead-end"; with Firefox 57's partial cache and synchronous IPC, "the performance with assistive technologies took a massive step backwards", and its Cache the World rewrite (2021–23) cut NVDA's load of a 12,000-row table from 128 s to 6 s and of the WHATWG HTML specification from 175 s to 15 s and was expected to let Mozilla remove about 20,000 lines ([Teh, "Cache the World"](https://www.jantrid.net/2022/12/22/Cache-the-World/); [HP 2.11–2.17][HP]). Windows screen readers still build their browse-mode buffers inside the browser process through IAccessible2, and NVDA's maintainers said in November 2025 that they "cannot move the default to UIA" ([nvaccess/nvda#19276](https://github.com/nvaccess/nvda/issues/19276); [NVDA 2026.2 User Guide](https://download.nvaccess.org/documentation/userGuide.html); [PA 2.3, 2.7][PA]). The parent process's tree code is now a primary sandbox-escape surface: 16 Chrome accessibility CVEs in 2026, most needing a compromised renderer, and Mozilla bug titles that name self-referential trees, ID-0 cycles and double parents ([NVD query](https://services.nvd.nist.gov/rest/json/cves/2.0?keywordSearch=Accessibility%20in%20Google%20Chrome&keywordExactMatch); [SR 4.1–4.7][SR]). And any accessibility signal content can observe reveals that a user is disabled, which is why AOM's AT events died ([AOM explainer](https://wicg.github.io/aom/explainer.html); [W3C Design Principles §2.11](https://www.w3.org/TR/design-principles/); [HP 5.2–5.3][HP]). **The central idea: the renderer computes the semantic tree on its own thread from inputs the engine produces whether or not anyone is listening, so the script thread does the same work either way; it pushes per-field diffs to a broker mirror that validates every update as hostile and answers every operating-system query locally, through adapters Izcal writes for UIA and IAccessible2, NSAccessibility, AT-SPI and Android; and every name, event and platform mapping is explainable, replayable and anchored to its specification.** The decisions that matter most: (1) the tree is computed on its own thread from inputs the engine produces at every level (the sealed mutation-log segment, style change records and the immutable fragment epoch), so nothing on the script thread depends on whether anyone is listening (AX-D3); (2) the broker answers every OS query from a validated mirror and no adapter can wait on a renderer (AX-D4); (3) "accessibility on" is replaced by per-document levels driven by what clients actually ask (AX-D6); (4) Windows gets both UIA and MSAA with IAccessible2, the accessibility surface runs on its own broker thread, and whether the broker can keep code-integrity mitigations with screen readers injecting is measured, not assumed (AX-D7, AX-D9); (5) AT actions enter the page as the full keyboard or pointer sequence they stand for, with the remaining differences published, and security prompts accept them only with evidence that a person is present (AX-D11); (6) operating-system preferences are exposed exactly as the specifications define and no finer, with forced colours honest because they cannot be hidden (AX-D18).

## 1. The problem and who has it

Izcal has no producer for the accessibility tree that every other spec consumes. HGBA-3 B8 and Stage 1 Section 5.8 planned to feed the operating systems through AccessKit, used unmodified; the JavaScript engine spec's owner-accepted **JS-D11** classifies AccessKit as engine work, so the Phase 3 ledger lists the gap twice: **G-10** (B8 and 5.8 rest on a superseded dependency) and **G-17** (accessibility unowned) ([FR][FR]). Meanwhile the layout spec supplies bounds, line records, `reading-flow` order and the `content-visibility` rule (A-S1-45), the text stack supplies per-character bounds and word boundaries (A-S1-44), the graphics spec paints forced colours with backplates (GR-D25), the media spec promises accessible native controls (MD-12) and OS caption styles (MD 6.12), the DevTools accessibility view reads "the AccessKit update stream" (DT-D6), and the shell "exposes its tree through the same AccessKit path as content" (shell 4.9). Each of those sentences now points at nothing.

| Who | What they need | What goes wrong today (evidence) |
|---|---|---|
| **Screen-reader users** (Windows 90.7% of primary screen-reader use; JAWS 55.0%, NVDA 32.9%, VoiceOver 6.5%, Orca 1.4%) ([UX 1.2, 1.7][UX]) | A complete, fast, correct tree in the browse-mode reader they already use; nothing that tells a site they use AT | NVDA builds its browse-mode buffer **inside the browser process** through IAccessible2 and falls back to UIA only when it cannot inject ([PA 2.3, 2.6][PA]); a sandboxed multi-process browser that answered queries by synchronous IPC was 21× slower on a 12,000-row table (128 s against 6 s) than one answering from a cache ([HP 2.17][HP]); AOM's AT events were dropped because they reveal AT use ([HP 5.2–5.3][HP]) |
| **Magnifier, switch and voice-control users** | Accurate bounds, an "actionable" flag, names that match visible labels, a caret the magnifier can follow | WCAG 2.5.3 exists because names that differ from visible text break speech commands ([UX 5.1][UX]); a custom-drawn caret is invisible to Windows magnifiers unless mirrored as a system caret ([PA 3.4][PA]) |
| **Low-vision, motion-sensitive and cognitively disabled users without AT** | Forced colours, zoom and text scaling that reflow, reduced motion, captions in their style | 37% of Android and 34% of iOS users change the system text size, yet under 10% of sites honour it on Android ([UX 1.21, 7.3][UX]); on macOS no major browser responds to OS text scaling ([UX 7.4][UX]); one engine ignores OS caption preferences (MD 3) |
| **Developers and accessibility practitioners** | "Why does the screen reader say this?", "what did it receive?", "does it differ by platform?" | Computed role and name are 87–97% interoperable in WPT, but the platform layer is barely measured ([SS 8.5–8.7, 9.5][SS]); the AAMs leave text, caret and hypertext unspecified ([SS 5.12][SS]) |
| **Agents** (B9, AI on) | The semantic tree as a compact observation format | Agents read accessibility trees through debug protocols today (HGBA-3 B8); their needs must not distort what AT users get |
| **Izcal's engineers** | A tree that is deterministic (B1), checkable against a from-scratch path (B5), bounded in memory, and safe when a renderer is compromised (I-1, IPC-R17) | Servo's AccessKit adapter **panics** on out-of-order updates and has no iframe support ([HP 4.7][HP]); Chrome says 5–10% of users have accessibility on, often because of password managers and antivirus software, and calls the cost on Android "steep" ([HP 2.8, 2.10][HP]) |

**What Izcal's specs already decide.** HGBA-2 2.3.3: M-1 (no engine libraries, MIT only, spec anchors, no third-party project's source ever evidence, screen readers included), P1–P6, I-1 (process per (site, container), cross-site iframes out of process). HGBA-3: **B8** (semantic plane with stable IDs, provenance, visibility flags, snapshots and diffs, repair overlay), B9, **B10** (repair classes R0–R4; "accessibility unaffected" by the AI switch; R0 OS preferences never normalized), A11Y-1 to A11Y-4, Q-10. Stage 1: G1, G4, **G8** (`hg-semantic-repair` cannot write content state), G9, 5.8. JavaScript engine: **JS-D4** (one-copy DOM; workers read node columns only during a flush with the script thread blocked), **JS-D15** (timing-dependent decisions are recorded B1 inputs), **JS-D11**. Style: the environment snapshot (one per document per frame; D-S1 quantization in one place), ST-D17. Layout: **LY-16** (AT leaves no layout trace), LY-D11, G38, fragment handles with epochs. Footprint: the semantic tree is a class (a) store built only when AT is active; FP-1's broker budget of 20 MiB†. Security scope 0.3: **CON-R1** (consent only from broker-observed input), **CON-R3** (the broker owns native windows and routes input), IPC-R1 to IPC-R17, and the broker's code-integrity and extension-point blocking "to be measured at M5 against the IME and assistive-technology compatibility list". Shell UI design: UP4 (the shell is a page), 4.9, UI-1, UI-2. DevTools: DT-D6, DT-8. The full list is in the frame note ([FR][FR]).

**Success** is measured by Section 8: WPT and platform-API conformance at or above the best shipping engine; screen-reader task parity with Chrome measured with real readers and real users; every OS query answered without touching a renderer; AT use invisible to content; no compromised renderer able to crash the broker or reach another site's tree; and a memory and CPU cost of zero with no AT client and a bounded one with.

## 2. History: what came before and why it was built that way

### 2.1 Chronology

| Year | Project | What it did | Why that way | Fate and why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1997 | **MSAA** (`IAccessible`, WinEvents) | One COM interface, fixed roles, broadcast events | A Windows 95 add-in for in-process clients; COM was the extension model | Survives as the base of IA2. No text model, cannot be extended without breaking the interface, "tends to run slowly" out of process, so screen readers **inject code** into applications ([HP 1.1–1.4][HP]) |
| 2001 | **ATK / AT-SPI** over CORBA | Toolkit-side ATK, client-side AT-SPI | Sun's GNOME accessibility programme; CORBA was GNOME's object bus | Moved to D-Bus with GNOME 3.0 (April 2011) because ORBit was deprecated and memory-hungry; the pull design was made before per-app sandboxing and assumed X11 global coordinates ([HP 1.14–1.17][HP]) |
| 2002–14 | **NSAccessibility** | Key-based attribute API (from about 10.2, recollection), method-based protocol (10.10, 2014) | Per-view objects answering synchronous queries on the main thread | Survives. macOS 26 (2025) makes the web attributes public SDK constants (`AXWebConstants.h`): about 59† web attributes, 32† text-marker parameterised attributes and 42† search keys, counts measured black-box at AX-M0 ([PA 4.7–4.9][PA]) |
| 2005–09 | **UI Automation** | Providers and clients, control patterns, TextPattern, registered events | Out-of-process clients without injection | First managed-only (2005), rewritten as COM for Windows 7 (2009) ([HP 1.6–1.8][HP]). Still one cross-process call per property; web semantics second-class: one live-region event, one `LabeledBy` target, landmarks as "custom" ([HP 1.26][HP]) |
| 2006 | **IAccessible2** | Extends MSAA with relations, text, tables, attributes | IBM's ODF accessibility project; extends MSAA so vendors kept their MSAA investment | Little change since IA2 1.3 (drafted 2010–11, published 2013) yet still NVDA's default for web content in 2026 ([HP 1.10–1.13][HP]) |
| 2008 | **Chrome** sandboxed renderers | Content leaves the process screen readers inject into | Security | Chrome first proxied synchronous queries through the UI process, so the entire UI and the content process were blocked until each query finished, then moved to a **push model**: renderers serialize tree updates; the browser caches and answers ([HP 2.2–2.4][HP]) |
| 2009–12 | **Android** `AccessibilityNodeInfo`, virtual views | Pull over Binder; virtual hierarchies (API 16) | Services run in their own process | Survives; prefetch is capped at 50 nodes per request ([HP 1.24][HP]; [PA 7.3][PA]) |
| 2015–20 | **Edge Legacy** (EdgeHTML), UIA-only | Web content through UIA alone | Microsoft's own API, no injection | At launch "no 3rd party assistive technologies were able to provide any meaningful access"; two years later browse mode was still "significantly slower" and had no live regions ([PA 2.9][PA]) |
| 2017 | **Firefox 57** multi-process accessibility | Minimal parent cache; other queries relayed synchronously; COM handler with partial cache | Shipping multiprocess was urgent; a full cache risked wrong information | Performance "took a massive step backwards"; COM "was never designed to handle the massive number of objects"; deadlock risk ([HP 2.11–2.15][HP]) |
| 2017– | **AOM** | ARIA reflection, custom-element semantics, AT events, virtual nodes, computed tree | Give scripts direct access to the accessibility layer | Reflection and ElementInternals shipped; AT events and virtual nodes **abandoned** because they reveal AT use; the computed tree became test-only ([HP 5.1–5.3][HP]) |
| 2019–26 | **WebKit isolated tree** | A tree ATs read from a secondary thread | The stated reason is "performance"; main-thread hangs are an inference | Made unconditional on macOS in January 2026 ([HP 2.24–2.26][HP]) |
| 2021–23 | **Firefox Cache the World** | Content processes push; the parent answers every query from its cache | Synchronous IPC was slow, deadlock-prone and 19,000 lines of Windows-only code | 12,000-row table 128 s → 6 s; WHATWG HTML spec 175 s → 15 s; 2–3× on everyday tasks; about 20,000 lines expected to become removable ([HP 2.14–2.18][HP]) |
| 2021– | **AccessKit** | A push-based schema and adapters for five platforms | Make non-standard toolkits accessible | Excluded for Izcal by JS-D11; its own README says the adapters "don't yet support rich text or hypertext" ([HP 4.3–4.4][HP]) |
| 2022–24 | **Newton** (GNOME) | Push trees over a Wayland protocol, frame-synchronized | Sandboxed apps, no global coordinates | Prototype only (June 2024); no 2025–26 status found ([PA 6.1–6.8][PA]) |
| 2024–25 | **Chromium native UIA** | Native UIA provider beside IA2 | Narrator and Voice Access need UIA | On by default in Chrome 138; MSAA and IA2 clients still connect directly, "that's not changing"; NVDA maintainers: "We cannot move the default to UIA" (November 2025) ([PA 2.7, 2.10–2.11][PA]) |
| 2025–26 | **`ariaNotify`** | Page-requested announcements | Live regions fail on timing and registration | In all three engines (Chrome 141, Firefox 150, Safari 27); designed to avoid timing differences that reveal a screen reader ([HP 5.4–5.7][HP]; [UX 3.15][UX]) |
| 2026 | **WPT `aamtest`** | Tests that query AT-SPI, UIA, IA2 and AX like an AT | The platform layer had no shared tests | Merged 27 March 2026; macOS not yet in CI ([SS 9.4–9.5][SS]) |

### 2.2 Technology mismatches: assumptions that no longer hold

| Assumption | Born with | Why it no longer holds | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content lives in the process the AT can inject into | MSAA, IA2, virtual buffers | Sandboxing (2008) and site isolation (2018) moved content away; injection now lands in the browser's privileged process | [HP 2.1–2.2, 6.2][HP] |
| The OS can ask the app synchronously and get a fast answer | Every OS API | True only if the answer is local; across processes it costs 12–21× | [HP 2.17, 3.1][HP] |
| Asynchronous OS APIs will come | A recurring hope | Nine obstacles; the author of Cache the World is "skeptical about the return on investment" | [HP 3.2][HP] |
| "Accessibility on" is one global switch set by detecting an AT | Chrome's heuristics | 5–10% of users have it on, often because of password managers and antivirus software; Linux detection was paused in 2020 for false positives | [HP 2.7–2.8, 6.4][HP] |
| Apps know global screen coordinates | X11-era AT-SPI | Wayland has none; AT-SPI 3 plans to remove screen coordinates | [PA 5.11][PA] |
| The web fits the OS role model | MSAA, UIA | UIA has one live-region event and one label target; MathML cells are "TBD" for MSAA, IA2 and UIA | [HP 1.26][HP]; [SS 1.7][SS] |
| The specifications define what an engine exposes | AAM documents | Text, caret, text attributes and hypertext are absent from the AAMs; 20 points are open | [SS 5.12, 11][SS] |
| Accessibility work is invisible to pages | Single-threaded engines | Main-thread accessibility work is a timing signal; CSS Containment 2 now forbids it for skipped content | [SS 6.2][SS] |

## 3. Today: the landscape and its gaps

**The landscape in October 2026.** Chrome and Firefox have both converged on renderer push plus a parent-process cache; WebKit uses a secondary-thread tree inside its content process ([HP 2.25, 6.3][HP]). Chrome serves UIA natively since Chrome 138 and keeps IA2 ([PA 2.11][PA]); Firefox answers every query from its cache since Firefox 113 ([HP 2.18][HP]); WebKit made its secondary-thread tree unconditional on macOS in January 2026 ([HP 2.25][HP]). Computed role and name are 87–97% interoperable in WPT, and accessibility is an Interop 2026 investigation at 66.1% progress ([SS 8.4–8.7][SS]). The platform layer has only just got shared tests (`aamtest`, merged March 2026, no macOS CI yet, [SS 9.4–9.5][SS]); ARIA-AT's July 2026 minutes count 21 test plans in interop reporting, while its API in October 2026 counts 3 Recommended of 45 plans (the two counts use different categories, 11.2), and AT Driver has one editor ([GO 6.1–6.2][GO]; [SS 9.7][SS]). Screen-reader users are overwhelmingly on Windows with JAWS or NVDA and a Chromium browser (70.9% of pairs, [UX 1.6][UX]), and their main complaints have not changed in 14 years ([UX 1.14][UX]).

**The security picture changed in 2026.** NVD lists 16 Chrome accessibility CVEs in 2026 alone, most needing a compromised renderer and several stated as sandbox escapes or site-isolation bypasses; Mozilla's accessibility component has 36 public security bugs filed in 2026, against 1–9 a year in 2018–2025 ([SR 4.1–4.6][SR]; corrected count in [FC][FC]). The parent-process tree code, written in C++ in both engines, is now a primary escape surface.

| **Agent observations** | Full snapshots cost 8k–50k tokens on ordinary pages; no tool offers stable ids across mutations or diffs ([PE 7.4][PE]; [GO 7.6][GO]) | Stable-ID diffs with provenance (B8, 6.14) |
| **Automated checking of what AT receives** | Checkers run in page script against the DOM and find 13–57% of issues depending on how one counts ([GO 5.1–5.4][GO]) | Checks over the semantic plane and adapter output (6.15) |
| **OS text scaling on the desktop web** | No major macOS browser responds to OS text scaling; Android sites honour it under 10% of the time ([UX 7.3–7.4][UX]) | One zoom model with OS text scale on every platform (6.11) |
| **Rich text in toolkit adapters** | AccessKit's adapters "don't yet support rich text or hypertext" ([HP 4.4][HP]) | One text model across four platforms (6.8) |

**What Izcal could expose that no one does** ([GO 11][GO]): the derivation of every name, linked to the specification and its tests; the per-platform mapping of every node, side by side; the record of every event sent, merged, dropped or throttled, with its reason; deterministic replay of a screen-reader session against a page; and agent observations with stable ids, diffs and provenance that never become a second tree for authors to optimize at screen-reader users' expense ([GO 7.8–7.9][GO]).

**Which interactions are new.** A developer on macOS asking "what would NVDA get here?" and getting the IA2 and UIA projection from the same code that serves NVDA; a screen-reader user attaching a replayable recording to a bug report instead of a description; an auditor running checks against what the OS actually received.

## 4. First principles

### 4.1 Users' real tasks

| # | Task | Who | What it needs from the engine |
|---|---|---|---|
| U1 | Read and navigate a page by structure (headings first: 67.8% of screen-reader users start there) | Screen-reader users | The whole tree, including off-screen content, with roles, levels and landmarks, answered in microseconds ([UX 1.12, 3.4][UX]) |
| U2 | Operate controls, forms, menus, dialogs | All AT users | Correct roles, states and names; focus that follows; actions that behave as keyboard or pointer input does; events when state changes ([UX 1.14][UX]) |
| U3 | Read and edit text: by character, word, line, sentence; select and copy | Screen-reader, braille, magnifier users | One text model with stable positions across element boundaries; caret and selection settable by the AT; formatting attributes ([UX 3.3, 3.5][UX]; [PA 3.1–3.4, 4.6–4.8][PA]) |
| U4 | Hear changes they did not cause | Screen-reader users | Live regions and `ariaNotify` delivered reliably, once, in order, without depending on when the AT happened to register ([HP 5.4–5.6][HP]) |
| U5 | Point, speak or scan at things on screen | Magnifier, voice, switch users | Bounds, hit testing, an actionable flag, names that contain the visible label ([UX 5.1–5.5][UX]) |
| U6 | See the page in their colours, at their size, without motion, with their caption style | Low-vision, vestibular, deaf and hard-of-hearing users | Forced colours, text scale and zoom that reflow, reduced motion and transparency, OS caption styles ([UX 6, 7, 8][UX]) |
| U7 | Use the browser itself: tabs, address bar, prompts, settings, downloads | Every AT user | The shell and prompts exposed exactly as pages are; F6 cycling; prompts announced without stealing focus ([UX 4.7–4.8, 9.5][UX]) |
| U8 | Not be identified as an AT user | Every AT user | Nothing content can observe (timing, events, layout work, API results) differs with AT on or off ([HP 5.3][HP]; [SS 6.2][SS]) |
| U9 | Find out why the screen reader says what it says | Developers, auditors | The tree, how each name was computed, what each platform received, and when ([SS 3.9, 11][SS]) |

### 4.2 Platform primitives

- **The specifications**: WAI-ARIA 1.2 (Recommendation) and 1.3 (draft), AccName 1.2, Core-AAM 1.2 (now with Android), HTML-AAM, SVG-AAM, MathML-AAM (editor's draft only), DPUB-ARIA and DPUB-AAM 1.1, Graphics ARIA and Graphics-AAM 1.0, CSS Display 3 and 4, CSS Contain 2 §4.6, CSS Color Adjust 1, Media Queries 5, WebVTT, HTML's focus, `inert`, top-layer and `ElementInternals` sections ([SS 1, 6, 7][SS]). Most are drafts, so each is pinned at a dated snapshot (M-1 part 2).
- **The OS interfaces**: UIA, MSAA with IAccessible2 1.3, NSAccessibility with the HIServices text-marker API and the macOS 26 web constants, AT-SPI2 on the accessibility bus, Android `AccessibilityNodeProvider` ([PA][PA]). All are **synchronous and pull-based** at the client edge; none can be changed by Izcal.
- **The engine's own data**: the one-copy DOM and its mutation log (JS-D4, B4), computed style and the environment snapshot (ST), the immutable fragment tree with paint order and per-character geometry (LY, TX), the frame tree and focus in the broker (CON-R3), the deterministic substrate (B1).

### 4.3 Izcal's principles that bind this design

P1 (security and privacy first): renderers have no OS access, the broker trusts no renderer (IPC-R17), and AT use is private data. P2 (compatibility): the bar is what JAWS and NVDA users get in Chrome today, because "existing comfort/expertise" decides which browser they use (49.3%, [UX 9.4][UX]). P3 (robustness): a hung renderer must not hang the screen reader. P4: the cost with no AT client is zero, and with one it is bounded. P6: every number here is measured or marked †. M-1: no AccessKit, no engine code; adapters written from OS documentation and black-box observation.

### 4.4 Assumptions kept and broken

| Inherited assumption | Keep or break | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| The privileged process answers OS queries from a cache that renderers push | **Keep** | Every OS API is synchronous; Chrome and Firefox both reached this after costly detours; 12–21× faster ([HP 2.4, 2.16–2.17, 6.3][HP]) |
| The tree is computed on the main (script) thread | **Break** | It is a timing signal (Contain 2 §4.6) and Chrome's full mode tripled main-thread cost in a black-box test ([PE 8.3][PE]); Izcal computes it on a semantic thread from inputs produced at every level (AX-D3) |
| "Accessibility on" is one global mode, entered by guessing that an AT is present | **Break** | 5–10% false positives; graded, demand-driven materialization instead (AX-D6) |
| A changed node is re-sent whole | **Break** | Chrome had to add a bounds-only fast path (up to 825%); diffs are per field from the start ([PE 1.5, 4.3][PE]) |
| Bounds are absolute | **Break** | Store bounds relative to scroll containers and transforms; scrolling sends container offsets only ([PE 4.1–4.2][PE]) |
| Live regions announce whatever the AT happens to observe | **Break** | Announcements are computed deterministically in the renderer from tree diffs, so a region is live from the moment it enters the tree ([HP 5.4][HP]) |
| Windows needs IA2 as well as UIA | **Keep** | NVDA defaults to IA2 by injection, and its maintainers will not move the default until Chromium-class UIA matches IA2 in browse mode (November 2025); Edge Legacy shows the cost of UIA-only ([PA 2.6–2.9][PA]) |
| ATs inject code into the browser | **Keep, contained** | It is how NVDA and JAWS work; the broker tolerates it and the cost is recorded in the security scope (AX-D9) |
| Node IDs are per frame; the browser stitches frames | **Keep, harden** | Chrome uses unguessable tree IDs; Izcal's broker mints embedding tokens and never trusts a renderer's claim about where its tree attaches ([PE 2.1][PE]) |
| Hit testing asks the renderer | **Break** | Push a paint-ordered list of hit regions with each update, as Firefox does and Newton chose; no synchronous renderer call ([PE 2.4–2.5][PE]) |
| AT actions are distinct from user input | **Break, with published residuals** | AT actions enter the renderer as the full keyboard or pointer sequence they stand for; the differences that remain are listed (AX-D11) |
| The shell's UI has its own accessibility path | **Break** | The shell is a page (UP4); its tree arrives through the same protocol as any renderer's |
| The AAMs specify the text model | **Break (they do not)** | Izcal designs one text model from OS documentation and AT behaviour ([SS 5.12][SS]) |
| The tree may lag the DOM by an unknown amount | **Break** | Each semantic update carries the flush number it reflects; tests wait on it, removing the flakiness WPT's `aamtest` reports ([SS 9.4][SS]) |

## 5. Lessons learned

Lessons are ideas and evidence, never code or a template (M-1).

| Project | What happened | Lesson | How Izcal applies it |
|---|---|---|---|
| MSAA | Out-of-process clients were slow, so screen readers injected code ([HP 1.4][HP]) | A pull API across a process boundary pushes clients into the server's address space | Answer every query in the process the AT talks to, from local memory (AX-D4); accept injection where ATs still need it (AX-D9) |
| Chrome's first multi-process design | One blocking IPC per query; "a dead-end" ([PE 1.1][PE]) | Never let an OS query wait on a renderer | G102: no adapter path can name a renderer endpoint |
| Firefox 57 | Partial cache plus synchronous IPC; COM re-entry and deadlocks; screen readers regressed ([HP 2.11–2.13][HP]) | A half cache is worse than either extreme | The mirror is complete for the level it serves (AX-D6); no "fetch the rest synchronously" path exists |
| Firefox Cache the World | Push and cache; 12–21× faster; about 20,000 lines expected to become removable ([HP 2.16–2.17][HP]) | The full cache is simpler as well as faster | One platform-neutral mirror; adapters are translators over it (AX-D4) |
| Chrome's dirty-node serialization | Whole nodes re-sent; bounds-only fast path gave up to 825% on scroll tests ([PE 1.5, 4.3][PE]) | Diff by field, and keep geometry relative | Per-field diffs; bounds relative to scroll containers and transforms; scroll sends offsets (6.4) |
| Chrome's AT detection | 5–10% of desktop and ~7% of Android users run the code; Linux detection paused for false positives ([PE 5.7–5.8][PE]) | Guessing who is an AT is wrong both ways | Demand-driven levels set by what clients ask, not by who they seem to be (AX-D6) |
| Chrome and Firefox, 2026 | 16 Chrome accessibility CVEs in 2026 (31 since 2020), 12 of the 2026 ones needing a compromised renderer; Mozilla titles name self-referential trees, ID-0 cycles, double parents, cross-actor forgery ([SR 4.1–4.7][SR]) | The parent's tree code is the escape surface | Generated reader, structural validator, trees bound to a frame's endpoint, Rust without `unsafe`, adversarial-peer fuzzing (6.5, AX-D4) |
| Firefox 2026 re-entrancy bugs | Script ran during an AT action and freed what accessibility code held ([SR 4.8][SR]) | AT actions must not run script synchronously | Actions are queued tasks that enter the input pipeline (AX-D11) |
| Firefox 2026 derived-table bugs | Cached table structures went stale after `aria-owns` moves ([SR 4.9][SR]) | Derived structures must be recomputed, not patched | Tables, relations and reverse indexes are recomputed in the renderer per update and validated in the broker; the broker caches nothing derived across updates without rebuilding it |
| CVE-2017-5464 | DOM and accessibility trees fell out of sync ([SR 8.9][SR]) | Events from live mutation callbacks drift | Events come from the diff of two immutable trees per flush (AX-D15) |
| Servo with AccessKit | Adapter panics on out-of-order updates; no iframes ([HP 4.7][HP]) | Ordering errors must be survivable | Updates carry sequence numbers; a gap triggers a resync, never a panic (6.5) |
| Edge Legacy | UIA-only; no meaningful AT access at launch; still slow two years later ([PA 2.9][PA]) | AT vendors move slower than browsers | UIA and IA2 both, from the first Windows release (AX-D7) |
| AOM | AT events and virtual nodes abandoned because they reveal AT use ([HP 5.2–5.3][HP]) | Privacy kills accessibility APIs that leak | No web-exposed API depends on AT state; AT actions are full input sequences with published residuals (AX-D3, AX-D11) |
| Live regions | Fail on timing and registration; browser and AT pairs differ ([HP 5.4][HP]) | Announcement semantics belong to the engine | Announcements computed deterministically from diffs; same records for every platform (AX-D15) |
| `ariaNotify` design | Timing must not reveal a screen reader; spam and spoofing are risks ([SR 5.7, 7.3–7.4][SR]) | Constant-time API, provenance, rate limits | No return value; same work with or without AT; per-frame rate and length limits; content speech suppressed while focus is in browser UI (6.7) |
| WebKit isolated tree | Off-main-thread reads, but fallbacks to a busy main thread made VoiceOver unresponsive ([PE 1.19][PE]) | Any fallback to the producer's busy thread reappears as a hang | The broker's mirror has no fallback to the renderer at all |
| Newton | Push, frame-synchronized, surface-relative coordinates, hit regions client-side ([PA 6.1–6.4][PA]) | The OS-level future looks like Izcal's internal protocol | The update format is designed so a Newton-style transport can carry it (AX-D12) |
| Flutter web | Accessibility off until a hidden button is pressed ([HP 4.9][HP]) | An opt-in that users must discover is exclusion | No user step enables accessibility; cost is controlled by levels, not by asking |
| Overlays (accessiBe) | FTC order in 2025 for claiming compliance ([HGBA-3 B10][H3]) | Repairs are an aid, never a compliance claim | Repairs stay in B8's overlay with provenance and one-toggle revert (AX-D17) |
| WebAIM Million | 95.9% of home pages fail; ARIA pages average 59.1 errors against 42 ([UX 2.1–2.4][UX]) | Authors will get ARIA wrong at scale | Deterministic, documented handling of every author error; DevTools explains each one (AX-D16, AX-D22) |

## 6. Design

### 6.0 Architecture at a glance

```
 PRODUCERS: site renderer per (site, container) · shell renderer · prompt renderer · DevTools panes · PDF process
   script thread ──mutations──▶ mutation log (B4, JS-D4)
   update the rendering: style ─▶ layout ─▶ paint      (identical at every semantic level)
   flush n ends: log segment n sealed + style semantic records; fragment epoch n immutable
                                   │ O(1) pointer handoff to consumers (AX-D3)
                                   ▼
   semantic thread (exists only above L0): hg-semantic-input applies segment n (bootstrap:
                      fuzzy snapshot + log replay); reads epoch n fragments under a bounded pin
                                   │
                                   ▼
                      hg-semantic   (pure function: inputs at flush n → author tree; accname, AAM tables,
                                     relations, tables, text runs; incremental, from-scratch reference)
                      hg-semantic-repair (overlay, G8)   hg-semantic-agent (feature `ai`, G4)
                                   │ per-field diff + event records + hit regions, stamped n
                                   ▼
                      SemanticUpdate (.hgidl; bulk in sealed read-only shared memory)
 ═══════════════════════ endpoint bound to (site, container, frame) by the broker ════ TB-2
 BROKER
   hg-ax-mirror   copy-then-validate (IPC-R7), structural invariants, apply; one mirror per document;
                  stitches documents through broker-minted embedding tokens; composes coordinates with
                  broker-held frame geometry, window placement and device scale
   hg-ax-focus    surface focus (shell, page frames, prompt), F6, per-document focus memory
   hg-ax-events   event records → platform events; coalescing; rate and provenance rules
   hg-ax-uia │ hg-ax-ia2 (with MSAA) │ hg-ax-mac │ hg-ax-android   ← OS queries, answered from the mirror only
          (Windows: on a broker accessibility thread, or in a UI host per AX-D9; Linux: hg-ax-atspi in a sandboxed
           bridge process that maps mirror versions read-only, AX-D12)
          └── actions (default action, focus, set value, scroll, set selection) → SemanticAction → owning renderer
   hg-ax-prefs    OS accessibility preferences, caption styles, text scale → environment-snapshot inputs
 ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ OS
   UI Automation · MSAA + IAccessible2 · NSAccessibility + AX text markers · AT-SPI2 (a11y bus) · AccessibilityNodeProvider
```

Three properties carry the design. **The renderer computes; the broker only mirrors**: semantics (roles, names, relations) are computed once, in the process that holds the page, and the broker never runs an accname step or reads a web format (IPC-R15). **No OS query ever waits on a renderer**: every synchronous question is answered from the broker's mirror (G102), which is what made Firefox 12–21× faster ([HP 2.17][HP]). **The script thread does the same work whether or not anyone is listening**: the semantic computation runs on its own thread from inputs the engine produces at every level, and AT actions arrive as the input sequences they stand for; the residual channels (contention, memory, the user's own AT behaviour) are stated and measured, not claimed away (AX-D3, AX-D11, AX-15).

### 6.1 The semantic tree: what a node is (AX-D2)

The data model lives in `hg-semantic-model` (L2), the one crate both renderer and broker link. A **semantic node** has:

| Field | Representation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Id | `SemNodeId` = 32-bit slot + 32-bit generation, unique per document | DOM-backed nodes take the DOM node's slot and generation (B8 stable IDs); synthetic nodes (text runs, list markers, pseudo-elements, anonymous table parts, scrollbars, native-control parts) take ids from a per-document synthetic range keyed by (owner id, kind, ordinal), so they are stable across flushes |
| Role | 16-bit enum generated from the pinned ARIA, HTML-AAM, SVG-AAM, MathML-AAM, DPUB and Graphics tables, plus Izcal-internal roles for things no spec names (6.2) | The ARIA role string is kept as an interned atom for `xml-roles` and `AriaRole` ([SS 5.2][SS]) |
| States | 64-bit set plus tri-states (checked, pressed, expanded, selected, current, invalid kind, haspopup kind, sort, orientation, autocomplete) | Managed states (focused, offscreen, focusable, editable, multiline, visited, busy, modal) are computed, never authored |
| Name, description | Interned strings with a **source** tag (`aria-labelledby`, `aria-label`, host label, contents, tooltip, repaired) and a **visible-text flag** (does the name contain the rendered text of the node?) | The flag serves WCAG 2.5.3 checks and agent provenance ([UX 5.1][UX]; [SR 9.7][SR]) |
| Value | Text, or a numeric quadruple (min, max, now, step) in exact decimal form, plus `aria-valuetext` | — |
| Relations | Small arrays of node ids: labelledby, describedby, details, errormessage, controls, flowto, owns (resolved), activedescendant, label-for (reverse), and the reverse of each | Resolved once per flush by one IDREF resolver shared with the DOM's element reflection (6.2) |
| Structure | Parent id, child-range index | Accessibility children per ARIA §7.3: `aria-owns` applied, `generic` kept (pruning is per platform, 6.6) |
| Geometry | Rectangle relative to its **offset container** (the nearest scroll container or transformed box), in 1/64 CSS px fixed point (LY-D3's `LayoutUnit`); the container carries scroll offset and a transform | Scrolling and transform animation update the container only ([PE 4.1–4.2][PE]) |
| Text | For text leaves: UTF-8 text, a grapheme and UTF-16 offset index, line-start table, per-cluster advances (at level 3 only), text attributes as runs (font, size, weight, style, colours, decoration, language, spelling and grammar marks, `mark`/`insertion`/`deletion` role ranges) | Per-character geometry comes from `hg-text`'s cluster map through layout (A-S1-44) |
| Table and grid | Row and column indices and spans, header links, `aria-rowcount` and `aria-colcount`, index text | Computed in the renderer; the broker never derives table structure ([SR 4.9][SR]) |
| Live region | live, atomic, relevant, busy; container values inherited | Feeds announcement computation (AX-D15) |
| Other | Heading level, list position and size (computed), language (with R1's hint), keyboard shortcuts, role description, braille label and role description, placeholder, `ElementInternals` default semantics, DOM id and class (for DevTools and `aamtest` lookup), action set (default, focus, increment, decrement, expand, collapse, scroll-into-view, show-menu, set-value, set-selection) | — |
| Overlay | Optional reference into `hg-semantic-repair`'s sparse map | Never mixed into author fields (AX-D17) |

**Membership.** A node is in the tree when ARIA §7.1–7.2 and HTML-AAM say so, over the **flat tree** (shadow roots and slots as AccName and HTML say, [SS 3.6, 7.7][SS]) and computed style: `display: none`, `visibility: hidden | collapse`, `content-visibility: hidden` content, `inert` and modal-dialog blocking exclude; `aria-hidden="true"` excludes except for the focused node and on the root element (ARIA 1.3) ([SS 2.7–2.8, 2.13, 4.7, 6.2–6.4][SS]). `content-visibility: auto` skipped content stays in the tree, built from style and DOM only, with no layout work the visual path did not do (LY 6.9, LY-16, [CSS Contain 2 §4.6](https://drafts.csswg.org/css-contain-2/#cv-a11y), [SS 6.2][SS]); its bounds are "not determined", as LY 6.9 marks them, and adapters expose such nodes as offscreen with no coordinates; whether styles are computed for skipped subtrees is the style spec's rule, applied identically at every level (amendment, 9.5). `display: contents` elements keep their semantics as CSS Display requires ([SS 6.1][SS]); their bounds are the union of their children's.

**Canvas, media and embedded content.** A `<canvas>` exposes its fallback subtree as first-class content; `drawFocusIfNeeded()` sets the fallback element's bounds; canvases without fallback content are flagged in DevTools; HTML-in-Canvas, if admitted, places real elements and needs nothing extra ([GO 10.1–10.8][GO]). Native media controls are UA shadow trees with roles and names (MD-12). PDF content arrives from the PDF process as another producer (6.0).

**Order.** Children are in flat-tree order, then `aria-owns` re-parenting, then `reading-flow` reordering when the property is admitted (LY gives the order, [SS 6.7][SS]). **Top-layer elements** (modal dialogs, popovers, the customizable-select picker) stay at their flat-tree position, with a `top-layer` state and a `modal` state on modal dialogs; no specification says otherwise ([SS 6.6, 11.9][SS]), and keeping DOM position preserves reading order and relations. This is decision AX-O9 in 6.3's table, recorded as a black-box observation of where the shipping engines place them (Q-AX-7).

### 6.2 Mapping tables, names and the twenty open points (AX-D16)

**Generated tables.** `hg-ax-tables-build` reads the pinned snapshots of WAI-ARIA, Core-AAM, HTML-AAM, SVG-AAM, MathML-AAM, DPUB-AAM and Graphics-AAM (their mapping tables have fixed row labels: 146 element entries, 97 role entries, [SS 4.1, 5.1][SS]) and emits `hg-ax-mappings`: element-to-role rules, role properties (name from, children presentational, required context, supported states), per-platform role, state, relation and event mappings, and the anchor of every row (G9). The output is standards data carrying the W3C Software and Document License notice, allowed by HGBA-2 2.3.2's standards-body exception ([SS 10.1][SS]). A spec snapshot update regenerates the tables and the diff is reviewed like code (M-1 part 2). No role mapping is written by hand outside the generator's input of Izcal decisions (G103).

**Names (AccName 1.2).** `hg-semantic::accname` implements the computation steps 2.1–2.10 one function per step, each anchored ([SS 3.1][SS]), over the semantic input store. It is a pure function of the inputs at a flush, with:
- a **visited set** keyed by (node id, traversal kind), which defines "consulted only once" across `aria-labelledby`, `aria-owns`, shadow and slot paths (AX-O2, [SS 3.7][SS]);
- an explicit **depth bound** of 256† and **output bound** of 64 KiB† per name, with truncation at a grapheme boundary, because the specification sets none ([SR 8.1][SR]); both are measured against the corpus (no corpus name is expected to come near) and against the shipping engines black-box (Q-AX-10);
- a **dependency record**: every node whose data the name read, so the incremental engine (6.3) knows which names a mutation can change. This is the reverse index that Firefox's Gmail name-change gap lacked ([PE 4.6][PE]).

**Descriptions** follow AccName §4.2's precedence ([SS 3.11][SS]). HTML-specific naming follows HTML-AAM §4, with localized fallback strings from the shell's Fluent bundles ([SS 3.12][SS]).

**The open points.** The specifications leave twenty points open ([SS 11][SS]). Each gets an Izcal decision, a test, and an entry in DevTools' explanation (AX-D22). The default rule is: **follow the majority of shipping engines where they agree (measured black-box), otherwise choose what preserves the author's visible text and the DOM order**.

| AX-O20 | Icon glyphs in CSS `content`, `::marker` in names | Private-use code points are dropped from names unless the content has CSS alt text; `::marker` contributes as AccName says | accname#39; WPT 2–6 of 10 ([SS 8.8][SS]) |

Each decision is a row in `hg-ax-mappings`' decision input with its rationale and test; changing one is a spec change.

**IDREF resolution.** One resolver in `hg-dom` serves `ariaLabelledByElements` and friends and logs resolved targets for the semantic plane: content attributes resolve within one tree scope; explicitly set elements are weak and outward-only; Reference Target redirects inward through hosts ([SS 7.2–7.6][SS]). The DOM spec owns the resolver; this spec requires that the semantic plane and element reflection call the same function (amendment, 9.5).

**SVG's inputs.** `SvgSemanticSource` is the one interface through which `hg-semantic` reads what SVG-AAM's rules need: inclusion inputs, each name's sources, bounds and text (SVG spec 6.12, amendment R12). An SVG image's content is never in the tree; the `<img>`'s text alternative is. A top-level document in SVG's scriptless interactive mode is exposed like any document: its static document's own style scope and change log feed the semantic thread.

### 6.3 When and where the tree is computed (AX-D3)

The tree is computed on its own thread from inputs the engine produces **whether or not anyone is listening**, so no script-thread work depends on the semantic level. Draft 0.1 copied the inputs in a cut-off window beside paint; the design critique showed that paint is not specified to run inside JS-D4's script-blocked window, that the copy competed with paint for workers, that a large first copy could never finish, and that its idle-time fallback was itself a signal ([CR 1][CR]). The design below replaces it.

**Three input streams, all level-independent.**
1. **DOM inputs** come from the **mutation log** (B4 as restated by JS-D4: records with old and new values), which the DOM produces at every level for style invalidation and recording. At the end of each flush the flush's log segment is **sealed** (made immutable) and handed by pointer to every registered consumer; the handoff costs the same O(1) at every level. The records the semantic plane needs (element creation and removal, child-list changes, attribute changes with values, text changes, `ElementInternals` default semantics, explicitly set attr-elements as node ids with generations, focus, selection, top-layer changes) must therefore be logged as values, including the state the JavaScript engine keeps in its cage, which no worker may read (JS-D4, H-2; amendment to the DOM spec, 9.5).
2. **Style inputs** come from **semantic change records** that the style pass emits for nodes whose semantically relevant computed values changed (`display`, `visibility`, `content-visibility`, `content` and its alt text, `inert`, `speak-as` if admitted, writing direction, `text-transform` for DevTools only), through a `semantic` bit in the style spec's "affects" mask. The records are emitted at every level into the same sealed segment, so their cost does not depend on AT.
3. **Geometry** comes from the **fragment store** of the flush, which is immutable (LY 6.13). The semantic thread holds a read **pin** on that flush's fragment epoch, defers its reclamation for at most **2 flushes†**, and reads border boxes, offset containers, line records and inline items through `hg-fragment`'s read API (G38). If the pin would be held longer, it is released and the document re-reads geometry from the current epoch. Pins exist only above L0, so they shift reclamation timing by at most two flushes; that residual memory channel is stated in 6.14 and tested by AX-15.

**Bootstrap.** When a document escalates from L0, the semantic thread needs the whole current state without blocking script. It takes a **fuzzy snapshot**: it records the current log sequence number *s*, reads every node's columns with relaxed atomic loads (tear-free per field, never across fields), resolves interned strings from the append-only atom tables, and then replays every sealed segment from *s* on. Replaying the log over a fuzzy read yields exactly the state at the replayed flush, the same technique databases use for fuzzy checkpoints. Node slots are generation-checked, and slot memory stays mapped while a reader is active. This needs one amendment to JS-D4: node columns are stored as naturally aligned words written with relaxed atomic stores, which compile to ordinary loads and stores on x86-64, arm64 and riscv64, and the bootstrap reader is the one reader allowed outside a flush (9.5). Bootstrap runs at background priority on the semantic thread and publishes progressively: the top of the tree first, then subtrees in document order, so an AT gets a usable tree before a large document is complete (AX-12).

**The semantic thread.** One thread per renderer process, created on the first escalation and exiting 10 minutes† after its last document returns to L0, at the scheduler's background priority. It applies sealed segments in order to a **semantic input store** (the attributes, text and values it needs, by node id), then runs `hg-semantic`: membership, roles, names, relations, tables, text runs, events, live-region announcements, hit regions; then the repair overlay (when enabled) and agent extras (feature `ai`, when an agent is attached). It owns the **semantic store**, a per-document arena of node records (class (a) under FP 6.4). It never writes the DOM, style or fragment stores, and nothing on the script thread or the scheduler can read whether it exists (G105). Segments it has not consumed are retained for it; above **64 MiB†** of retained segments per document it drops them and re-bootstraps, so retention is bounded.

**What content can observe.** Nothing on the script thread runs differently with the level: the log, the semantic change records and the segment handoff are the same at L0 and L3; there is no extract, no cut-off and no idle flush. What remains is contention for cores, caches and memory bandwidth by the semantic thread, and the bounded reclamation delay of fragment pins. Both are residual channels (6.14), measured by AX-15 against an adversarial page suite.

**Why this placement.** Four options were weighed (6.18): (a) computing on the script thread, as Chrome and Firefox do: a direct timing signal, and up to 3.6× the main-thread cost of building a large DOM in Chrome's full mode in a black-box test ([PE 8.3][PE]; Chrome itself calls the cost "quite high", [Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/chromium-accessibility-performance)); (b) copying inputs inside the flush, the 0.1 design, rejected for the reasons above; (c) a constant-work extract run for every document at every level, which removes the signal by making everyone pay; and (d) the log-fed design above, which makes everyone pay only for what the engine produces anyway (log records with values and a few style records). **(d) is recommended**, with (c) as the reversal if AX-15 fails. The cost of (d) is latency: the tree trails the DOM by the time the semantic thread takes to apply a segment, typically within the next frame. Chrome's tree already lags the renderer "by a fraction of a second" ([PE 1.2][PE]); Izcal's lag is visible (every update carries its flush number) and measured (AX-17).

**Determinism.** The tree published for flush *n* is a pure function of the sealed segments up to *n*, the style records and the epoch-*n* fragments: there is no timing-dependent choice inside it, so no new JS-D15 decision point is needed. When the semantic thread publishes relative to OS queries is a broker-side fact, recorded with the AT session when one is recorded (6.15). The tree hash per flush joins LY-40's replay check.

**Incremental and from scratch (B5).** The semantic engine keeps, per node, the hash of its inputs and the dependency record of its name and description. A dirty node recomputes its own fields, and every node whose dependency record names it recomputes its name. Membership changes propagate to ancestors (children lists) and to relations that pointed at removed nodes. In CI, every frame's incremental tree is compared with a from-scratch build over the same inputs (INC rule, AX-4). Pathological fan-in (one label referenced by 10,000 nodes) is a bounded cost per update, measured by AX-22.

### 6.4 Activation: levels driven by what clients ask (AX-D6)

There is no global "accessibility on" switch. Each document has a **semantic level** that the broker sets from what its clients actually do:

| Level | Contents | Entered when | Typical client |
|---|---|---|---|
| L0 | Nothing computed; no semantic thread | No client has asked anything about this document | Most sessions |
| L1 | Focus chain (focused node and its ancestors), form controls with names, values and states, the document's title and URL | A client asks for the focused element, or for form fields | Password managers, IMEs, text expanders, Voice Access's first query |
| L2 | The full tree with roles, names, states, relations, structure and node bounds; text leaves' text but not their per-cluster geometry | A client walks children beyond the focus chain, asks for a search, or registers for structure events; macOS `AXEnhancedUserInterface` or `AXManualAccessibility` set; Android `AccessibilityManager` with an accessibility tool | Screen readers, voice and switch control |
| L3 | L2 plus line records, per-cluster advances and text attributes | A client asks for text ranges, character bounds, text attributes or markers | Screen readers in browse mode, magnifiers, braille |

The broker escalates a document as soon as a query needs the next level, answers the triggering query with what the mirror holds (for UIA and IA2, an empty or partial result is legal; for macOS, a valid but childless element), and fires the platform's "document changed" or "layout complete" event when the higher level arrives, so browse-mode readers rebuild their buffer. Level 2 and 3 are usually reached within one or two flushes of the first query (AX-12 measures it). Levels fall back to L0 after **10 minutes†** with no query and no event listener, or when the OS says no client listens (UIA `UiaClientsAreListening` and AdviseEvents, AT-SPI event registrations, Android `isEnabled`, macOS VoiceOver and Switch Control state), which is the auto-disable Chrome found worthwhile on Android ([PE 1.8][PE]).

**Privacy of level changes.** Level changes alter only work on the semantic thread and the bounded fragment pins, so no script-thread work changes (AX-D3); AX-15 tests level transitions as well as steady levels. Escalation is per document, so a client looking at one tab does not raise every renderer's cost.

**Print-to-PDF.** The PDF writer tags its output from the accessibility tree (PD-D18), so printing is a client too: the print document's tree is built in full over the paged fragment tree when the user prints. The user started it, so it carries no AT signal; its cost is measured with the print path ([CR 21][CR]).

**Developer and test clients.** DevTools, WebDriver's computed role and label, `aamtest` and agents request L2 or L3 through their own channels (IIP, BiDi, agent host); they are clients like any other and leave no content-visible trace either.

### 6.5 The protocol: `SemanticUpdate` and the broker's mirror (AX-D4, AX-D5)

**Message.** `SemanticUpdate` is a `.hgidl` message on the frame's bound endpoint (IPC-R2) with: document id (broker-minted), sequence number, flush number, level, and a payload in sealed read-only shared memory (Stage 1 4.1; IPC-R7) holding per-field node diffs (created, removed, and changed fields by field id), string-table additions, the event records of 6.7, the hit-region list (6.6), focus and selection, and a hash of the resulting tree (for the mirror's self-check in CI builds). Field diffs are encoded in the generated fixed wire format; no self-describing payload exists (IPC-R3). Strings are UTF-8, validated, length-bounded (64 KiB† for a name or description, 1 MiB† for a text leaf, splitting longer leaves).

**Validation in the broker.** `hg-ax-mirror` copies the payload into private memory, runs the generated reader (Kani: no panic, round trip, bounded allocation, VER-1), then a **structural validator** before applying anything:
1. sequence numbers are contiguous; a gap or a repeat closes the stream and asks the renderer for a full resync (never a panic, unlike the Servo adapter, [HP 4.7][HP]);
2. ids are in the document's range, never id 0, never reused while live (generation checked);
3. every created node has exactly one parent; no change makes a node its own ancestor (checked with the mirror's depth index in O(depth) per moved node, with depth bounded by rule 8); children lists match parent fields; an update moves at most **4,096†** existing nodes, and a larger move is sent as a subtree remove and insert, so validation cost per update is linear in its size;
4. every relation target exists in the same document after the update, or is dropped;
5. text offsets, line-start tables and cluster arrays are monotonic and consistent with text length; attribute runs cover the text without overlap;
6. rectangles are finite and within ±2²⁶ CSS px† of the offset container (larger values are clamped, as layout clamps);
7. **embedding**: a node may declare that it hosts a child document only by quoting an **embedding token** the broker minted for that frame's child navigable in *this* renderer's frame (6.6); any other token is a bad message;
8. **size bounds**: nodes per document ≤ 2²²† (about 4.2 million; the HTML specification has 0.89 million, [PE 8.1][PE]); tree depth ≤ 4,096†; mirror bytes per document ≤ 512 MiB†; mirror bytes for the whole browser ≤ min(2 GiB, 25% of RAM)†.

A failure of rules 2–7 is **structural** and is a bad message: the endpoint closes and the renderer is terminated (IPC-R5); the shell shows the usual crashed-frame UI. A failure of rule 8 is **not** a bad message, because a legitimate page can be large: the renderer is told the bound, cuts the offending subtree below a placeholder node with role `group`, name "Content too large to expose" (localized) and a DevTools entry, and continues; the browser-wide bound evicts the mirrors of documents in hidden tabs first (they are rebuilt on return). Large pages therefore never crash only for AT users ([CR 5][CR]). This answers each 2026 bug shape directly: self-referential trees, ID-0 cycles, double parents, cross-actor forgery and inconsistent message variants ([SR 4.4–4.7][SR]). The validator is a pure function over the mirror and the update, property-tested and fuzzed (IPC-R14) with an adversarial renderer in `hg-dst` (IPC-R17), and its "no cycle accepted" property is proved with Kani for bounded trees.

**Apply.** Updates apply atomically to a **new mirror version** (persistent, structurally shared node arrays), and the adapters' query threads read the version current when the query started. A query never sees half an update, and the broker's UI thread is never blocked by an apply. Platform objects and text markers hold (document id, node id, generation), never a version, and resolve against the current version on each call; a query holds a version only for its own duration, at most **4†** versions are live per document, and a query that would need an older one is answered from the newest.

**What the mirror holds.** Exactly what the renderer sent for the document's level, plus broker-side composed data: the document's position in the stitched tree, absolute geometry computed on demand from offset containers, the frame's position in the window from the broker's own frame geometry (LY 6.14's parent-supplied geometry, never renderer-claimed), the window's screen position and device scale. Renderers never learn screen coordinates (a fingerprint), and the mirror never takes them from a renderer.

**Memory.** The mirror stores compact records (6.13); text is stored once, in the mirror; the renderer's semantic store drops leaf text after sending it and keeps hashes for diffing, and text-change events take their offsets from the mutation log's old values, which the semantic input store already holds ([CR 25][CR]). Both stores are class (a).

### 6.6 Stitching, hit testing and focus across processes (AX-D5)

**Stitching.** The broker owns the frame tree (Stage 1 4.3). When a document embeds a child navigable (iframe, `object`, `embed`) or a broker-registered embedded surface (a PDF document's pages from the PDF process, PD-D11, and any later surface kind), the broker mints an **unguessable embedding token** per (parent frame, child navigable, epoch) and sends it to the parent renderer, which places it on the host node. The mirror attaches the child document's root under that node if and only if the token matches the broker's record and the child document is the navigable's active document; navigation increments the epoch, so updates for a dead document are dropped, not grafted (Servo's epoch lesson, [HP 4.7][HP]). Same-process child documents use the same mechanism, so there is one path, as in Chrome ([PE 2.1–2.2][PE]). The token is never shown to the child, and the child learns nothing about the parent's tree.

**Platform ids.** Platform-visible ids are derived in the broker: UIA runtime ids and AT-SPI object paths from (window, document id, node id), with document ids broker-minted and unrelated to site identity. A client sees one tree per window; it cannot learn which nodes share a renderer.

**Hit testing.** Each update carries a **hit-region list** for the document, relative to offset containers so a scroll changes only container offsets (AX-18): the paint-ordered list of nodes with on-screen regions (rectangles and, for transformed or clipped content, quads and clip rectangles), taken from `hg-paint-order` and the fragment tree (LY 6.14), with child-document regions as placeholders resolved through the stitched tree. The broker answers `ElementProviderFromPoint`, `accHitTest`, `AXUIElementCopyElementAtPosition`'s callee, `Component.GetAccessibleAtPoint` and Android's touch exploration from these lists, top layer first, as Firefox does and Newton chose ([PE 2.5][PE]; [PA 6.4][PA]). There is no synchronous renderer hit test. Regions carry exact shapes where `hg-paint-order` has them (rounded rectangles and clip paths in its bounded path format), not only boxes. The broker **clamps** every node's bounds and hit regions to the frame's broker-computed clip, recursively, as LY 6.14 requires for input hit-test regions, so a compromised child renderer cannot place nodes over shell, prompt or parent content ([CR 12][CR]). Points over content with `pointer-events: none` fall through as paint's hit testing says. The lists are sent at level 2 and above and only for documents in a visible tab.

**Focus.** `hg-ax-focus` in the broker is the single authority for **surface focus**: which of the shell document, a page frame or the prompt holds focus (CON-R3 already routes input through the broker). Each document reports its own focused element in its updates (focus inside a document is the renderer's; which surface holds focus is the broker's); the platform focus is the focused element of the deepest focused surface, and a focus event fires only when that changes ([PE 2.3][PE]). F6 and Shift+F6 cycle the shell's regions and the page, restoring each document's last focus (shell 4.9; [UX 4.7–4.8][UX]). A renderer can never move OS focus into the shell or another frame; script focus moves only within its own document and into its child frames through HTML's focus steps, which the broker checks against the frame tree.

### 6.7 Events and announcements (AX-D15)

**From diffs, not callbacks.** The semantic thread compares tree version *n* with *n−1* and emits **event records**: node created, removed, moved; field changed (by field); focus changed; selection and caret changed; value changed; text inserted and removed (with offsets); live-region announcement; document load states; menu open and close; scroll. Each record names the flush number. Records derive from two immutable trees, so the DOM and the tree cannot drift apart the way CVE-2017-5464 did ([SR 8.9][SR]), and duplicate events from overlapping mutations do not exist (Chrome's reason for moving to dirty-marking, [PE 1.4][PE]).

**Editing and composition events are not diffed.** Editable-text events come from the editing spec's `TextEdit` records, which carry the before and after state and the `inputType`, so an AT sees one event per edit rather than a guess reconstructed from a tree diff. Composition events come from the IME protocol, so an AT sees the composition's updates and its commit in the order the input method produced them (editing spec 6.14, ED-D5).

**Platform mapping.** `hg-ax-events` maps records to platform events with the Core-AAM §3.8 tables (generated): WinEvents and IA2 events, UIA property, structure, focus, text and notification events, AT-SPI signals, NSAccessibility notifications, Android `AccessibilityEvent`s ([SS 5.6–5.9][SS]). It raises UIA events only when clients listen ([PA 1.9][PA]), coalesces per platform (Android scroll events at most every 100 ms† and a single content-changed event above 5†, starting values that Chrome's prose reports, [PE 3.10][PE], measured against TalkBack under Q-AX-12), and orders events strictly after the mirror version they describe is visible, so no event ever names a node a client cannot yet query.

**Live regions, computed by the engine.** When a live region's subtree changes between versions, the semantic thread computes the **announcement** itself from ARIA's `aria-live`, `aria-atomic`, `aria-relevant` and `aria-busy` rules: the text to speak, its politeness and its source node. A region is live from the moment it enters the tree, independent of when an AT registered; a pre-populated region announces nothing on load (only changes are announced, which matches the specification and removes the browser-dependent part of the "why are my live regions not working" list, [HP 5.4][HP]). Each platform gets **one channel** per announcement source, so nothing is spoken twice ([CR 15][CR]): author live regions travel as the platform's live-region and text-change events (IA2 and AT-SPI with `container-live` attributes, UIA `LiveRegionChanged`, macOS `AXLiveRegionChanged`), because the major screen readers compute their own speech from those; `ariaNotify` and browser announcements use the notification primitive (UIA `UiaRaiseNotificationEvent`, AT-SPI `Announcement`, NSAccessibility `announcementRequested`; [PA 1.12, 4.11][PA]). The engine-computed announcement text is still produced for every live-region change, for DevTools and for platforms or readers that the AT lab shows need it (AX-9 checks duplicate speech).

**`ariaNotify`.** `Element.ariaNotify` and `Document.ariaNotify` enqueue an announcement record on the script thread in constant time, return nothing, do the same work whether or not any client exists, and are recorded B1 inputs; the record travels with the next update, and at L0, where no update exists, the record is dropped after the same constant work ([SS 2.4][SS]; [SR 5.7][SR]). Pending records per document are bounded at 64† (oldest dropped). [Core-AAM §4.1.1](https://w3c.github.io/core-aam/) gives the platform mapping ([SS 5.10][SS]); the [ariaNotify explainer](https://github.com/MicrosoftEdge/MSEdgeExplainers/blob/main/Accessibility/AriaNotify/explainer.md) warns that optimizing away its synchronous part would reveal a screen reader.

**Abuse limits.** Per document: announcements are capped at **8 per second†** and **2,000 characters†** per announcement after whitespace collapse, longer text truncated at a sentence or grapheme boundary; above the rate, announcements of the same priority coalesce to the most recent ([SR 7.4, 8.6][SR]). Cross-origin iframes may announce only with the `aria-notify` permissions-policy feature (default allowlist `*` as ARIA specifies, [SR 7.5][SR]). **Content announcements are held while surface focus is in the shell or a prompt** (the explainer's spoofing mitigation, [SR 7.3][SR]) and delivered, newest first and at most 4†, when focus returns to the page, so a chat message is not lost while the user is in the address bar ([CR 27][CR]). Announcements from the shell and prompt renderers carry browser provenance, which the broker sets from the endpoint's bound kind (IPC-R2, IPC-R12) and never from a field in the message, which the adapters express where the platform can (the UIA notification's activity id `izcal.browser`, an AT-SPI property, the macOS announcement attributes) so ATs can voice them differently. The limits, and Android's scroll-event interval of 100 ms† and coalescing above 5† content-changed events (values Chrome's design prose reports, [PE 3.10][PE], taken here as first-principles starting points), are tuned against screen readers' measured event budgets (Q-AX-12).

### 6.8 The text model (AX-D14)

The AAMs do not specify text ([SS 5.12][SS]), and AccessKit's authors call rich text the unsolved part ([PA 9.4][PA]). Izcal's model is designed from the four platforms' requirements.

- **Leaves.** Text lives in **text leaf** nodes (one per text run after `display` and inline formatting, including generated content and list markers); a text control's leaves read the editing model's piece table rather than a copied string (editing spec 6.14). Each has a stable id, its UTF-8 text, an index from grapheme boundaries to UTF-8, UTF-16 and code-point offsets (the four platforms count differently; the expected units, UTF-16 for IA2 and macOS and characters for AT-SPI and Android, are confirmed black-box at AX-M0), its attribute runs and, at L3, its line-start table and per-cluster advances.
- **Positions.** A **text position** is (leaf id, grapheme-aligned offset, affinity, mirror version). It never points inside a grapheme cluster. Platform positions wrap it: IA2 and AT-SPI offsets are computed per container by walking leaves (embedded objects count one U+FFFC each, as hypertext requires; the U+FFFC rule and each platform's offset unit are confirmed black-box at AX-M0, [PE 6.1–6.2][PE]); UIA ranges are pairs of positions; **macOS text markers** are `AXTextMarkerCreate` blobs containing (document id, leaf id, offset, affinity, version, and a keyed MAC so a forged marker from another app is rejected), using the public marker C API ([PA 4.6–4.8][PA]); Android uses node-relative offsets.
- **Boundaries.** Grapheme boundaries are computed in the broker with `hg-unicode`'s UAX 29 tables. **Word and sentence boundaries come from `hg-text` in the renderer**, which applies language tailoring and the dictionaries for Thai, Lao, Khmer and Myanmar (TX-D12) that plain UAX 29 lacks, so AT navigation agrees with the page's own editing and selection (TX's R24; [CR 14][CR]); at L3 they are sent as per-leaf break tables, validated for monotonic order like line tables. Lines come from the line-start tables. Nothing is cached per character in the broker beyond these tables.
- **Geometry.** Character bounds come from the cluster advances relative to the leaf's line box, so `BoundsForTextMarkerRange`, `IAccessibleText::characterExtents`, `ITextRangeProvider::GetBoundingRectangles` and AT-SPI `GetCharacterExtents` are answered locally. Offscreen and skipped text has no character bounds (Android returns null for offscreen characters, [PA 7.4][PA]).
- **Caret and selection.** The renderer reports the caret position and selection ranges as text positions in each update; the Windows adapter mirrors the caret into a **Win32 system caret** of the caret's size so magnifiers follow it ([PA 3.4][PA]). AT requests to set caret or selection become `SemanticAction`s (6.9), which the renderer performs through the editing and selection paths, so NVDA's native selection mode works ([UX 3.5][UX]).
- **Editable text.** `contenteditable`, `textarea` and text inputs expose editable text with insert and delete events from `TextEdit` records (6.7); edits requested by an AT (UIA `ValuePattern.SetValue`, IA2 `IAccessibleEditableText`, AT-SPI `EditableText`) go through `beforeinput` and `input` as user edits do. The editing spec owns the commands; this spec owns their exposure, including the spelling and grammar marks carried as text attributes.
- **Search.** Each adapter answers search queries (UIA `FindAll` with conditions as a server-side walk, AT-SPI `Collection.GetMatches`, macOS `UIElementsForSearchPredicate` with all 42 public search keys, IA2 relations) on the broker's mirror, so structural navigation is one call ([PA 4.9, 5.9][PA]).

### 6.9 Actions from assistive technology (AX-D11)

Every AT action is a `SemanticAction` message (node id, action, arguments, a broker-assigned action id) sent asynchronously from the broker to the renderer that owns the node; the adapter returns "accepted" to the OS when the node exists and supports the action, as Chrome does ([PE 4.10][PE]). In the renderer, the action is a **recorded B1 input** queued as a task on the event loop, never run synchronously inside an IPC handler, so no script can run while accessibility code holds references (the 2026 re-entrancy bug class, [SR 4.8][SR]).

**Input equivalence (G106).** Each action is executed as the **full input sequence** of the user input it stands for, so that content sees what a keyboard or pointer user's own input would produce. Core-AAM §3.7 asks only to "simulate a click"; a bare `click` with no key or pointer events around it is exactly how sites detect AT today, so Izcal synthesizes the whole sequence ([CR 3][CR]):

| AT action | Executed as |
|---|---|
| Default action (Invoke, `DoDefaultAction`, `AXPress`, AT-SPI `DoAction(0)`, `ACTION_CLICK`) on a focusable element | Focus the element as sequential navigation would (if not focused), then the keyboard activation sequence for its role: `keydown` Enter (or Space for checkboxes, radios and switches), `keypress` where UI Events fires it, the activation behaviour's `click` (`detail` 0), `keyup`; key timing drawn from the substrate's seeded entropy within the distribution of real key presses (recorded B1 input), matching [Core-AAM §3.7](https://w3c.github.io/core-aam/#mapping_actions)'s "simulate a click" ([SS 5.11][SS]) |
| Default action on an element that is not focusable (a `div` with a click handler) | The pointer sequence of a primary click at the centre of its first visible hit region: `pointerover`, `pointerenter`, `pointerdown`, `mousedown`, `pointerup`, `mouseup`, `click`, with the pointer left there, as a mouse user's click would |
| Focus | Sequential-focus-navigation focus (`focus` and `focusin` with `:focus-visible` matching), no scrolling beyond what keyboard focus scrolls |
| Set value (sliders, spin buttons) | The arrow-key or Page Up/Down sequence (`keydown`, `keyup`) the control's own keyboard behaviour takes to reach the value |
| Set value (text) | `beforeinput` and `input` with `insertReplacementText`, as dictation and autocorrect produce |
| Expand, collapse, increment, decrement | The keyboard sequence of the control's role when native; the default action otherwise |
| Scroll into view, scroll by page | The user scroll path (compositor scroll, `scroll` and `scrollend` events), respecting `prefers-reduced-motion` |
| Set caret or selection | The selection's user path (`selectionchange`), as caret browsing or a pointer selection moves it |
| Show context menu | The `contextmenu` event as the keyboard menu key produces it, preceded by its `keydown` |

**Residual differences, published rather than claimed solved.** Content may still notice: focus jumping to an element with no preceding Tab presses (a screen reader's quick navigation), a click on a non-focusable element with no prior pointer movement, key timings drawn from a model rather than a hand, and activation of elements a sighted keyboard user could not reach. These are what the user's AT does, not what the engine adds; AX-23 measures the engine's part against the full keyboard and pointer sequences, and 6.14 lists the residuals. Where synthesizing keys changes behaviour (a page's own Enter handler runs), that is the behaviour a keyboard user gets, which is the point.

**Consent prompts.** An AT action on a node of the **prompt renderer's** tree arrives at the broker's adapter, but the caller can be any program allowed to use the OS accessibility API: any process on the AT-SPI bus, which has no access control (Flatpak apps included), any medium-integrity UIA or MSAA client, any macOS app with the accessibility permission, any Android accessibility service, and desktop AI agents that drive these APIs ([SR 3.1–3.11][SR]; [CR 4][CR]). Treating such an action as a person's approval would reopen the case CON-R1 closes. The rule therefore depends on the prompt's class:
- **Low-risk prompts** (notifications, autoplay, pop-up permission): an AT action counts as broker-observed input, under CON-R5's 500 ms† rule.
- **High-risk prompts** (credentials, payments, agent confirmations, automation attach, the protected-media switch): an AT action counts only with **evidence that a person is present**: a broker-observed physical input in the same window (any key, switch or pointer event) within the 30 s† before the action, or an OS-attested assistive state where the OS provides one and the user turned it on (macOS VoiceOver, Switch Control or Voice Control enabled; Windows Narrator or Voice Access running as UIAccess-signed processes). Voice and switch users therefore keep a non-keyboard path, which AX-40 checks with them.
This is proposed to the security owner as a change to CON-R1 rather than recorded unilaterally (9.5); AX-25 tests both the positive paths and scripted non-AT clients that must fail.

### 6.10 The platform adapters

All adapters share `hg-ax-platform`: the read API over a mirror version, the projection rules (which semantic nodes become platform objects, from the generated tables), and the action and event plumbing. Each adapter is a crate listed in `unsafe-inventory.toml` with a named owner (G5), because COM, Objective-C and JNI calls need `unsafe`; the rest of the accessibility stack has none.

**Windows (AX-D7).** Each top-level window, owned by the broker (CON-R3), has a child HWND for its accessibility surface, created on a dedicated broker **accessibility thread** that is not the input-routing thread. `WM_GETOBJECT` and in-process MSAA and IA2 calls therefore run on the accessibility thread, so a screen reader building a large buffer never delays input routing ([CR 13][CR]); input messages that reach the child HWND are forwarded to the routing thread unchanged. On `WM_GETOBJECT`: `UiaRootObjectId` returns the UIA fragment root; `OBJID_CLIENT` returns the MSAA/IA2 root. Both are views over the same mirror. AX-11 bounds input-routing latency while NVDA builds the HTML specification's buffer.
- **UIA**: fragment, fragment root, the control patterns the role tables name (Invoke, Toggle, ExpandCollapse, Value, RangeValue, Selection and SelectionItem, Grid and GridItem, Table and TableItem, Scroll and ScrollItem, Text and TextRange with TextPattern2, LegacyIAccessible), `AriaRole` and `AriaProperties`, `IsControlElement` and `IsContentElement` set per HTML-AAM's UIA rules ([SS 4.9][SS]), notification events. Providers use `ProviderOptions_UseComThreading` with free-threaded objects, so UIA calls are answered on COM threads reading a mirror version, not on the UI thread ([ProviderOptions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/api/uiautomationcore/ne-uiautomationcore-provideroptions); [PA 1.4][PA]).
- **MSAA + IAccessible2 1.3**: `IAccessible`, `IAccessible2_2/_3`, `IAccessibleText2`, `IAccessibleHypertext2`, `IAccessibleEditableText`, `IAccessibleTable2`, `IAccessibleTableCell`, `IAccessibleValue`, `IAccessibleAction`, `IAccessibleRelation`, `IAccessibleComponent`, `IAccessibleImage`, `IAccessibleApplication`, object attributes (`xml-roles`, `container-live` and friends) and IA2 events. In-process clients (NVDA's and JAWS's injected code) call these on the thread they are on; the objects read immutable mirror versions, so in-process calls are lock-free and fast, which is what NVDA's virtual buffer needs ([PA 2.3–2.4][PA]). The IA2 object and text attributes that NVDA and JAWS rely on beyond the AAMs are recorded at AX-M0 by `hg-ax-probe` enumerating Chrome's and Firefox's IA2 and UIA output on the corpus, black-box, as observations ([CR 22][CR]). What NVDA, JAWS, Orca and TalkBack expect beyond that (the attributes, events and call patterns their browse modes depend on) is learned only from their documentation (user and developer guides, NVDA's `nvdaHelper` readme), vendor liaison, issue discussions and the AT lab's **characterisation suite** (AX-D25): `hg-ax-ref` serves ablation pages that add or remove one attribute, event or convention at a time, and the lab records each reader's speech, braille and browse-mode output against Chrome's and Firefox's on the same page. No screen reader's source is read. Whether Izcal must register an IA2 proxy/stub for out-of-process IA2 clients is measured at AX-M0 (Q-AX-3).
- **IA2 interface definitions** (AX-D8): the COM interface identifiers and method layouts are interface facts needed for binary compatibility. Written from the IAccessible2 1.3 reference documentation, which is itself generated from the BSD-3-Clause IDL, they are in substance a transcription of the interface. The owner is asked to classify them under M-1 part 1 as an OS-interface definition (the class that already covers the Windows SDK), together with Apple's `AXWebConstants.h` constants (9.4); any IA2 proxy/stub (Q-AX-3) falls under the same decision.
- **Accessibility software injection** (AX-D9): NVDA and JAWS load their helpers into the process that owns the window ([SR 2.2–2.3][SR]), so a mitigation that blocks them in that process removes browse mode. The security scope leaves the broker's code-integrity and extension-point blocking "to be measured at M5" with a separate window-owning UI-host process as its fallback; this spec keeps that gate and designs both outcomes to the same depth ([CR 7][CR]). **(a)** If NVDA, JAWS, Dragon, ZoomText and Magnifier work with a mitigation on, it stays on in the broker. **(b)** For each mitigation they break, the window, the accessibility thread, a read-only mapping of the mirror and the in-process MSAA/IA2 surface move to a **UI-host process**: it owns the HWNDs and receives OS input, forwards raw input events to the broker as untrusted data, and holds no grants, cookies, credentials or consent state; **it also hosts TSF and its text store**, so third-party input-method DLLs leave the broker without gaining anything (editing spec ED-D5, which amends TX-D23); the broker keeps CON-R1's judgement, hit-testing forwarded input against prompt rectangles it placed. The UI host parses no renderer data (it maps the broker's validated mirror read-only), so injected AT code and the renderer-facing parser never share a process. Renderers, the shell renderer and the prompt renderer keep full CIG and win32k lockdown in every outcome, since no AT needs them ([SR 1.3][SR]). The decision criteria are measurable: mitigations lost, assets reachable from injected code, input-routing latency and AT-attributable crash rate (Q-AX-2). Crash reports name loaded AT modules only on the device, or with per-report consent, because AT use is disability data ([CR 16][CR]).
- **System settings**: `SPI_GETHIGHCONTRAST` and system colours, animation, transparency, caret width, focus border, `TextScaleFactor` and caption properties feed `hg-ax-prefs` ([PA 3.5–3.10][PA]).

**macOS (AX-D10).** The broker's `NSWindow` content view returns an `NSAccessibilityElement` tree from the mirror: role, subrole and the macOS 26 public web constants (`AXWebArea`, the 59 web attributes, 32 parameterised text-marker attributes, 8 web notifications, 56 web subroles) and `UIElementsForSearchPredicate` with the 42 search keys ([PA 4.7–4.10][PA]); `NSAccessibilityCustomRotor` where it helps. AppKit services requests on the main thread, so every getter reads a mirror version in microseconds and never blocks ([PA 4.5][PA]). The constants are strings and are implemented for every supported macOS version; behaviour where the header gives only names (marker validity after mutation, "next" across element boundaries) is set by a black-box observation of VoiceOver with a test provider (Q-AX-5), never by reading another engine.

**Disclosing repairs (HGBA-3 Q-10).** A repaired name or role reaches each platform as the repaired value, and the adapters can disclose it in three ways, to be chosen by A11Y-3's disclosure arm: a suffix on the description ("name inferred by Izcal", works everywhere), an object attribute (IA2 and AT-SPI `izcal-repaired:true`, UIA `AriaProperties`, a macOS custom attribute; silent unless ATs adopt it), or both. The adapters implement all three behind a setting until the study decides ([CR 30][CR]).

**Generated labels (ML service amendment, 21 October 2026).** A name or description produced by the accessibility models (R4's **ML-IMG**, ML service spec 9.3) carries `generated: true` and the **model id** as semantic-field metadata, alongside the repaired source tag, so a generated label is never presented as author text and the disclosure can name the model. The flag and id are broker-side metadata, never page-observable; the model rows are the ML service spec's, not this spec's (9.3).

**Linux (AX-D12).** AT-SPI, unlike the Windows and macOS APIs, does not need the window-owning process: any process can register on the accessibility bus, and no AT injects code. Every request on that bus comes from an unauthenticated local client ([SR 3.1][SR]). The AT-SPI adapter therefore runs in a **sandboxed accessibility bridge process**, the better first-principles placement ([CR 6][CR]): it maps the broker's mirror versions read-only from sealed shared memory (no copy), holds the only connection to the bus, and sends actions to the broker as typed messages that the broker validates like any other. The bridge connects to the accessibility bus (address from `org.a11y.Bus`), registers with `Socket.Embed` when `org.a11y.Status.IsEnabled` is true, and exports objects under `/org/a11y/atspi/accessible/<doc>_<node>` with the Accessible, Component, Text, EditableText, Hypertext, Hyperlink, Table, TableCell, Selection, Value, Action, Image, Document, Collection and Application interfaces, `Cache.GetItems` for bulk load, and the event signals ([PA 5.3–5.10][PA]). Coordinates are window-relative first (Wayland has no global coordinates, [PA 5.11][PA]). D-Bus is spoken through `zbus` (MIT); the interface definitions are written from the AT-SPI developer documentation. Registration is limited to documents, the shell and the **native recovery window**, including when the engine is down (shell SH-30); nothing is exported for hidden tabs' documents at L0. When a Newton-style push protocol ships, the bridge sends the same per-field diffs over it. The broker-hosted placement stays the alternative, with memory and latency measured against the bridge at AX-M0.

**Android (AX-D13; delivered with S-8).** The platform-neutral parts (projection, prefetch rules, sensitive-field marking) are built from AX-M0 with the other adapters; the Android adapter itself is verified and shipped when Izcal has an Android port and a device lab to verify it against (S-8), which is a dependency, not a schedule choice ([CR 23][CR]). The browser activity's content view hosts an `AccessibilityNodeProvider` subclass (a small Java class, the only non-Rust code, calling into Rust through `jni`, MIT OR Apache-2.0) that serves `createAccessibilityNodeInfo` from the mirror with tab-unique virtual ids, supports the prefetch flags (up to 50 nodes, [PA 7.3][PA]), text granularities and per-character location extras, and marks password and payment fields `setAccessibilityDataSensitive` so only declared accessibility tools read them ([SR 3.11][SR]). Text scale follows Android 14's non-linear curve (6.11).

**iOS** is not a target. If it becomes one, BrowserEngineKit's remote-element bridge would let a renderer serve its subtree directly; this spec's default would still be broker-served for the repair overlay and provenance ([PA 8.5][PA]).

### 6.11 Preferences, forced colours, motion, zoom and captions (AX-D18, AX-D19)

**One preference record.** `hg-ax-prefs` reads the OS settings on start and on every change signal (`WM_SETTINGCHANGE` and UISettings events, `accessibilityDisplayOptionsDidChangeNotification`, the XDG settings portal's `SettingChanged`, Android configuration and listener callbacks) into one typed `AccessibilityPreferences` record: forced colours (on/off and the palette's system colours), contrast (more, less, custom, none), reduced motion, **Izcal's global and per-site motion level (R, S, X) with the flash-protection setting**, Android's duration scale where present (internal only), Apple's separate image and flash settings, reduced transparency, inverted colours, colour scheme, text scale (as a curve, not a scalar), caption style (fonts, sizes, colours, opacities, edge style, display type), caret width, focus-ring width, minimum font size (ST-D17), and the user's Izcal-level overrides (shell settings, R0). Unknown is a value ([PA 10][PA]). Every change is a **recorded B1 input**. The record reaches renderers as part of the style spec's environment snapshot, the one place D-S1's quantization is applied ([ST 6.10][ST]); [Media Queries 5](https://drafts.csswg.org/mediaqueries-5/) names these features a fingerprinting risk ([SR 6.1][SR]).

**Exposure policy (R0).** HGBA-3 B10 says R0 preferences are exposed "exactly as the specs require" and "not normalized by anti-fingerprinting". This spec makes that precise: media features expose only their specification values (`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, `prefers-contrast: more | less | custom`, `forced-colors: active`, `prefers-reduced-transparency`, `inverted-colors`), never finer data; `env(preferred-text-scale)` is bucketed into 7 steps, the count Chrome's published prose gives ([UX 7.5][UX]); the step values are set here from first principles (0.85, 1, 1.15, 1.3, 1.5, 1.75, 2†) until a black-box measurement of the shipping engines (Q-AX-17) shows whether matching them reduces entropy; minimum font size is exposed only through the computed sizes it produces; forced-colours system colours are the user's real palette, because forced colours are "highly observable via other means" and cannot be hidden ([SR 6.4][SR]). **R0 holds in the Hardened profile (S-4a) exactly as in the Standard profile.** Only Izcal's opt-in full-uniformity profile (D-S1) reports the preference media features' defaults uniformly and the shell says that pages will not see the user's preferences while forced colours, zoom and captions still apply to rendering; in that profile the motion-level R behaviours follow the reported value and the shell says reduced motion then stops working, while levels S and X remain explicit user choices with a warning that they are detectable (animation spec 6.10, AN-55); there are no per-query toggles ([SR 6.5][SR]). PRIV-2 reports the entropy these add (Q-AX-11).

**Forced colours.** Style computes the CSS Color Adjust 1 forced values and paint draws backplates (GR-D25, [UX 6.2][UX]); the semantic tree is unaffected (colours reach the text attributes as forced). The shell and prompts render under the same mode (`forced-colors` is not special-cased for browser UI; [UX 9.2][UX]). On macOS, Linux and Android, where the OS has no forced-colours mode, Izcal's shell offers one with a few fixed palettes (light, dark, and two high-contrast palettes†) rather than custom colours, because no other browser reports `forced-colors: active` there and a custom palette would be close to unique; the shell says that pages can see the mode, and PRIV-2 reports its entropy ([CR 26][CR]).

**Reduced motion and transparency.** When set, the engine itself: disables smooth scrolling for user and AT scrolls ([UX 6.8][UX]), pauses `marquee` and **inline SMIL** (the same case: UA-driven motion authors cannot gate with a media query; SVG images render their t = 0 sample with MD-D19's play control, SVG spec 6.9), applies MD-D19's animated-image and muted-video controls, and the shell drops its own animations and backdrop blur (GR-D25's proposals to the shell, accepted here as requirements on the shell).

**Zoom and text scale (AX-D19).** Three factors, carried in the environment snapshot: **page zoom** (CSS px to device px, reflows, per site), **text scale** (scales font sizes, reflows), and **pinch scale** (visual viewport only). The OS text scale sets the default text scale: pages opting in with `<meta name="text-scale">` get `medium` = 16 px × OS scale, and `env(preferred-text-scale)` reports the bucketed scale ([UX 7.5–7.6][UX]); legacy pages get the OS scale as **page zoom** on every OS (as Windows browsers do today, and unlike macOS browsers, which ignore it, [UX 7.4][UX]). Android's and Windows' non-linear curves are applied per font size through the style spec's font-size computation, not as one multiplier ([PA 3.8, 7.8][PA]). LY-D11's scrollbars stay 15 device-independent px at any zoom.

**Captions.** OS caption styles (macOS `MACaptionAppearance`, Windows `ClosedCaptionProperties`, Android `CaptioningManager`; an Izcal setting on Linux, which has no OS API) are applied by the media stack with one precedence rule ([CR 20][CR]): as MD 6.12 says, the OS style applies to the caption properties the page has not styled; where the user has marked a property as overriding content (macOS's per-value behaviour; a "my caption style always wins" setting in the shell on the other platforms), the user's value wins over the author's. The styles are a user-origin `::cue` sheet and are not observable by script, as WebVTT requires ([UX 8.1–8.4][UX]); MD 6.12 is amended to add the override case (9.5). Generated captions are MD-D25's, computed by the ML service's **ML-ASR** under the accessibility grant (ML service spec ML-D4, ML-D5): the captions path records the **grant-state signal (Q-ML-14)** and its **ML-M3** measurement, because it is the one accessibility signal a page can observe through the media surface, and the accessibility owner accepts that signal before the caption path ships.

### 6.12 Caret browsing, keyboard and the shell (AX-D20, AX-D21)

**Caret browsing.** F7 toggles **engine-provided caret browsing** on `hg-caret` in content and in the shell: a caret drawn in non-editable text, moved by grapheme, word and line with `hg-unicode` and layout's line records, focusing links and controls it passes as the HTML focus steps allow, and exposed to ATs as the caret (Orca's browse mode depends on it, [UX 3.9, 4.1–4.3][UX]). The first F7 shows a confirmation, as the shipped implementations do.

**Keyboard and focus.** `:focus-visible` follows HTML's heuristic; the modality decision is a recorded input. Sequential focus includes links by default (the Chromium habit most screen-reader users have, [UX 4.5][UX]).

**The shell and prompts.** The shell renderer and the prompt renderer are ordinary semantic producers (UP4). The broker composes each window's root: the window node, the shell document (toolbar, tab strip, panels as landmarks per shell 4.9), the page's stitched tree at the shell's web-view placeholder (embedding token), and the prompt document above both when shown. Prompts announce their content with browser provenance when they appear and are reachable with F6; they take focus only when the user's own action raised them (for example "Save password"), and never when a page's request did, so a page cannot steal a screen-reader user's focus with a permission request ([UX 9.5][UX]). UI-1 and UI-2 apply unchanged, and AX-41 adds a screen-reader task study for the shell.

**Extension surfaces (registered 21 October 2026).** The extension UI that the shell owns is in the AT bar on the terms the shell already has: every Ring A slot (the toolbar action button and its popup, an extension's options page, its menus, and the tier-3 tab badge and site-panel state) is reachable by keyboard and AT shortcut and carries a computed name and role from the same labelling rules as any shell surface (6.2, 6.9; AX-D11; the shell UI design's Extensions page). An extension-provided name is exposed as the extension wrote it and explained as **extension-provided** by AX-32's name-explanation rule, never passed off as a browser string; the popup document is a semantic producer like any other extension-origin document, so an inaccessible popup fails the same conformance gates as an inaccessible page. No extension receives AT data, actions or event streams: extensions are a Stage 2 surface with no accessibility-API client status and no `chrome.accessibility*` surface (the extensions spec's 6.14).

### 6.14 Security and privacy

**Trust boundaries.** Renderers are untrusted (ATK-2); the mirror treats every update as hostile (6.5). The broker runs no accname step, no CSS, no text shaping, and parses no web format (IPC-R15): it validates typed records and runs `hg-unicode` grapheme segmentation over validated UTF-8 text. **Local accessibility clients are a second untrusted input**, a trust boundary the security scope does not yet list (amendment, 9.5): every adapter entry point parses requests from other programs (UIA conditions and VARIANTs, IA2 calls, client-supplied text-marker bytes, ranges and search predicates, D-Bus messages, Binder calls). Each entry point validates its arguments as hostile, has a structure-aware fuzz target, and is driven by an adversarial-client mode in `hg-dst` (AX-20; [CR 6][CR]); the AT-SPI surface, the most exposed, is outside the broker (6.10). The OS decides who may read (AT-SPI has no access control, macOS gates only clients, Windows UIPI and UIAccess apply, [SR 3.1–3.6][SR]); Izcal decides what it exposes and what a client's request may cause (6.9's consent rule).

**What is exposed.** Exactly what the specifications map, with three restrictions: password field values are never exposed (only their length as bullets, as native fields do); autofill previews are not in the tree until accepted (CON-R4 keeps them out of renderers anyway); and URLs in the tree are the node's own `href` after the renderer's normal resolution, never cross-origin data the renderer holds for other reasons. The VoiceOver password-reading CVEs ([SR 4.12][SR]) are the reason for an explicit test (AX-26).

**AT use is private (AX-D3).** By construction, content cannot observe whether any client is attached or at what level through the script thread's work (6.3: the same log, style records and handoff at every level), through `ariaNotify` (constant time, no return, the same work at L0), through `content-visibility` behaviour (LY-16), or through the repair overlay (A11Y-1). Residual channels remain and are stated, not claimed solved: (1) **contention** for cores, caches and memory bandwidth by the semantic thread and, at escalation, the bootstrap; (2) **memory**: the semantic store and mirror use memory, and fragment pins delay reclamation by at most two flushes; the semantic store and pins are charged to an accessibility budget that does not move other stores' discard thresholds in the footprint spec's pressure ladder, so only real OS memory pressure can couple them; (3) **AT actions**: the residual differences listed in 6.9; (4) **behaviour** the user's own AT produces (browse-mode focus patterns, [SR 5.12][SR]). AX-15 bounds (1) and (2) against an adversarial page suite; AX-23 bounds the engine's part of (3).

**Agents (AX-D24).** With AI on (B10), `hg-semantic-agent` adds, per node, origin and frame provenance (from the document's binding), the visibility flags of B8, and a **label-in-name mismatch flag** (name not containing the rendered text), which is the injection path that hidden `aria-label` text and faint text open ([SR 9.3–9.5, 9.7][SR]). Agents get stable-ID diffs and scoped snapshots by default, because a full snapshot of an ordinary page is 8k–50k tokens and the HTML specification about 2.9 million ([PE 7.4][PE]). The agent view never changes what ATs receive.

### 6.15 Observability: the semantic domain (AX-D22)

The DevTools spec's DT-D6 is rebuilt on Izcal's own stream (amendment, 9.5). The IIP **`semantic` domain** offers:
- the author tree, the repaired tree and their diff; per-node fields with their source (attribute, `ElementInternals` default, computed);
- a **name explanation**: rerunning AccName with tracing for one node, returning each step taken, the nodes read and the spec anchor of each step, and any AX-O decision used ("why does the screen reader say this?");
- **platform projections**: how a node appears to UIA, IA2, AX and AT-SPI, computed by running the adapters' projection code over the mirror, so a developer on macOS sees what NVDA would get;
- the **event log**: event records and the platform events actually raised, including coalesced and rate-limited ones, with their reasons;
- an **AT query log** for the local user only, while DevTools is open: which queries the screen reader made and how long each took;
- a **timeline** linking each tree change to its mutation and flush (DT-D6), and focus history;
- checks: missing names, label-in-name mismatches, heading-level skips, contrast on rendered pixels (GR's O9), all from the tree and paint data.

All of it is computed only while a DevTools session holds a grant for that target (DevTools 6.5), costs nothing otherwise, and lives outside feature `ai`. A **content-free local diagnostics surface** (`about:diagnostics`, shell 6.14) shows the AT bridge state and the last 20 accessibility events; no page can read it (shell amendment, 21 October 2026). The AT query log, the event log's AT-specific entries and the platform projections reveal that the local user runs AT, so they are served only to the local user's own DevTools frontend, never to remote CDP or BiDi clients or to agents ([CR R21][CR]).

**AT session recording.** Under DET-1 recording, adapter queries and actions are recorded broker-side as inputs, so a screen-reader session against a page replays exactly in DST: the page side replays from its inputs, the broker side replays the queries. A bug report from a screen-reader user can then be reproduced without the screen reader. AT actions recorded in an ordinary DevTools recording are AT-specific inputs: they are excluded from any export unless the user includes them, and the export manifest says so (a new REC-R rule, 9.5; [CR 16][CR]). An AT session recording is proof that the user runs AT and holds page content, so it is made only on the user's explicit request, stays on the device, and is shared only by the user ([GO 11.8][GO]); the screen reader's own speech is outside the replay, and can be attached through AT Driver's `capturedOutput` when the user runs one.

### 6.18 Alternatives

| Decision | Alternatives | Recommendation | Reversal trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Where the tree is computed (AX-D3) | (a) script thread; (b) inputs copied inside the flush (draft 0.1); (c) a constant-work extract for every document at every level; (d) a semantic thread fed by level-independent inputs (sealed log segments, style semantic records, pinned fragment epochs) with a fuzzy-snapshot bootstrap | **(d)** | AX-15 (c) fails, or the JS owner declines the atomic-column amendment: (c), costed against A11Y-4 and FP-2 for every user |
| How OS queries are answered (AX-D4) | (a) mirror in the broker; (b) synchronous IPC to renderers; (c) direct OS access from renderers (iOS remote elements, UIA in content) | **(a)** | None foreseeable: (b) is Chrome's dead end and Firefox 57's regression; (c) cannot cross out-of-process iframes and breaks sandboxing ([HP 3.3][HP]) |
| Activation (AX-D6) | (a) one global mode on AT detection; (b) always on; (c) demand-driven levels per document; (d) L1 always computed, demand-driven L2 and L3 | **(c)** | AX-12's correctness clause fails: pre-escalate to L3 on any client that reads the root's children; AX-28 fails: tighten triggers; if L0→L1 transitions are found observable under AX-15: (d) |

Where the specifications are silent (AX-O1 to AX-O20, text behaviour of markers), behaviour follows recorded black-box observations of shipping engines and screen readers, never their source.

**Instruction sets.** Nothing in this spec is ISA-specific: the semantic crates, the mirror and the adapters are portable Rust over OS interfaces, with no SIMD or generated code. CI builds and tests the renderer and broker accessibility crates on x86-64, arm64 and riscv64 (owner decision of 19 October 2026), the AT-SPI adapter runs with Orca on a riscv64 Linux rig in the AT lab, and AX-30's replay hashes are compared across the three ISAs as DET-1's cross-platform rule requires.

**Evidence rule.** No research note or section of this spec cites any third-party project's source code (HGBA-2 2.3.3 M-1 part 3): no engine, AccessKit, screen reader (NVDA, JAWS, Orca, TalkBack, VoiceOver, Narrator), library, tool or OS component. Chromium's and Firefox's prose documents in repositories and NVDA's `nvdaHelper` readme were read as prose under OD-7; OS SDK headers are used only to call the interfaces they declare (AX-D8), and counts first taken from them are black-box measurements at AX-M0; every value the prose did not state is a black-box measurement (Section 11). Screen-reader behaviour comes from documentation, issue discussions and the AT lab (AX-D25).
